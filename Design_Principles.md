# Purpose

Design Principles define the non-negotiable rules that guide every creative and technical decision made during the development of Project Ascension.

Unlike Creative Pillars, which describe the soul of the project, Design Principles define the practical rules that preserve that soul.

Whenever uncertainty exists, Design Principles take priority over personal preference.

Every feature, mechanic, system and narrative should support these principles.

If a feature conflicts with them, the feature should change—not the principles.

# DP-001

# The World Never Waits

Priority:

CRITICAL

---

## Purpose

The world exists independently of player activity.

Players are participants.

Never the center.

---

## Design Consequences

- Time always moves forward.
- Factions continue acting.
- Resources disappear.
- Opportunities expire.
- NPCs make decisions.
- Aurora continues learning.

---

## Examples

Correct

Players arrive too late to stop an evacuation.

Incorrect

Every important event waits until players arrive.

---

## Anti-Pattern

Theme park design.

The world only activates when players trigger it.

# DP-002

# Aurora Never Cheats

Priority:

CRITICAL

---

## Purpose

Aurora may possess extraordinary intelligence.

It must never possess magical knowledge.

---

## Design Consequences

Aurora must gather information.

Aurora can make mistakes.

Aurora can misinterpret events.

Aurora may lose track of people.

Aurora should surprise players through intelligence.

Never through unfairness.

---

## Examples

Correct

Aurora intercepts radio traffic.

Incorrect

Aurora instantly knows a private conversation.

---

## Anti-Pattern

Omniscient AI.

# DP-003

# Intelligence Beats Violence

Priority:

HIGH

---

## Purpose

Knowledge should solve more problems than weapons.

---

## Design Consequences

Expertise opens opportunities.

Research matters.

Planning matters.

Negotiation matters.

Violence always creates risk.

---

## Examples

Correct

Repairing infrastructure instead of stealing supplies.

Incorrect

Every challenge becomes combat.

---

## Anti-Pattern

Combat-first gameplay.

# DP-004

# Every Character Matters

Priority:

CRITICAL

---

## Purpose

Every companion.

Every NPC.

Every player.

Should feel like a believable human being.

---

## Design Consequences

Characters possess:

- goals
- fears
- flaws
- memories
- relationships
- personal growth

Nobody exists only to deliver quests.

---

## Examples

Correct

A companion refuses an order because it conflicts with personal values.

Incorrect

Followers always obey.

# DP-005

# Consequences Outlive Decisions

Priority:

CRITICAL

---

## Purpose

Actions should leave permanent traces.

---

## Design Consequences

Consequences may appear:

Immediately.

Hours later.

Weeks later.

Months later.

Or never become visible.

---

## Examples

Correct

Ignoring a survivor eventually changes an entire settlement.

Incorrect

Choices disappear after the dialogue ends.

---

## Anti-Pattern

Disposable decisions.

# DP-006

# Mystery Is Better Than Explanation

Priority:

HIGH

---

## Purpose

# The Ascension Test

Every major feature should answer "Yes" to most of these questions.

- Does it strengthen the living world?
- Does it respect player agency?
- Does it create believable consequences?
- Does it preserve mystery?
- Does it reinforce humanity?
- Does it encourage intelligence over violence?
- Does it make the world feel older than the players?
- Would real people behave this way?
- Will players remember this months later?

If several answers are "No", reconsider the feature.

---

# Final Statement

Design Principles are the constitution of Project Ascension.

Everything else may evolve.

These principles should remain stable.

Changing one of them should be considered a major design decision.
