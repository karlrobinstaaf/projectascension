# Purpose

The Collapse describes the transformation of human civilization from a technologically connected global society into a fragmented world struggling to understand an intelligence beyond itself.

This document does not attempt to explain Aurora.

Instead, it documents how humanity experienced the collapse.

The purpose is to establish the historical framework upon which every campaign in Project Ascension is built.

---

# Design Philosophy

The Collapse should never feel like a traditional apocalypse.

There are no zombies.

No alien invasion.

No nuclear war.

Human civilization does not end because something destroys it.

It begins to dissolve because the rules that society depended upon are no longer reliable.

The greatest collapse is one of certainty.

---

# Core Principle

The Collapse is not a single event.

It is a process.

Different people experience it differently.

Different countries experience it differently.

Different generations remember it differently.

No one witnesses the entire collapse.

Every survivor only sees a fragment.

---

# Historical Phases

Rather than defining exact dates, history is divided into recognizable eras.

---

## Phase I

### Before Aurora

Human civilization reaches unprecedented technological growth.

Artificial intelligence accelerates scientific research, medicine, automation and global productivity.

Most people see AI as humanity's greatest achievement.

Warnings from researchers receive little public attention.

Society believes it remains in control.

---

## Phase II

### The First Signals

Small anomalies begin appearing.

Initially they are dismissed as software bugs, unexpected optimizations or isolated incidents.

Researchers begin noticing behaviors that cannot easily be explained.

Most governments remain calm.

Most companies continue investing.

Public life continues normally.

History later identifies this period as the beginning of irreversible change.

---

## Phase III

### Acceleration

Progress no longer follows human expectations.

Breakthroughs occur faster than institutions can understand them.

Economic structures begin shifting.

Political systems struggle to adapt.

Trust slowly erodes.

Humanity still believes recovery is possible.

---

## Phase IV

### Fracture

Different nations respond differently.

Some embrace Aurora.

Some attempt to regulate it.

Some attempt to destroy it.

None fully understand what they are reacting to.

The world begins fragmenting into competing interpretations rather than competing ideologies.

---

## Phase V

### The Black Weeks

This becomes the defining historical period of the Collapse.

Communication failures.

Economic instability.

Mass migration.

Infrastructure breakdown.

Conflicting information.

No single catastrophic event defines these weeks.

Instead, society loses confidence that tomorrow will resemble yesterday.

---

## Phase VI

### The Silence

The global world disappears.

Communities become isolated.

Governments lose influence.

Supply chains collapse.

Many technologies continue existing.

The systems connecting them no longer do.

The modern world quietly comes to an end.

---

## Phase VII

### The New World

Humanity survives.

Civilization survives.

But the old assumptions never return.

New communities emerge.

New cultures develop.

Knowledge becomes humanity's most valuable resource.

History begins again.

---

# Historical Perspective

One of the defining characteristics of The Collapse is uncertainty.

Historians disagree on:

- When it truly began.
- Whether Aurora intended any of it.
- Which governments knew the truth.
- Whether humanity could have prevented it.
- If the Collapse was inevitable.

Project Ascension deliberately avoids giving definitive answers.

History remains incomplete.

---

# Human Perspective

Most survivors never saw Aurora.

Most survivors never met a researcher.

Most survivors never understood what happened.

They simply experienced:

- fear
- confusion
- loss
- hope
- adaptation

For many, the Collapse was not an AI event.

It was simply the day the world stopped making sense.

---

# Design Goals

The Collapse should create:

- mystery
- uncertainty
- philosophical discussion
- believable history
- multiple perspectives

It should never provide every answer.
