# 1. Purpose

Republic already has:

```text
GEOGRAPHY

INFRASTRUCTURE

RESOURCE
PRESSURE

COMMUNICATION
FAILURE

AND

UNCERTAINTY.
```

Now it needs people.

The purpose of this document is to create a small group of Characters who already belong to Republic when the players arrive.

They are not:

```text
QUEST GIVERS

CLUE MACHINES

ENEMIES

OR

STORY ROLES.
```

They are people responding to the same uncertain situation in different ways.

---

# 2. Character Design Rule

Each initial NPC only needs enough definition to answer:

```text
WHO ARE THEY?

WHY ARE THEY HERE?

WHAT DO THEY
KNOW?

WHAT DO THEY
THINK THEY KNOW?

WHAT DO THEY
WANT RIGHT NOW?

WHAT ARE THEY
WORRIED ABOUT?

WHO DO THEY
TRUST?

HOW DO THEY
REACT TO
OUTSIDERS?
```

Anything beyond this should develop through gameplay.

---

# 3. No Predetermined Villain

None of the initial Characters is designated:

```text
THE BAD GUY.
```

Some may be:

- unpleasant
- prejudiced
- selfish
- dishonest
- frightened
- controlling
- opportunistic

Others may be genuinely helpful.

The Game Master should preserve contradiction.

---

# 5. Sheriff Nathan Cole

## Basic State

```text
NAME:
Nathan Cole

AGE:
Approx. 52

ROLE:
Ferry County Sheriff

LOCAL STATUS:
Long-term local

CURRENT LOCATION:
Sheriff's Office /
moving through Republic
```

---

## 5.1 Surface Impression

Cole appears:

```text
CONTROLLED

DIRECT

SUSPICIOUS

TIRED

NOT
PARTICULARLY
FRIENDLY.
```

He dislikes uncertainty.

He dislikes outsiders asking questions when he does not yet have answers.

---

## 5.2 Current Goal

Cole's immediate goal is simple:

> **Prevent the local situation from becoming chaotic before he understands what is happening.**

He wants:

- roads monitored
- unnecessary travel reduced
- emergency services protected
- fuel access kept orderly
- rumors contained

---

## 5.3 What Cole Knows

Cole currently knows more than most residents about:

- local calls
- road reports
- emergency-service activity
- communication failures
- which officers have been able to reach which areas

He does **not** possess reliable national information.

---

## 5.4 What Cole Believes

Cole currently believes:

```text
THE DISRUPTION
IS SERIOUS

BUT

PROBABLY
REGIONAL.
```

This may be wrong.

---

## 5.5 Pressure

Cole is receiving contradictory demands.

People want him to:

```text
TELL THEM
WHAT IS
HAPPENING

KEEP ROADS
OPEN

CLOSE ROADS

PROTECT
FUEL

FIND PEOPLE

CONTACT
STATE AUTHORITIES

AND

STOP PANIC.
```

He cannot satisfy all of them.

---

## 5.6 Outsider Reaction

Cole is noticeably more suspicious of outsiders than locals.

This is partly professional.

Partly cultural.

Potential prejudice may exist, but its exact form should emerge through play rather than being pre-scripted as his defining trait.

---

## 5.7 Important Contradiction

Cole may be genuinely difficult to deal with while also genuinely trying to protect people.

```text
UNPLEASANT

≠

MALICIOUS.
```

# 17. No Character Exists Only for Exposition

Characters should not dump everything they know because players asked one question.

Information should depend on:

- relationship
- context
- trust
- time
- what was asked
- what the Character remembers
- what they want

# 28. Stereotype Principle

Several Characters deliberately present recognizable surfaces.

Examples:

```text
SUSPICIOUS
SMALL-TOWN
SHERIFF

ROUGH
OLD LOCAL

RESTLESS
YOUNG MAN

FRIENDLY
DINER OWNER

STRANDED
OUTSIDER.
```

This is intentional.

Players can quickly understand the social surface.

But:

> **The stereotype is the player's first model of the person, not the complete Character.**

---

# 29. Subversion Should Not Be Automatic

Do not mechanically invert every stereotype.

Bad pattern:

```text

SHERIFF

SECRETLY
SAINT.


FRIENDLY
WOMAN

SECRETLY
EVIL.
```

That becomes another predictable formula.

Sometimes the obvious interpretation is partly correct.

Sometimes it is wrong.

Sometimes it is irrelevant.

# 43. Important Seed Rule

Not every NPC gets a Seed.

Not every Seed involves an NPC.

Not every Seed activates.

And not every Seed is true in the way players first interpret it.

---

# 44. North Star

Republic should feel populated by people who were already living their lives when the world stopped answering clearly.

The sheriff did not wake up to become an antagonist.

The doctor did not arrive to explain the crisis.

The mechanic did not open his business to provide a clue.

The diner owner does not know everything.

The old man is not automatically hiding a secret.

The young mechanic is not destined to disappear.

The traveler is not guaranteed to be telling the truth.

They are simply there.

They know different pieces of the world.

They want different things.

They trust different people.

And then the players arrive.

> **The Game Master does not place people around the story. The story emerges when people collide.**
