---

# 1. Purpose

This Vertical Slice tests the opening experience of Project Ascension.

It is not a scripted adventure.

It is a starting state.

The players begin inside a world where:

```text
SOMETHING
HAS ALREADY
HAPPENED.

BUT

THEY DO NOT
KNOW WHAT.

OR

HOW FAR
IT EXTENDS.
```

The immediate gameplay question is not:

```text
HOW DO WE
SAVE THE WORLD?
```

It is:

```text
WHAT IS
HAPPENING?

WHO CAN
WE TRUST?

AND

WHAT DO
WE DO NEXT?
```

---

# 2. Core Opening Principle

> **The players encounter consequence before explanation.**

They do not watch the initiating event happen.

They wake, arrive, return or otherwise find themselves in a place where normal systems are no longer behaving normally.

Something has changed.

The explanation is missing.

---

# 3. Why This Opening

Project Ascension contains a deep historical and systemic world.

But the players should not need to study that world before becoming interested in it.

The opening therefore begins with immediate uncertainty.

```text
ISOLATION

+

INCOMPLETE
INFORMATION

+

ORDINARY
PEOPLE

+

STRANGE
BEHAVIOR

+

LIMITED
RESOURCES

=

CURIOSITY.
```

The wider history remains underneath the experience.

Players discover it through play.

---

# 4. The World Is Not Yet Known to Be Collapsed

Critical principle:

```text
LOCAL
ISOLATION

≠

GLOBAL
COLLAPSE.
```

At the start, neither the players nor the Characters know whether the problem is:

- local
- regional
- national
- temporary
- deliberate
- accidental
- already resolving
- getting worse

This uncertainty is fundamental.

---

# 6. Why the Northern United States

The region provides:

- geographic isolation
- forests
- lakes
- long roads
- severe weather potential
- small towns
- limited redundancy
- dependence on vehicles
- sparse population
- strong local social networks
- distance from major population centers
- strong visual identity

It also supports the desired atmosphere:

```text
BEAUTIFUL

QUIET

FAMILIAR

ISOLATED

AND

SLIGHTLY
WRONG.
```

---

# 7. Tonal Direction

The desired emotional atmosphere may contain elements reminiscent of:

- northern mystery
- small-town unease
- Fargo-like normality and strangeness
- Twin Peaks-like social ambiguity
- rural isolation

But Project Ascension remains grounded.

The world is not automatically supernatural.

# 9. The Characters' Reason for Being There

The Characters must have a normal reason for being in the region.

The isolation should not require:

```text
THEY ALL
HAPPENED TO
BE PREPPERS
IN A CABIN.
```

Possible starting contexts include:

- work
- research
- conference
- family visit
- remote facility
- vacation
- university activity
- temporary project
- shared journey

The exact reason should emerge from the final Character setup.

---

# 10. No Survivalist Starting Group

The Characters are ordinary capable people.

They have what people plausibly brought with them.

They do not begin with:

- months of food
- tactical equipment
- generators
- weapons cache
- satellite communications
- survival compound

unless a specific Character's history plausibly supports a limited element.

# 12. Information Age

The Game Master should track when information was last verified.

Example:

```text
CELL NETWORK:
Last reliable connection
11 hours ago.

REGIONAL NEWS:
Last confirmed broadcast
7 hours ago.

HIGHWAY STATUS:
Unverified.

NEAREST CITY:
Unknown current state.
```

This immediately makes information a resource.

---

# 13. No Single Reliable Authority

The players may receive information from:

- radio
- local police
- locals
- travelers
- old online data
- family messages
- emergency broadcasts
- businesses
- other vehicles

These sources may contradict each other.

---

# 14. Contradictory Reports

Example:

```text
RADIO:
Highway south remains open.

LOCAL:
State police closed it.

TRUCK DRIVER:
He came through two hours ago.

SHERIFF:
Nobody is leaving town.

ONLINE MAP:
No closure displayed.

LAST UPDATE:
14 hours ago.
```

The player must decide what to believe.

---

# 15. The Game Does Not Announce Which Source Is Correct

The Field Menu may record:

```text
SOURCE

TIME

CLAIM

VERIFICATION.
```

It does not reveal:

```text
TRUE

OR

FALSE.
```

unless the Characters actually verify it.

---

# 16. Something Has Already Happened

The opening should contain visible evidence that the situation began before player control.

Examples:

- fuel shortage
- abandoned vehicle
- closed business
- failed communications
- unusual police activity
- people attempting to leave
- hospital on backup power
- missing emergency service
- repeated broadcast

Not all evidence needs to share one cause.

---

# 19. Opening World Context

The introduction should provide only broad context already known to the Characters.

For example:

```text
AI systems have become
part of everyday life.

Energy infrastructure is
under increasing pressure.

Political arguments about
technology, security and
control dominate the news.

Most people continue
living ordinary lives.
```

No explanation of future Collapse.

No Aurora exposition.

# 20. Opening Image

The first image should establish:

```text
ISOLATION

DISTANCE

WEATHER

ORDINARY
AMERICA.
```

Possible image direction:

A road through dense northern forest approaching a small settlement.

A few lights.

Wet asphalt or snow.

Utility lines.

A gas station.

A diner.

Little traffic.

Nothing visibly catastrophic.

---

# 21. Opening Sound

Possible environmental sounds:

- wind
- light rain
- tires on wet road
- distant generator
- radio static
- occasional vehicle
- forest ambience

Music should be restrained.

Unease should grow through context rather than loud dramatic scoring.

---

# 22. Opening Narration Direction

The opening narration should be short.

Example tone:

> The network went down sometime during the night.
>
> Nobody seems to know exactly when.
>
> Power returned twice this morning and disappeared again less than an hour later.
>
> The radio says the problem is regional.
>
> The man at the gas station says his brother heard the same thing from someone nearly two hundred miles south.
>
> Your phones still show the last messages they received.
>
> None are recent.
>
> The road south is still open.
>
> At least according to the map.

Then:

```text
THE GAME MASTER
STOPS.
```

# 25. They May Choose Not To

Players can decide:

```text
WE STAY.
```

The Game Master must support this.

If remaining becomes dangerous, that danger must arise from world conditions.

Not from the GM forcing the intended journey.

A strong early group discussion is:


The group should not immediately be starving.

- fuel

- food

- transportation

# 34. Community State

Possible local conditions:

- businesses partially open
- limited fuel
- intermittent power
- people gathering information
- nervous families
- local authority present
- some people preparing to leave
- others refusing to leave

Normality and disruption coexist.

# 37. The Sheriff

The local sheriff is a strong early Character.

Surface presentation may be:

```text
SUSPICIOUS

BLUNT

```

But the underlying Character must remain human.

---

# 38. Sheriff Possibilities

The sheriff may be:

- genuinely trying to maintain order
- prejudiced
- frightened
- protecting someone
- abusing temporary authority
- operating on incomplete information
- hiding something unrelated
- correct about some dangers
- wrong about others

The player should not know immediately.

---

# 39. Regional Prejudice

The region may contain Characters who express:

- racial prejudice
- hostility toward outsiders
- class prejudice
- distrust of urban people
- political hostility
- suspicion of government

Such attitudes should emerge through specific Characters and histories.

Never as:

```text
EVERYONE HERE
IS LIKE THIS.
```

---

# 40. Hospitality Can Coexist With Prejudice

A Character may be deeply suspicious of outsiders while still:

- feeding someone
- helping with a vehicle
- protecting a child
- offering shelter

Human contradiction is a primary design goal.

---

# 41. The Diner

A diner is a strong shared-information environment.

It naturally provides:

- conversation
- rumors
- food
- radio
- locals
- travelers
- disagreements

It can become an early information hub without being a quest hub.

---

# 42. The Gas Station

The gas station naturally provides:


Fuel availability should not be guaranteed.

---

# 43. The Motel

A motel can provide:

- temporary shelter
- strangers
- rooms
- parked vehicles
- overheard conversations
- private spaces

It may also produce mystery Seeds.

Nothing requires it to be dangerous.

---

# 44. Local Medical Access

A small clinic, emergency facility or paramedic presence can provide another source of information.

It also introduces early evidence about whether infrastructure disruption has affected:

- medicine
- power
- communications
- emergency response

# 49. Seed D — Southbound Reports

Multiple people claim different things about conditions farther south.


# 50. Seed E — Sheriff Restriction

The sheriff may begin discouraging or preventing certain movement.

The reason should not be immediately obvious.

Possible causes:

- genuine hazard
- incomplete orders
- prejudice
- desire to retain control
- local incident
- misinformation

The player determines how much attention to give this.

---

# 51. Seed F — Character Creation Connection

One Character Creation answer may intersect naturally with the opening.

Example:

A Character fears isolation.

Or has family farther south.

Or recognizes a company name.

Or remembers something about a nearby place.

This must remain subtle.

---

# 52. Character Seed Restraint

Do not activate every player's personal Seed during the first session.

A single meaningful intersection is enough.

The opening should not feel constructed specifically around their questionnaires.

---

# 54. Why This Matters

Players must learn:

```text
STRANGE

≠

IMPORTANT.
```

Otherwise every unusual observation becomes a quest marker.

---

# 55. First Information Problem

The group should quickly face a question where:

```text
TWO
REASONABLE
SOURCES

DISAGREE.
```

There should be no immediate way to know which is correct.

---

# 56. First Human Trust Problem

The players should encounter someone who is:

```text
UNPLEASANT

BUT

POSSIBLY
USEFUL.
```

This teaches that social presentation and reliability are separate.

---

# 57. First Helpful Person

They should also encounter someone who appears warm or helpful.

That person does not need to be secretly dangerous.

The world requires genuine kindness too.

---

# 58. No Universal Suspicion

If everyone is secretly manipulating the players:

```text
THE PLAYERS
STOP CARING
WHO TO TRUST.
```

Trust only becomes interesting when some people actually deserve it.

---

# 59. First Open Roll

A natural early open resolution may arise through:

- repairing something
- persuading someone
- moving an obstacle
- navigating uncertain road conditions
- another player-created action

The test should not force a roll merely to demonstrate mechanics.

---

# 60. First Hidden Roll

Possible hidden checks include:

- noticing someone observing the group
- recognizing an inconsistency
- NPC deception
- perception of a subtle environmental detail

The player should not know a roll occurred.

---

# 61. First Expertise Reveal

At least one Character should automatically recognize something others do not because of Expertise.

Example:

```text

lose power.

```

The Character decides whether to share this.

---

# 62. First Private Information

At least one Character should have the possibility of receiving information the group does not automatically receive.

This validates:

```text

KNOWLEDGE.
```

# 70. Vehicle as Security and Risk

The vehicle is valuable.

It also:

- identifies the group
- requires fuel
- can be blocked
- can break
- can be stolen
- limits some routes

This immediately creates strategic choices.

---

# 71. The Road South

The road south should gradually become psychologically important.

Not because the UI marks it.

Because it represents:

```text
CONTACT

INFORMATION

INFRASTRUCTURE

FAMILY

AND

ANSWERS.
```

---

# 72. Leaving Safety

If the current location appears relatively stable, leaving may itself feel dangerous.

The choice becomes:

```text
KNOWN
UNCERTAINTY

VS

UNKNOWN
ROAD.
```

This is stronger than a forced escape.

---

# 73. Staying Has Consequences

If the group waits:

- fuel may become unavailable
- information may improve
- information may worsen
- roads may change
- new people may arrive
- local authority may harden
- communications may return

The Game Master follows causal state.

---

# 74. Leaving Has Consequences

If the group leaves:

- they lose immediate local support
- they gain mobility
- they consume fuel
- they encounter new information
- they may discover the scale of disruption

Neither path is narratively privileged.

---

# 75. If They Go North

The system must support it.

The world does not say:

```text
WRONG
DIRECTION.
```

They may find:

- deeper isolation
- functioning community
- blocked route
- nothing unusual
- new Seeds

The wider world determines the result.

---

# 76. If They Split Up

Players may decide to divide the group.

This creates:

```text
MORE
INFORMATION
COVERAGE

BUT

LESS
CAPABILITY

LESS
SHARED
KNOWLEDGE

AND

GREATER

```

The Game Master must support separate Character experiences.

---

# 77. Group Size

The opening should work with:

```text
1

TO

6
HUMAN PLAYERS.
```

A larger group has greater:

- Expertise coverage
- carrying capacity
- observation
- social options

But also greater:

- food needs
- transport requirements
- disagreement potential
- visibility

---

# 78. Solo Experience

A solo player accompanied by NPC Characters should still experience disagreement and independent companion agency.

The player does not command NPCs automatically.

---

# 79. Normal Life Must Still Exist

Even during the isolation, the starting community should contain:

- coffee
- food
- jokes
- complaints
- family arguments
- bored people
- children
- routine

The situation should feel unsettling because normal life still exists inside it.

---

# 80. No Immediate Combat Requirement

The opening does not require violence.

A compelling first hour should be possible with:

```text
NO COMBAT.
```

Tension comes from:

- uncertainty
- social interaction
- incomplete information
- resources
- geography
- decision-making

---

# 81. Violence Can Still Occur

If player actions or world conditions create violence:

```text
THE GAME
MUST SUPPORT IT.
```

But violence is consequence.

Not mandatory content.

---

# 82. No Monster Reveal

Do not end the first session by revealing:

```text
THE REAL
ENEMY.
```

The point is that players still do not fully understand what is happening.

---

# 83. Aurora

Aurora should not be obviously present in the opening.

If Aurora-related causality exists somewhere in the wider world, players do not know that.

Do not use:

- mysterious AI voice
- cryptic computer message
- obvious Aurora symbol

as an early hook.

Too much would collapse the mystery prematurely.

---

# 84. Human Mystery First

The first hours should make players wonder about:

```text
THE SHERIFF

THE RADIO

THE ROADS

THE LOCALS

THE POWER

THE MISSING
AMBULANCE

AND

THEIR OWN
INFORMATION.
```

The cosmic/systemic layer comes later.

---

# 85. The World Beyond

The Game Master internally knows enough world state to respond if the players travel.

But the entire country's exact state does not need to be fully simulated at high resolution.

Use adaptive resolution.

---

By the end of the first session, players should naturally be asking:

> **How bad is this outside of here?**

That question can drive enormous amounts of future exploration.

The players know only what their Characters plausibly know.

This should be dramatically less than the Game Master's state.

The GM may not keep changing reality based on player theories.

# 95. Vertical Slice Success Criteria

The opening succeeds if players naturally begin:

- discussing what happened
- comparing sources
- questioning NPC motives
- checking resources
- checking the map
- contacting people
- deciding what to carry
- debating whether to stay or move
- forming theories
- disagreeing with each other

without being assigned objectives.

---

# 96. Strongest Success Signal

The best possible early signal is:

```text
THE GM
STOPS TALKING.

THE PLAYERS
KEEP TALKING.
```

They begin planning without being prompted.

---

# 97. Another Strong Signal

A player says something like:

> "Before we go anywhere, let's figure out what we actually know."

That means the Information system is working.

---

# 98. Another Strong Signal

A player asks:

> "Who has the radio?"

or:

> "Did we leave that in the car?"

That means physical possession is working.

---

# 99. Another Strong Signal

Players say:

> "Maybe the sheriff is right."

after previously distrusting him.

That means human ambiguity is working.

# 115. Closing Principle

The world does not end with an explosion.

At least not here.

The first thing the players notice is that their phones do not work.

Then the power goes out.

Then comes back.

Then disappears again.

Someone says it is a storm.

Someone else says the weather service isn't reporting anything unusual.

The radio announcer says the outage is regional.

A truck driver says he heard Chicago lost power.

The sheriff says the road south is unsafe.

A woman at the diner says her daughter drove it three hours ago.

Neither of them can prove it.

The gas station still has fuel.

Not much.

People are buying more than they need.

Nobody calls it panic.

Not yet.

Outside, the trees move slowly in the wind.

The road disappears between them.

South.

Somewhere beyond those trees are larger towns.

Cities.

Hospitals.

Government offices.

Families.

Answers.

Probably.

The Characters do not know whether leaving is smart.

They do not know whether staying is smart.

They do not know whether the systems will return in an hour.

They do not know whether the rest of the country is eating dinner, watching television and complaining about another temporary outage.

Or whether millions of people are asking exactly the same questions.

The Game Master gives them what they can see.

A road.

A car.

Limited fuel.

A handful of people who disagree about what is happening.

And a world that has stopped answering clearly.

Then the Game Master becomes quiet.

> **What do you do?**
