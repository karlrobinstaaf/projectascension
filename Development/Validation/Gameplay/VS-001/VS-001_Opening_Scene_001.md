# 1. Purpose

This is the first playable scene of VS-001.

Its purpose is not to explain Project Ascension.

Its purpose is to place the Character or Characters inside the world and then release control.

The scene establishes only what the Characters can plausibly observe:

```text
THEY ARE
TRAVELING

↓

THE VEHICLE
HAS DEVELOPED
A PROBLEM

↓

COMMUNICATION
IS UNRELIABLE

↓

REPUBLIC
IS AHEAD

↓

THEY MUST
DECIDE
WHAT TO DO.
```

No quest is created.

No destination is imposed.

No mystery is explained.

---

# 2. Runtime Principle

The opening should be short.

The Game Master should resist the temptation to explain:

- the wider crisis
- Republic
- the United States
- Aurora
- the Emergence Event
- the Seeds
- NPC motivations
- what the players are expected to do

The Characters already lived in this world before player control began.

The players receive only the context necessary to inhabit them.

> **Start with experience, not exposition.**

---

# 3. Scene Architecture

The opening is divided into six runtime layers:

```text
SHARED TV VISUAL

↓

MUSIC / AMBIENCE

↓

SHARED OBSERVATION

↓

GAME MASTER NARRATION

↓

CHARACTER-SPECIFIC INFORMATION

↓

PLAYER CONTROL.
```

These layers must remain epistemically separate.

---

# 4. World Truth Behind the Scene

The Game Master knows:

```text
COMMUNICATION
DISRUPTION

IS REAL.


POWER
INSTABILITY

IS REAL.


THE VEHICLE
PROBLEM

IS REAL.


THE VEHICLE
PROBLEM

IS NOT CAUSED
BY THE WIDER
DISRUPTION.
```

The Characters do not automatically know these relationships.

---

# 5. Geographic State

The Character or group is traveling on a real road approaching Republic, Washington.

The exact road and direction should be selected from the group's established travel origin and intended destination.

The route must respect real geography.

The Game Master must not create a fictional approach road merely to improve the scene.

---

# 6. Temporal State

Recommended opening:

```text
SEASON:
Early November

TIME:
Approximately 09:20

DAYLIGHT:
Full daylight

WEATHER:
Cold / wet

SKY:
Low overcast

ROAD:
Wet

VISIBILITY:
Generally good

TEMPERATURE:
Cold but not immediately dangerous.
```

The opening deliberately occurs during daylight.

Fear is not manufactured through darkness.

---

# 7. World Appearance

The outside world should still appear largely normal.

Players see:

- forest
- wet road
- utility poles
- occasional buildings
- road signs
- normal vehicles
- low cloud
- distant terrain

They do not see:

- burning cities
- abandoned highways
- military convoys
- crowds fleeing
- bodies
- destroyed infrastructure
- obvious apocalypse imagery

---

# 8. Shared TV Visual — Opening

The screen begins black.

No interface is visible.

After a brief pause:

```text
WASHINGTON
```

Then:

```text
FERRY COUNTY
```

Then the date.

The exact year must follow the locked campaign timeline once established.

The text fades.

---

# 9. First Image

The shared television transitions to the view through the windshield of the lead vehicle.

The image should be grounded in the verified geography of the approach to Republic.

Visual characteristics:

```text
WET
ASPHALT

DARK
EVERGREEN
FOREST

LOW
CLOUD

MOUNTAIN
TERRAIN

ROAD
MARKINGS

ORDINARY
TRAFFIC

NO

CATASTROPHE.
```

If two vehicles are present, the second vehicle may be visible where physically appropriate.

Which vehicle is ahead depends on the established travel state.

---

# 10. Visual Fidelity Rule

The image is not generic Pacific Northwest scenery.

Before final game art is generated, the camera position should be based on:

- actual road geometry
- actual terrain
- plausible vegetation
- real settlement density
- real approach direction

> **Reality remains the level design.**

---

# 11. Music

Music begins quietly.

Desired emotional qualities:

```text
DISTANCE

COLD

MOVEMENT

UNCERTAINTY

CURIOSITY.
```

Avoid:

```text
HORROR

DOOM

COMBAT

APOCALYPSE

OBVIOUS
DANGER.
```

The music should not tell players how to interpret the world.

---

# 12. Environmental Sound

Under the music:

- tires on wet asphalt
- windshield wipers
- engine
- ventilation
- occasional road noise
- light rain against glass

If two vehicles exist, radio or phone communication between them only exists if the Characters have actually established such a channel.

---

# 13. First Mechanical Signal

The failing vehicle already has a visible warning state.

Recommended default:

```text
BATTERY /
CHARGING
WARNING.
```

The warning appeared earlier during the journey.

It is now persistent.

---

# 14. Progressive Symptoms

Depending on how long the Characters have continued driving, they may also have observed:

- intermittent electrical behavior
- slightly dimmer lights
- infotainment instability
- unusual instrument behavior
- increasingly weak electrical systems

The vehicle remains mobile at scene start.

---

# 15. Communication State

At least one Character has already noticed unusual mobile service.

Possible observations:

```text
NO DATA

INTERMITTENT
SIGNAL

FAILED CALL

DELAYED
MESSAGE

MESSAGE
STUCK
SENDING.
```

Do not present all symptoms unless they have actually occurred.

---

# 16. Cached Navigation

The group's navigation device or phone retains cached geographic information where plausible.

It can still show:

```text
ROAD

POSITION

REPUBLIC

DISTANCE.
```

It may not provide:

```text
LIVE
TRAFFIC

CURRENT
CLOSURES

BUSINESS
STATUS

REAL-TIME

```

---

# 17. Opening Narration

The Game Master speaks.

The exact wording may adapt to player count and established Character state.

Recommended baseline:

> The warning light came on about twenty miles ago.
>
> At first, it disappeared again.
>
> It hasn't disappeared for a while now.
>
> The car is still running.
>
> Your phones have been less cooperative.
>
> According to the map, Republic is the nearest town with fuel, food and somewhere that might be able to look at the car.
>
> It's a few miles ahead.

Pause.

Do not explain more.

---

# 18. Solo Variant

For one player, the presentation should emphasize factual isolation without melodrama.

The Character is alone in the vehicle.

No NPC voice fills the silence.

The Game Master may add:

> There hasn't been enough traffic to make being alone feel unusual.
>
> Not yet.

Use only if appropriate to the actual road state.

---

# 19. Two-to-Four Player Variant

For 2–4 players:

```text
ONE
VEHICLE

ALL
CHARACTERS
PRESENT.
```

The GM does not narrate how they feel about the problem.

Players may immediately begin discussing it.

That discussion is gameplay.

---

# 20. Five-to-Six Player Variant

For 5–6 players:

```text
TWO
VEHICLES.
```

One required vehicle has the developing fault.

The other remains operational unless prior state says otherwise.

If the failing vehicle is behind, Characters in the lead vehicle do not automatically know every symptom.

They know only what has been communicated or observed.

---

# 21. Two-Vehicle Communication

If the group has hands-free calling or another communication channel, degraded network conditions may affect it.

Players may instead communicate by:

- pulling over
- signals
- following behavior
- direct conversation when stopped

The Game Master does not create telepathic group communication.

---

# 22. Character-Specific Information

After shared narration, the GM evaluates each Character's:

- Expertise
- Profession
- previous experience
- current position
- observations
- vehicle familiarity

Some Characters may receive additional information.

---

# 23. Mechanical Expertise Example

A Character with relevant automotive or engineering capability may privately receive:

> The warning light by itself could mean several things, but combined with the electrical behavior, a charging-system problem is likely.
>
> If the alternator has stopped charging properly, the engine may currently be running on whatever charge remains in the battery.
>
> If that's what is happening, you don't know how much longer you have.

This is not certainty unless the available evidence supports certainty.

---

# 24. No Mechanical Expertise

A Character without relevant knowledge receives no hidden mechanical diagnosis.

They know:

```text
WARNING
LIGHT

+

STRANGE
ELECTRICAL
BEHAVIOR

+

CAR STILL
RUNNING.
```

That is enough.

---

# 25. Local Geographic Knowledge

If a Character has plausible prior knowledge of northeastern Washington or Republic, additional information may be available.

Example:

> Republic is small, but it isn't just a crossroads. You remember there being fuel, basic services and a hospital.

Only provide this if Character history supports it.

---

# 26. Navigation Knowledge

A Character looking at the cached map can see the real road network.

The map does not indicate:

```text
SAFE ROUTE

DANGER

QUEST

SEED

OR

CORRECT
DIRECTION.
```

It is geographic information.

---

# 27. Phone Interaction

If a player checks their phone, resolve the action immediately from World State.

Possible current result:

```text
SIGNAL:
Weak / intermittent

VOICE:
Unreliable

DATA:
Unavailable

SMS:
Possible but delayed.
```

Exact result depends on current location and network state.

---

# 28. Example — Sending a Message

Player:

```text
"I text my sister and tell
her we're having car trouble."
```

The Character writes the message.

The phone displays:

```text
SENDING...
```

That is what the Character knows.

The Information system determines whether and when it is actually transmitted.

---

# 29. Example — Calling Roadside Assistance

The result follows actual communication state.

Players may decide:

---

# 31. No Suggested Action Menu

The screen must not display:

```text
A) DRIVE TO REPUBLIC

B) CHECK ENGINE

C) CALL FOR HELP

D) TURN AROUND.
```

Players state what they want to do naturally.

---

# 32. The First Player Control Point

After shared and Character-specific information has been delivered, the Game Master asks only:

> **What do you do?**

Then the Game Master stops.

---

# 33. Silence Is Gameplay

If nobody immediately responds:

do nothing.

Do not say:

```text
"Perhaps you
should check
the car."
```

Do not repeat the options.

Do not trigger an event merely because the room is quiet.

Let the players talk.

---

# 34. Group Discussion

Players may spend:

```text
30 SECONDS

5 MINUTES

10 MINUTES

OR MORE
```

discussing what to do.

That is gameplay.

---

# 35. Time During Discussion

Real-world player discussion does not necessarily equal identical World Time second-for-second.

However, prolonged in-character discussion while the Characters continue driving does consume World Time and distance.

The GM tracks the actual physical state.

---

# 36. If They Continue Driving While Talking

The vehicle continues toward Republic.

Distance decreases.

Mechanical state may continue degrading.

The world does not freeze while players discuss.

---

# 37. If They Pull Over

The world continues.

They can inspect the vehicle.

Traffic may pass.

Weather continues.

Phones remain subject to network conditions.

Nothing special needs to happen.

---

# 38. If They Inspect the Vehicle

The Game Master resolves what can actually be observed.

Possible observations may include:

- belt condition
- battery connections
- engine sound
- smell
- visible damage
- voltage if appropriate tools exist

Expertise determines interpretation.

Do not roll merely to open the hood.

---

# 39. If They Attempt Repair

First establish:

```text
WHAT DO
THEY THINK
IS WRONG?

WHAT DO
THEY TRY
TO DO?

WHAT TOOLS
DO THEY
HAVE?

DO THEY
KNOW HOW?
```

Then determine whether uncertainty requires resolution.

---

# 40. If They Continue Past Republic

Allow it.

Republic is not an invisible boundary.

The Game Master updates:

```text
DISTANCE

VEHICLE
STATE

COMMUNICATION
STATE

ROAD
CONDITIONS

TIME.
```

If the vehicle eventually fails elsewhere, that becomes World Truth.

---

# 41. If They Turn Around

Allow it.

Resolve the actual road, distance, vehicle condition and known services behind them.

The Vertical Slice must survive the players refusing the expected direction.

---

# 42. If They Reach Republic

Do not immediately trigger a scripted event.

They enter a real place.

The players decide what they seek.

Possible practical needs include:

```text
MECHANIC

PARTS

FUEL

FOOD

TOILET

PHONE
SIGNAL

INFORMATION

LODGING

MEDICAL
HELP.
```

---

# 43. Location Selection

If players say:

> "We need a mechanic."

The Game Master determines what they can plausibly find from:

- visible signs
- cached map data
- prior knowledge
- asking someone
- real geographic baseline

The GM does not simply teleport them to the most narratively useful NPC.

---

# 44. NPC Introduction Rule

An NPC appears because:

```text
THE NPC
IS THERE

AND

THE PLAYERS
ENCOUNTER
THEM.
```

Not because the story requires the NPC's information.

---

# 45. Seed Activation Rule

No Seed must activate during Scene 001.

Possible early contact with Seeds may occur naturally.

For example:

```text
CHECK RADIO
→ possible information Seed

SEEK FUEL
→ possible Locals First pressure

WAIT IN TOWN
→ possible later Seed visibility.
```

But nothing is forced.

---

# 46. The Vehicle Problem Is Enough

The Game Master should trust the mundane opening.

There is no need to add:

```text
A SCREAM

A GUNSHOT

A CRASH

A ROADBLOCK

A MISSING
CHILD

OR

A MYSTERIOUS
FIGURE
```

to make the opening interesting.

The uncertainty already exists.

---

# 47. Information Discipline

At all times:

```text
WORLD TRUTH

≠

CHARACTER
OBSERVATION

≠

CHARACTER
KNOWLEDGE

≠

PLAYER
THEORY.
```

The opening should demonstrate this immediately.

# 49. Normality Is Important

During the approach, players may see another vehicle traveling normally.

Someone may be driving to work.

A store may be open.

A person may be walking a dog.

This is desirable.

The world has not visually transformed into a disaster zone.

---

# 50. First Contact With Republic

If the Characters enter town, visual presentation should initially emphasize recognizable ordinary geography.

Examples:


---

# 51. No Apocalypse Filter

Visuals should not automatically use:

- abandoned cars everywhere
- desaturated horror grading
- smoke
- trash blowing through empty streets
- boarded windows
- military presence

unless World State later causes those things.

---

# 52. Soundtrack Discipline

Music may fade significantly once player control begins.

Environmental sound can dominate.

This allows conversation to become the center of the experience.

---

Once player control begins, the distinction between:


Players should experience continuous reality.

# 57. Player Experience Target

The desired initial reaction is not:

```text
"WHAT IS
THE QUEST?"
```

It is closer to:

```text

WHAT DO
WE ACTUALLY
WANT TO DO?"
```

Followed eventually by:

```text

WHY ISN'T
ANYTHING

```

---

# 58. Solo Experience Target

For a solo player:

```text
"I NEED TO
FIGURE THIS
OUT."
```

The absence of a companion should be felt naturally.

Not narrated dramatically.

---

# 59. Multiplayer Experience Target

For multiple players:

```text
"WHAT DO
YOU GUYS
THINK?"
```

The first meaningful gameplay should ideally occur between the players themselves.

---

# 60. Large Group Experience Target

With two vehicles, an additional immediate question may emerge:

```text
"DO WE
ALL STOP?"
```

That is valuable.

The game has already produced group decision-making before any mystery is investigated.

---

# 61. Failure Condition

The opening is weaker if players feel:

```text
THE GAME
OBVIOUSLY
WANTS US

TO GO TO
REPUBLIC.
```

Republic should be the sensible option.

Not the mandatory one.

---

# 62. Strong Success Condition

The opening succeeds if the players begin discussing what to do before the GM needs to provide anything else.

Ideal runtime moment:

```text
GM:

"What do you do?"


SILENCE.


PLAYER:

"How far did you say
Republic was?"


PLAYER:

"Forget that.
Can we even make it there?"


PLAYER:

"Who here actually
knows anything about cars?"


PLAYER:

"Before we do anything,
I'm trying my phone again."


THE GAME
HAS STARTED.
```

---

# 63. Vertical Slice Observation

During testing, record:

- how long before first player action
- whether players discuss the vehicle
- whether anyone checks communications
- whether Republic feels forced
- whether players inspect the map
- whether Expertise becomes relevant naturally
- whether players form theories
- whether they confuse the vehicle fault with the wider disruption
- whether silence feels uncomfortable or productive
- whether the GM talks too much
- whether players understand they may refuse Republic

---

# 64. Do Not Correct Useful Uncertainty

If players ask:

> "Is the car problem connected to the phone problem?"

The Game Master should not answer from omniscient World Truth.

The GM answers only through Character-accessible information.

Possible response:

> You don't currently have any evidence connecting them.

That is different from:

> No.

---

# 65. The First Mystery

The first mystery is therefore not:

```text
WHO CAUSED
THE COLLAPSE?
```

It is:

```text
ARE THESE
THINGS

CONNECTED?
```

The answer may be:

```text
NO.
```

That matters.

---

# 66. The First Resource

The first resource lost is not necessarily fuel.

It is:

```text
CERTAINTY.
```

The Characters still have:

- transportation
- food
- money
- phones
- infrastructure
- society
- institutions

But they can no longer assume those systems will answer when asked.

---

# 67. The First Transformation

At the beginning:

```text
"WE HAVE
CAR TROUBLE."
```

Later:

```text
"WE MAY HAVE
TO STAY HERE."
```

Later still:

```text
"WE DON'T KNOW
WHAT'S HAPPENING
OUTSIDE THIS
PLACE."
```

The transition should emerge through experience.

---

# 68. Opening Scene North Star

Project Ascension should begin with something small enough to understand.

A warning light.

A wet road.

A town ahead.

A phone that cannot connect.

Nothing visibly catastrophic has happened.

There is still traffic.

There are still businesses.

There are still people going about their morning.

The Character or Characters still believe their problem is temporary.

They still believe someone will know what is happening.

They still believe the systems around them will begin working again.

Maybe they are right.

The Game Master does not tell them.

The map says Republic is ahead.

The vehicle is still moving.

For now.

The Game Master asks:

> **What do you do?**

And then:

> **The Game Master stops talking. The players take over.**
