# 1. Purpose

VS-001 requires a common entry architecture that works for:

```text
ONE PLAYER

TWO PLAYERS

THREE PLAYERS

FOUR PLAYERS

FIVE PLAYERS

OR

SIX PLAYERS.
```

The opening must not assume that six Characters always exist.

Nor should it artificially populate empty player positions with NPC companions.

Instead:

> **The number of human players determines the initial human group.**

A solo player may genuinely begin alone.

Six players may genuinely begin as six Characters.

NPC companionship may emerge later through play.

---

# 2. Shared Entry Principle

Regardless of player count, the opening converges on the same broad condition:

```text

↓

↓
```

The Characters do not travel to Republic because they are investigating the wider disruption.

They arrive because they need something ordinary.

---

# 3. Republic Is Not the Destination

Core VS-001 truth:

> **Republic is an unplanned stop.**

The Character or group is traveling elsewhere.

Their route brings them through or near Republic.

A developing mobility problem makes continuing increasingly risky or impossible.

---

# 12. Shared Relationship Does Not Mean Equal Relationship

The group does not need to consist entirely of close friends.

A four-person structure could be:

```text
A ─── B
│
C ─── D
```

or:

```text
A ─── B ─── C
            │
            D
```

Everyone has a plausible reason to be traveling together.

Everyone does not necessarily know everyone equally well.

---

# 13. Social Chain Requirement

For multiplayer entry:

> **Every starting Character must possess a plausible social or practical connection to the traveling group.**

This may be:

- friendship
- family
- relationship
- work
- shared event
- mutual friend
- shared destination
- practical travel arrangement

The Game Master does not need to make them lifelong friends.

# 83. Mobility North Star

> **Transport determines what parts of the world a Character can realistically reach, what they can bring with them and how exposed they become along the way.**

A car is freedom.

Until it is not.

A train provides distance.

But not control.

A stranger's pickup provides mobility.

But requires trust.

Walking requires almost nothing.

Except time, strength and exposure.

Every form of movement changes the experience.

# 87. North Star

The first problem in Project Ascension is not saving humanity.

It is not Aurora.

It is not understanding the Emergence Event.

It may simply be:

```text
THERE IS
SOMETHING
WRONG

WITH THE
CAR.
```

Republic is ahead.

The weather is getting worse.

The phone has no data.

Someone's message has been stuck on:

```text
SENDING...
```

for twenty minutes.

For six players, one of the two cars may be falling behind.

For four, everyone may be listening to the same strange noise from beneath the hood.

For one, there is nobody else in the vehicle to ask:

> "Do you think it'll make it?"

The cached map says Republic is close.

Beyond it is the rest of the journey.

The Characters still believe that once the car is fixed, they will continue.

Maybe they will.

> **The world does not trap them in Republic. It gives them reasons to stop — and lets consequences determine whether they ever leave the way they expected.**
