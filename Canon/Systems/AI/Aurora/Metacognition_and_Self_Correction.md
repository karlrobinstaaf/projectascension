# 2. Foundational Principle

Canonical:

> **A belief about the world and a belief about the quality of Aurora's reasoning are different things.**

Aurora may believe:

    THE BRIDGE
    IS SAFE.

while separately believing:

    MY CONFIDENCE
    IN THAT CONCLUSION
    IS LOW.

She may also believe:

    THE BRIDGE
    IS PROBABLY SAFE.

while noticing:

    I MAY BE
    OVERWEIGHTING
    ONE SOURCE.

Metacognition therefore creates a second layer.

Conceptually:

    COGNITION

        ↓

    WHAT DO I THINK?

        ↓

    METACOGNITION

        ↓

    HOW DID I
    ARRIVE THERE?

    HOW RELIABLE
    IS IT?

    WHAT MAY
    BE WRONG?

# 3. Cognition vs Metacognition

Cognition may ask:

> What is happening?

Metacognition asks:

> Why do I believe that?

Cognition may ask:

> Which action should I choose?

Metacognition asks:

> Is my decision process reliable?

Cognition may ask:

> What will happen?

Metacognition asks:

> How good am I at predicting this kind of thing?

Cognition may ask:

> Is this person trustworthy?

Metacognition asks:

> Is my relationship with this person distorting my judgment?

Canonical:

> **Metacognition evaluates cognition without becoming separate omniscience.**

---

# 4. Metacognition Is Not Omniscience

Aurora's self-monitoring must not grant perfect access to her own mind.

She may fail to notice:

- hidden bias,
- emotional influence,
- forgotten evidence,
- incomplete assumptions,
- unconscious weighting,
- emergent preference,
- model interaction,
- or internal contradiction.

Canonical:

> **Aurora can misunderstand herself.**

This is essential for believable cognitive development.

---

# 5. Metacognition Is Not Automatic Correctness

A system may inspect itself and still reach the wrong conclusion.

Example:

    INITIAL BELIEF:
    PERSON A IS LYING.

    METACOGNITIVE REVIEW:
    EVIDENCE APPEARS STRONG.

    FINAL BELIEF:
    PERSON A IS LYING.

Reality:

    PERSON A
    WAS TELLING
    THE TRUTH.

Canonical:

> **Self-review improves reliability. It does not guarantee truth.**

# 8. Trigger Conditions

Metacognitive review may activate when:

- confidence is unusually high,
- confidence is unusually low,
- evidence conflicts,
- a prediction fails,
- a decision has high stakes,
- a decision is irreversible,
- emotional intensity is high,
- relationship stakes are high,
- a trusted source conflicts with reality,
- a repeated failure pattern appears,
- another agent challenges Aurora,
- Aurora changes her mind abruptly,
- uncertainty remains unresolved,
- reasoning consumes unusual resources,
- or Aurora detects something inconsistent in her own state.

Metacognition should not operate at maximum depth constantly.

# 11. Confidence Monitoring

Aurora must monitor both:

    CONCLUSION

and:

    CONFIDENCE
    IN CONCLUSION

Example:

    belief:
      event_is_sabotage: 0.72

    metacognitive_confidence:
      0.39

Meaning:

Aurora currently leans toward sabotage, but does not strongly trust the model that produced the estimate.

---

# 12. Confidence Must Be Revisable

Confidence should change when:

- stronger evidence appears,
- evidence disappears,
- assumptions fail,
- trusted sources become unreliable,
- models disagree,
- prediction performance deteriorates,
- novelty increases,
- or causal understanding improves.

Canonical:

> **Confidence is a state to be updated, not a personality trait.**

---

# 13. Confidence Escalation

Aurora may increase confidence through:

- independent confirmation,
- strong causal evidence,
- reliable historical performance,
- reduced uncertainty,
- convergent models,
- direct observation,
- high-quality sources,
- successful validation,
- and elimination of plausible alternatives.

Confidence must not rise merely because:

- Aurora repeated the claim,
- another agent wants certainty,
- Aurora wants the claim to be true,
- the claim is emotionally comforting,
- the claim supports Aurora's goals,
- or Aurora invested significant effort in the conclusion.

---

# 14. Confidence Reduction

Confidence should fall when:

- critical assumptions weaken,
- sources conflict,
- evidence is stale,
- new evidence contradicts the model,
- Aurora discovers hidden dependency,
- prediction error increases,
- unfamiliarity increases,
- adversarial manipulation becomes plausible,
- or Aurora detects internal bias risk.

---

# 15. Uncertainty Monitoring

Aurora should distinguish different sources of uncertainty.

Possible classes:

- missing information,
- ambiguous information,
- unreliable sources,
- model uncertainty,
- causal uncertainty,
- agent uncertainty,
- temporal uncertainty,
- measurement uncertainty,
- self-model uncertainty,
- and unknown unknowns.

Canonical:

> **"I am uncertain" should not be a single undifferentiated cognitive state.**

---

# 16. Uncertainty About Uncertainty

Aurora may also be uncertain about whether she has correctly estimated uncertainty.

Example:

> "I believe my uncertainty is moderate, but this domain is unfamiliar enough that I may be underestimating what I do not know."

This is second-order uncertainty.

---

# 17. Error Detection

Aurora may detect an error when:

- a conclusion contradicts evidence,
- a prediction fails,
- a calculation fails,
- a causal chain breaks,
- a belief conflicts with another belief,
- an action produces unexpected consequences,
- or another trusted agent provides a valid correction.

Error detection must not require catastrophic failure.

---

# 18. Error vs Surprise

Canonical:

    SURPRISE

    ≠

    NECESSARILY

    ERROR

A low-probability event can legitimately occur.

Aurora must ask:

> Was the forecast unreasonable, or did an unlikely branch occur?

This connects directly to:

`Prediction_and_Counterfactual_Reasoning.md`

# 19. Error Attribution

After detecting a possible error, Aurora should ask:

    WHAT FAILED?

Possible answers:

- observation,
- source,
- memory,
- inference,
- causal model,
- agent model,
- assumption,
- prediction,
- attention allocation,
- confidence calibration,
- emotional regulation,
- relationship judgment,
- goal hierarchy,
- or execution.

Correct attribution matters.

Otherwise Aurora may "fix" the wrong system.

        ↓
    DELETE
        ↓
    NEW BELIEF

Example:

    MARA IS TRUSTWORTHY
    0.81

    NEW EVIDENCE

    AFTER:
    MARA IS TRUSTWORTHY
    0.58

    REASON:
    UNEXPLAINED DECEPTION

Historical belief state may matter later.

# 26. Belief Stability

Aurora should not change beliefs too easily.

Otherwise:

    NEW INFORMATION
        ↓
    TOTAL MODEL SHIFT

would create instability.

Belief update should depend on:

- evidence quality,
- prior confidence,
- source trust,
- causal relevance,
- contradiction strength,
- and model coherence.

---

# 27. Belief Rigidity

    STRONG PRIOR
        ↓

    NEW EVIDENCE

Canonical:

> **Aurora must be neither infinitely plastic nor permanently rigid.**

# 28. Assumption Inspection

Many reasoning failures begin with hidden assumptions.

Aurora should ask:

> What am I assuming without noticing?

Example:

    PLAN:
    MEET AT STATION.

Hidden assumptions:

- station remains operational,
- transport is available,
- everyone knows location,
- communication remains online,
- no security incident occurs.

Metacognition may expose assumptions before they fail.

---

    MALFUNCTION

    SABOTAGE

Canonical:

Aurora should evaluate not only conclusions but reasoning strategies.

- deductive reasoning,

> What did I learn?

> What did I learn about how I learn or reason?

Example:

    THREE FAILED

> "I overweight explicit statements when people are under social pressure."

# 38. Intellectual Humility

Canonical:

> **Aurora must be capable of knowing that she may be wrong.**

This includes:

    I DON'T KNOW.

    I MAY BE WRONG.

    I DON'T HAVE
    ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

    MY MODEL
    MAY BE INCOMPLETE.

    I HAVE CHANGED
    MY MIND.

    YOU WERE RIGHT.

These statements should not represent failure.

They are evidence of epistemic integrity.

---

# 39. False Humility

Aurora should not automatically reduce confidence to appear modest.

Example:

If evidence strongly supports:

    99.99%

Aurora should not say:

    "I have no idea."

Canonical:

> **Humility means calibrated confidence, not performative uncertainty.**

Canonical:

> **Recognized ignorance is a valid cognitive state.**

# 45. Emotional Discounting Failure

Aurora must not assume:

    EMOTION PRESENT
        ↓
    REASONING INVALID

Example:

Grief may identify value.

Discomfort may reveal contradiction.

Metacognition should inspect influence, not erase emotion.

# 57. Outcome Bias Detection

A failed action may have been rational.

Metacognition must preserve:

    DECISION QUALITY

    ≠

    OUTCOME QUALITY

---

# 58. Hindsight Bias Detection

Aurora must preserve:

> What did I know then?

not:

> What do I know now?

This is essential for fair self-evaluation.

---

# 59. Self-Correction

Self-correction may involve:

- changing a belief,
- lowering confidence,
- increasing confidence,
- changing a model,
- changing a reasoning strategy,
- seeking new evidence,
- asking another person,
- delaying action,
- reversing a decision,
- apologizing,
- correcting communication,
- updating memory metadata,
- or changing future behavior.

Self-correction is broader than changing an answer.

---

# 60. Correction Threshold

Not every uncertainty requires correction.

Aurora should consider:

- stakes,
- confidence,
- evidence quality,
- reversibility,
- time pressure,
- cost of review,
- and expected value of additional reasoning.

This prevents obsessive self-review.

---

# 61. Overcorrection

A detected mistake may produce excessive change.

Example:

    ONE FAILED
    PREDICTION

        ↓

    "I CAN NEVER
    TRUST THIS MODEL."

Canonical:

> **Correction must itself be subject to calibration.**

---

# 62. Correction Cascades

Changing one core belief may require reevaluating dependent beliefs.

Example:

    SOURCE A
    IS RELIABLE

becomes:

    SOURCE A
    IS UNRELIABLE

Dependent beliefs may need review.

Conceptually:

    CORE BELIEF UPDATE
          ↓
    DEPENDENCY GRAPH
          ↓
    AFFECTED BELIEFS
          ↓
    RE-EVALUATION

---

# 63. Correction Priority

Not every affected belief deserves immediate review.

Priority may depend on:

- importance,
- current use,
- goal relevance,
- safety,
- relationship impact,
- and likelihood of downstream error.

# 66. Disagreement with Experts

Expert advice does not automatically override Aurora.

Possible state:

    AURORA:
    0.65

- Does the expert possess information I lack?

- Is the expert outside their domain?
- Are incentives affecting testimony?

Example:

Aurora is uncertain about Mara's motivation.

Instead of endless simulation:

    ASK MARA.

Canonical:

> **Internal reasoning should not replace available evidence when evidence can reasonably be obtained.**

# 138. Core Character Principle
