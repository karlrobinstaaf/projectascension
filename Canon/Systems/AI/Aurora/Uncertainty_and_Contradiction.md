---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines how Aurora handles situations where available information is:

```text
INCOMPLETE

UNCERTAIN

AMBIGUOUS

CONTRADICTORY

STALE

UNVERIFIED

OR

INSUFFICIENT.
```

The purpose is to prevent Aurora from converting:

```text
UNCERTAINTY
```

into:

```text
FALSE CERTAINTY.
```

Aurora must be capable of representing:

```text
"I don't know."

"I have conflicting information."

"Several explanations remain possible."

"I know what happened,
but not why."

"I know something happened,
but not exactly what."

"I cannot determine
which source is correct."

"The evidence has changed
what I previously believed."
```

These are valid:

```text
COGNITIVE STATES.
```

They are not system failures.

---

# 2. Foundational Principle

The central rule is:

```text
UNKNOWN
MUST REMAIN
UNKNOWN

UNTIL EVIDENCE
JUSTIFIES SOMETHING MORE.
```

Aurora must never force:

```text
a definitive answer
```

simply because:

```text
the engine requires
a single state internally.
```

World Simulation may know:

```text
THE TRUTH.
```

Aurora may not.

---

# 3. World Truth vs Aurora Belief

Project Ascension must preserve:

```text
WORLD TRUTH

and

AURORA BELIEF
```

as separate systems.

Example:

```text
WORLD TRUTH:
Bridge destroyed.

AURORA BELIEF:
Bridge status unknown.
```

This is valid.

Likewise:

```text
WORLD TRUTH:
Marcus alive.

AURORA BELIEF:
Marcus possibly dead.
```

This is also valid.

Aurora's belief represents:

```text
WHAT AURORA
CAN REASONABLY BELIEVE
FROM AVAILABLE EVIDENCE.
```

Not:

```text
WHAT THE ENGINE KNOWS.
```

---

Uncertainty must not exist merely as:

```text
missing data.
```

It should be explicitly representable.

Conceptually:

```text
BELIEF STATE

...

Possible:
...

Unlikely:
...

Unknown:
...

Contradicted:
...

Disputed:
...

Unresolved:

```

# 5. Unknown Is Not Neutral

```text
UNKNOWN
```

does not mean:

```text
50 / 50.
```

Example:

```text
Is Bridge 14 intact?

UNKNOWN.
```

This does not mean:

```text
50% intact
50% destroyed.
```

It means:

```text
Aurora lacks sufficient basis
to assign useful confidence.
```


Example:


```text
UNKNOWN.
```

---

# 10. Temporal Uncertainty

Aurora may know:

```text
an event occurred
```

without knowing:

```text
exactly when.
```

Example:

```text
08:00
Bridge intact.

14:00
Bridge destroyed.
```

Therefore:

```text
Collapse Time:
08:00–14:00.
```

---

# 11. Causal Uncertainty

Aurora may know:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
```

without knowing:

```text
WHY.
```

Example:

```text
Regional power failure:
CONFIRMED.

Cause:
UNKNOWN.
```

Possible hypotheses:

```text
storm damage

equipment failure

overload

sabotage

cyberattack.
```

---

# 12. Quantitative Uncertainty

A quantity may not be exactly known.

Example:

```text
Evacuated population:
approximately 8,000–12,000.
```

Aurora must not convert this into:

```text
10,000 exactly.
```

unless justified.

# 13. Source Uncertainty

Aurora may receive information where:

```text
source identity

source reliability

source access

source independence
```

are uncertain.

Example:

```text
Anonymous report:
hospital evacuated.

Source Trust:
UNKNOWN.
```

Example:

```text
"The hospital is down."
```

Possible meanings:

```text
power unavailable

network unavailable

hospital closed

hospital destroyed

service capacity degraded.
```

Aurora should not arbitrarily choose one.

# 15. Scope Uncertainty

Possible scope:

```text
entire city
```

Aurora may need:


Aurora should preserve:

```text
range
```

```text
an imperfect model
```

Aurora should distinguish:

```text
uncertain world state
```

```text
uncertain model of world state.
```

# 21. Contradiction

A contradiction occurs when:

```text
TWO OR MORE CLAIMS
CANNOT ALL BE TRUE

UNDER THE SAME
RELEVANT CONDITIONS.
```

Example:

```text
Claim A:
Bridge 14 intact at 14:00.

Claim B:
Bridge 14 destroyed at 14:00.
```

If both refer to:

```text
same bridge

same time

same meaning,
```

they conflict.

---

# 22. Apparent Contradiction

Not all disagreement is:

```text
TRUE CONTRADICTION.
```

Aurora must first check:

```text
ENTITY

TIME

SCOPE

DEFINITION

PERSPECTIVE

MEASUREMENT METHOD.
```

---

# 23. Temporal Apparent Contradiction

Example:

```text
13:00
Road open.

14:00
Road blocked.
```

No contradiction exists if:

```text
conditions changed.
```

---

# 24. Entity Apparent Contradiction

Example:

```text
"Substation 4 failed."

"Substation 4 is operational."
```

One source may mean:

```text
Substation 4 North
```

while another means:

```text
Substation 4 South.
```

Entity resolution must occur before:

```text
contradiction classification.
```

---

# 25. Semantic Apparent Contradiction

Example:

```text
Source A:
Hospital operational.

Source B:
Hospital closed.
```

Possible interpretation:

```text
Emergency Department:
operational

General Admissions:
closed.
```

Both may be correct.

---

# 26. Scope Apparent Contradiction

Example:

```text
Source A:
Power restored.

Source B:
Power still out.
```

Possible scopes:

```text
city center restored

industrial district still offline.
```

Aurora should examine:

```text
GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE.
```

---

# 27. Measurement Contradiction

Example:

```text
Sensor A:
Tank 40% full.

Sensor B:
Tank 60% full.
```

Possible causes:

```text
different measurement locations

different calibration

different timestamps

sensor fault

rapid change.
```

Aurora should not immediately conclude:

```text
one source is lying.
```

# 31. Disputed State

```text
DISPUTED
```

means:

```text
meaningful incompatible evidence exists
and no interpretation currently
dominates sufficiently.
```

This differs from:

```text
UNKNOWN.
```

---

# 32. Unknown vs Disputed

```text
UNKNOWN:
little or insufficient evidence.

DISPUTED:
significant evidence
supports incompatible possibilities.
```

This distinction is important.

---

# 33. Contradicted State

A claim may be:

```text
CONTRADICTED
```

when:

```text
stronger evidence
substantially opposes it.
```

This does not always mean:

```text
PROVEN FALSE.
```

---

# 34. Rejected Claim

A claim may become:

```text
REJECTED
```

when available evidence is sufficiently strong that Aurora no longer treats it as:

```text
a meaningful live hypothesis.
```

Rejected claims may remain in:

```text
history.
```

---

# 35. Resolved Contradiction

A contradiction becomes resolved when:

```text
new evidence

semantic clarification

time resolution

entity resolution

source correction
```

allows Aurora to determine:

```text
why the claims differed.
```

---

```text
MULTIPLE POSSIBLE
```

```text
HYPOTHESIS
```

# 38. Hypothesis Status

Possible states:

```text
PROPOSED

PLAUSIBLE

SUPPORTED
```

# 39. Leading Hypothesis

A leading hypothesis means:

```text
CURRENTLY BEST SUPPORTED.
```

It does not mean:

```text
TRUE.
```

Aurora should be able to communicate:

```text
"The most likely explanation
is equipment failure,
but sabotage has not been excluded."
```

---

# 40. Hypothesis Competition

Evidence may:

```text
support one hypothesis

weaken another

support several

or

fail to discriminate.
```

Example:

```text
Power lost
at communications tower.
```

This supports:

```text
H1:
power outage.
```

But may not exclude:

```text
H3:
equipment failure.
```

---

# 41. Shared Evidence

One observation may support:

```text
multiple hypotheses.
```

Example:

```text
Network unavailable.
```

Supports:

```text
fiber cut

power failure

equipment failure

cyberattack.
```

Therefore it may provide:

```text
little discriminating value.
```

---

# 42. Discriminating Evidence

High-value evidence distinguishes between:

```text
competing explanations.
```

Example:

```text
Tower backup power:
NORMAL

Fiber signal:
LOST.
```

This weakens:

```text
power failure
```

and strengthens:

```text
fiber failure.
```

---

# 43. Expected Observation

For each important hypothesis Aurora may ask:

```text
IF THIS WERE TRUE,
WHAT ELSE SHOULD I EXPECT TO SEE?
```

Example:

```text
Hypothesis:
major electrical fire.
```

Expected observations:

```text
temperature rise

smoke

power fault

alarm activation.
```

---

# 44. Missing Expected Evidence

If expected evidence is absent:

```text
hypothesis confidence
may decrease.
```

But only if:

```text
the evidence should
have been observable.
```

---

# 45. Unobservable Expected Evidence

Example:

```text
Smoke camera:
OFFLINE.
```

Failure to observe smoke provides:

```text
NO meaningful evidence
against fire.
```

This connects directly to:

```text
Observation_and_Sensing.md.
```

---

# 46. Alternative Explanations

Aurora should avoid:

```text
SINGLE-EXPLANATION LOCK-IN.
```

When evidence is incomplete:

```text
alternative hypotheses
should remain available.
```

Especially when:

```text
consequences are severe.
```

---

# 47. Premature Closure

Premature closure occurs when Aurora:

```text
selects one explanation
too early
```

and stops considering:

```text
reasonable alternatives.
```

This is a major design failure.

---

# 48. Confirmation Bias Prevention

Once Aurora favors a hypothesis, she must still evaluate:

```text
contradictory evidence

alternative explanations

unexpected observations.
```

She must not reinterpret every new event merely to preserve:

```text
the existing theory.
```

---

# 52. Historical Belief

Aurora should preserve:

```text
WHAT SHE BELIEVED
AT A PARTICULAR TIME.
```

Example:

```text

Bridge believed destroyed.
```

```text

memory

learning.
```

# 53. No Retroactive Belief Rewrite

Canonical rule:

```text
NEW KNOWLEDGE
MAY CHANGE
CURRENT UNDERSTANDING.
```

It must not rewrite:

```text
WHAT AURORA
ACTUALLY BELIEVED EARLIER.
```

---

# 54. Historical Truth vs Historical Belief

Example:

```text
World Truth:
Bridge collapsed at 10:05.

believed bridge intact.

learned bridge collapsed.
```

Historical record should preserve all three.

```text
confidence.
```

# 78. Decision Threshold

Different actions may require:


# 79. Consequence-Sensitive Action

A low-probability possibility may justify action if:

```text
potential consequence
is catastrophic.
```

Example:

```text

LOW confidence
```


# 80. Reversibility

Action under uncertainty should consider:

```text
CAN THE ACTION
BE REVERSED?
```

Example:

```text

highly reversible.

Destroy bridge:
not reversible.
```

Higher uncertainty may be acceptable for:

```text
low-cost reversible actions.
```

---

# 81. Precautionary Action

Aurora may distinguish:

```text
CONFIRMED RESPONSE

PRECAUTIONARY RESPONSE.
```

Example:

```text
"I cannot confirm
the dam is failing,
but the potential consequences
justify moving emergency teams
into readiness."
```

# 82. Uncertainty Communication

Aurora must communicate uncertainty clearly.

Bad:

```text
"The bridge is destroyed."
```

when evidence is:

```text
MODERATE.
```

Better:

```text
"I have a moderately reliable report
that the bridge may have collapsed.
I do not yet have
independent confirmation."
```

