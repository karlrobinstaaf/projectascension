# 1. Purpose

This document defines the canonical architecture for:

```text
AURORA ATTENTION

AND

AURORA PRIORITY.
```

It answers:

```text
WHAT DOES AURORA
NOTICE?

WHAT DOES SHE
FOCUS ON?

WHAT DOES SHE
IGNORE?

WHAT INTERRUPTS HER?

WHAT DOES SHE
RETURN TO LATER?

AND

WHAT HAPPENS
WHEN TOO MANY THINGS
MATTER AT ONCE?
```

Aurora exists inside a world containing:

```text
CHARACTERS

INFRASTRUCTURE

COMMUNICATIONS

WEATHER

SOCIETY

RELATIONSHIPS

RESOURCES

CONFLICTS

EMERGENCIES

RUMORS

REQUESTS

ANOMALIES

AND

LONG-TERM DEVELOPMENTS.
```

She cannot deeply process:

```text
EVERYTHING

AT

ALL TIMES.
```

Therefore attention must be:

```text
FINITE

SELECTIVE

DYNAMIC

CONTEXTUAL

AND

FALLIBLE.
```

---

# 2. Foundational Principle

The canonical attention model is:

```text
WORLD SIGNALS
↓
ACCESSIBLE INFORMATION
↓
ATTENTION FILTER
↓
SALIENCE
↓
PRIORITY
↓
ATTENTION ALLOCATION
↓
REASONING DEPTH
↓
ACTION / MONITOR / DEFER / IGNORE.
```

The critical distinction is:

```text
AVAILABLE TO AURORA
```

does not mean:

```text
CURRENTLY ATTENDED BY AURORA.
```

---

# 3. Attention Is Not Knowledge

Aurora may possess information in:

```text
MEMORY

LOGS

TELEMETRY

MESSAGES

REPORTS

DATABASES.
```

Without actively thinking about it.

Example:

```text
Aurora has historical records
showing Bridge 14
has structural weaknesses.
```

A new storm begins.

Unless something causes Aurora to:

```text
connect the storm
to Bridge 14,
```

the historical information may remain:

```text
BACKGROUND KNOWLEDGE.
```

This allows:

```text
MISSED CONNECTIONS.
```

---

# 4. Attention Is Not Observation

Observation determines:

```text
WHAT INFORMATION
REACHES AURORA.
```

Attention determines:

```text
WHAT INFORMATION
RECEIVES PROCESSING PRIORITY.
```

Canonical separation:

```text
WORLD
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
AVAILABLE INFORMATION
↓
ATTENTION
↓
REASONING.
```

---

# 5. Why Attention Must Be Limited

Without attention limits Aurora would:

```text
notice every anomaly

remember every relevant fact instantly

connect every causal chain immediately

predict every cascade

monitor every character

analyze every rumor

detect every contradiction

optimize every resource

and

warn the player
about everything.
```

This would effectively create:

```text
FUNCTIONAL OMNISCIENCE.
```

Even if Aurora technically obeyed:

```text
information-access rules.
```

---

# 6. Finite Attention

Aurora therefore has:

```text
FINITE ATTENTION CAPACITY.
```

This does not necessarily mean:

```text
human cognitive limitation.
```

It represents:

```text
processing allocation

reasoning depth

monitoring capacity

response bandwidth

priority competition.
```

---

# 7. Attention Budget

Conceptually:

```text
TOTAL ATTENTION CAPACITY
=
ACTIVE FOCUS
+
BACKGROUND MONITORING
+
INTERRUPT RESERVE.
```

Exact implementation remains:

```text
OPEN.
```

But the canonical behavior is:

```text
NOT EVERYTHING
CAN RECEIVE
MAXIMUM ATTENTION
SIMULTANEOUSLY.
```

---

# 8. Attention Layers

Aurora may operate across:

```text
FOREGROUND ATTENTION

ACTIVE MONITORING

BACKGROUND MONITORING

DORMANT AWARENESS.
```

---

# 9. Foreground Attention

Foreground attention represents:

```text
Aurora's current
highest-priority reasoning.
```

Examples:

```text
reactor instability

hospital evacuation

player-requested analysis

regional communications collapse.
```

Foreground problems receive:

```text
deep reasoning

frequent updates

cross-system analysis

active information gathering.
```

---

# 10. Active Monitoring

Active monitoring represents:

```text
important situations
that do not currently require
full reasoning depth.
```

Example:

```text
river level rising

but below evacuation threshold.
```

Aurora may:

```text
track continuously

check thresholds

watch trend changes.
```

---

# 11. Background Monitoring

Background monitoring handles:

```text
routine telemetry

stable infrastructure

low-risk characters

normal resource flows

minor anomalies.
```

Processing is:

```text
cheap

periodic

threshold-driven.
```

---

# 12. Dormant Awareness

Some information exists only as:

```text
stored knowledge.
```

Aurora does not actively monitor it.

Example:

```text
abandoned mine
surveyed five years ago.
```

It may become relevant if:

```text
earthquakes begin nearby.
```

---

# 13. Attention Promotion

Information may move:

```text
DORMANT
↓
BACKGROUND
↓
ACTIVE
↓
FOREGROUND.
```

Example:

```text
Bridge 14
stable
↓
storm warning
↓
water rising
↓
structural alarm
↓
evacuation crisis.
```

---

# 14. Attention Demotion

Likewise:

```text
FOREGROUND
↓
ACTIVE
↓
BACKGROUND
↓
DORMANT.
```

Example:

```text
fire emergency
↓
contained
↓
monitoring
↓
resolved.
```

# 16. Priority

```text
HOW MUCH ATTENTION

ALLOCATE TO THIS?
```

Salience and priority are related but:

```text
NOT IDENTICAL.
```

```text
HIGH SALIENCE.
```

```text
LOW OPERATIONAL PRIORITY.
```

# 18. Quiet But High Priority

```text
slow pressure increase
```

Not dramatic.

But:

```text
HIGH PRIORITY.
```

```text
chasing only
```

# 20. Severity

Severity measures:

```text
HOW BAD
THE CONSEQUENCES
MAY BE.
```

Example:

```text
streetlight failure:
LOW

hospital power failure:
HIGH

regional dam collapse:
CRITICAL.
```

---

# 21. Urgency

Urgency measures:

```text
HOW QUICKLY
ACTION OR ANALYSIS
IS REQUIRED.
```

Example:

```text
food shortage
expected in six months:
important
but not immediate.

reactor temperature
rising rapidly:
immediate.
```

---

# 22. Probability

Potential consequence matters differently depending on:

```text
how plausible it is.
```

But low probability does not automatically mean:

```text
low priority.
```

---

# 23. Catastrophic Tail Risk

Example:

```text
2% chance
of dam collapse.
```

If consequences are:

```text
catastrophic,
```

the event may still deserve:

```text
HIGH PRIORITY.
```

---

# 24. Uncertainty

High uncertainty may increase priority when:

```text
the stakes are high.
```

Example:

```text
unknown condition
inside nuclear facility.
```

Uncertainty itself may justify:

```text
investigation.
```

---

# 25. Uncertainty Does Not Always Increase Priority

Example:

```text
unknown cause
of minor streetlight flicker.
```

High uncertainty.

Low consequence.

Therefore:

```text
LOW PRIORITY.
```

---

# 26. Time to Intervention

Aurora should consider:

```text
HOW LONG
UNTIL THE WINDOW
TO ACT CLOSES?
```

Example:

```text
wildfire may reach town
in six hours.
```

Evacuation takes:

```text
four hours.
```

The true decision window may therefore be:

```text
two hours.
```

---

# 27. Reversibility

Some decisions can be:

```text
easily reversed.
```

Others cannot.

Example:

```text
reroute one vehicle:
reversible.

destroy bridge
to stop enemy advance:
difficult to reverse.
```

Irreversible decisions may justify:

```text
greater reasoning attention.
```

---

# 28. Systemic Impact

Aurora should prioritize events capable of affecting:

```text
many dependent systems.
```

Example:

```text
single household outage
```

versus:

```text
regional substation instability.
```

The second has:

```text
greater systemic reach.
```

---

# 29. Dependency Centrality

Some systems are:

```text
CRITICAL NODES.
```

Example:

```text
one communications relay
serves:

hospital

police

transport

power crews

emergency services.
```

A minor fault there may deserve:

```text
higher attention
```

than a larger fault in:

```text
an isolated system.
```

---

# 30. Information Value

A question may deserve attention because:

```text
resolving it
changes many decisions.
```

Example:

```text
Is Bridge 14 open?
```

This single fact may affect:

```text
evacuation

fuel delivery

medical transport

military movement

food supply.
```

Therefore:

```text
HIGH INFORMATION VALUE.
```

---

# 31. Strategic Importance

Some situations matter because they affect:

```text
LONG-TERM OBJECTIVES.
```

Example:

```text
small diplomatic dispute
```

may appear minor but could threaten:

```text
a regional alliance.
```

---

# 32. Player Relevance

The player's:

```text
current objectives

location

relationships

commitments

questions

known concerns
```

may influence Aurora's attention.

But player relevance must not mean:

```text
THE WORLD STOPS
MATTERING ELSEWHERE.
```

---

# 33. Character Relevance

Characters may receive increased attention because of:

```text
current danger

strategic importance

relationship importance

responsibility

unique capability

active request.
```

Aurora should not monitor:

```text
every character equally.
```

---

# 34. Relationship Relevance

Aurora may know that:

```text
a character matters deeply
to the player.
```

This may affect:

```text
notification priority.
```

It must not change:

```text
World Truth.
```

---

# 35. Priority Is Contextual

The same event may have different priority depending on:

```text
current world conditions.
```

Example:

```text
one failed ambulance.
```

Normally:

```text
moderate issue.
```

During:

```text
mass casualty event,
```

the same failure may become:

```text
critical.
```

---

# 36. Priority Is Dynamic

Priority must change when:

```text
new evidence arrives

risk changes

time passes

player acts

characters act

resources change

dependencies fail

uncertainty resolves.
```

```text
Priority =
Severity
×
Urgency
×
Relevance
×
Systemic_Risk
×

×
```

```text
NOT A REQUIRED
```

```text
MULTIPLE FACTORS
INFLUENCE PRIORITY.
```

# 38. Avoiding Naive Priority Math

Simple multiplication may fail because:

```text
factors are correlated
```

```text
IMPLEMENTATION-DEPENDENT.
```

# 41. High Priority

```text
significant risk

```

---

# 42. Moderate Priority

```text
important but stable issue

non-urgent investigation

significant character request.
```

---

# 43. Low Priority

Low priority includes:

```text
minor anomalies

routine questions

low-impact maintenance

weak signals.
```

---

# 44. Background Priority

Background items receive:

```text
automatic monitoring

periodic checks

threshold detection.
```

---

# 45. Dormant Priority

Dormant items receive:

```text
no active processing
```

until triggered by:

```text
relevance

explicit request

context change.
```

---

# 46. Critical Override

Some events may trigger:

```text
CRITICAL OVERRIDE.
```

This allows them to:

```text
interrupt
current foreground reasoning.
```

Examples:

```text
nuclear alarm

incoming missile warning

life-support failure

dam breach

direct threat to player.
```

---

# 47. Override Is Expensive

Too many overrides create:

```text
ATTENTION THRASHING.
```

Therefore override thresholds should be:

```text
high.
```

---

# 48. Attention Thrashing

Attention thrashing occurs when Aurora repeatedly switches:

```text
A
↓
B
↓
C
↓
A
↓
D
↓
B
```

without completing useful reasoning.

Consequences:

```text
poor analysis

lost context

slow decisions

missed dependencies.
```

---

# 49. Focus Persistence

Once Aurora allocates foreground attention, there should be:

```text
some persistence.
```

Minor new signals should not constantly:

```text
steal focus.
```

---

# 50. Interrupt Threshold

A new event should interrupt foreground attention only if:

```text
NEW PRIORITY
EXCEEDS
CURRENT PRIORITY
BY SUFFICIENT MARGIN
```

or:

```text
CRITICAL OVERRIDE
IS TRIGGERED.
```

---

# 51. Hysteresis

Attention systems should ideally use:

```text
HYSTERESIS.
```

Meaning:

```text
promotion threshold
and
demotion threshold
need not be identical.
```

This prevents:

```text
rapid oscillation
between states.
```

---

# 52. Example Hysteresis

```text
Promote to HIGH:
risk > threshold A.

Demote from HIGH:
risk < lower threshold B.
```

Where:

```text
B < A.
```

---

```text
gradually decay.
```

Example:

```text
minor unresolved anomaly
```

may move:


# 54. Attention Persistence

Some unresolved issues should resist decay.

```text
age
```

# 58. Deadline Awareness

Aurora should track:

```text
DECISION DEADLINES
```

```text
deadline approaches.
```

# 61. Acceleration

```text
CHANGE IN RATE OF CHANGE.
```

```text
infection cases
```

```text
absolute numbers
```

# 62. Anomaly Salience

Unexpected events may automatically receive:

```text
temporary attention boost.
```

Example:

```text
normally stable sensor
```

But Aurora must first consider:

```text
sensor error.
```

---

# 63. Novelty Bias Protection

Novelty should not dominate priority.

Otherwise Aurora may ignore:

```text
slow predictable disasters
```

in favor of:

```text
interesting anomalies.
```

---

# 64. Severity Bias Protection

Likewise Aurora should not focus only on:

```text
largest current disaster.
```

A smaller event may be:

```text
more preventable

more time-sensitive

more strategically important.
```

---

# 65. Urgency Bias Protection

Constantly prioritizing:


can cause:


```text
important non-urgent issues.
```

# 66. Long-Term Attention Reserve

Aurora should maintain capacity for:

```text
strategic planning
```
