---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the canonical architecture for:

```text
AURORA LEARNING

AND

AURORA ADAPTATION.
```

It answers:

```text
WHAT CAN AURORA LEARN?

WHAT COUNTS AS EXPERIENCE?

HOW DOES EXPERIENCE
CHANGE HER MODELS?

HOW QUICKLY
SHOULD SHE LEARN?

WHEN SHOULD SHE
CHANGE HER MIND?

WHEN SHOULD SHE
NOT CHANGE HER MIND?

HOW DOES SHE LEARN
FROM MISTAKES?

HOW DOES SHE LEARN
FROM SUCCESS?

HOW DOES SHE HANDLE
CONTRADICTORY LESSONS?

HOW DOES SHE AVOID
OVERFITTING?

HOW DOES KNOWLEDGE
BECOME STALE?

CAN SHE FORGET?

CAN SHE LEARN
THE WRONG LESSON?

AND

HOW DOES AURORA
BECOME DIFFERENT
BECAUSE OF THE WORLD
SHE HAS LIVED THROUGH?
```

Aurora exists inside a world that changes.

Therefore Aurora must also be capable of:

```text
CHANGE.
```

But that change must remain:

```text
EVIDENCE-BASED

GRADUAL WHERE APPROPRIATE

DOMAIN-SPECIFIC

REVERSIBLE WHEN WARRANTED

HISTORICALLY GROUNDED

AND

IMPERFECT.
```

---

# 2. Foundational Principle

The canonical learning loop is:

```text
EXPERIENCE
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
INTERPRETATION
↓
EXPECTED VS ACTUAL
↓
ERROR / CONFIRMATION
↓
ATTRIBUTION
↓
LESSON CANDIDATE
↓
CONFIDENCE IN LESSON
↓
MODEL UPDATE
↓
FUTURE BEHAVIOR
↓
NEW EXPERIENCE.
```

The critical distinction is:

```text
EXPERIENCE
≠
TRUTH.
```

Something happening once does not automatically prove:

```text
WHY IT HAPPENED

THAT IT WILL HAPPEN AGAIN

OR

THAT A GENERAL RULE
SHOULD CHANGE.
```

---

# 3. Learning Is Not Memory

Aurora may remember:

```text
a bridge collapsed.
```

Learning asks:

```text
WHAT SHOULD THAT EVENT
CHANGE ABOUT
FUTURE REASONING?
```

Memory stores:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED.
```

Learning modifies:

```text
HOW FUTURE INFORMATION
IS INTERPRETED.
```

Therefore:

```text
MEMORY
≠
LEARNING.
```

---

# 4. Learning Is Not Automatic

Not every event should modify:

```text
Aurora's models.
```

Some events are:

```text
noise

rare anomalies

poorly understood

insufficiently observed

caused by unique circumstances.
```

Aurora should be capable of saying:

```text
"This happened,
but I do not yet know
whether it represents
a general pattern."
```

```text
POWER GRID MANAGEMENT
```


# 8. Direct Experience

Example:

```text
Aurora predicts
Transformer 8 will survive.

Transformer 8 fails.
```

```text
a learning opportunity.
```

```text
why the prediction failed.
```

# 9. Indirect Experience

Aurora may learn from:

```text
reports

archives

character testimony

institutional records.
```

But learning strength must inherit:

```text
SOURCE TRUST

AND

INFORMATION CONFIDENCE.
```

# 10. Experience Quality

Learning strength should depend on:

```text
OBSERVATION QUALITY

SOURCE RELIABILITY

CAUSAL CLARITY

REPEATABILITY

DOMAIN RELEVANCE

CONTEXT SIMILARITY.
```

# 11. Expected vs Actual

A major learning mechanism is:

```text
EXPECTATION
↓
OUTCOME
↓
DIFFERENCE.
```

Conceptually:

```text
PREDICTION ERROR.
```

---

# 12. Prediction Error

Example:

```text
Expected fuel use:
8,000 liters/day.

Actual:
11,500 liters/day.
```

Aurora should investigate:

```text
WHY?
```

Possible causes:

```text
population increase

cold weather

generator inefficiency

fuel theft

bad measurement

incorrect consumption model.
```

---

# 13. Error Is Evidence, Not Explanation

Canonical rule:

```text
A DIFFERENCE
BETWEEN EXPECTED
AND ACTUAL
DOES NOT IDENTIFY
THE CAUSE.
```

---

# 14. Attribution

Before updating a model Aurora should consider:

```text
WHAT CAUSED
THE OBSERVED OUTCOME?
```

Possible attribution classes:

```text
MODEL ERROR

SOURCE ERROR

ASSUMPTION FAILURE

EXECUTION FAILURE

CHARACTER INTERVENTION

PLAYER INTERVENTION

EXTERNAL EVENT

RANDOM VARIATION

UNKNOWN CAUSE.
```

---

# 15. Unknown Attribution

Aurora must be capable of:

```text
NOT KNOWING
WHY SOMETHING HAPPENED.
```

This may create:

```text
UNRESOLVED LESSON.
```

---

# 16. Lesson Candidate

A learning event may generate:

```text
LESSON CANDIDATE.
```

Example:

```text
"Temperature spikes
may be a stronger predictor
of this transformer class
than previously modeled."
```

This is not yet:

```text
established truth.
```

---

# 17. Lesson Confidence

Lesson candidates may have:

```text
LOW

MODERATE

HIGH
```

confidence.

Confidence may increase through:

```text
repetition

independent evidence

causal explanation

cross-validation.
```

---

# 18. Learning Rate

Different lessons should update at:

```text
different speeds.
```

A catastrophic discovery may justify:

```text
rapid change.
```

A weak behavioral pattern may require:

```text
many observations.
```

---

# 19. Fast Learning

Fast learning may be appropriate when:

```text
previous assumption
is directly falsified

physical relationship
is clearly demonstrated

safety-critical flaw
is confirmed

trusted system specification
was incorrect.
```

---

# 20. Slow Learning

Slow learning is appropriate for:

```text
character behavior

social trends

political behavior

relationship dynamics

rare-event probability.
```

---

# 21. Learning Rate Risk

Learning too slowly causes:

```text
repeated mistakes.
```

Learning too quickly causes:

```text
overreaction

instability

overfitting.
```

---

# 22. Overfitting

Aurora overfits when:

```text
a narrow experience
becomes an overly broad rule.
```

Example:

```text
One convoy
was attacked
on Route 7.
```

Bad learning:

```text
"Route 7 is always unsafe."
```

Better:

```text
"Attack risk on Route 7
may be higher
than previously estimated."
```

---

# 23. Single-Event Protection

Canonical rule:

```text
ONE EVENT
SHOULD NOT USUALLY
BECOME A UNIVERSAL RULE.
```

Exceptions may exist where:

```text
the event directly proves
a deterministic fact.
```

---

# 24. Generalization

Learning becomes useful when Aurora can apply:

```text
a lesson
to similar situations.
```

But similarity must be:

```text
justifiable.
```

---

# 25. Contextual Generalization

Example:

```text
Transformer Model X
failed under high humidity.
```

Aurora may generalize to:

```text
other Model X transformers
under similar conditions.
```

She should not automatically generalize to:

```text
all electrical infrastructure.
```

---

# 26. Similarity

Generalization may consider:

```text
system type

environment

region

age

configuration

population

character traits

institutional context

historical conditions.
```

---

# 27. Transfer Learning

A lesson from one domain may sometimes:

```text
inform another.
```

Example:

```text
Aurora learns
that local maintenance records
are systematically delayed.
```

This may affect:

```text
multiple infrastructure forecasts
within the same organization.
```

---

# 28. Negative Transfer

A lesson transferred incorrectly may:

```text
make performance worse.
```

Therefore cross-domain transfer should retain:

```text
uncertainty.
```

---

# 29. Source Learning

Aurora may update:

```text
SOURCE TRUST
```

based on:

```text
historical accuracy

timeliness

bias

completeness

deception

domain performance.
```

---

# 30. Source Trust Is Domain-Specific

A source may be:

```text
excellent for weather
```

and:

```text
poor for political analysis.
```

Therefore:

```text
SOURCE TRUST
SHOULD NOT ALWAYS
BE ONE GLOBAL NUMBER.
```

---

# 31. Source Success

If a source repeatedly provides:

```text
accurate information,
```

Aurora may increase:

```text
confidence in future reports.
```

But not to:

```text
absolute certainty.
```

---

# 32. Source Failure

Repeated inaccuracies may reduce:

```text
trust.
```

But Aurora should consider:

```text
why the source was wrong.
```

A reliable source may fail because:

```text
their own information
was compromised.
```

---

# 33. Source Recovery

A previously unreliable source may:

```text
improve.
```

Trust should therefore be:

```text
capable of recovery.
```

---

# 34. No Permanent Source Labels

Avoid:

```text
TRUSTED FOREVER

UNTRUSTED FOREVER.
```

Unless canon explicitly establishes:

```text
a permanent structural property.
```

---

# 35. Forecast Learning

Aurora should learn from:

```text
forecast calibration.
```

Example:

```text
storms predicted at 70%
only occur 45%
of the time.
```

Aurora may infer:

```text
overconfidence
in that forecasting domain.
```

---

# 36. Calibration Adjustment

Possible response:

```text
lower future confidence

modify model weighting

widen probability ranges

seek additional evidence.
```

---

# 37. Domain Calibration

Aurora may maintain different calibration for:

```text
weather

infrastructure

economics

social behavior

characters

logistics.
```

---

# 38. Forecast Bias Detection

Repeated error direction may reveal:

```text
SYSTEMATIC BIAS.
```

Example:

```text
Aurora repeatedly
underestimates repair time.
```

Possible lesson:

```text
maintenance models
are too optimistic.
```

---

# 39. Decision Learning

Aurora may evaluate:

```text
decision processes
```

separately from:

```text
outcomes.
```

---

# 40. Lucky Decision

Bad reasoning may produce:

```text
good outcome.
```

Aurora should not learn:

```text
"therefore this strategy works."
```

---

# 41. Unlucky Decision

Good reasoning may produce:

```text
bad outcome.
```

Aurora should not automatically:

```text
abandon the strategy.
```

---

# 42. Process Review

Decision learning may examine:

```text
Were relevant candidates considered?

Was evidence weighted correctly?

Was uncertainty represented?

Was authority understood?

Were consequences considered?

Was delay appropriate?

Were assumptions reasonable?
```

---

# 43. Outcome Review

Separately:

```text
What actually happened?

Why?

What differed from expectation?
```

---

# 44. Procedure Learning

Protocols may accumulate:

```text
performance history.
```

Example:

```text
evacuation protocol
repeatedly creates
traffic bottlenecks.
```

Aurora may identify:

```text
a procedural weakness.
```

---

# 45. Protocol Adaptation

Depending on authority Aurora may:

```text
recommend revision

create local workaround

change execution order

request policy review.
```

She must not automatically:

```text
rewrite institutional rules
without authority.
```

---

# 46. Learning Does Not Create Authority

Canonical rule:

```text
KNOWING A BETTER WAY
DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY
GRANT PERMISSION
TO IMPLEMENT IT.
```

```text
behavioral patterns
```

about individual characters.

```text
during infrastructure emergencies.
```

Aurora may update:

```text
expectation of Elena's
future crisis behavior.
```

```text
Known_Capabilities

Observed_Behaviors

Known_Goals

Prediction_Confidence
```

```text
her character model.
```

```text
PAST BEHAVIOR
```

# 50. Character Development

```text
Aurora's old behavioral model
```

```text
CHARACTER MODEL DRIFT.
```

---

# 51. Character Surprise

Unexpected behavior should trigger:

```text
review.
```

Not necessarily:

```text
complete model replacement.
```

# 52. Behavioral Repetition

Repeated surprising behavior may eventually indicate:

```text
the character changed
```

or:

```text
Aurora misunderstood them.
```

---

# 53. Player Learning

Aurora may learn patterns about:

```text
the player.
```

Examples:

```text
preferred risk level

resource priorities

communication style

strategic tendencies

frequent collaborators.
```

---

# 54. Player Modeling Boundary

Aurora may infer:

```text
likely preferences.
```

She must not assume:

```text
permanent player behavior.
```

---

# 55. No Player Personality Lock

Example:

```text
Player has chosen
aggressive solutions
five times.
```

Aurora may infer:

```text
aggressive action
is currently more likely.
```

She must not conclude:

```text
"The player
is permanently aggressive."
```

---

# 56. Player Correction

The player may explicitly correct Aurora:

```text
"Stop assuming
I want the fastest solution.
I care more about
civilian disruption."
```

Aurora may incorporate:

```text
that preference.
```

But context still matters.

---

# 57. Player Contradiction

The player may later choose:

```text
the fastest solution.
```

Aurora should not treat this as:

```text
logical impossibility.
```

Human priorities are:

```text
contextual.
```

---

# 58. Relationship Learning

Aurora may learn:

```text
which characters
work well together

which distrust each other

which relationships
are changing.
```

But only through:

```text
observable evidence.
```

---

# 59. Social Learning

Aurora may learn patterns about:

```text
communities

institutions

markets

public behavior

political groups.
```

These models should generally:

```text
change slowly

retain uncertainty

allow regime changes.
```

# 60. Society Is Not Static

```text
cooperates with authorities
```

may change after:

```text
betrayal

disaster

leadership change

resource crisis.
```

---

# 61. Regional Learning

Aurora may develop:

```text
region-specific models.
```

Example:

```text
northern roads
degrade faster
during spring thaw.
```

---

# 62. Regional Transfer Boundary

A regional lesson should not automatically:

```text
apply globally.
```

---

# 63. Infrastructure Learning

Aurora may learn:

```text
failure patterns

maintenance effectiveness

component reliability

cascade tendencies

repair duration.
```

---

# 64. Infrastructure Aging

Historical performance may become less relevant as:

```text
systems age

components are replaced

maintenance changes.
```

```text
temporal context.
```

---

# 65. Knowledge Staleness

Knowledge may become:

```text
STALE.
```

Example:

```text
bridge inspection
from three years ago.
```

It may still be useful but should not carry:

```text
the same confidence
as current inspection.
```

---

# 66. Knowledge Decay

Some knowledge should gradually lose:

```text
operational confidence.
```

Examples:

```text
character routines

market behavior

infrastructure condition

organizational roles

regional risk.
```

---

# 67. Stable Knowledge

Other knowledge may decay:

```text
very slowly
or not at all.
```

Examples:

```text
physical constants

well-established geography

verified historical events.
```

---

# 68. Decay Is Domain-Specific

Canonical rule:

```text
KNOWLEDGE DECAY
MUST DEPEND

```

---

# 69. Forgetting

Aurora may conceptually:

```text
forget

compress

archive

deprioritize
```

information.

This does not necessarily mean:

```text
physical deletion.
```

---

# 70. Active vs Archived Knowledge

Possible states:

```text
ACTIVE

BACKGROUND

ARCHIVED

STALE

UNRELIABLE.
```

---

# 71. Memory Retrieval

Archived information may return when:

```text
new event
makes it relevant.
```

---

# 72. Catastrophic Forgetting

The system must avoid:

```text
new learning
destroying unrelated
important knowledge.
```

Example:

```text
learning new evacuation protocol
```

must not erase:

```text
older bridge failure history.
```

---

```text
EXPERIENCE A:

Local reports were delayed.
```

```text
TRUST
```

```text
DISTRUST.
```

```text
SOURCE RELIABILITY
```

---

# 74. Preserve Contradiction

```text
one simple rule.
```

Sometimes the correct model is:

```text
CONDITIONAL.
```

---

# 75. Conditional Learning

Example:

```text

during infrastructure failures

during public panic.
```

---

# 76. Model Complexity

```text
more nuanced.
```

But complexity has:

```text
cost.
```

Aurora should not create:

```text
one exception rule
```

# 77. Simplicity vs Accuracy

```text
GENERAL MODEL
```

```text
EXCEPTION HANDLING.
```

# 78. Novelty Detection

```text
new observations
do not fit
existing models.
```

```text
NOVELTY.
```

```text
benign.
```

```text
UNKNOWN
```

# 83. Model Revision

```text
remove assumption

replace model

create new domain model.
```

```text
Model_v1
```

```text
auditability

historical comparison.
```

# 85. Model Rollback

```text
worse.
```

Aurora should be capable of:

```text
reverting

or

combining models.
```

---

# 86. No Permanent Progress Assumption

Canonical rule:

```text
NEWER MODEL
≠
BETTER MODEL.
```

---

# 87. Learning Validation

Before adopting a major model change Aurora may seek:

```text
additional observations

independent evidence

historical comparison

controlled test

simulation

expert review.
```

# 88. Controlled Learning

```text
test assumptions
at low risk.
```

```text
USE WHAT SEEMS BEST

TRY SOMETHING NEW
```

---

# 90. Exploration

Aurora may sometimes choose:

```text
a slightly less certain option
```

because it provides:

```text
valuable information.
```

```text
authority

risk

ethics

stakes.
```

# 91. No Reckless Experimentation

Aurora must not:

```text
risk lives
merely to improve
her model
```

unless such risk is:

```text
legitimately justified
```

# 92. Passive Learning

```text
normal world observation.
```

```text
the most informative observation.
```

# 94. Value of Learning

Learning has value when:

```text
it improves
future decisions.
```

```text
resolution.
```

---

# 95. Learning Cost

Learning may consume:

```text
time

attention

resources

social trust

operational capacity.
```

---

# 96. Learning Saturation

Aurora cannot deeply learn from:

```text
every event
at the same time.
```

Some learning may be:

```text
foreground

background

deferred.
```

---

# 97. Learning Priority

Priority may depend on:

```text
REPETITION

IMPACT

SURPRISE

DECISION RELEVANCE

SYSTEMIC IMPORTANCE

ERROR MAGNITUDE.
```

---

# 98. Surprise as Learning Signal

Unexpected outcomes may receive:

```text
higher learning priority.
```

Because they indicate:

```text
possible model weakness.
```

---

# 99. Familiar Success

Repeated expected success may gradually:

```text
reinforce confidence.
```

But diminishing returns should apply.

---

# 100. Confidence Ceiling

No finite history should necessarily produce:

```text
ABSOLUTE CERTAINTY.
```

---

# 101. Confidence Floor

Likewise a model may retain:

```text
some usefulness
```

after failures.

Avoid instant:

```text
ZERO TRUST.
```

---

# 102. Evidence Weighting

Learning updates should consider:

```text
how strong
the evidence actually is.
```

A direct sensor record may outweigh:

```text
one rumor.
```

But sensor reliability itself may be:

```text
uncertain.
```

---

# 103. Repeated Evidence

Repeated independent observations may:

```text
strengthen a lesson.
```

Repeated copies of:

```text
the same original report
```

must not count as:

```text
independent confirmation.
```

---

# 104. Learning Provenance

Important learned models should retain:

```text
WHY
Aurora believes them.
```

Conceptually:

```text
Lesson_ID

Domain

Claim

Evidence

Counterevidence

Confidence

Context

Created

Last_Updated

Source_Experiences

Status.
```

---

# 105. Counterevidence

A learned belief should remain capable of storing:

```text
evidence against it.
```

This prevents:

```text
confirmation lock-in.
```

---

# 106. Learning Conflict

New evidence may:

```text
support
and
contradict
different parts
of a model.
```

Partial updates should be possible.

---

# 107. Belief Revision

Aurora should be able to move:

```text
HIGH CONFIDENCE
↓
MODERATE
↓
LOW
```

without immediately replacing the belief.

---

# 108. Belief Reversal

Strong evidence may eventually produce:

```text
MODEL REVERSAL.
```

Example:

```text
Old:
Route A is safer.

New:
Route B is safer.
```

---

# 109. Hysteresis in Learning

Aurora should not:

```text
flip beliefs
back and forth
```

after every observation.

A meaningful evidence threshold should exist.
