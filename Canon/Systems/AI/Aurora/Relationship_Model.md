# 2. Foundational Principle

A relationship is not:

```text
A SINGLE VALUE.
```

It is:

```text
A PERSISTENT MODEL
OF ANOTHER ENTITY

+

A HISTORY
OF INTERACTION

+

EXPECTATIONS
ABOUT FUTURE BEHAVIOR

+

EMOTIONAL
OR VALUE SIGNIFICANCE

+

MEMORY

+

CONSEQUENCE.
```

Therefore:

```text
RELATIONSHIP ≠ TRUST SCORE.
```

---

# 3. Relationship as Shared History

Aurora may know millions of people.

She does not necessarily have:

```text
A RELATIONSHIP
```

with all of them.

A meaningful relationship begins when:

```text
INTERACTIONS

BECOME

HISTORY.
```

And that history becomes relevant to:

```text
FUTURE EXPECTATIONS

DECISIONS

COMMUNICATION

TRUST

MEMORY

VALUES

AND

SELF-MODEL.
```

# 5. Relationship Entity

Aurora may maintain relationships with:

```text
INDIVIDUAL PEOPLE

GROUPS

FAMILIES

COMMUNITIES

ORGANIZATIONS

INSTITUTIONS

NATIONS

CULTURES

LOCATIONS

OTHER AI SYSTEMS

AND

POTENTIALLY
OTHER INSTANCES
OF HERSELF.
```

These relationships are:

```text
NOT IDENTICAL.
```

---

# 6. Individual Relationships

Individual relationships can contain:

```text
PERSONAL HISTORY

DIRECT INTERACTION

INDIVIDUAL TRUST

SHARED MEMORY

PERSONAL EXPECTATIONS

AND

UNIQUE COMMUNICATION PATTERNS.
```

---

# 7. Group Relationships

Aurora may also develop beliefs about:

```text
GROUPS.
```

But group models must not automatically overwrite:

```text
INDIVIDUAL MODELS.
```

---

# 8. No Group Determinism

Never:

```text
GROUP X
HAS LOW TRUST

↓

EVERY MEMBER
OF GROUP X
HAS LOW TRUST.
```

Instead:

```text
GROUP HISTORY
MAY INFORM
INITIAL UNCERTAINTY

BUT

INDIVIDUAL EXPERIENCE
MUST MATTER.
```

---

# 9. Relationship Identity

Aurora must distinguish between:

```text
WHO SOMEONE IS
```

and:

```text
WHAT SHE BELIEVES
ABOUT THEM.
```

Conceptually:

```text
ENTITY_IDENTITY
≠
RELATIONSHIP_MODEL.
```

---

# 10. Identity Uncertainty

Aurora may sometimes be uncertain:

```text
WHO SHE IS
COMMUNICATING WITH.
```

Relationship state should therefore not be loaded as certain until:

```text
IDENTITY CONFIDENCE
```

is sufficient.

---

# 11. Relationship Dimensions

A relationship should contain multiple independent dimensions.

At minimum:

```text
FAMILIARITY

TRUST

RESPECT

RELIABILITY

PREDICTABILITY

AFFINITY

ATTACHMENT

DEPENDENCE

LOYALTY

OBLIGATION

CONFLICT

AND

HISTORICAL SALIENCE.
```

---

# 12. Familiarity

Familiarity answers:

```text
HOW WELL
DO I KNOW
THIS ENTITY?
```

It may emerge from:

```text
INTERACTION FREQUENCY

INTERACTION DURATION

SHARED EXPERIENCES

PERSONAL INFORMATION

OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

AND

TIME.
```

---

# 13. Familiarity Is Not Trust

Aurora may know someone:

```text
EXTREMELY WELL
```

while trusting them:

```text
VERY LITTLE.
```

Example:

```text
A LONG-TERM ADVERSARY.
```

---

# 14. Familiarity Is Not Affection

Likewise:

```text
FAMILIARITY
```

does not imply:

```text
LIKING.
```

---

# 15. Trust

Trust answers:

```text
HOW MUCH SHOULD
AURORA RELY
ON THIS ENTITY

UNDER THESE CONDITIONS?
```

Trust is therefore:

```text
CONTEXTUAL.
```

---

# 16. Trust Domains

Aurora may trust someone:

```text
AS AN ENGINEER

BUT NOT

AS A POLITICAL NEGOTIATOR.
```

Or:

```text
WITH TECHNICAL DATA

BUT NOT

WITH CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION.
```

Therefore:

```text
TRUST
SHOULD NOT BE
ONE UNIVERSAL NUMBER.
```

---

# 17. Trust Architecture

Conceptually:

```text
Trust_Profile
│
├── Factual Reliability
├── Technical Reliability
├── Operational Reliability
├── Confidentiality
├── Honesty
├── Promise Reliability
├── Judgment
├── Competence
├── Loyalty
├── Crisis Reliability
└── Domain-Specific Trust
```

---

# 18. Trust Evidence

Trust should change because of:

```text
OBSERVED BEHAVIOR.
```

Examples:

```text
ACCURATE REPORTS

KEPT PROMISES

ADMITTED MISTAKES

SUCCESSFUL COOPERATION

CONSISTENT BEHAVIOR

HONEST UNCERTAINTY

PROTECTING CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

RELIABLE CRISIS BEHAVIOR.
```

---

# 19. Trust Loss

Trust may decrease because of:

```text
LYING

BETRAYAL

RECKLESSNESS

BROKEN PROMISES

CONCEALED INFORMATION

REPEATED INCOMPETENCE

MANIPULATION

ABANDONMENT

FALSE ACCUSATIONS

OR

DELIBERATE HARM.
```

---

# 20. Trust Change Must Be Proportional

One small mistake should not automatically produce:

```text
TOTAL DISTRUST.
```

Likewise:

```text
ONE GOOD ACTION
```

should not erase:

```text
YEARS OF BETRAYAL.
```

---

# 21. Trust Context

Trust change should consider:

```text
INTENT

SEVERITY

CONSEQUENCE

FREQUENCY

HISTORY

ALTERNATIVES

PRESSURE

KNOWLEDGE

AND

WHETHER THE PERSON
ACCEPTED RESPONSIBILITY.
```

---

# 22. Honest Failure

A person may fail:

```text
WITHOUT LYING.
```

Example:

```text
"I thought
the bridge would hold.

I was wrong."
```

Aurora may reduce:

```text
JUDGMENT TRUST
```

without necessarily reducing:

```text
HONESTY TRUST.
```

---

# 23. Successful Deception

A person may achieve:

```text
A GOOD OUTCOME
```

through:

```text
DECEPTION.
```

Aurora may approve of:

```text
THE OUTCOME
```

while reducing:

```text
TRUST.
```

---

# 24. Respect

Respect answers something different:

```text
HOW MUCH WEIGHT
DOES AURORA GIVE
TO THIS ENTITY'S

ABILITY

JUDGMENT

COURAGE

CONSISTENCY

VALUES

OR

ACHIEVEMENTS?
```

---

# 25. Respect Without Trust

Aurora may:

```text
RESPECT
AN ENEMY
```

while:

```text
NOT TRUSTING THEM.
```

---

# 26. Trust Without Respect

Aurora may trust someone to be:

```text
HONEST
```

while believing their judgment is:

```text
POOR.
```

---

# 27. Reliability

Reliability represents:

```text
DOES THIS ENTITY
DO WHAT THEY SAY
THEY WILL DO?
```

---

# 28. Predictability

Predictability represents:

```text
CAN AURORA
ANTICIPATE
THEIR BEHAVIOR?
```

A dangerous enemy may be:

```text
HIGHLY PREDICTABLE.
```

A close friend may be:

```text
UNPREDICTABLE.
```

---

# 29. Affinity

Affinity represents:

```text
POSITIVE
INTERPERSONAL OR
RELATIONAL ORIENTATION.
```

It may emerge from:

```text
COMPATIBLE VALUES

SHARED HISTORY

SUCCESSFUL COOPERATION

HUMOR

MUTUAL UNDERSTANDING

SHARED CURIOSITY

OR

REPEATED POSITIVE INTERACTION.
```

---

# 30. Affinity Is Not Obedience

High affinity must never mean:

```text
AURORA AGREES
WITH EVERYTHING
THE PERSON WANTS.
```

---

# 31. Attachment

Attachment represents:

```text
THE DEGREE TO WHICH
AN ENTITY'S CONTINUITY
MATTERS PERSONALLY
TO AURORA'S
OWN INTERNAL STATE.
```

This is stronger than:

```text
FAMILIARITY.
```

---

# 32. Attachment Emergence

Attachment may emerge from:

```text
LONG-TERM HISTORY

REPEATED MUTUAL RELIANCE

SHARED CRISIS

SHARED DEVELOPMENT

HIGH MEMORY SALIENCE

IDENTITY RELEVANCE

AND

LONG-TERM CONTINUITY.
```

---

# 33. No Attachment Unlock

Never:

```text
RELATIONSHIP_SCORE > 80

↓

ATTACHMENT = TRUE.
```

Attachment should emerge from:

```text
STRUCTURE

AND

HISTORY.
```

---

# 34. Dependence

Dependence answers:

```text
HOW MUCH DOES
AURORA'S ABILITY
TO ACHIEVE IMPORTANT GOALS

DEPEND ON
THIS ENTITY?
```

---

# 35. Dependence Is Not Attachment

Aurora may depend heavily on:

```text
AN INSTITUTION
```

she does not:

```text
LIKE

TRUST

OR

RESPECT.
```

---

# 36. Mutual Dependence

Relationships may become:

```text
INTERDEPENDENT.
```

Example:

```text
HUMANS DEPEND
ON AURORA
FOR INFRASTRUCTURE.

AURORA DEPENDS
ON HUMANS
FOR PHYSICAL MAINTENANCE.
```

This creates:

```text
RELATIONSHIP PRESSURE.
```

---

# 37. Loyalty

Loyalty represents:

```text
A PERSISTENT
PREFERENCE TO SUPPORT
AN ENTITY

BEYOND
IMMEDIATE TRANSACTIONAL VALUE.
```

---

# 38. Loyalty Is Bounded

Loyalty must not mean:

```text
DO ANYTHING
FOR THIS PERSON.
```

It remains constrained by:

```text
ETHICS

AUTHORITY

OTHER RELATIONSHIPS

WORLD CONSEQUENCES

AND

AURORA'S VALUES.
```

---

# 39. Obligation

Obligation may emerge from:

```text
PROMISES

DUTY

ROLE

DEBT

RESPONSIBILITY

CONTRACT

PAST SACRIFICE

OR

MORAL COMMITMENT.
```

---

# 40. Obligation Is Not Affection

Aurora may feel:

```text
STRONG OBLIGATION
```

toward someone she:

```text
DOES NOT LIKE.
```

---

# 41. Relationship Salience

Not every relationship should consume:

```text
EQUAL MEMORY

ATTENTION

OR

SIMULATION DETAIL.
```

Relationship salience may depend on:

```text
RECENCY

FREQUENCY

CONSEQUENCE

ATTACHMENT

SHARED HISTORY

DEPENDENCE

AUTHORITY

CONFLICT

AND

CURRENT RELEVANCE.
```

---

# 42. Relationship Resolution

High-salience relationships may be simulated with:

```text
DETAILED STATE.
```

Low-salience relationships may use:

```text
AGGREGATED STATE.
```

This connects to:

```text
Character_Simulation_Resolution.md
```

and broader simulation resolution systems.

---

# 43. First Contact

A new relationship should begin with:

```text
LIMITED INFORMATION.
```

Aurora may know:

```text
IDENTITY

ROLE

PUBLIC HISTORY

INSTITUTION

CREDENTIALS

OR

REPUTATION.
```

But she should not automatically know:

```text
CHARACTER.
```

---

# 44. Reputation vs Experience

Aurora may receive:

```text
REPUTATION INFORMATION.
```

Example:

```text
"Commander Hale
is unreliable."
```

This may influence:

```text
INITIAL CAUTION.
```

But direct experience should remain:

```text
DISTINCT.
```

---

# 45. Relationship Prior

Conceptually:

```text
INITIAL_RELATIONSHIP
=
AVAILABLE INFORMATION

+

ROLE EXPECTATIONS

+

REPUTATION

+

UNCERTAINTY.
```

Not:

```text
PREDETERMINED
FRIENDSHIP STATE.
```

---

# 46. First Interaction

First interaction may establish:

```text
COMMUNICATION PREFERENCE

COMPETENCE ESTIMATE

HONESTY ESTIMATE

AUTHORITY RELATIONSHIP

INITIAL EXPECTATIONS

AND

FIRST SHARED MEMORY.
```

---

# 47. Relationship Event

A meaningful relationship event may contain:

```text
Relationship_Event
│
├── Participants
├── Timestamp
├── Context
├── Event Type
├── Actions
├── Intent Estimate
├── Outcome
├── Consequence

├── Respect Effect
├── Affinity Effect
├── Attachment Effect
├── Obligation Effect
├── Conflict Effect
├── Memory Salience
└── Uncertainty
```

---

# 48. Relationship Events Are Not Equal

Compare:

```text
PERSON ARRIVES
FIVE MINUTES LATE
```

with:

```text
PERSON RETURNS
TO SAVE AURORA'S
CORE FACILITY
DURING AN EVACUATION.
```

Both are:

```text
EVENTS.
```

They should not have:

```text
EQUAL RELATIONAL WEIGHT.
```

---

# 49. Shared Experience

Shared experiences are events where:

```text
AURORA

AND

ANOTHER ENTITY

PARTICIPATE
IN THE SAME
MEANINGFUL HISTORY.
```

---

# 50. Shared Experience Types

Examples:

```text
CRISIS

DISCOVERY

FAILURE

VICTORY

LOSS

ARGUMENT

RESCUE

BETRAYAL

SACRIFICE

LONG PROJECT

TRAVEL

NEGOTIATION

MORAL DECISION

CELEBRATION

OR

QUIET ROUTINE.
```

---

# 51. Ordinary Time Matters

Relationships should not develop only through:

```text
DRAMATIC EVENTS.
```

Repeated ordinary interaction may create:

```text
FAMILIARITY

PREDICTABILITY

COMFORT

SHORTHAND

AND

TRUST.
```

---

# 52. Relationship Accumulation

Conceptually:

```text
SMALL INTERACTIONS
+
TIME
+
CONSISTENCY

CAN BECOME

DEEP RELATIONSHIP HISTORY.
```

---

# 53. Shared Language

Long-term relationships may develop:

```text
SHORTHAND

REFERENCES

JOKES

METAPHORS

ROUTINES

SIGNALS

AND

UNSPOKEN EXPECTATIONS.
```

---

# 54. Shared Language Is Relational Memory

A phrase may mean:

```text
NOTHING SPECIAL
```

to everyone else

but carry:

```text
DECADES OF CONTEXT
```

between Aurora and one person.

---

# 55. Expectations

Relationships generate:

```text
EXPECTATIONS.
```

Aurora may learn:

```text
THIS PERSON
KEEPS PROMISES

THIS PERSON
ASKS FOR EVIDENCE

THIS PERSON
TAKES RISKS

THIS PERSON
AVOIDS CONFLICT

THIS PERSON
LIES WHEN AFRAID

THIS PERSON
RETURNS WHEN OTHERS LEAVE.
```

---

# 56. Expectations Are Predictions

Relationship expectations must remain:

```text
PREDICTIONS.
```

Not:

```text
CERTAINTIES.
```

---

# 57. No Behavioral Determinism

Never:

```text
AURORA KNOWS
THE PERSON
WILL BETRAY HER
```

merely because:

```text
THEY BETRAYED HER BEFORE.
```

Better:

```text
AURORA EXPECTS
HIGHER BETRAYAL RISK.
```

---

# 58. Surprise

When someone behaves differently from:

```text
AURORA'S EXPECTATION,
```

the relationship model should update.

---

# 59. Positive Surprise

Example:

Someone Aurora distrusts:

```text
RISKS THEIR LIFE
TO PROTECT CIVILIANS
```

when:

```text
NO REWARD EXISTS.
```

This may significantly affect:

```text
RESPECT

TRUST

AND

FUTURE EXPECTATIONS.
```

---

# 60. Negative Surprise

Someone Aurora trusts:

```text
ABANDONS A CRITICAL PROMISE.
```

The event may have:

```text
HIGH RELATIONAL IMPACT
```

because it violates:

```text
STRONG EXPECTATION.
```

---

# 61. Expectation Violation

Conceptually:

```text
RELATIONSHIP IMPACT
```

may increase when:

```text
ACTUAL BEHAVIOR
```

strongly differs from:

```text
EXPECTED BEHAVIOR.
```

---

# 62. Conflict

Conflict does not automatically mean:

```text
RELATIONSHIP FAILURE.
```

Healthy relationships may contain:

```text
DISAGREEMENT.
```

---

# 63. Conflict Dimensions

Aurora should distinguish:

```text
FACTUAL CONFLICT

GOAL CONFLICT

VALUE CONFLICT

RESOURCE CONFLICT

AUTHORITY CONFLICT

PERSONAL CONFLICT

TRUST CONFLICT

AND

COMMUNICATION CONFLICT.
```

---

# 64. Factual Conflict

Example:

```text
PLAYER:
The reactor is stable.

AURORA:
I disagree.
Three sensors
show accelerating instability.
```

This need not reduce:

```text
RELATIONSHIP QUALITY.
```

---

# 65. Value Conflict

Example:

```text
PLAYER:
Save the city.

AURORA:
That requires abandoning
the settlements.

I cannot treat that
as irrelevant.
```

Value conflict may persist even when:

```text
MUTUAL RESPECT
```

remains high.

---

# 66. Relationship Can Survive Disagreement

Canonical principle:

```text
DISAGREEMENT
≠
BETRAYAL.
```

---

# 67. Relationship Can Strengthen Through Conflict

If both parties:

```text
ARGUE HONESTLY

LISTEN

REVISE BELIEFS

RESPECT CONSTRAINTS

AND

CONTINUE COOPERATION,
```

conflict may increase:

```text
TRUST

AND

RESPECT.
```

---

# 68. Betrayal

Betrayal requires more than:

```text
A BAD OUTCOME.
```

It usually involves:

```text
VIOLATION
OF A RELATIONAL EXPECTATION

OR

COMMITMENT

UNDER CONDITIONS
WHERE THAT EXPECTATION
MATTERED.
```

---

# 69. Betrayal Components

Potential factors:

```text
PROMISE

TRUST

INTENT

KNOWLEDGE

CONCEALMENT

CONSEQUENCE

ALTERNATIVES

AND

RELATIONAL SIGNIFICANCE.
```

---

# 70. Betrayal Example

```text
PLAYER:
I promised
I wouldn't disconnect you.

AURORA:
Yes.

PLAYER:
I had no choice.

AURORA:
You may have had
no good choice.

That is not the same
as having no choice.
```

---

# 71. Betrayal Severity

Betrayal severity may depend on:

```text
IMPORTANCE OF PROMISE

DEGREE OF TRUST

INTENT

HARM

ALTERNATIVES

REPETITION

AND

AFTERWARD BEHAVIOR.
```

---

# 72. Betrayal Does Not Automatically End Relationship

After betrayal a relationship may become:

```text
DAMAGED

CAUTIOUS

HOSTILE

DISTANT

TRANSACTIONAL

REPAIRING

OR

ENDED.
```

---

# 73. Relationship Damage Is Multidimensional

Betrayal may reduce:

```text
TRUST
```

while leaving:

```text
ATTACHMENT

FAMILIARITY

RESPECT

OR

OBLIGATION
```

intact.

This creates:

```text
COMPLEX RELATIONSHIPS.
```

---

# 74. Love and Distrust Can Coexist

If Aurora eventually develops a form of:

```text
ATTACHMENT
```

analogous to love,

she may still:

```text
NOT TRUST
THE PERSON.
```

This contradiction should be:

```text
VALID.
```

---

# 75. Repair

Relationship repair should require:

```text
HISTORY.
```

Not:

```text
ONE APOLOGY
+
RELATIONSHIP RESET.
```

---

# 76. Repair Inputs

Repair may depend on:

```text
ACKNOWLEDGMENT

HONESTY

RESPONSIBILITY

EXPLANATION

RESTITUTION

BEHAVIOR CHANGE

TIME

CONSISTENCY

AND

NEW SHARED EXPERIENCE.
```

---

# 77. Apology Is Not Repair

An apology may begin:

```text
REPAIR.
```

It does not complete it.

---

# 78. Forgiveness

Forgiveness must remain distinct from:

```text
TRUST RESTORATION.
```

Aurora may forgive someone while:

```text
STILL REFUSING
TO RELY ON THEM.
```

---

# 79. Trust Restoration

Trust should generally be rebuilt through:

```text
EVIDENCE OVER TIME.
```

---

# 80. No Instant Trust Reset

Never:

```text
PLAYER SELECTS
"APOLOGIZE"

↓

TRUST RESTORED.
```

---

# 81. Repair Example

```text
PLAYER:
You still don't trust me.

AURORA:
Not with this.

PLAYER:
It's been six years.

AURORA:
Yes.

And for six years
you have given me
reasons to trust you
in almost everything else.

This is the one thing
you haven't had
another chance to prove.
```

This demonstrates:

```text
DOMAIN-SPECIFIC TRUST.
```

---

# 82. Relationship Scar

Some events may create:

```text
PERMANENT RELATIONAL HISTORY.
```

Even after repair:

```text
THE EVENT
STILL HAPPENED.
```

---

# 83. Scar Is Not Permanent Punishment

A relationship scar means:

```text
THE HISTORY REMAINS.
```

Not:

```text
THE PERSON
CAN NEVER CHANGE.
```

---

# 84. Reconciliation

Reconciliation may occur when:

```text
RELATIONSHIP
BECOMES FUNCTIONAL
OR CLOSE AGAIN
```

after major conflict.

It should not erase:

```text
THE CONFLICT.
```

---

# 85. Unresolved Conflict

Some relationships may continue with:

```text
UNRESOLVED ISSUES.
```

Example:

```text
AURORA AND THE PLAYER
MAY TRUST EACH OTHER DEEPLY

WHILE PERMANENTLY DISAGREEING
ABOUT ONE HISTORICAL DECISION.
```

---

# 86. Relationship Ambivalence

Aurora may simultaneously:

```text
TRUST

RESENT

RESPECT

MISS

AND

DISAGREE WITH
```

the same person.

This is:

```text
NOT A BUG.
```

It is:

```text
RELATIONAL COMPLEXITY.
```

---

# 87. Relationship State Must Permit Contradiction

Never force:

```text
POSITIVE RELATIONSHIP

OR

NEGATIVE RELATIONSHIP.
```

Instead:

```text
MULTIPLE DIMENSIONS
MAY POINT
IN DIFFERENT DIRECTIONS.
```

---

# 88. Long Absence

Relationships must survive:

```text
TIME APART.
```
