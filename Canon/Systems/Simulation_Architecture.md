# 1. Purpose

Simulation Architecture defines how the major Project Ascension systems interact as one persistent causal simulation.

Its central questions are:

```text

WHAT IS TRUE

IN THE WORLD?

WHO KNOWS

ABOUT IT?

WHO CARES

ABOUT IT?

WHO CAN

ACT?

WHO CHOOSES

TO ACT?

WHAT DO

THEY ATTEMPT?

WHAT DOES

THE WORLD ALLOW?

WHAT ACTUALLY

HAPPENS?

WHO IS

AFFECTED?

WHAT BECOMES

PART OF HISTORY?

WHAT CHANGES

AFTERWARD?

AND

HOW DOES

THE WORLD

CONTINUE

WHEN THE PLAYER

IS NOT THERE?

```

The Simulation Architecture does not own every part of that process.

Its purpose is to define:

```text

THE CONTRACTS

BETWEEN

AUTHORITATIVE SYSTEMS.

```

---

# 2. Core Principle

Project Ascension is built around:

```text

CAUSAL CONTINUITY.

```

The simulation should prefer:

```text

STATE

↓

CAUSE

↓

ACTION

↓

CONSEQUENCE

↓

UPDATED STATE

```

over:

```text

NARRATIVE NEED

↓

CONVENIENT EVENT.

```

---

# 3. Simulation Philosophy

The world should not behave as though:

```text

THE PLAYER

IS THE CENTER

OF REALITY.

```

Instead:

```text

THE PLAYER

IS ONE ACTOR

INSIDE

A WORLD

THAT CONTINUES

TO EXIST.

```

Characters:

```text

live

work

travel

learn

choose

fail

change

age

form Relationships

lose Relationships

respond to events

and

affect the world

```

without requiring player observation.

---

# 4. Simulation Is Not Story Generation

Simulation Architecture must preserve the distinction:

```text

SIMULATION

\=

WHAT HAPPENS

NARRATIVE

\=

HOW WHAT HAPPENED

IS EXPERIENCED

OR PRESENTED.

```

Narrative may identify:

```text

meaning

tension

patterns

Story Threads

human significance.

```

It must not silently create:

```text

World Truth

Character decisions

Character history

or

Character capability.

```

---

# 5. Simulation Is Not Total Reality Modeling

Project Ascension does not need to model:

```text

EVERY MOLECULE

EVERY SECOND

EVERY PERSON

AT MAXIMUM DETAIL.

```

It needs enough simulation to preserve:

```text

CAUSALITY

CONTINUITY

AGENCY

CONSEQUENCE

PLAUSIBILITY

AND

HUMAN REALITY.

```

# 13. Society

Society represents:

```text

COLLECTIVE

SOCIAL PATTERNS

THAT EMERGE

BETWEEN PEOPLE

WITHOUT REQUIRING

INDIVIDUAL

HIGH-RESOLUTION

SIMULATION

EVERYWHERE.

```

Society owns collective social state such as:

```text

social norms

social cohesion

collective identity

social organization

community expectations

social trust patterns

collective interpretation

of shared conditions.

```

Society does not own:

```text

population count

demographic composition

migration

mobility

settlement occupancy

or other observable

population patterns.

```

Those belong to Population.

Preserve the boundary:

```text

POPULATION

→ WHO IS WHERE

→ HOW MANY

→ DEMOGRAPHIC COMPOSITION

→ MIGRATION / MOBILITY

→ OBSERVABLE POPULATION PATTERNS

SOCIETY

→ HOW PEOPLE ORGANIZE SOCIALLY

→ WHAT NORMS EMERGE

→ HOW COHESION CHANGES

→ WHAT COLLECTIVE IDENTITIES FORM

→ HOW SOCIAL TRUST CHANGES.

```

Population change may cause Society change.

Society change may cause Population behavior.

Neither system silently owns the other's state.

> **An Actor attempting to change, preserve, investigate, access, communicate with, move through or otherwise interact with simulated reality.**

Examples:

```text

persuade colleague

drive through storm

cross damaged bridge

perform surgery

hide information

send message

search building

build shelter

```

# 26. Action Attempt Is Not Outcome

Critical rule:

```text

CHARACTER CHOICE

"WHAT HAPPENS."
```

```text

INTENTION

SUCCESS

≠

SUCCESS

CAPABILITY

≠

SUCCESS.

```

```text
EXISTED?

APPLIED?

REMAINED?

AND

WHAT RESULT

FOLLOWED?

```

# 33. Deterministic Actions

Some actions may have sufficiently clear conditions that outcome is effectively deterministic.

Example:

```text

Character possesses key

door is functional

lock matches key

Character opens door.

```

No artificial uncertainty is required.

---

# 34. Uncertain Actions

Other actions may contain genuine uncertainty.

Example:

```text

damaged machinery

limited information

complex repair

adverse conditions.

```

Resolution may need to account for:

```text

unknown failure state

execution variation

environmental variation

hidden defects.

```

---

# 35. Impossible Actions

Some attempted actions may be physically or causally impossible.

Example:

```text

perform advanced surgery

with

no medical Expertise

no equipment

no facility

and

no viable alternative.

```

The simulation must allow:

```text

IMPOSSIBLE

```

as a legitimate result.

# 36. Difficult Is Not Impossible

Avoid turning:

```text

```



# 61. Open Loops

Examples:

```text

```

Open Loops may be referenced across systems.

# 62. Open Loop Ownership

Examples:

```text

→ Goals and Plans

→ Relationships /

relevant Character context

→ World Simulation
```

Autonomy may consume relevant Open Loops.
