# 1. Purpose

The Relationships System defines persistent interpersonal relationships in Project Ascension.

Its purpose is to ensure that interactions between people create lasting relational consequences.

Relationships may include:

- friendship
- family
- affection
- love
- trust
- loyalty
- professional connection
- mentorship
- dependence
- obligation
- respect
- rivalry
- resentment
- fear
- distrust
- hostility
- betrayal
- reconciliation
- shared history

The Relationships System exists because human interaction should not reset when a scene ends.

If two people:

```text
HELP ONE ANOTHER

BETRAY ONE ANOTHER

TRAVEL TOGETHER

SURVIVE TOGETHER

LIE TO ONE ANOTHER

TRUST ONE ANOTHER

LOVE ONE ANOTHER

OR

LOSE SOMEONE TOGETHER
```

their future relationship should be capable of changing because of it.

---

# 2. Canonical Role

Within the Project Ascension systems architecture:

```text
CHARACTER A
        ↕
RELATIONSHIP STATE
        ↕
CHARACTER B
```

The Relationships System owns the persistent relational state connecting actors.

It provides continuity between interactions.

Conceptually:

```text
INTERACTION
↓
RELATIONAL EVENT
↓
INTERPRETATION
↓
RELATIONSHIP CHANGE
↓
PERSISTENT RELATIONAL STATE
↓
FUTURE INTERACTION
```

The relationship then becomes part of the context affecting what happens next.

---

# 3. Authoritative Ownership

The Relationships System owns:

```text
PERSISTENT INTERPERSONAL RELATIONSHIP STATE

SHARED RELATIONAL HISTORY

RELATIONAL TRUST

RELATIONAL LOYALTY

RELATIONAL OBLIGATION

RELATIONAL CONFLICT

RELATIONAL FAMILIARITY

RELATIONAL DEPENDENCE

RELATIONAL REPUTATION BETWEEN ACTORS

RELATIONAL COMMITMENTS

RELATIONAL RUPTURES

AND

RELATIONAL CONTINUITY.
```

The Relationships System does not own:

```text

PRIVATE CHARACTER MEMORY

SOCIETAL STATE

PUBLIC REPUTATION

WORLD TRUTH

AURORA'S INTERNAL MODEL

CAMPAIGN RELEVANCE

```

Those belong to other systems.

---

# 4. Relationship State vs Character State

This distinction is foundational.

The Relationships System may establish:

```text
A AND B HAVE A LONG-STANDING
PROFESSIONAL RELATIONSHIP

WITH HIGH HISTORICAL TRUST.
```

Character A may currently believe:

```text
B IS HIDING SOMETHING.
```

Character B may currently believe:

```text
A NO LONGER TRUSTS ME.
```

Both may be wrong.

The shared relationship history still exists.

Therefore:

```text
RELATIONSHIP STATE
≠
CHARACTER A'S INTERPRETATION

RELATIONSHIP STATE
≠
CHARACTER B'S INTERPRETATION.
```

# 5. Relationship Truth

Relationship truth is more complex than a single numerical value.

A relationship may simultaneously contain:

```text
TRUST

AFFECTION

RESENTMENT

DEPENDENCE

LOYALTY

FEAR

OBLIGATION

AND

SHARED HISTORY.
```

For example:

```text
A loves B.

A trusts B professionally.

A resents B personally.

A depends on B for survival.

B feels responsible for A.

Neither wants to admit how dependent
they have become on the other.
```

This is not contradictory.

It is human.

# 7. No Universal Relationship Score

Project Ascension should avoid collapsing all relational state into one universal:

```text
LIKE / DISLIKE
```

axis.

A person may:

```text
DISLIKE SOMEONE
BUT TRUST THEIR COMPETENCE.
```

A person may:

```text
LOVE SOMEONE
BUT NOT TRUST THEM.
```

A person may:

```text
FEAR SOMEONE
WHILE REMAINING LOYAL.
```

A person may:

```text
RESPECT SOMEONE
THEY CONSIDER AN ENEMY.
```

The Relationships System must allow these states to coexist.

# 11. Expectation Matters

The same action may produce different relational consequences depending on expectation.

Example:

```text
A stranger refuses to lend you money.
```

This may produce little relational change.

But:

```text
your closest friend refuses to help
during an emergency after promising
they always would.
```

may produce substantial change.

Therefore:

```text
EVENT
+
RELATIONSHIP HISTORY
+
EXPECTATION
=
RELATIONAL CONSEQUENCE.
```

---

# 12. Context Matters

Relationships must interpret behavior within context.

For example:

```text
B leaves A behind.
```

This could represent:

```text
BETRAYAL
```

or:

```text
A DESPERATE ATTEMPT
TO SAVE B'S CHILD.
```

The physical action alone does not define the relational meaning.

Context matters.

---

# 13. Intention vs Consequence

Relationship change does not depend solely on intention.

A person may intend to help and still cause harm.

A person may accidentally save someone.

A person may lie for protective reasons.

Therefore:

```text
INTENTION
≠
OUTCOME
≠
INTERPRETATION.
```

All three may influence relational development.

---

# 14. Perceived Events

Relationships are partly shaped by what participants believe happened.

This creates an important distinction between:

```text
WORLD TRUTH
```

and:

```text
RELATIONAL EXPERIENCE.
```

Example:

```text
WORLD TRUTH:

B never received A's message.
```

A may believe:

```text
B ignored me.
```

The relationship may deteriorate even though B did nothing intentionally wrong.

Later discovery of the truth may produce:

```text
RECONSIDERATION

REPAIR

GUILT

OR

NEW CONFLICT.
```

---

# 15. Shared History vs Private Interpretation

The relationship may contain an authoritative history of relational events.

But each participant may remember or interpret that history differently.

Conceptually:

```text
RELATIONAL EVENT HISTORY
        ↓
     /     \
    /       \
A MEMORY   B MEMORY
    ↓         ↓
A BELIEF   B BELIEF
```

The Relationships System preserves the relationship.

Characters preserve their own subjective experience.

---

# 16. Trust

Trust is one of the most important relational dimensions.

Trust should not be treated as a single universal quantity.

Possible trust domains include:

```text
PERSONAL TRUST

PROFESSIONAL TRUST

INFORMATION TRUST

PHYSICAL SAFETY TRUST

MORAL TRUST

CONFIDENTIALITY TRUST

DECISION-MAKING TRUST

SURVIVAL TRUST.
```

A person may trust someone in one domain and distrust them in another.

---

# 17. Trust Formation

Trust may grow through:

```text
CONSISTENCY

RELIABILITY

HONESTY

COMPETENCE

SHARED EXPERIENCE

VULNERABILITY

RECIPROCITY

AND

TIME.
```

Trust should normally require evidence.

Instant trust may occur.

But it should arise from:

```text
PERSONALITY

CONTEXT

CULTURE

NECESSITY

OR

PERCEPTION.
```

---

# 18. Trust Loss

Trust may decline through:

```text
BETRAYAL

DECEPTION

BROKEN PROMISES

INCOMPETENCE

ABANDONMENT

INCONSISTENCY

MANIPULATION

OR

REPEATED FAILURE.
```

The magnitude should depend on:

```text
PREVIOUS TRUST

EXPECTATION

SEVERITY

MOTIVE

CONSEQUENCE

AND

RELATIONSHIP HISTORY.
```

---

# 19. Trust Is Asymmetric

A may trust B more than B trusts A.

Therefore:

```text
TRUST(A → B)
≠
TRUST(B → A).
```

This asymmetry is fundamental.

Many relational dimensions may be directional.

Conceptually:

```text

↓
B MAY FEEL
INCREASED TRUST
OR OBLIGATION.
```

A person may:

```text

reject the debt

or

```

Character context matters.

# 26. Familiarity

Familiarity reflects accumulated exposure and knowledge between people.

Long-term familiarity may influence:

- communication efficiency
- expectation
- conflict
- prediction
- trust
- emotional intensity
- interpretation
- cooperation

Familiarity does not imply positive relationship.

Enemies may know one another extremely well.

# 27. Intimacy

It may include:

```text
EMOTIONAL INTIMACY

SHARED SECRETS.
```

Intimacy creates potential:

```text
TRUST

CONNECTION

AND

VULNERABILITY.
```

---

# 28. Conflict

Conflict is not equivalent to relationship failure.

Healthy relationships may contain disagreement.

Conflict may:

```text
damage relationships

clarify boundaries

increase understanding

create respect

reveal incompatibility

or

strengthen trust after resolution.
```

The outcome depends on context and behavior.

---

# 29. Betrayal

Betrayal requires:

```text
EXPECTATION
+
VIOLATION.
```

Without expectation there may be harm.

But betrayal specifically involves a relationship-based expectation being broken.

The severity of betrayal may depend on:

```text
TRUST

INTIMACY

DEPENDENCE

CONSEQUENCE

INTENT

AND

HISTORY.
```

---

# 30. Betrayal Persistence

Betrayal should not disappear because a scene ends.

It may create:

```text
LONG-TERM DISTRUST

ANGER

FEAR

REVENGE

WITHDRAWAL

CHANGED EXPECTATIONS

RELATIONSHIP RUPTURE

OR

EVENTUAL RECONCILIATION.
```

Relational consequences persist.

---

# 31. Forgiveness

Forgiveness must not function as:

```text
RESET RELATIONSHIP.
```

A person may forgive someone while still:

```text
trusting them less

maintaining boundaries

remembering the event

refusing previous dependence

or

ending the relationship.
```

Forgiveness changes the relationship.

It does not erase history.

---

It may require:

```text
ACKNOWLEDGMENT

ACCOUNTABILITY

TIME

CONSISTENT BEHAVIOR

RESTITUTION

SHARED NEED

OR

NEW UNDERSTANDING.
```

Reconciliation should not automatically restore the previous relationship.

The repaired relationship may become:

```text
DIFFERENT.
```

- betrayal
- incompatibility
- violence
- abandonment
- distance
- ideological conflict
- death
- changing priorities
- accumulated resentment
- institutional pressure

Ending a relationship does not erase its history.


```text

LIFE-SAVING SACRIFICE

DEEP LOVE

TRAUMATIC CONFLICT.
```

Decay should therefore be selective.


# 45. Reputation vs Relationship

Reputation is broader than interpersonal relationship.

Example:

```text
A has never met B.

A has heard that B is dangerous.
```

This is not yet a developed interpersonal relationship.

It is:

```text
REPUTATIONAL INFORMATION.
```

Once interaction occurs, reputation may influence initial expectations.

Society or another appropriate system may own broader reputation.

Relationships owns persistent actor-to-actor relational state.

---

# 46. Relationship Networks

Individual relationships create networks.

Conceptually:

```text
A ↔ B
A ↔ C
B ↔ D
C ↔ D
D ↔ E
```

These networks may influence:

- information spread
- trust propagation
- cooperation
- conflict
- social access
- reputation
- resource access
- community structure

However:

```text
A TRUSTS B

AND

B TRUSTS C
```

does not automatically mean:

```text
A TRUSTS C.
```

Relationship state should not propagate mechanically.

---

# 47. Social Triangles

Relationships involving three or more actors may create emergent dynamics.

Example:

```text
A trusts B.

B trusts C.

A distrusts C.
```

B may experience:

```text
LOYALTY CONFLICT.
```

Another example:

```text
A and B are rivals.

Both trust C.
```

C may become:

```text
MEDIATOR

INFORMATION BRIDGE

OR

SOURCE OF COMPETITION.
```

These patterns should emerge from individual relationships rather than require scripted drama.

---

# 48. Group Relationships

```text
SOCIETY
```

or another appropriate collective system.

Relationships owns the interpersonal links contributing to that cohesion.

Therefore:

```text
GROUP COHESION
≠
SUM OF FRIENDSHIP SCORES.
```

# 50. No Relationship Farming

Project Ascension should avoid conventional mechanics where repeating a simple action mechanically increases relationship status.

For example:

```text
GIVE GIFT
+5 RELATIONSHIP
```

should not be the governing model.

Instead:

```text
ACTION
+
CONTEXT
+
PERSON
+
HISTORY
+
EXPECTATION
=
RELATIONAL CONSEQUENCE.
```

A gift may:

```text
delight one person

embarrass another

insult a third

create obligation

or

be interpreted as manipulation.
```

---

# 51. Player Persuasion

```text

```

```text

negotiate

deceive

appeal

threaten

or

```

# 52. Relationship Leverage

Existing relationships may create opportunities.

For example:

```text

```

may increase willingness to share information.

```text

```

may increase willingness to help.

```text

```

---

# 53. Coercion

Compliance created through threat is not equivalent to trust.

Therefore:

```text
COMPLIANCE
≠
LOYALTY

FEAR
≠
RESPECT

DEPENDENCE
≠
AFFECTION.
```

A system that confuses these would produce unrealistic human behavior.

# 54. Secrets

```text

TRUST

VULNERABILITY

OBLIGATION

OR

RISK.
```

Revealing that secret to others may create betrayal.

# 55. Promises

Promises create relational expectations.

A promise should ideally preserve:

```text
WHO PROMISED

WHAT WAS PROMISED

TO WHOM

WHEN

UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS

AND

WHETHER IT WAS FULFILLED.
```

Broken promises may create relational consequences.

Fulfilled promises may strengthen reliability.

---

# 56. Debt and Obligation

Likewise, someone may reject an expected obligation.

The Relationships System should distinguish:

```text
FORMAL DEBT
```

from:

```text
PERCEIVED RELATIONAL OBLIGATION.
```

over:

```text
Trust increased to 82.
```


over:

```text
Relationship decreased by 5.
```

The underlying system may use structured state.

The player experience should remain human.

# 89. Relational Inertia

Established relationships possess inertia.

One minor event should not normally reverse years of history.

For example:

```text

```

should not automatically disappear because of:

```text
ONE MINOR DISAGREEMENT.
```

Likewise:

```text
YEARS OF HOSTILITY
```

should not become friendship after:

```text
ONE POLITE CONVERSATION.
```

History matters.

# 103. Development Locks

Do not reduce relationships to a single score.

Do not use simple like/dislike as the complete relational model.

Do not make trust automatically symmetric.

Do not make affection automatically imply trust.

Do not make loyalty automatically imply affection.

Do not make fear automatically imply respect.

Do not make dependence automatically imply loyalty.

Do not make compliance automatically imply trust.

Do not make family automatically imply closeness.

Do not make romance automatic.

Do not use gifts as mechanical relationship farming.

Do not allow communication skill to function as mind control.

Do not make one minor interaction erase years of history.

Do not erase betrayal because a character apologized.

Do not erase relational history after reconciliation.

Do not freeze relationships when players leave.

Do not make every relationship dramatically unstable.

Do not make every Fractured World relationship transactional.

Do not make every stressful event destroy relationships.

Do not give third parties automatic knowledge of private relational events.

Do not give Aurora automatic knowledge of relationship truth.

Do not allow Aurora's Relationship Model to replace the Relationships System.

Do not allow Narrative to create relational history merely for dramatic convenience.

Do not allow the Living Campaign Engine to invent relationships because they would create useful missions.

Do not confuse public reputation with interpersonal relationship.

Do not confuse societal cohesion with interpersonal trust.

Do not rewrite established relational history when resolution increases.

```text
GOVERNMENTS

ECONOMIES
```
