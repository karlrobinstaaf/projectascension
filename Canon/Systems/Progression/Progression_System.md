---

# 1. Purpose

The Progression System defines how Characters change meaningfully over time.

It answers:

> **How does a person develop through lived experience without reducing growth to XP, levels or automatic statistical improvement?**

Project Ascension does not treat progression as:

```text
XP
↓
LEVEL UP
↓
BETTER STATS
↓
STRONGER CHARACTER.
```

Instead, progression emerges from life.

Characters:

- learn
- practice
- adapt
- recover
- deteriorate
- gain responsibility
- lose capability
- build Relationships
- lose Relationships
- change Goals
- acquire new knowledge
- revise Beliefs
- gain or lose resources
- become more or less capable in specific contexts

Progression is therefore:

> **Causal long-term human development.**

---

# 2. Core Definition

Progression is:

> **The coordinated process through which meaningful Character change emerges from experience, time, practice, consequence and changing circumstances, with each underlying state remaining owned by its authoritative system.**

Conceptually:

```text
EXPERIENCE
+
TIME
+
PRACTICE
+
CONSEQUENCE
+
OPPORTUNITY
+
HUMAN RESPONSE
↓
POSSIBLE DEVELOPMENT.
```

Progression defines when change is plausible.

It does not automatically own the state being changed.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The central Progression principle is:

> **Characters change because their lives change, not because the game awards them levels.**

Therefore:

```text
PLAY TIME
≠
PROGRESSION

SESSION COMPLETION
≠
PROGRESSION

MISSION SUCCESS
≠
LEVEL UP

COMBAT
≠
EXPERIENCE POINTS

SURVIVAL
≠
AUTOMATIC IMPROVEMENT.
```

Meaningful development requires causality.

---

# 4. Progression Is Not Leveling

Project Ascension does not use a universal Character level.

Avoid:

```text
LEVEL 1 CHARACTER

LEVEL 10 CHARACTER

LEVEL 50 CHARACTER.
```

A human being may improve in one area while declining in another.

They may become:

```text
MORE EXPERIENCED

BUT

LESS PHYSICALLY CAPABLE.
```

Or:

```text
MORE KNOWLEDGEABLE

BUT

LESS CERTAIN.
```

Or:

```text
MORE TRUSTED

BUT

MORE ISOLATED.
```

A single level cannot represent this.

---

# 5. Progression Is Multi-Dimensional

Character development occurs across several domains.

Conceptually:

```text
EXPERTISE

PSYCHOLOGY

RELATIONSHIPS

MEMORY

BELIEFS

GOALS

RESPONSIBILITY

SOCIAL POSITION

RESOURCES

PHYSICAL CONDITION

LIFE HISTORY.
```

These dimensions may change independently.

There is no universal direction called "better."

---

# 6. Progression Ownership

Progression coordinates development rules.

It does not replace authoritative systems.

Conceptually:

```text
EXPERTISE CHANGE
→ Expertise

PSYCHOLOGICAL CHANGE
→ Human Psychology

RELATIONSHIP CHANGE
→ Relationships

MEMORY CHANGE
→ Memory

LIFE EVENT
→ Life

GOAL CHANGE
→ Characters

BELIEF CHANGE
→ Character cognition

SOCIAL POSITION
→ Society / Factions

RESOURCE CHANGE
→ World / Character resource state

PHYSICAL CHANGE
→ Character / physical state.
```

Progression defines:

```text
WHEN CHANGE
IS PLAUSIBLE

WHY CHANGE
IS PLAUSIBLE

HOW CHANGE
SHOULD BE
VALIDATED.
```

---

# 7. What Progression Owns

Progression may own:

- general development principles
- causal development requirements
- progression validation
- long-term change pacing
- cross-system change coordination
- development opportunity
- development constraints
- progression history references
- progression guardrails

It does not own every resulting value.

# 8. What Progression Does Not Own

Progression does not own:

```text
EXPERTISE LEVELS

PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE

RELATIONSHIP STATE

MEMORY CONTENT

BELIEFS

GOALS

LIFE EVENTS

ATTRIBUTES

REPUTATION

RESOURCES

WORLD TRUTH.
```

These systems remain authoritative.

---

# 9. Development Is Not Always Improvement

One of the most important principles is:

```text
DEVELOPMENT
≠
IMPROVEMENT.
```

Characters may:

- improve
- decline
- recover
- adapt
- stagnate
- specialize
- lose familiarity
- become outdated
- gain confidence
- lose confidence
- change priorities
- become less trusting
- become more trusting
- become more capable
- become more dependent

All are forms of development.

---

# 10. Human Development Is Uneven

Humans do not improve uniformly.

A Character may gain:

```text
ENGINEERING EXPERTISE
```

while losing:

```text
PHYSICAL ENDURANCE.
```

They may develop:

```text
STRONGER COMMUNITY RELATIONSHIPS
```

while experiencing:

```text
INCREASING PSYCHOLOGICAL STRAIN.
```

Progression must support this asymmetry.

---

# 11. Progression Through Experience

Experience may create development opportunities.

But:

```text
EXPERIENCE
≠
AUTOMATIC GROWTH.
```

A Character repeatedly exposed to a situation may:

- learn
- fail to learn
- reinforce bad habits
- become exhausted
- become desensitized
- become more cautious
- become overconfident

Outcome depends on context.

---

# 12. Relevant Experience

Development requires relevant experience.

Example:

```text
REPAIRING
POWER SYSTEMS
```

may contribute toward Engineering development.

It does not contribute meaningfully toward:

```text
MEDICINE.
```

Progression must preserve domain relevance.

---

# 13. Practice

Practice is one of the strongest drivers of capability growth.

Conceptually:

```text
RELEVANT PRACTICE
+
TIME
+
FEEDBACK
+
DIFFICULTY
↓
POSSIBLE EXPERTISE
DEVELOPMENT.
```

Practice should be meaningful.

Repeated trivial actions should not create unlimited improvement.

---

# 14. Learning

Learning may come from:

- education
- mentorship
- reading
- experimentation
- observation
- training
- instruction
- failure
- collaboration

Learning can contribute to progression.

But information exposure alone does not guarantee capability.

---

# 15. Mentorship

Mentorship may accelerate development.

A mentor may provide:

- correction
- context
- access
- opportunities
- judgment
- feedback

But:

```text
MENTORSHIP
≠
INSTANT EXPERTISE.
```

The learner still requires practice and time.

---

# 16. Time

Time matters.

Some development requires:

```text
DAYS

WEEKS

MONTHS

YEARS.
```

Project Ascension must resist instant progression.

Examples:

```text
LEARNING
BASIC RADIO PROCEDURE
→ DAYS / WEEKS

DEVELOPING
PROFESSIONAL COMPETENCE
→ MONTHS / YEARS

BECOMING
WORLD-CLASS
→ YEARS / DECADES.
```

Exact implementation remains future work.

---

# 17. Opportunity

Development requires opportunity.

A Character cannot train advanced surgery without:

- patients
- instruction
- tools
- environment
- time

Likewise, a Character cannot develop practical Engineering expertise without relevant systems or practice.

Therefore:

```text
INTENT
≠
OPPORTUNITY.
```

---

# 18. Resources

Learning may depend on resources.

Examples include:

- books
- devices
- laboratories
- tools
- training environments
- instructors
- infrastructure

Resource scarcity may slow or prevent development.

---

# 19. Expertise Progression

Expertise growth is one of the most concrete forms of progression.

But it must obey the Expertise System.

Conceptually:

```text
CURRENT EXPERTISE
+
RELEVANT PRACTICE
+
TRAINING
+
TIME
+
FEEDBACK
+
OPPORTUNITY
↓
POSSIBLE
EXPERTISE GROWTH.
```

Progression validates the process.

Expertise owns the resulting level.

---

# 20. Expertise Thresholds

Higher Expertise levels require increasingly significant evidence.

Conceptually:

```text
LEVEL 0 → 1
=
BASIC LEARNING

LEVEL 1 → 2
=
MEANINGFUL TRAINING

LEVEL 2 → 3
=
SUBSTANTIAL PRACTICAL EXPERIENCE

LEVEL 3 → 4
=
DEEP MASTERY

LEVEL 4 → 5
=
EXCEPTIONAL,
FIELD-DEFINING DEVELOPMENT.
```

No universal XP thresholds are canonical.

---

# 21. Expertise Specialization Growth

A Character may develop a specialization without equally improving the broader domain.

Example:

```text
MEDICINE 2

EMERGENCY MEDICINE 3.
```

Progression should support narrow growth.

---

# 22. Expertise Decay

Progression must also support decline.

Possible causes include:

- disuse
- changing technology
- physical limitation
- loss of access
- aging
- interrupted practice

Decay should be contextual.

Avoid arbitrary level loss.

---

# 23. Expertise Obsolescence

A Character may retain deep capability while becoming less current.

Example:

```text
CYBERSECURITY 4

BUT

CURRENT SYSTEM
FAMILIARITY
IS LOW.
```

Progression may change familiarity without deleting foundational mastery.

---

# 24. Human Attributes

Human Attributes are relatively stable.

Therefore they should change much more slowly than Psychology.

Progression may allow long-term Attribute change only through sustained life development.

Avoid:

```text
ONE EVENT
↓
ATTRIBUTE CHANGES.
```

Prefer:

```text
REPEATED EXPERIENCE
+
LONG-TERM ADAPTATION
+
TIME
↓
POSSIBLE ATTRIBUTE
SHIFT.
```

---

# 25. Attribute Stability

Human Attributes should provide Character continuity.

Therefore:

```text
ATTRIBUTE CHANGE
=
SIGNIFICANT DEVELOPMENT.
```

It should be uncommon enough to remain meaningful.

---

# 26. Psychology Progression

Psychology can change much more rapidly than Attributes.

Characters may:

- recover
- deteriorate
- become stressed
- regain confidence
- become exhausted
- become hopeful
- become anxious

Human Psychology owns these states.

Progression defines how sustained psychological development may become part of longer Character change.

---

# 27. Recovery

Recovery is progression.

A Character may gradually recover from:

- exhaustion
- fear
- grief
- stress
- injury
- loss

Recovery requires plausible conditions.

Examples include:

- time
- safety
- support
- rest
- resolution
- relationships

Recovery is not automatic.

---

# 28. Psychological Deterioration

Psychological state may worsen through:

- sustained pressure
- uncertainty
- isolation
- repeated failure
- danger
- loss

But Progression must avoid deterministic trauma models.

Different people respond differently.

---

# 29. Relationships

Relationships develop over time.

Possible changes include:

```text
TRUST

AFFECTION

RESENTMENT

DEPENDENCE

LOYALTY

OBLIGATION

DISTANCE.
```

Progression does not directly own these values.

Relationships does.

---

# 30. Relationship Development Requires History

Avoid:

```text
ONE POSITIVE
DIALOGUE CHOICE
↓
DEEP TRUST.
```

Prefer:

```text
REPEATED
RELIABLE ACTION
+
SHARED EXPERIENCE
+
TIME
↓
POSSIBLE
TRUST DEVELOPMENT.
```

Relationship change should feel earned.

---

# 31. Relationship Decline

Relationships may deteriorate through:

- betrayal
- neglect
- disagreement
- conflicting goals
- repeated unreliability
- distance

But one event does not always destroy a Relationship.

Context matters.

---

# 32. Memory Development

Memory changes over time.

Characters may:

- form new Memories
- reinforce Memories
- forget detail
- reinterpret events
- recover previously inaccessible Memories
- discover remembered information was wrong

Memory owns the state.

Progression provides long-term change context.

---

# 33. Memory Is Not Progress Currency

Avoid treating Memory as:

```text
COLLECT MEMORY
↓
GAIN BONUS.
```

Memory exists because Characters live through events.

It is part of human continuity.

---

# 34. Belief Development

Beliefs may change through:

- evidence
- experience
- relationships
- contradiction
- reflection
- cultural exposure
- failure
- discovery

But:

```text
NEW INFORMATION
≠
AUTOMATIC BELIEF CHANGE.
```

Humans may resist evidence.

---

# 35. Belief Reinforcement

Existing beliefs may strengthen when Characters encounter supporting experiences.

But reinforcement should remain contextual.

Confirmation alone does not guarantee permanent certainty.

---

# 36. Goal Progression

Goals may:

- be achieved
- fail
- become impossible
- be abandoned
- become less important
- transform
- generate new Goals

Progression should support changing human priorities.

---

# 37. Goal Completion

Completing a Goal should not automatically create a reward package.

Instead:

```text
GOAL COMPLETED
↓
WORLD CONSEQUENCE
+
PERSONAL CONSEQUENCE
+
POSSIBLE NEW GOALS.
```

The reward is often what changes in the Character's life.

---

# 38. Goal Failure

Goal failure is also progression.

A failed Goal may produce:

- grief
- changed priorities
- new Relationships
- new Life Events
- new Beliefs
- new responsibilities

Failure creates development.

---

# 39. Responsibility

Characters may gain responsibility.

Examples include:

- leadership
- caregiving
- command
- community responsibility
- institutional role
- family obligation

Responsibility changes available choices.

It should be treated as meaningful progression.

---

# 40. Responsibility Is Not Power

A Character may gain more responsibility without gaining more freedom.

Conceptually:

```text
MORE RESPONSIBILITY
MAY MEAN

MORE CONSTRAINT.
```

This is legitimate development.

---

# 41. Social Position

Characters may change social position through:

- reputation
- institutional role
- leadership
- wealth
- community standing
- Faction membership

Social position may rise or fall.

It is not equivalent to Character strength.

---

# 42. Reputation Progression

Reputation changes through information propagation.

Avoid:

```text
MISSION COMPLETE
↓
GLOBAL REPUTATION +10.
```

Prefer:

```text
ACTION
↓
OBSERVERS
↓
INFORMATION
↓
INTERPRETATION
↓
REPUTATION CHANGE
WHERE RELEVANT.
```

---

# 43. Resources

Characters may gain or lose:

- money
- tools
- transportation
- shelter
- access
- equipment
- institutional resources

Resource change is progression in circumstance.

It is not necessarily personal development.

---

# 44. Material Progression

Project Ascension should distinguish:

```text
CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT
```

from:

```text
MATERIAL ACCUMULATION.
```

Owning better equipment does not make a person inherently more developed.

---

# 45. Physical Progression

Characters may experience:

- conditioning
- aging
- injury
- healing
- disability
- fatigue
- recovery

Physical state may change through time.

This should remain grounded in realistic causality.

---

# 46. Aging

Long campaigns may involve aging.

Aging may produce:

- greater experience
- physical decline
- changing responsibility
- changing social role
- different priorities

Aging is not simply a penalty system.

---

# 47. Life Events

Life Events are one of the main sources of long-term progression.

Conceptually:

```text
EVENT
↓
LIFE EVENT
↓
MEMORY
+
PSYCHOLOGY
+
RELATIONSHIPS
+
GOALS
+
POSSIBLE EXPERTISE CHANGE
↓
DEVELOPMENT.
```

Life owns the event.

Other systems own resulting state.

---

# 48. Progression Chains

Meaningful development often occurs through chains.

Example:

```text
COMMUNICATION FAILURE
↓
CHARACTER TAKES
RESPONSIBILITY
FOR LOCAL RADIO
↓
REPEATED PRACTICE
↓
COMMUNICATION
EXPERTISE GROWS
↓
COMMUNITY
BEGINS RELYING
ON CHARACTER
↓
SOCIAL POSITION
CHANGES
↓
NEW RESPONSIBILITY.
```

This is Progression.

Not:

```text
MISSION COMPLETE
↓
+1 LEVEL.
```

---

# 49. Cross-System Development

Progression is strongest when change crosses systems.

Example:

```text
PLAYER SAVES
A COMMUNITY
↓
LIFE EVENT
↓
NPC MEMORIES
↓
RELATIONSHIP CHANGE
↓
LOCAL REPUTATION
↓
NEW RESPONSIBILITY
↓
NEW GOAL
↓
FUTURE STORY.
```

No universal reward system is required.

---

# 50. Development Through Failure

Failure may create more development than success.

A Character who fails may gain:

- knowledge
- humility
- caution
- motivation
- changed Relationships
- new expertise opportunities

But failure does not automatically make someone wiser.

---

# 51. Development Through Success

Success can also change people.

It may create:

- confidence
- reputation
- responsibility
- new opportunities
- higher expectations
- dependency from others

Success may make future life harder.

---

# 52. Development Through Ordinary Life

Progression should not require crisis.

Characters may develop through:

- work
- education
- family
- practice
- conversation
- mentorship
- routine
- friendship

Human growth is not limited to dramatic events.

---

# 53. No Grind

Project Ascension should avoid grind-based progression.

Avoid:

```text
PERFORM SAME ACTION
100 TIMES
↓
SKILL INCREASE.
```

Repetition without challenge, feedback or relevance should have diminishing developmental value.

---

# 54. No XP

There is no universal XP pool.

Therefore:

```text
KILL
≠
XP

MISSION
≠
XP

DISCOVERY
≠
XP

SESSION
≠
XP.
```

Specific systems may recognize relevant experience.

There is no shared experience currency.

---

# 55. No Character Level

Characters do not possess one overall level.

A Character may be:

```text
WORLD-CLASS
AI RESEARCHER

BASIC
SURVIVAL SKILLS

POOR CURRENT
PHYSICAL CONDITION

STRONG LOCAL
RELATIONSHIPS.
```

One number would destroy this complexity.

---

# 56. No Skill Points

Players should not ordinarily receive generic points to spend arbitrarily across unrelated capabilities.

Avoid:

```text
MISSION COMPLETE
↓
3 SKILL POINTS
↓
MEDICINE +1.
```

without any causal medical experience.

---

# 57. Player Choice in Progression

Players should still have meaningful influence over development.

They may choose:

- what to practice
- who to learn from
- what responsibilities to accept
- where to spend time
- which Goals to pursue
- what risks to take

Player agency affects opportunity.

It does not bypass plausibility.

---

# 58. Intentional Training

A player may decide:

```text
I WANT
TO LEARN MEDICINE.
```

That creates an intention.

The Character still needs:

- mentor or material
- time
- practice
- opportunity
- relevant experience

Intent begins a path.

It does not grant the result.

---

# 59. Tradeoffs

Development consumes resources.

Training may require:

- time
- safety
- access
- money
- tools
- attention

Therefore:

```text
PROGRESSION
HAS OPPORTUNITY COST.
```

Time spent learning one thing cannot always be spent elsewhere.

---

# 60. Specialization Tradeoff

Deep specialization requires substantial time.

A Character becoming world-class in one field should have a life history that reflects that investment.

This naturally limits universal mastery.

---

# 61. Group Progression

Groups may develop collectively.

Examples include:

- improved coordination
- shared procedures
- stronger Trust
- shared knowledge
- better resource organization

But Group Progression should emerge from actual shared history.

---

# 62. Team Familiarity

Repeated collaboration may improve:

- communication
- coordination
- role understanding
- anticipation

But this should not become a generic "party level."

It belongs to Relationships and group history.

---

# 63. Faction Progression

Factions may also develop.

They may gain or lose:

- resources
- territory
- capability
- cohesion
- influence
- leadership quality

Faction systems own their state.

Progression principles may still apply conceptually.

---

# 64. Society Progression

Society changes through:

- institutions
- migration
- Trust
- norms
- conflict
- adaptation

This is social development.

It belongs to Society rather than Character Progression.

The Progression system may provide shared development philosophy but must not take ownership.

---

# 65. World Progression

World States and World Simulation change independently of Character development.

Therefore:

```text
CHARACTER PROGRESSION
≠
WORLD PROGRESSION.
```

The world may improve while the Character deteriorates.

Or the reverse.

---

# 66. Campaign Progression

Campaign Progression describes how the campaign evolves.

This belongs primarily to the Living Campaign Engine.

Avoid confusing:

```text
CAMPAIGN ADVANCEMENT
```

with:

```text
CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT.
```

---

# 67. Narrative Progression

A Story Thread progressing does not mean the Character has improved.

Narrative owns story development.

Progression owns development principles.

---

# 68. Progression and Story Rewards

Traditional games often use rewards to drive play.

Project Ascension should prefer consequences.

Example:

```text
HELP SETTLEMENT
↓
SETTLEMENT SURVIVES
↓
RELATIONSHIP CHANGES
↓
ACCESS CHANGES
↓
NEW HISTORY.
```

This is more meaningful than:

```text
+500 XP.
```

---

# 69. Recognition

Characters may receive recognition.

Possible outcomes:

- reputation
- trust
- authority
- invitation
- access
- leadership

Recognition may feel rewarding.

It should arise from observer knowledge and Society / Faction state.

---

# 70. Access

Progression may unlock access naturally.

Example:

```text
CHARACTER
PROVES CAPABILITY
↓
INSTITUTION
TRUSTS THEM
↓
ACCESS TO
RESTRICTED SYSTEM.
```

This is not a level gate.

It is a world consequence.

---

# 71. Capability Gates

Some actions genuinely require minimum capability.

Example:

```text
COMPLEX SURGERY
```

may be effectively unavailable to a Character with no medical Expertise.

This is not an arbitrary game gate.

It reflects plausible human capability.

---

# 72. Knowledge Gates

Expertise may reveal:

- information
- interpretations
- options

A Character may become capable of understanding things previously inaccessible.

This creates natural progression in how the world is perceived.

---

# 73. Relationship Gates

Some opportunities may require:

- Trust
- reputation
- existing Relationships
- institutional access

These are social conditions.

They should not be replaced by Character Level requirements.

---

# 74. World Gates

Some opportunities may require:

- specific location
- infrastructure
- time
- resources
- World State conditions

Progression must respect World Truth.

---

# 75. Development Pacing

Progression pacing should follow plausible human change.

Avoid:

```text
ONE SESSION
=
ONE MAJOR IMPROVEMENT.
```

Some changes may occur quickly.

Others require years.

---

# 76. Short-Term Development

Short-term development may include:

- familiarity
- temporary confidence
- basic procedural learning
- recovery
- changing Goal priority

These can occur over hours or days.

---

# 77. Medium-Term Development

Medium-term development may include:

- meaningful training
- Relationship development
- changing reputation
- stronger role familiarity
- moderate Expertise growth

These may require weeks or months.

---

# 78. Long-Term Development

Long-term development may include:

- major Expertise mastery
- deep Attribute change
- major identity shifts
- institutional leadership
- cultural integration
- career transformation

These may require months or years.

---

# 79. Persistent Development

Meaningful changes persist.

Progression should not reset between:

- sessions
- regions
- Story Threads
- campaigns where continuity exists

Development becomes part of Character history.

---

# 80. Regression

Characters may regress.

Examples include:

- lost confidence
- lost capability
- deteriorating Relationships
- physical decline
- lost social status
- lost access

Regression is not punishment.

It is consequence.

---

# 81. Setbacks

A setback may temporarily reduce performance without permanently reducing underlying capability.

Example:

```text
INJURY
↓
CURRENT PERFORMANCE
REDUCED

BUT

EXPERTISE
UNCHANGED.
```

Progression must preserve this distinction.

---

# 82. Permanent Change

Some events may create durable change.

Examples may include:

- permanent injury
- loss of social role
- major career shift
- long-term Relationship breakdown
- major Expertise development

Permanent change requires appropriate causality.

---

# 83. Reversibility

Not all progression is reversible.

Some changes may be:

```text
REVERSIBLE

PARTIALLY REVERSIBLE

IRREVERSIBLE.
```

This depends on the underlying system.

---

# 84. Death

Death is the ultimate end of Character development.

But death may create progression elsewhere through:

- Life Events
- Memories
- Relationship changes
- Faction consequences
- Society consequences

The dead Character no longer develops.

Their history may continue to matter.

---

# 85. Player Character Death

A Player Character's death should not erase campaign progression.

The world remains changed.

Relationships remain affected.

History remains.

A replacement Character enters an existing world.

---

# 86. New Characters

New Characters should not automatically begin at a universal "party level."

They should enter with plausible:

- Life history
- Expertise
- Relationships
- resources
- social position

A new Character may be more capable in some areas and less capable in others.

---

# 87. NPC Progression

NPCs use the same fundamental progression principles.

They may:

- learn
- improve
- deteriorate
- gain responsibilities
- change Relationships
- become experts
- lose capability

NPCs do not freeze when players leave.

---

# 88. NPC Off-Screen Development

Off-screen NPC development should be simulated at appropriate resolution.

Major changes may include:

- profession shift
- relationship change
- training
- migration
- injury
- leadership
- changing Goals

Minor detail may be compressed.

---

# 89. Off-Screen Progression

Progression can occur off-screen when causal conditions exist.

Example:

```text
CHARACTER
TRAINS WITH MEDIC
FOR SIX MONTHS
OFF-SCREEN
↓
MEDICAL EXPERTISE
MAY PLAUSIBLY CHANGE.
```

Player observation is not required for causality.

---

# 90. Simulation Resolution

Progression should operate across resolution levels.

```text
LOW RESOLUTION
=
TRACK MAJOR
DEVELOPMENT EVENTS

MEDIUM RESOLUTION
=
TRACK RELEVANT
TRAINING,
RELATIONSHIPS
AND CHANGE

HIGH RESOLUTION
=
TRACK ACTIVE
LEARNING,
PRACTICE
AND IMMEDIATE
DEVELOPMENT CONTEXT.
```

Resolution changes detail.

Not causality.

---

# 91. Progression Compression

The system does not need to simulate every hour of practice.

Instead it should preserve enough information to answer:

```text
WHY DID
THIS CHANGE?

HOW LONG
DID IT TAKE?

WHAT CONDITIONS
SUPPORTED IT?
```

If those answers are clear, compression is acceptable.

---

# 92. Progression History

Meaningful development may reference its causal history.

Example:

```text
ENGINEERING 3

SUPPORTED BY:

4 YEARS
FIELD EXPERIENCE

GRID REPAIR
DURING TRANSITION

MENTORSHIP
UNDER SENIOR ENGINEER.
```

This improves validation and Character continuity.

# 93. Development Opportunities

The simulation may identify Development Opportunities.

Example:

```text
EXPERT AVAILABLE

TRAINING FACILITY AVAILABLE

TIME AVAILABLE

CHARACTER INTERESTED.
```

This does not guarantee development.

It means the conditions exist.

```text
↓
CHARACTER
REPEATEDLY PERFORMS
BASIC MEDICAL TASKS
↓
POSSIBLE
BASIC DEVELOPMENT.
```

Need may create opportunity.

It does not create mastery instantly.

# 95. Development Intent

Characters may intentionally seek change.

Examples:

```text
BECOME STRONGER.

REPAIR THIS RELATIONSHIP.

I WANT TO
STOP BEING
AFRAID OF THIS.
```

Intent matters.

Outcome remains uncertain.

Before any significant Character development becomes canonical, validate:


# 97. Progression Propagation

A change in one system may create changes in others.

Example:

```text
EXPERTISE GROWS
↓
PROFESSIONAL ROLE
CHANGES
↓
SOCIAL POSITION
CHANGES
↓
NEW RESPONSIBILITY
↓
RELATIONSHIPS CHANGE.
```

Progression should coordinate propagation without silently mutating unrelated state.

---

# 98. No Automatic Propagation

Avoid:

```text
EXPERTISE +1
↓
CONFIDENCE +1
↓
TRUST +1
↓
REPUTATION +1.
```

Each downstream change requires its own causal justification.

---

# 99. Progression and Agency

Progression changes what becomes possible.

It must not determine future choice.

Conceptually:

```text
DEVELOPMENT
↓
NEW CAPABILITY
+
NEW CONTEXT
+
NEW OPTIONS
↓
AGENCY
↓
CHOICE.
```

The Character remains free to act differently.

---

# 100. Progression and Character Identity

Development should allow Characters to change while remaining recognizable.

The target is:

```text
THE PERSON
HAS CHANGED

BUT

THE CHANGE
HAS HISTORY.
```

Sudden unexplained transformation should be avoided.

---

# 101. Progression Is Historical

Every meaningful development becomes part of personal history.

Therefore:

```text
PROGRESSION
=
HISTORY ACCUMULATING
INSIDE THE CHARACTER.
```

This is more important than numerical advancement.

# 102. Progression and World States

World States change development opportunities.

Connected World may provide:

- institutions
- formal education
- digital learning
- specialization

Fractured World may provide:

- practical apprenticeship
- scarcity-driven learning
- limited formal education
- local specialization

Neither automatically creates more or less capable humans.

# 103. Connected World Progression

The Connected World supports:

- formal education
- institutional certification
- advanced specialization
- remote learning
- automation-supported work

But automation may also reduce practical familiarity.

# 104. Transition Progression

The Transition may force people to apply expertise outside ideal systems.

This may create:

- improvisational competence
- new responsibilities
- cross-domain learning
- accelerated practical experience

But intense exposure does not automatically create mastery.

# 105. Fractured World Progression

The Fractured World may increase value of:

Reconnection may create opportunities for:

- cross-regional learning

Characters who developed during fracture may encounter entirely new development paths.

# 116. Development Locks

Future Progression development must not introduce:

- universal Character levels
- generic XP
- kill XP
- mission XP as universal development currency
- session-completion XP
- arbitrary skill points
- arbitrary Attribute points
- automatic improvement through survival
- instant Expertise growth
- universal party level
- level-scaled enemies
- level-scaled world state
- progression disconnected from Life history
- NPCs frozen off-screen
- automatic Relationship rewards
- automatic reputation rewards
- automatic psychological growth
- automatic positive development after trauma
- one-directional "stronger is always better" progression
- Character power as the primary measure of development

Avoid:

```text
MISSION COMPLETE

GAIN
1 EXPERTISE LEVEL.
```

Prefer:

```text
MISSION CREATED
RELEVANT EXPERIENCE

WHICH MAY
CONTRIBUTE TO
FUTURE DEVELOPMENT
IF SUPPORTED BY
PRACTICE,
TIME
AND CONTEXT.
```

Avoid:

```text
PLAYER HAS
PLAYED 20 HOURS

THEREFORE

CHARACTER IS
STRONGER.
```

Prefer:

```text
WHAT DID
THE CHARACTER
ACTUALLY DO
DURING THOSE
20 HOURS?
```

# 119. Progression North Star

The Progression System succeeds when players can look back and understand how their Characters became different people.

The target is:

```text

GAIN LEVELS.

THROUGH THINGS.

I LEARNED.

I FAILED.

CHANGED AROUND ME.

CHANGED.
```

# 120. Closing Principle

Human development is not a ladder.

People do not move steadily upward.

They learn.

Forget.

Recover.

Decline.

Adapt.

Specialize.

Change their minds.

Become responsible for others.

Lose confidence.

Gain confidence.

Become more capable.

Become more cautious.

Leave old lives behind.

Return to things they once abandoned.

Sometimes they become stronger.

Sometimes they become more fragile.

Often they become both.

Project Ascension should treat Character development as accumulated history rather than accumulated points.

The central principle is:

> **Progression is the lasting change produced when a human life continues long enough for experience, time, consequence and choice to become part of who that person is.**
