---

# 1. Purpose

The Factions system defines organized collective Actors.

It answers:

> **Which organized groups are capable of acting in the world with sufficient coherence to pursue goals, make decisions, use resources and create consequences over time?**

A Faction is not simply a group of people.

It is not a population category.

It is not a cultural identity.

It is not a Society.

A Faction exists when enough structure, coordination and continuity exist for a collective to act as a meaningful Actor.

Examples may include:

- political organizations
- military commands
- criminal organizations
- research consortia
- corporate networks
- mutual-aid coalitions
- regional alliances
- insurgent groups
- ideological movements
- security organizations
- surviving government structures
- institutional coalitions

Factions create agency at collective scale.

---

# 2. Core Definition

A Faction is:

> **An organized collective with sufficient identity, structure, continuity, resources, goals and decision-making capability to act coherently within the world.**

Conceptually:

```text
FACTION
=
ORGANIZED COLLECTIVE

WITH

IDENTITY
+
STRUCTURE
+
GOALS
+
RESOURCES
+
DECISION-MAKING
+
CONTINUITY
+
AGENCY.
```

Not every group qualifies.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The central Faction principle is:

> **A Faction can act as a collective Actor without requiring every member to think, believe or want the same thing.**

Therefore:

```text
FACTION AGENCY
≠
MEMBER UNIFORMITY.
```

A Faction may act coherently while containing:

- disagreement
- internal rivalry
- competing priorities
- dissent
- factions within factions
- weak loyalty
- opportunistic membership

Collective action does not imply collective mind.

---

# 4. Why Factions Matter

Project Ascension contains consequences operating above individual scale.

Many of these require organized Actors.

Examples include:

- controlling infrastructure
- governing territory
- conducting military operations
- negotiating agreements
- distributing resources
- coordinating research
- maintaining intelligence networks
- enforcing rules
- moving populations
- securing trade
- competing for influence
- responding to Aurora

Without Factions, large-scale action risks becoming abstract.

Factions give organized intent a concrete owner.

---

# 5. What Factions Own

Factions may own persistent state involving:

- Faction identity
- Faction goals
- strategic priorities
- leadership structure
- organizational structure
- membership structure
- resources
- capabilities
- assets
- controlled territory
- access
- alliances
- rivalries
- external relationships
- operational plans
- internal divisions
- decision processes
- information held by the Faction
- reputation
- influence
- legitimacy where relevant
- current actions

Factions own organized collective agency.

---

# 6. What Factions Do Not Own

Factions do not own:

```text
INDIVIDUAL CHARACTER STATE

INDIVIDUAL PSYCHOLOGY

INDIVIDUAL MEMORY

INDIVIDUAL BELIEFS

INDIVIDUAL GOALS

PAIRWISE RELATIONSHIPS

CULTURE

SOCIETY STATE

PUBLIC OPINION

WORLD TRUTH

SETTLEMENT STATE

NARRATIVE PRESENTATION

AURORA INTERNAL STATE.
```

These systems may influence Factions.

They remain separate.

---

# 7. Faction vs Society

This distinction is fundamental.

```text
SOCIETY
=
COLLECTIVE SOCIAL CONDITION

FACTION
=
ORGANIZED COLLECTIVE ACTOR.
```

Society may contain:

- populations
- institutions
- norms
- Trust patterns
- cohesion
- fragmentation
- social conflict

Factions may act within that Society.

A Faction may try to:

- influence Society
- govern part of Society
- exploit Society
- protect Society
- recruit from Society
- transform Society

But:

```text
SOCIETY
DOES NOT
HAVE ONE GOAL

FACTION
MAY.
```

---

# 8. Faction vs Community

A Community is not automatically a Faction.

A Community may exist through:

- shared location
- shared identity
- shared history
- social connection

But unless it possesses organized decision-making and collective agency, it remains a Community.

Conceptually:

```text
COMMUNITY
=
SOCIAL GROUP

FACTION
=
ORGANIZED ACTOR.
```

A Community may create a Faction.

---

# 9. Faction vs Institution

An Institution is a persistent organized structure fulfilling social functions.

A Faction is defined by organized agency.

Some Institutions may also qualify as Factions.

Others may not.

Example:

```text
LOCAL HOSPITAL
=
INSTITUTION
```

but if its leadership organizes:

- resource acquisition
- security
- negotiations
- territorial operations

it may also function as a Faction in practice.

Classification depends on agency.

---

# 10. Faction vs Organization

Not every organization needs Faction status.

A small organization may exist without world-relevant strategic agency.

Faction status should be used when collective action becomes consequential to simulation.

Conceptually:

```text
ORGANIZATION
+
STRATEGIC AGENCY
+
PERSISTENCE
+
WORLD CONSEQUENCE
=
POSSIBLE FACTION.
```

---

# 11. Faction vs Government

A Government may contain several Factions.

It may also function as one Faction under certain simulation resolutions.

Examples include:

```text
FEDERAL GOVERNMENT

STATE GOVERNMENT

MUNICIPAL GOVERNMENT

REGIONAL AUTHORITY.
```

But:

```text
GOVERNMENT
≠
AUTOMATICALLY
ONE FACTION.
```

Internal agencies may have:

- competing goals
- separate leadership
- different resources
- conflicting authority

Resolution determines useful representation.

---

# 12. Faction vs Settlement

A Settlement is a geographic and inhabited place.

A Faction may control, influence or operate within a Settlement.

But:

```text
SETTLEMENT
≠
FACTION.
```

A town may contain:

- local government
- criminal organization
- mutual-aid group
- religious organization
- security coalition

Several Factions may coexist inside one Settlement.

---

# 13. Faction vs Culture

Culture defines shared meaning and social context.

A Faction may possess its own organizational culture.

But:

```text
FACTION
≠
CULTURE.
```

A Culture may span many Factions.

A Faction may include members from several cultures.

---

# 14. Faction vs Ideology

Ideology may influence Faction goals.

But:

```text
IDEOLOGY
≠
FACTION.
```

An ideology may exist without organization.

Multiple Factions may claim the same ideology.

One Faction may contain ideological disagreement.

---

# 15. Faction vs Movement

A social movement may begin without centralized structure.

It may later develop:

- leadership
- resources
- strategy
- coordination

At that point it may become a Faction or produce multiple Factions.

The transition should be causal.

---

# 16. Faction Identity

Every Faction requires an identity.

This may include:

- name
- origin
- public identity
- internal identity
- symbols
- history
- claimed purpose
- geographic base
- institutional lineage

Identity should be recognizable enough that members and outsiders can understand the collective as a distinct entity.

---

# 17. Public Identity vs Internal Reality

A Faction's public identity may differ from internal reality.

Example:

```text
PUBLIC CLAIM:
PROTECT THE REGION

INTERNAL PRIORITY:
PRESERVE LEADERSHIP
AND RESOURCE CONTROL.
```

This does not automatically imply deception.

Organizations often contain competing narratives about themselves.

---

# 18. Origin

Every significant Faction should have an origin.

Possible origins include:

- government continuity
- institutional collapse
- military restructuring
- corporate survival
- community organization
- ideological movement
- criminal consolidation
- scientific coalition
- emergency response
- secession
- merger

Factions should not appear from nowhere.

---

# 19. Historical Causality

Faction creation should follow history.

Conceptually:

```text
HISTORICAL CONDITIONS
↓
PRESSURE
↓
ORGANIZATION
↓
SHARED NEED / GOAL
↓
STRUCTURE
↓
RESOURCES
↓
FACTION EMERGENCE.
```

The Faction should exist because conditions made its formation plausible.

---

# 20. Faction Goals

Factions may possess one or more persistent goals.

Examples include:

- maintain control
- protect territory
- restore infrastructure
- expand influence
- obtain resources
- preserve knowledge
- defeat a rival
- secure autonomy
- rebuild government
- control trade
- investigate Aurora
- contain Aurora
- cooperate with Aurora
- survive

Goals create direction.

They do not guarantee coherent action.

---

# 21. Strategic vs Immediate Goals

Faction goals may exist at different scales.

Example:

```text
STRATEGIC GOAL:
CONTROL REGIONAL
POWER INFRASTRUCTURE

IMMEDIATE GOAL:
SECURE SUBSTATION 14.
```

Separating these levels improves continuity.

---

# 22. Goals May Conflict

A Faction may contain competing goals.

Example:

```text
PRESERVE LEGITIMACY

VS

USE FORCE
TO RESTORE CONTROL.
```

Or:

```text
EXPAND TERRITORY

VS

PRESERVE RESOURCES.
```

Internal tension is legitimate.

---

# 23. Leadership

Leadership determines how organized intent becomes action.

Leadership may be:

- individual
- council-based
- hierarchical
- elected
- appointed
- inherited
- informal
- distributed

No universal leadership model exists.

---

# 24. Leadership Is Not the Faction

A leader may strongly influence a Faction.

But:

```text
LEADER
≠
FACTION.
```

The Faction may survive:

- resignation
- death
- replacement
- coup
- election

Leadership is one part of organizational continuity.

---

# 25. Leadership Change

Leadership change may alter:

- goals
- priorities
- alliances
- legitimacy
- strategy
- internal cohesion

But previous structures and history remain.

Therefore:

```text
NEW LEADER
≠
NEW FACTION
BY DEFAULT.
```

---

# 26. Decision-Making

A Faction requires some mechanism for collective decisions.

Possible mechanisms include:

- command hierarchy
- voting
- council
- executive authority
- consensus
- informal leadership
- competing internal centers of power

Decision-making determines how internal preferences become collective action.

---

# 27. Decision Failure

Faction decision-making may fail.

Possible causes include:

- internal conflict
- missing leadership
- communication failure
- disputed authority
- incomplete information
- resource disagreement
- institutional paralysis

A Faction may remain intact while being temporarily incapable of coherent action.

---

# 28. Membership

Factions contain members.

Membership may be:

- voluntary
- professional
- contractual
- ideological
- coerced
- inherited
- opportunistic

Membership does not imply full loyalty.

---

# 29. Member Motivation

Members may participate because of:

- belief
- salary
- protection
- identity
- family
- opportunity
- fear
- obligation
- survival

Therefore:

```text
FACTION GOAL
≠
MEMBER GOAL.
```

This distinction is important.

---

# 30. Membership Commitment

Members may vary in commitment.

Conceptually:

```text
CORE LEADERSHIP

COMMITTED MEMBERS

PROFESSIONAL MEMBERS

DEPENDENT MEMBERS

OPPORTUNISTIC MEMBERS

COERCED MEMBERS

SYMPATHIZERS.
```

These are conceptual categories.

They do not need to become rigid runtime classes.

---

# 31. Internal Diversity

Every major Faction should allow internal variation.

Avoid:

```text
ALL MEMBERS
BELIEVE X.
```

Prefer:

```text
THE FACTION
OFFICIALLY SUPPORTS X

WHILE INTERNAL
VIEWS VARY.
```

Faction coherence must not erase human complexity.

---

# 32. Internal Groups

Large Factions may contain:

- departments
- wings
- commands
- political blocs
- ideological camps
- regional branches

These may eventually qualify as sub-Factions.

Use only when simulation value justifies it.

---

# 33. Internal Conflict

Faction conflict may involve:

- leadership
- strategy
- ideology
- resources
- territory
- succession
- external alliances

Internal conflict may weaken or transform Faction behavior.

It does not automatically split the Faction.

---

# 34. Faction Splits

A Faction may split when internal conflict becomes organizational separation.

Conceptually:

```text
INTERNAL CONFLICT
↓
LOSS OF COMMON
DECISION PROCESS
↓
SEPARATE LEADERSHIP
↓
SEPARATE RESOURCES
↓
SEPARATE GOALS
↓
NEW FACTIONS.
```

Faction splits should create historical continuity.

---

# 35. Faction Mergers

Factions may merge through:

- alliance
- necessity
- conquest
- political integration
- institutional consolidation

A merger may create:

- one new Faction
- one dominant Faction absorbing another
- a coalition structure

The resulting identity should be explicit.

---

# 36. Resources

Factions may control resources such as:

- money
- food
- fuel
- weapons
- vehicles
- data
- infrastructure
- personnel
- medical supplies
- manufacturing capacity
- communication systems

Resources determine what the Faction can realistically attempt.

---

# 37. Resources Are Not Capability

Possession does not guarantee effective use.

Conceptually:

```text
RESOURCE
+
EXPERTISE
+
ACCESS
+
ORGANIZATION
=
POSSIBLE CAPABILITY.
```

A Faction may possess advanced equipment without enough Expertise to maintain it.

---

# 38. Capability

Faction capability describes what organized action the Faction can actually perform.

Possible domains include:

- military
- intelligence
- logistics
- governance
- research
- engineering
- medical
- economic
- communication
- transportation
- information operations

Capability should emerge from people, resources and structure.

---

# 39. Faction Expertise

Factions do not possess abstract Expertise independently of humans and systems.

Faction-level capability may derive from:

```text
MEMBER EXPERTISE
+
INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE
+
PROCEDURES
+
TOOLS
+
ORGANIZATION.
```

Loss of key experts may therefore reduce capability.

---

# 40. Institutional Knowledge

Factions may preserve knowledge through:

- records
- procedures
- archives
- training
- experienced personnel

This may allow capability to persist beyond individual members.

But institutional knowledge is not identical to Human Expertise.

---

# 41. Territory

Some Factions control territory.

Others do not.

Territory may provide:

- population
- resources
- infrastructure
- security
- legitimacy
- access

But:

```text
FACTION
DOES NOT
REQUIRE TERRITORY.
```

A distributed network may still be a major Faction.

---

# 42. Control vs Influence

A Faction may:

```text
CONTROL
```

a territory or institution.

Or merely:

```text
INFLUENCE
```

it.

These are different.

Control implies stronger operational authority.

Influence changes outcomes without direct ownership.

---

# 43. Territorial Control

Territorial control should consider:

- physical presence
- authority
- security
- enforcement
- infrastructure
- population compliance
- rival presence

A map label alone should not establish real control.

---

# 44. Influence

Influence may arise from:

- reputation
- resources
- information
- leadership
- institutional authority
- military power
- economic power
- social support

Influence should remain contextual.

---

# 45. Power

Faction power is multi-dimensional.

Avoid one universal:

```text
POWER SCORE.
```

A Faction may possess:

```text
HIGH MILITARY POWER

LOW LEGITIMACY

HIGH INFORMATION ACCESS

LOW LOGISTICS CAPACITY.
```

This complexity is desirable.

---

# 46. Legitimacy

Some Factions may claim authority.

Legitimacy describes how that authority is socially recognized.

It may come from:

- law
- elections
- history
- competence
- protection
- tradition
- consent

Legitimacy belongs partly to Society state.

The Faction may claim it.

Society determines how widely it is accepted.

---

# 47. Reputation

Faction Reputation describes how external Actors or populations perceive the Faction.

Reputation may vary by observer.

Therefore:

```text
FACTION REPUTATION
≠
ONE GLOBAL VALUE.
```

One community may see the Faction as protectors.

Another may see the same Faction as occupiers.

---

# 48. Reputation vs World Truth

Reputation is perception.

It may differ from actual Faction behavior.

Therefore:

```text
REPUTATION
≠
WORLD TRUTH.
```

This distinction should remain explicit.

---

# 49. Alliances

Factions may form alliances.

Alliances may be based on:

- shared goals
- mutual threats
- trade
- ideology
- geography
- necessity
- historical relationship

Alliance does not imply friendship or full Trust.

---

# 50. Alliance Scope

An alliance may be limited.

Example:

```text
COOPERATE
ON SECURITY

BUT

COMPETE
FOR POLITICAL CONTROL.
```

Relationships between Factions should therefore be domain-specific where useful.

---

# 51. Rivalries

Rivalries may involve:

- territory
- ideology
- resources
- authority
- reputation
- history

Rivalry does not necessarily mean active war.

---

# 52. Hostility

Faction hostility may exist at multiple levels:

```text
COMPETITION

POLITICAL HOSTILITY

ECONOMIC CONFLICT

COVERT ACTION

OPEN VIOLENCE

WAR.
```

Escalation should require causality.

---

# 53. Diplomacy

Factions may negotiate.

Diplomacy may involve:

- agreements
- ceasefires
- trade
- access
- recognition
- prisoner exchange
- resource sharing
- alliance

Negotiations should reflect actual incentives and information.

---

# 54. Faction Relationships

Faction-to-Faction Relationships may track persistent state such as:

- Trust
- hostility
- dependency
- alliance
- rivalry
- obligation
- trade
- historical grievances

The Relationship architecture may eventually own these edges if generalized to collective Actors.

Until then, Factions may reference them conceptually.

---

# 55. Information

Factions possess information.

They do not automatically know World Truth.

A Faction may hold:

- intelligence
- reports
- rumors
- records
- intercepted communication
- member testimony
- sensor data

Its conclusions may be wrong.

---

# 56. Faction Knowledge

Always preserve:

```text
WORLD TRUTH
≠
FACTION KNOWLEDGE
≠
FACTION BELIEF
≠
PUBLIC CLAIM.
```

These layers may diverge.

---

# 57. Intelligence Capability

Some Factions may possess strong intelligence systems.

Others may rely on:

- local contacts
- observation
- rumor
- trade networks

Information capability should emerge from resources and structure.

---

# 58. Secrets

Factions may possess secrets.

A Faction secret may involve:

- plans
- capabilities
- leadership
- internal conflict
- resources
- alliances
- historical actions

But information distribution inside the Faction may be uneven.

---

# 59. Need-to-Know

Large Factions should not assume universal internal knowledge.

Conceptually:

```text
FACTION KNOWS X
```

may actually mean:

```text
SPECIFIC LEADERSHIP
OR DEPARTMENT
HAS X.
```

Information access may be compartmentalized.

---

# 60. Orders

Factions may issue orders through leadership and organizational structures.

But:

```text
ORDER ISSUED
≠
ORDER OBEYED.
```

Compliance may depend on:

- authority
- communication
- member motivation
- conditions
- competing orders
- local judgment

---

# 61. Faction Action

Faction action should emerge through:

```text
GOALS
+
INFORMATION
+
LEADERSHIP
+
RESOURCES
+
CAPABILITIES
+
INTERNAL STATE
+
EXTERNAL CONDITIONS
↓
DECISION
↓
ACTION.
```

Faction action is therefore constrained.

---

# 62. Agency

Factions possess collective agency only when they have enough organization to make and execute decisions.

This agency is emergent from structures and humans.

It is not supernatural collective consciousness.

---

# 63. Action Failure

Faction actions may fail because of:

- poor intelligence
- inadequate resources
- bad leadership
- internal resistance
- logistics
- weather
- enemy action
- infrastructure failure
- unexpected consequences

A powerful Faction must still remain fallible.

---

# 64. Unintended Consequences

Faction actions may produce effects they did not intend.

Example:

```text
FACTION
SECURES FOOD
FOR ITS OWN MEMBERS
↓
REGIONAL SHORTAGE
WORSENS
↓
PUBLIC HOSTILITY
INCREASES.
```

Intent and consequence remain separate.

---

# 65. Strategy

Strategy connects long-term goals to planned action.

A Faction may possess:

```text
GOAL
↓
STRATEGY
↓
OPERATIONS
↓
TACTICAL ACTION.
```

Not every Faction needs highly sophisticated strategy.

---

# 66. Strategy Is Not Omniscience

A strategy is based on what the Faction believes.

Therefore:

```text
GOOD STRATEGY
BASED ON
BAD INFORMATION

MAY STILL FAIL.
```

This keeps Factions grounded in information boundaries.

---

# 67. Faction Time Horizon

Different Factions may operate with different time horizons.

Examples:

```text
SURVIVE THIS WEEK

CONTROL REGION
THIS YEAR

REBUILD NATIONAL
INSTITUTION

PRESERVE KNOWLEDGE
FOR NEXT GENERATION.
```

Time horizon influences behavior.

---

# 68. Faction Survival

Factions seek continuity in different ways.

They may prioritize:

- leadership
- membership
- territory
- ideology
- assets
- knowledge
- legitimacy

Not all Factions define survival identically.

---

# 69. Faction Death

A Faction may cease to exist when it loses enough:

- structure
- identity
- decision-making
- continuity
- membership
- capability

But historical consequences remain.

---

# 70. Dormant Factions

Some Factions may become inactive without disappearing entirely.

They may retain:

- identity
- network
- records
- hidden resources
- surviving members

Dormancy may later end.

---

# 71. Faction Evolution

Factions may evolve.

Possible changes include:

- mission change
- ideological shift
- institutionalization
- radicalization
- decentralization
- militarization
- democratization
- criminalization

Changes should follow internal and external pressures.

---

# 72. Faction Identity Drift

Over time, a Faction may retain its name while becoming fundamentally different.

Conceptually:

```text
NAME PERSISTS

BUT

MEMBERSHIP
LEADERSHIP
GOALS
AND STRUCTURE
CHANGE.
```

Historical continuity must still be preserved.

---

# 73. Recruitment

Factions may recruit members.

Recruitment may depend on:

- ideology
- employment
- security
- family
- reputation
- necessity
- coercion
- opportunity

Recruitment should not imply instant loyalty.

---

# 74. Recruitment Pools

Faction recruitment depends on Society.

Relevant conditions may include:

- unemployment
- insecurity
- migration
- political discontent
- social identity
- local Trust
- reputation

Society creates conditions.

Faction chooses recruitment strategy.

---

# 75. Defection

Members may leave.

Possible causes include:

- ideological disagreement
- fear
- opportunity
- leadership change
- betrayal
- family responsibility
- resource failure

Defection may create information risk.

---

# 76. Loyalty

Faction membership does not equal loyalty.

Loyalty may exist toward:

- organization
- leader
- comrades
- ideology
- mission

These may conflict.

---

# 77. Faction Internal Trust

Internal Trust may affect:

- delegation
- secrecy
- coordination
- leadership
- cohesion

But Faction-level Trust should not erase member-level Relationships.

---

# 78. Faction Cohesion

Faction Cohesion describes organizational ability to remain coordinated despite disagreement.

Cohesion may depend upon:

- leadership
- shared goals
- structure
- Trust
- resources
- threat
- identity

Cohesion is not consensus.

---

# 79. Cohesion Failure

Low cohesion may produce:

- delays
- disobedience
- corruption
- factionalism
- defection
- split

But low cohesion does not automatically dissolve the organization.

---

# 80. Faction Resources and Economy

Factions may participate in:

- trade
- taxation
- extraction
- production
- distribution
- confiscation
- aid

Detailed economy should remain outside Factions if a dedicated Economy system exists.

Factions own strategic resource control and use.

---

# 81. Logistics

Faction capability depends heavily on logistics.

A military Faction may possess personnel and weapons but still fail because it cannot:

- feed them
- fuel vehicles
- maintain equipment
- communicate
- transport supplies

Logistics must remain consequential.

---

# 82. Infrastructure

Factions may:

- control infrastructure
- operate infrastructure
- protect infrastructure
- sabotage infrastructure

But infrastructure remains World state.

Faction ownership or access is a relationship to that state.

---

# 83. Territory and Population

Controlling territory does not mean owning the people within it.

A Faction may claim authority over a population.

That population may:

- support
- tolerate
- resist
- ignore
- cooperate selectively

Society tracks these collective responses.

---

# 84. Occupation

Military control and social legitimacy are separate.

A Faction may control a city physically while having extremely low legitimacy.

This distinction is essential.

---

# 85. Governance

Some Factions may govern.

Governance may involve:

- law
- resource allocation
- security
- administration
- public services
- dispute resolution

A governing Faction becomes deeply integrated with Society.

It still remains an Actor.

---

# 86. Criminal Factions

Criminal organizations may operate as Factions when they possess:

- structure
- continuity
- resources
- territory
- leadership
- goals

They should not automatically be portrayed as chaotic.

Organized crime may provide highly structured systems.

---

# 87. Corporate Factions

Corporations or corporate remnants may become Factions when they possess strategic agency.

They may control:

- infrastructure
- knowledge
- employees
- security
- resources
- logistics

But corporate identity alone does not automatically create a Faction.

---

# 88. Military Factions

Military commands may become independent Factions if central command fragments.

Possible outcomes include:

- loyal remnants
- regional commands
- autonomous units
- coalition forces

They should inherit real institutional history.

---

# 89. Research Factions

Research institutions may become Factions when they coordinate:

- protected research
- resources
- security
- intelligence
- negotiations

These may be especially important around Aurora.

---

# 90. Mutual-Aid Factions

Mutual-aid networks may develop sufficient structure to become Factions.

They may coordinate:

- food
- healthcare
- shelter
- logistics
- local protection

Faction status does not require coercive power.

---

# 91. Political Factions

Political organizations may persist or emerge across World States.

They may operate through:

- institutions
- movements
- local governance
- networks

Political identity should remain historically grounded.

---

# 92. Religious Factions

Religious organizations may qualify as Factions if they possess organized agency.

Religion itself is not a Faction.

A specific organized institution or coalition may be.

---

# 93. Ideological Factions

Ideological Factions may form around explicit visions of society.

But avoid creating simplistic:

```text
IDEOLOGY
=
MORAL ALIGNMENT.
```

Their actions should emerge from goals, conditions and internal structure.

# 94. Faction Morality

# 125. Faction Invariants

## FAC-INV-001 — Factions Are Organized Collective Actors

A Faction requires sufficient structure and agency to act coherently.

---

## FAC-INV-002 — Factions Are Not Societies

Society describes collective social state; Factions possess organized agency.

---

## FAC-INV-003 — Factions Are Not Collective Minds

Members may disagree, dissent or pursue different goals.

---

## FAC-INV-004 — Faction Goals Are Not Member Goals

Membership does not imply identical motivation.

---

## FAC-INV-005 — Faction Identity Requires History

Major Factions must have plausible origins and continuity.

---

## FAC-INV-006 — Faction Capability Requires Resources and Organization

Intent alone does not create capability.

---

## FAC-INV-007 — Resources Do Not Guarantee Capability

Tools and assets require people, knowledge and logistics.

---

## FAC-INV-008 — Faction Information Is Not World Truth

Faction knowledge may be incomplete or wrong.

---

## FAC-INV-009 — Reputation Is Observer-Dependent

No single global Faction reputation value is sufficient.

---

## FAC-INV-010 — Control Is Not Legitimacy

Physical power and accepted authority remain distinct.

---

## FAC-INV-011 — Alliance Is Not Trust

Factions may cooperate while distrusting one another.

---

## FAC-INV-012 — Leadership Is Not the Faction

Organizations may persist through leadership change.

---

## FAC-INV-013 — Factions May Change

Goals, structures, leadership and identity may evolve causally.

---

## FAC-INV-014 — Factions Can Split and Merge

Organizational continuity must remain historical and causal.

---

## FAC-INV-015 — Factions Persist Off-Screen

Faction state and action continue without player observation.

---

## FAC-INV-016 — Faction Action Requires Information Pathways

Factions do not automatically know what players or rivals have done.

---

## FAC-INV-017 — Player Importance Does Not Override Scale

Player actions influence Factions only through plausible consequence.

---

## FAC-INV-018 — Factions Are Not Moral Alignments

No Faction is canonically reduced to good or evil.

---

## FAC-INV-019 — Aurora Does Not Define Faction Identity

Faction goals and history must remain broader than their stance toward Aurora.

---

## FAC-INV-020 — Factions Create World Consequences

Organized collective action may propagate into World, Society, Life and future history.

---

# 126. Development Locks

Future Faction development must not introduce:

- good / evil Faction alignment
- one universal Faction power score
- one universal reputation score
- one universal loyalty score
- membership as personality
- Society and Faction as the same system
- Settlement and Faction as the same system
- Government and Faction as automatically identical
- Faction omniscience
- instant player reputation propagation
- all members knowing all Faction information
- static leadership
- static Faction goals
- territory without real control
- resources without logistics
- capability without Expertise
- automatic loyalty
- automatic obedience
- instant faction collapse after leader death
- Factions created solely to provide enemies
- factions defined solely as pro-Aurora or anti-Aurora

Avoid:

```text
THIS FACTION
IS EVIL.
```

Prefer:

```text
THIS FACTION
HAS GOALS,
METHODS,
HISTORY
AND CONSEQUENCES

THAT DIFFERENT
ACTORS MAY
JUDGE DIFFERENTLY.
```

Avoid:

```text
THE FACTION
CONTROLS THE CITY.
```

without explaining:

```text
HOW?

THROUGH WHAT
INSTITUTIONS?

WITH WHAT
SECURITY?

WITH WHAT
LEGITIMACY?

AGAINST WHAT
OPPOSITION?
```

# 135. Closing Principle

Factions are how human organization becomes agency at scale.

Individuals create groups.

Groups create structure.

Structure creates capability.

Capability creates power.

Power creates consequence.

But no organization escapes the humans inside it.

Leaders disagree.

Members defect.

Resources fail.

Information is wrong.

Goals conflict.

Institutions change.

Alliances collapse.

Enemies cooperate.

Organizations survive the people who founded them.

Others disappear while their consequences remain.

Project Ascension should therefore treat Factions neither as static labels nor as collective personalities.

They are organized historical Actors.

The central principle is:

> **A Faction is a collective capable of acting with purpose, but never a collective mind free from human complexity, limited information and consequence.**
