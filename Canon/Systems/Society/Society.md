# 1. Purpose

The Society system defines persistent collective human state.

It answers:

> **How are people collectively organized, what social conditions exist between them, and how does that collective state change over time?**

Society exists above the individual Character level.

It does not attempt to simulate a single collective mind.

Instead, it represents patterns and structures that emerge when many humans:

- live together
- depend on one another
- create institutions
- establish norms
- exchange resources
- exercise authority
- cooperate
- compete
- disagree
- migrate
- organize
- fragment
- rebuild

Society is therefore one of the major bridges between human behavior and World State.

---

# 2. Core Definition

Society is:

> **The persistent collective social structures, conditions and patterns through which populations organize human life.**

Conceptually:

```text
INDIVIDUAL HUMANS
+
RELATIONSHIPS
+
FAMILIES
+
CULTURES
+
INSTITUTIONS
+
ECONOMIC CONDITIONS
+
AUTHORITY
+
SHARED HISTORY
+
MATERIAL CONDITIONS
↓
SOCIETY
```

Society is emergent.

It is not a Character.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The central Society principle is:

> **Society influences the conditions under which people live and choose, but it does not determine what every individual believes or does.**

Therefore:

```text
SOCIETY
INFLUENCES

BUT

SOCIETY
DOES NOT
DETERMINE
THE INDIVIDUAL.
```

A stable society may contain desperate people.

A violent society may contain compassionate people.

A distrustful society may contain deeply trusting relationships.

Collective state and individual state must remain separate.

---

# 4. Society Is Not Collective Psychology

Society must never become a giant Character.

Avoid:

```text

THE REGION
IS ANGRY.

```

These may be useful narrative descriptions.

```text

WIDESPREAD

PUBLIC CONFIDENCE
HAS DECLINED

PROTEST ACTIVITY
IS INCREASING

INSTITUTIONAL TRUST
IS COLLAPSING.
```

Individual Psychology belongs to humans.

Society owns collective patterns.

# 21. Social Cohesion

Social Cohesion describes the degree to which people within a population are capable of maintaining cooperative social order despite disagreement.

Cohesion may be supported by:

- Trust
- shared institutions
- common norms
- mutual dependence
- legitimate authority
- shared identity
- functioning services

Cohesion does not mean everyone agrees.

---

# 22. Cohesion Is Not Harmony

A cohesive society may contain:

- political disagreement
- protest
- inequality
- rivalry
- cultural conflict

Cohesion means disagreement does not automatically destroy collective function.

Therefore:

```text
COHESION
≠
CONSENSUS.
```

Fragmentation occurs when collective structures become increasingly disconnected.

- institutional breakdown

- economic separation

# 24. Fragmentation Is Not Immediate Collapse

A fragmented society may continue functioning.

```text

FOOD SUPPLY FAILURE.
```

Therefore:

```text
RESILIENCE
IS DOMAIN-SPECIFIC.
```

---

# 90. Social Adaptation

Societies adapt when people change collective structures or practices in response to conditions.

Adaptation may involve:

- new institutions
- new norms
- new trade systems
- migration
- new authority
- technology change
- cooperation

Adaptation is not automatically positive.

---

# 91. Social Adaptation Is Not Progress

A Society may adapt through:

- authoritarian control
- exclusion
- criminal governance
- forced labor

These may stabilize some functions while producing serious human cost.

Therefore:

```text
ADAPTATION
≠
PROGRESS.
```

---

# 92. Social Change

Social change should generally have causal pathways.

Conceptually:

```text

↓

↓
```

Not every pressure creates change.

---

# 93. Social Momentum

Societies possess inertia.

Institutions, norms and expectations often persist after their original conditions change.

Therefore social change should not occur instantly without sufficient cause.

---

# 94. Path Dependence

Past Society states influence future possibilities.

Conceptually:

```text
PAST STRUCTURE
↓
CURRENT OPTIONS
↓
FUTURE DEVELOPMENT.
```

Two communities facing the same crisis may respond differently because their histories differ.

---

# 95. Society Persistence

Society state persists off-screen.

Communities continue to:

- organize
- trade
- migrate
- cooperate
- conflict
- adapt
- rebuild
- decline

The world does not wait for the players.

# 97. Off-Screen Society

Off-screen social simulation should prioritize consequential changes.

Examples include:

- institutional collapse
- migration
- leadership change
- major conflict
- resource crisis
- community formation
- changing Trust

Minor social detail may be compressed.

---

# 98. Society Compression

The system should not attempt to simulate every citizen individually.

Instead:

```text
INDIVIDUALS
WHEN RELEVANT

POPULATION PATTERNS
WHEN APPROPRIATE

INSTITUTIONS
WHEN CONSEQUENTIAL.
```

The simulation should use the lowest resolution capable of preserving causality.

---

# 99. Population Representation

Large populations should be represented through distributions and patterns rather than uniform traits.

Examples:

```text

HIGH — 20%
MODERATE — 35%
LOW — 30%
HOSTILE — 15%
```

This is conceptually preferable to:

```text

```

Exact implementation remains future work.

# 100. Social State Is Multi-Dimensional

A community may simultaneously possess:

```text

HIGH LOCAL TRUST

SEVERE RESOURCE PRESSURE

STRONG COMMUNITY CAPACITY.
```

This complexity is desirable.

# 101. Society and Time

```text
DAYS

MONTHS

YEARS
→ norm change, demographic change

GENERATIONS
→ deep cultural and structural transformation.
```

The system should respect appropriate time scale.

# 123. Closing Principle

Society is not a machine above humanity.

It is what emerges when humans live together long enough to create expectations, institutions, obligations, dependencies and shared ways of organizing life.

Those structures influence the people who inherit them.

Those people then reinforce, challenge, abandon or rebuild those structures.

During Project Ascension, technology changes.

Institutions fail.

New authorities emerge.

Communities fracture.

Others cooperate.

Trust shifts.

People migrate.

Old systems persist longer than expected.

New systems emerge where none were planned.

And none of this happens uniformly.

The central principle is:

> **Society creates the conditions under which people live together, while human choices continuously reshape the society they inherit.**
