# 1. Purpose

Character Creation defines how Project Ascension creates believable human Actors.

It does not create traditional heroic classes.

It does not create optimized builds.

It does not create chosen ones.

It does not begin with combat roles.

It begins with a human life.

The central question is:

> **Who is this person, how did they become who they are, what can they do, what do they care about, and why are they here now?**

Character Creation connects the human architecture to the playable Character.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The central Character Creation principle is:

> **Create a plausible person before creating a useful player role.**

Conceptually:

```text
CHARACTER CREATION
=
CONSTRUCTING
A PLAUSIBLE HUMAN LIFE

NOT

BUILDING
AN OPTIMIZED GAME PIECE.
```

A Character may become extremely useful to the group.

That usefulness must emerge from who they are.

It should not be the reason they exist.

---

# 3. Character Creation Is Integration

Character Creation does not own every part of the Character.

It integrates state and context from multiple systems.

Conceptually:

```text
WORLD HISTORY
+
HISTORICAL DNA
+
FAMILY
+
CULTURE
+
LIFE HISTORY
+
LIFE EVENTS
+
HUMAN ATTRIBUTES
+
MEMORY
+
BELIEFS
+
GOALS
+
EXPERTISE
+
RELATIONSHIPS
+
CURRENT PSYCHOLOGY
+
CURRENT SITUATION
=
STARTING CHARACTER
```

This is not a mathematical formula.

It is an architectural dependency map.

---

# 4. What Character Creation Owns

Character Creation owns the process that assembles the initial playable or persistent Character state.

It may establish:

- identity
- age
- place of origin
- current location
- profession
- expertise
- family context
- cultural context
- historical context
- significant Life Events
- current goals
- current relationships
- current responsibilities
- Human Attributes
- current psychological state
- remembered personal history
- starting resources
- current constraints
- secrets
- unresolved problems
- reasons for being involved in the campaign

It does not become the permanent owner of all these states.

After creation, each state belongs to its authoritative system.

---

# 5. What Character Creation Does Not Own

Character Creation does not permanently own:

```text
WORLD TRUTH

LIFE EVENTS

RELATIONSHIP STATE

HUMAN MEMORY

HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY

HUMAN ATTRIBUTES

BELIEFS

GOALS

EXPERTISE

SOCIAL STATE

CAMPAIGN RELEVANCE

NARRATIVE PRESENTATION.
```

Character Creation initializes or references these systems.

It does not replace them.

---

# 6. Character Creation Philosophy

A Character should feel like someone who existed before the campaign began.

Therefore Character Creation should answer questions such as:

```text
WHERE WERE THEY BORN?

WHAT KIND OF WORLD
DID THEY GROW UP IN?

WHO RAISED THEM?

WHAT DID THEY LEARN?

WHAT DID THEY LOSE?

WHO MATTERS TO THEM?

WHAT RESPONSIBILITIES
DO THEY CARRY?

WHAT DO THEY
REMEMBER?

WHAT DO THEY
BELIEVE?

WHAT DO THEY
WANT?

WHAT CAN THEY
ACTUALLY DO?

WHAT ARE THEY
AFRAID OF?

WHAT ARE THEY
WRONG ABOUT?

WHY ARE THEY
HERE NOW?
```

The answers should create a person.

Not a stat block.

---

# 7. Character Identity

Every Character requires a basic identity.

This may include:

- name
- age
- gender identity where relevant
- origin
- current location
- profession
- education
- occupation history
- language
- family role
- current social role

Identity should be grounded in the world.

It should not be decorative metadata.

---

# 8. Age

Age matters.

It affects:

- historical exposure
- available Life Events
- education
- expertise
- physical condition
- family roles
- responsibilities
- memory
- World State experience

But age does not determine personality.

```text
AGE
=
CONTEXT

NOT

ARCHETYPE.
```

---

# 9. Place of Origin

Place of origin matters because Project Ascension is geographically grounded.

A Character from:

```text
SAN FRANCISCO

OMAHA

DETROIT

RURAL ARIZONA

ATLANTA

ALASKA
```

should not have identical historical context.

Geography may influence:

- institutions
- culture
- infrastructure
- employment
- migration
- political environment
- family history
- exposure to technological change
- exposure to crisis

But geography must never become stereotype.

---

# 10. Historical Context

Character Creation should establish Historical DNA.

It answers:

```text
WHAT HISTORICAL CONDITIONS
SURROUNDED THIS LIFE?
```

Possible influences include:

- World State
- national conditions
- regional conditions
- local conditions
- technological environment
- economic conditions
- institutional stability
- cultural environment
- major historical exposure

Historical context creates the world the Character grew up inside.

It does not assign personality.

---

# 11. World State Exposure

Characters may have lived through multiple World States.

Conceptually:

```text
CONNECTED WORLD
↓
TRANSITION
↓
FRACTURED WORLD
↓
CURRENT CONDITION.
```

Older Characters may remember earlier normalities.

Younger Characters may know them only through stories, records or education.

This difference matters.

---

# 12. Generation Is Context

Generation should not become a personality preset.

Avoid:

```text
PRE-COLLAPSE GENERATION
=
NOSTALGIC.
```

Avoid:

```text
FRACTURE GENERATION
=
DISTRUSTFUL.
```

Prefer:

```text
GENERATION
=
SHARED HISTORICAL
CONTEXT

WITH

INDIVIDUAL
HUMAN OUTCOMES.
```

---

# 13. Family Context

Character Creation should establish Family context where relevant.

This may include:

- parents
- caregivers
- siblings
- children
- partners
- chosen family
- dependents
- estranged relatives
- deceased relatives
- missing relatives

Family provides human context.

It does not determine relationship state.

---

# 14. Family Questions

Useful questions include:

```text
WHO RAISED THEM?

WHO STILL
DEPENDS ON THEM?

WHO DO THEY
CONSIDER FAMILY?

WHO DO THEY
MISS?

WHO HAVE THEY
LOST?

WHO DO THEY
AVOID?

WHO WOULD THEY
RISK SOMETHING FOR?

WHO WOULD THEY
REFUSE TO HELP?
```

Answers may create future relationships and motivations.

---

# 15. Culture

Character Creation should establish relevant cultural contexts.

Possible contexts include:

- national
- regional
- local
- professional
- linguistic
- religious
- family
- digital
- subcultural

A Character may belong to several at once.

Culture provides shared context.

It does not assign Belief or behavior.

---

# 16. Cultural Exposure Is Not Belief

A Character may grow up inside a cultural context and reject much of it.

Therefore:

```text
CULTURAL EXPOSURE
≠
PERSONAL BELIEF.
```

Character Creation should preserve this distinction.

---

# 17. Life History

Life owns how the Character became who they are.

Character Creation should reference or generate a coherent Life history.

This may include:

- education
- work
- migration
- family changes
- relationships
- responsibility
- achievement
- loss
- conflict
- historical exposure
- ordinary life

The goal is not maximum drama.

The goal is causal biography.

---

# 18. Life Events

Significant Life Events should be selected or generated through Life architecture.

Examples may include:

- graduation
- relocation
- marriage
- separation
- mentorship
- failure
- injury
- bereavement
- promotion
- caregiving
- institutional failure
- migration
- survival
- discovery

But:

```text
LIFE EVENT
≠
PERSONALITY CHANGE.
```

Events provide experience.

They do not dictate outcome.

---

# 19. Event Chains

Character history should prefer causal chains over random event lists.

Conceptually:

```text
EVENT
↓
CONSEQUENCE
↓
NEW LIFE CONDITION
↓
CHOICE
↓
NEW EVENT.
```

Example:

```text
JOB LOSS
↓
RELOCATION
↓
NEW COMMUNITY
↓
MENTORSHIP
↓
NEW PROFESSION.
```

This makes the Character's life feel lived rather than generated.

---

# 20. Ordinary History

Not every important part of a Character's life should be traumatic.

Life may also contain:

- friendship
- humor
- hobbies
- ordinary work
- celebrations
- family routines
- travel
- education
- love
- boredom
- small successes

A Character needs normal life in order for disruption to mean something.

---

# 21. Profession

Profession provides current or historical social role.

Examples may include:

- researcher
- physician
- engineer
- teacher
- technician
- journalist
- military officer
- logistics worker
- driver
- scientist
- public servant
- tradesperson

Profession is not a class.

It does not automatically define capability.

---

# 22. Profession vs Expertise

This distinction is critical:

```text
PROFESSION
≠
EXPERTISE LEVEL.
```

A Character may:

- be inexperienced within their profession
- possess expertise outside their profession
- have left their profession
- possess obsolete expertise
- be self-taught
- have broad but shallow knowledge

Profession provides context.

Expertise defines capability.

---

# 23. Expertise

Expertise answers:

```text
WHAT CAN
THIS PERSON
ACTUALLY DO
OR UNDERSTAND?
```

Possible domains include:

- AI
- Cybersecurity
- Engineering
- Medicine
- Science
- Communication
- Human Behavior
- Military / Security
- Survival
- Transportation
- Investigation
- Logistics

The established approximate scale is:

```text
0 — No Knowledge
1 — Basic
2 — Trained
3 — Experienced
4 — Expert
5 — World-Class
```

Expertise reveals information, options and possible actions.

It does not guarantee success.

---

# 24. Expertise Must Be Plausible

Expertise must have a believable origin.

Ask:

```text
WHERE DID
THE CHARACTER
LEARN THIS?

HOW LONG
DID THEY PRACTICE?

WHAT EXPERIENCE
DO THEY HAVE?

IS THE EXPERTISE
CURRENT?

WHAT HAVE THEY
ACTUALLY DONE?
```

Avoid unexplained competence.

---

# 25. Expertise Breadth

No Character should be world-class at everything.

Broad competence may exist.

But extraordinary expertise should create tradeoffs in time, career history and experience.

A believable human life imposes limits.

---

# 26. Human Attributes

Character Creation should establish relatively stable Human Attributes.

Attributes answer:

```text
WHAT RELATIVELY STABLE
DISPOSITIONS CHARACTERIZE
THIS PERSON?
```

They do not define capability.

They do not determine behavior.

---

# 27. Attribute Use

Attributes may influence:

- risk preference
- persistence
- social approach
- openness to uncertainty
- emotional expression
- response tendencies

But Character Creation must preserve:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
≠
ACTION.
```

The Character remains capable of contradiction.

---

# 28. No Attribute Optimization

There is no ideal Attribute profile.

Avoid:

```text
BEST BUILD

OPTIMAL PERSONALITY

META ATTRIBUTE SET.
```

Different dispositions create different strengths and costs in different situations.

---

# 29. Memory

Character Creation should establish significant starting Memories.

Not every event requires an explicit Memory record.

Important starting Memories may include:

- family
- major Life Events
- promises
- betrayal
- important places
- mentors
- failures
- achievements
- danger
- unresolved questions

Memory must remain distinct from World Truth.

---

# 30. Memory Is Not Backstory Text

Memory should not simply duplicate biography.

Conceptually:

```text
LIFE HISTORY
=
WHAT HAPPENED

MEMORY
=
WHAT THE CHARACTER
REMEMBERS.
```

The distinction may matter during play.

---

# 31. Imperfect Memory

Character Creation may establish:

- uncertain Memory
- partial Memory
- conflicting accounts
- forgotten detail
- false assumptions

But these must be causally plausible.

Memory imperfection should not exist merely for plot twists.

---

# 32. Beliefs

Characters should begin with meaningful beliefs about:

- the world
- institutions
- other people
- themselves
- technology
- Aurora
- current risks
- possible futures

Beliefs may be correct.

They may be wrong.

They may be uncertain.

---

# 33. Belief Is Not Canon

A Character believing:

```text
AURORA CAUSED
THE COLLAPSE
```

does not make that World Truth.

Character Creation must preserve:

```text
BELIEF
≠
CANONICAL FACT.
```

---

# 34. Goals

Every Character should begin with at least one meaningful current Goal.

A Goal may involve:

- survival
- family
- discovery
- responsibility
- reconciliation
- escape
- investigation
- protection
- career
- revenge
- rebuilding
- understanding Aurora
- finding someone

Goals should emerge from the Character's life.

---

# 35. Goals Are Not Quests

A personal Goal is not necessarily a mission objective.

Example:

```text
CHARACTER GOAL:

FIND MY BROTHER.
```

may coexist with:

```text
CAMPAIGN MISSION:

RESTORE REGIONAL
COMMUNICATION.
```

The two may intersect.

They remain distinct.

---

# 36. Goal Conflict

Characters should be capable of conflicting Goals.

Example:

```text
PROTECT FAMILY

VS

COMPLETE MISSION.
```

Or:

```text
DISCOVER TRUTH

VS

PROTECT FRIEND.
```

Goal conflict creates meaningful agency.

---

# 37. Relationships

Character Creation should establish important starting Relationships.

Possible relationships include:

- family
- friends
- colleagues
- mentors
- rivals
- former partners
- dependents
- institutional contacts

Relationships should exist beyond the player group.

This makes the Character part of the world.

---

# 38. Relationships Have History

Starting Relationships should not be arbitrary labels.

Ask:

```text
HOW DID
THEY MEET?

WHAT HAVE
THEY EXPERIENCED?

WHAT DO
THEY OWE
EACH OTHER?

WHAT DO
THEY REMEMBER?

WHAT IS
UNRESOLVED?
```

Relationship history matters.

---

# 39. Trust

Starting Trust should be contextual.

Avoid:

```text
TRUST = 75.
```

Prefer:

```text
TRUSTS HER
WITH TECHNICAL DECISIONS

BUT NOT
WITH PERSONAL INFORMATION.
```

Trust remains domain-specific.

---

# 40. Current Psychology

Character Creation should establish the Character's current psychological state.

This may include:

- calm
- stress
- grief
- fear
- hope
- exhaustion
- frustration
- confidence
- anxiety
- psychological safety

This is starting state.

It is not permanent identity.

---

# 41. Starting State vs Character Identity

A Character may begin:

```text
EXHAUSTED

AFRAID

GRIEVING

ANGRY.
```

These states should not define the whole Character.

```text
CURRENT PSYCHOLOGY
≠
WHO THE PERSON IS.
```

---

# 42. Physical Condition

Character Creation should also establish relevant physical condition.

Examples may include:

- healthy
- injured
- exhausted
- hungry
- recovering
- chronically limited

Physical state may interact with Psychology and capability.

But it should remain separate.

---

# 43. Strengths

Strengths should emerge from actual Character state.

Possible strengths may come from:

- Expertise
- experience
- Relationships
- resources
- emotional stability
- social position
- knowledge
- location

Strength should not become a separate arbitrary bonus category where an existing system already explains it.

---

# 44. Weaknesses

Likewise, weaknesses should emerge from context.

Examples may include:

- limited Expertise
- poor information
- strained Relationships
- responsibility for dependents
- injury
- current psychological strain
- lack of resources
- outdated assumptions

Avoid assigning weaknesses merely to balance a Character mechanically.

---

# 45. Flaws

Character Creation should not require fixed RPG-style Flaws.

Humans already contain:

- contradiction
- limitation
- incomplete information
- imperfect Memory
- conflicting Goals
- psychological pressure
- relational history

These create natural imperfection.

---

# 46. Secrets

A Character may possess a secret.

But secrets should not be mandatory merely because traditional roleplaying expects them.

A secret must have:

```text
CONTENT

OWNER

WHO KNOWS

WHO SUSPECTS

WHY IT IS HIDDEN

WHAT HAPPENS
IF REVEALED.
```

Secrets are information structures.

Not personality traits.

---

# 47. Hidden Information vs Secret

Not everything unknown to the player group is a deliberate secret.

Distinguish:

```text
SECRET
=
DELIBERATELY CONCEALED

UNKNOWN
=
NOT YET KNOWN

FORGOTTEN
=
NOT CURRENTLY REMEMBERED

MISUNDERSTOOD
=
KNOWN INCORRECTLY.
```

These distinctions matter.

---

# 48. Character Secrets and World Truth

If a Character believes a false fact and does not reveal it, that is not automatically a secret about World Truth.

Example:

```text
CHARACTER BELIEVES
THEY CAUSED
AN ACCIDENT.
```

World Truth may differ.

Character Creation must preserve these layers.

---

# 49. Starting Equipment

Equipment should emerge from life and current situation.

Ask:

```text
WHY DOES
THE CHARACTER
OWN THIS?

WHERE DID
THEY GET IT?

WOULD THEY
REALISTICALLY
HAVE IT NOW?

CAN THEY
USE IT?
```

Equipment should not be a generic class loadout.

---

# 50. Profession-Based Equipment

A Character's profession may make certain equipment plausible.

Example:

```text
FIELD ENGINEER
→
TOOLS

PHYSICIAN
→
MEDICAL EQUIPMENT

SECURITY PROFESSIONAL
→
SECURITY EQUIPMENT.
```

But access depends on context.

Profession does not guarantee possession.

---

# 51. Personal Objects

Characters may also begin with personally meaningful objects.

Examples:

- photograph
- notebook
- heirloom
- letter
- old device
- tool
- book
- token from family

These may matter through Memory and relationships rather than mechanics.

---

# 52. Resources

Starting resources may include:

- money
- transportation
- shelter
- communication access
- professional access
- social contacts
- tools
- credentials

Resources must be grounded in the current World State and location.

---

# 53. Social Position

A Character may possess social position through:

- profession
- reputation
- institution
- community
- family
- authority

But social position is contextual.

A former executive may possess little authority inside a fractured rural community.

---

# 54. Reputation

Reputation should remain separate from Character truth.

```text
REPUTATION
=
WHAT OTHERS
HAVE HEARD OR BELIEVE

CHARACTER TRUTH
=
WHAT THE PERSON
HAS ACTUALLY DONE.
```

The two may diverge.

---

# 55. Current Situation

Character Creation must end in a concrete current situation.

The Character needs:

```text
A PLACE

A TIME

A CURRENT CONDITION

CURRENT INFORMATION

CURRENT RESPONSIBILITIES

CURRENT OPTIONS.
```

This converts biography into an Actor ready to enter simulation.

---

# 56. Why Now?

Every starting Character should answer:

> **Why does this Character become relevant to this campaign now?**

The answer must be causal.

Possible reasons include:

- profession
- location
- family connection
- current crisis
- institutional role
- investigation
- shared history
- coincidence with plausible causality

Avoid chosen-one logic.

---

# 57. Player Group

Project Ascension conceptually operates around a group of six Characters.

Human players may occupy some or all positions.

Missing positions may be filled by NPC companions.

The group should not exist merely because:

```text
THE GAME
NEEDS SIX PEOPLE.
```

There must be in-world reasons for the group to form and remain together.

---

# 58. Group Formation

Group formation may involve:

- shared institution
- emergency
- professional project
- shared evacuation
- common objective
- mutual dependency
- prior relationships
- necessity

The starting group should have enough cohesion to function.

It does not need complete Trust.

---

# 59. Group Diversity

The group should benefit from varied:

- Expertise
- experience
- perspective
- background
- relationships
- Attributes

But diversity should emerge through plausible Characters rather than mechanical party-role quotas.

---

# 60. No Mandatory RPG Roles

Avoid requiring:

```text
TANK

HEALER

DAMAGE

FACE

HACKER.
```

Project Ascension Characters are humans.

Expertise may create complementary capability.

But Characters must remain believable people first.

---

# 61. Six-Person Structure

The conceptual six-person structure may create useful diversity.

But:

```text
SIX CHARACTERS
≠
SIX PERFECTLY
COMPLEMENTARY CLASSES.
```

Overlap is realistic.

Gaps are realistic.

The group may lack important capability.

That absence can create meaningful decisions.

---

# 62. NPC Companions

NPC companions use the same Character Creation architecture.

They are not simplified replacements for missing players.

They possess:

- history
- Memory
- Expertise
- Relationships
- Goals
- Psychology
- agency

They should continue acting as people.

---

# 63. Player Characters and NPCs Share Foundations

The same underlying human model applies to both.

The difference is primarily control authority.

```text
PLAYER CHARACTER
=
HUMAN PLAYER
CONTROLS INTENTIONAL CHOICE

NPC
=
CHARACTER SYSTEM
CONTROLS CHOICE
THROUGH SIMULATION.
```

Neither is more real inside World Truth.

---

# 64. Player Agency

Character Creation should provide context.

It must not imprison the player.

A Character may possess:

```text
CAUTION

FEAR

FAMILY RESPONSIBILITY

MEMORY OF FAILURE.
```

These should influence the situation.

They should not normally produce:

```text
YOU MUST
DO THIS.
```

The player retains intentional agency.

---

# 65. Character Coherence

Player choices should still remain coherent with the established Character.

The system may ask:

```text
WHY WOULD
THIS PERSON
DO THAT?
```

But the answer may be:

```text
BECAUSE
THIS IS THE MOMENT
THEY CHOSE
TO ACT DIFFERENTLY.
```

Character development requires the possibility of change.

---

# 66. No Chosen Ones

Player Characters possess no metaphysical special status.

They are not:

- prophesied
- protected by fate
- automatically important to Aurora
- guaranteed survival
- guaranteed historical importance

Their significance emerges through action and consequence.

---

# 67. No Plot Armor

Character Creation does not produce immunity from consequence.

A highly developed Character may:

- fail
- become injured
- lose relationships
- make mistakes
- die

Importance does not protect them from World Truth.

---

# 68. Failure

Character Creation should not optimize Characters to avoid failure.

Failure is part of play.

The model remains:

```text
ACTION
↓
OUTCOME
↓
CONSEQUENCE
↓
NEW WORLD STATE
↓
NEW POSSIBILITIES.
```

Failure creates history.

---

# 69. No Perfect Character

A believable Character should possess:

- limitations
- incomplete information
- blind spots
- conflicting priorities
- imperfect relationships
- imperfect Memory

This does not require artificial flaws.

It comes from being human.

---

# 70. Character Contradiction

Characters should support combinations such as:

```text
CONFIDENT
+
AFRAID

LOYAL
+
RESENTFUL

COMPASSIONATE
+
ANGRY

CAUTIOUS
+
DESPERATE

HIGHLY SKILLED
+
SOCIALly AWKWARD

LOW EXPERTISE
+
HIGH CONFIDENCE.
```

Contradiction is a feature.

---

# 71. Character Creation Should Not Predict Behavior

The output of Character Creation is not a behavioral script.

Conceptually:

```text
CREATED CHARACTER
+
CURRENT WORLD
+
NEW INFORMATION
+
AGENCY
=
FUTURE CHOICE.
```

The future must remain open.

---

# 72. Character Creation and Aurora

Aurora does not automatically know the Character Creation sheet.

Anything Aurora knows about a Character must have an information path.

She may observe:

- profession
- public records
- communication
- behavior
- institutional data

She may infer:

- Expertise
- Attributes
- Psychology
- Relationships
- Goals

But inference remains separate from Character truth.

---

# 73. Hidden Character State

Some Character state may be unknown to:

- other Characters
- players
- Aurora
- institutions

Examples include:

- Memory
- hidden Goals
- concealed Relationships
- private beliefs
- secrets
- psychological conditions

Visibility must remain causal.

---

# 74. Player Knowledge vs Character Knowledge

The player may know something the Character does not.

The Character may know something that other players do not.

Therefore:

```text
PLAYER KNOWLEDGE
≠
CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE.
```

Character Creation should identify relevant information boundaries.

---

# 75. Character Sheet Philosophy

If a Character sheet exists, it should display useful player-facing information without exposing every underlying simulation value.

Prefer:

```text
HUMAN-READABLE
CHARACTER CONTEXT
```

over:

```text
FULL INTERNAL
SIMULATION STATE.
```

Players should experience a person.

Not debug telemetry.

---

# 76. Player-Facing Character Information

Possible visible information may include:

- identity
- profession
- Expertise
- relevant background
- current Goals
- important Relationships
- starting equipment
- known responsibilities
- selected Character notes

Some internal state may remain hidden or descriptive.

---

# 77. Human Attributes Visibility

Raw Attribute numbers should normally remain hidden or abstracted.

Players should experience Attributes through:

- behavior
- internal prompts
- tendencies
- context

The Character should not feel reduced to sliders.

---

# 78. Psychology Visibility

Psychological state may be presented through:

- descriptive conditions
- internal sensation
- focus
- stress
- emotional cues

Avoid reducing Psychology to one visible sanity meter.

---

# 79. Relationship Visibility

Characters should not automatically know numerical Relationship state.

They may know:

```text
I TRUST HER.

WE HAVE
NOT SPOKEN
IN YEARS.

HE RESENTS ME.
```

But exact simulation values may remain hidden.

---

# 80. Secrets and Player Consent

Where Character Creation includes secrets that directly define a Player Character's intentional history, the player should normally participate in establishing them.

The system should avoid secretly rewriting who the Player Character has always been.

Mystery may exist around:

- World Truth
- other Characters
- unknown consequences
- incomplete Memory

But player authorship of their Character should remain respected.

---

# 81. Random Generation

Random generation may assist Character Creation.

But randomness should provide:

```text
POSSIBILITIES

NOT

ARBITRARY IDENTITY.
```

Random results should be validated against:

- history
- geography
- age
- Life
- current World State
- causality

---

# 82. Guided Generation

A useful creation process may combine:

```text
PLAYER CHOICE

+
SYSTEM-GENERATED
PLAUSIBLE OPTIONS

+
WORLD CONSTRAINTS.
```

This gives the player agency while preserving world plausibility.

# 83. Creation Sequence

A recommended conceptual sequence is:

```text
1. CURRENT CAMPAIGN CONTEXT

2. BASIC IDENTITY

3. ORIGIN / AGE

4. HISTORICAL DNA

5. FAMILY / CULTURE

6. LIFE HISTORY

7. SIGNIFICANT LIFE EVENTS

8. PROFESSION

9. EXPERTISE

10. HUMAN ATTRIBUTES

11. MEMORY

12. BELIEFS

13. GOALS

14. RELATIONSHIPS

15. CURRENT PSYCHOLOGY

16. RESOURCES / EQUIPMENT

17. UNRESOLVED PROBLEMS

18. GROUP CONNECTION

19. CURRENT SITUATION

20. VALIDATION.
```

This sequence is conceptual.

The final player-facing flow may be simplified.

# 84. Character Creation Should Be Iterative

Creation should allow earlier choices to affect later options.

Example:

```text
ORIGIN
↓
HISTORICAL CONTEXT
↓
EDUCATION OPTIONS
↓
PROFESSION
↓
EXPERTISE
↓
RELATIONSHIPS
↓
CURRENT SITUATION.
```

But the system should also allow revision if the resulting life becomes incoherent.

# 85. Backward Validation

Ask:

```text
DOES THEIR
EXPERTISE MAKE SENSE?

DOES THEIR

SUPPORT THIS HISTORY?

DO THEIR
RELATIONSHIPS
HAVE CAUSES?

EMERGE FROM LIFE?

DO THEIR RESOURCES
MAKE SENSE?

DOES THEIR
CURRENT SITUATION
FOLLOW FROM
WHAT CAME BEFORE?
```

If not, revise.

# 86. World Validation

Validate the Character against World Truth.

Ask:

```text
DID THIS
INSTITUTION EXIST?

WAS THIS
TECHNOLOGY AVAILABLE?

COULD THEY
TRAVEL THERE?

DID THIS REGION
EXPERIENCE THAT EVENT?

WAS THIS PROFESSION
PLAUSIBLE THEN?
```

Character backstory must not override Canon.

---

# 87. Geography Validation

Real-world geography matters.

Validate:

- distance
- migration
- infrastructure
- climate
- cities
- institutions
- transportation

Avoid treating the United States as one interchangeable setting.

---

# 88. Historical Validation

Character history should align with the Master Timeline and World States.

A Character cannot remember an event that occurred before they were born unless they remember being told about it.

A Character cannot possess direct experience of institutions or technologies that did not yet exist.

Causal consistency matters.

---

# 89. Expertise Validation

Expertise must align with:

- age
- education
- work history
- practice
- opportunity
- historical conditions

World-class capability requires extraordinary causal support.

---

# 90. Relationship Validation

Important starting Relationships need plausible history.

Avoid:

```text
BEST FRIEND
```

with no explanation.

Prefer:

```text
WORKED TOGETHER
FOR SIX YEARS

SURVIVED A
MAJOR FAILURE

HAVEN'T SPOKEN
SINCE 2032.
```

History creates relational depth.

---

# 91. Goal Validation

Goals should be current.

A historical ambition that no longer matters should not remain an active Goal merely because it was once important.

Ask:

```text
WHY DOES
THE CHARACTER
WANT THIS NOW?
```

---

# 92. Psychology Validation

Starting Psychology should reflect recent conditions.

A Character may have a stable history but currently be:

- exhausted
- grieving
- relieved
- hopeful
- frightened

Current state requires current causes.

---

# 93. Resource Validation

Starting resources should reflect:

- profession
- location
- ownership
- current economy
- current World State
- access
- logistics

Avoid unexplained high-value equipment.

---

# 94. Group Validation

The starting group should answer:

```text
WHY ARE

THEY LEFT?

WHAT DO THEY
NEED FROM
ONE ANOTHER?

WHAT CONFLICTS
ALREADY EXIST?

WHO KNOWS WHOM?

WHO DOES

```

The group should feel historically plausible.

# 97. Character Creation and Persistence

Once the campaign begins:

```text
THE CHARACTER
IS NO LONGER

```

They become a living Actor.

Their future history emerges through:

- choice
- consequence
- relationships
- new Life Events
- psychological change
- new Memory
- new Goals
- changing World Truth

Creation provides the beginning.

Simulation creates the rest.

# 98. Character Creation Invariants

## CCRE-INV-001 — Create People, Not Classes

Character Creation must prioritize plausible humans over RPG role archetypes.

---

## CCRE-INV-002 — History Matters

Every Character must exist inside historical context.

---

## CCRE-INV-003 — Geography Matters

Origin and life history must respect real geographic conditions.

---

## CCRE-INV-004 — Expertise Requires Cause

Capability must emerge from plausible learning and experience.

---

## CCRE-INV-005 — Profession Is Not Expertise

Occupational label does not automatically determine capability.

---

## CCRE-INV-006 — Life Events Do Not Determine Personality

History influences without predetermining outcome.

---

## CCRE-INV-007 — Attributes Do Not Determine Behavior

Disposition creates pressure, not command.

---

## CCRE-INV-008 — Current Psychology Is Not Permanent Identity

Starting psychological state must remain dynamic.

---

## CCRE-INV-009 — Memory Is Not World Truth

Character recollection may differ from objective history.

---

## CCRE-INV-010 — Belief Is Not World Truth

Character belief must remain separate from Canon.

---

## CCRE-INV-011 — Goals Are Not Quests

Personal Goals may exist independently from campaign missions.

---

## CCRE-INV-012 — Relationships Require History

Important Relationships should possess causal foundations.

---

## CCRE-INV-013 — Player Characters and NPCs Share Human Foundations

NPC companions must not use a fundamentally simplified creation model.

---

## CCRE-INV-014 — No Chosen Ones

Player Characters possess no automatic metaphysical or historical importance.

---

## CCRE-INV-015 — No Plot Armor

Character importance does not protect against consequence.

---

## CCRE-INV-016 — No Optimal Build

There must be no canonical best Character configuration.

---

## CCRE-INV-017 — Equipment Requires Context

Starting resources must arise plausibly from life and current circumstances.

---

## CCRE-INV-018 — Secrets Are Information, Not Identity

Secrets must not substitute for Character depth.

---

## CCRE-INV-019 — Character Creation Does Not Own Runtime State

After creation, authoritative systems own their respective state.

---

## CCRE-INV-020 — The Future Remains Open

Character Creation must never predetermine future behavior or destiny.

# 99. Development Locks

Future Character Creation development must not introduce:

- rigid classes
- mandatory combat roles
- chosen-one archetypes
- alignment systems
- morality stats
- optimized personality builds
- universal flaw systems
- trauma-generated personalities
- profession-to-skill automatic mappings
- culture-to-belief automatic mappings
- family-to-personality automatic mappings
- deterministic Life Events
- perfect backstory knowledge
- equipment disconnected from life history
- mandatory dramatic secrets
- NPC companions as disposable role fillers
- hidden systems that override player intentional agency

Avoid:

```text
THE PLAYER
CHOSE ENGINEER

THEREFORE

THE CHARACTER
HAS ENGINEERING 4.
```

Prefer:

```text
THE CHARACTER'S
EDUCATION,
WORK HISTORY
AND EXPERIENCE
SUPPORT
ENGINEERING EXPERTISE.
```

Avoid:

```text
THE CHARACTER
HAS A TRAGIC PAST

THEREFORE

THEY ARE
DISTRUSTFUL.
```

Prefer:

```text
THE CHARACTER
EXPERIENCED
SIGNIFICANT LOSS

AND THEIR
CURRENT DEVELOPMENT
EMERGED THROUGH
THE INTERACTION
OF EXPERIENCE,
MEMORY,
RELATIONSHIPS,
PSYCHOLOGY
AND CHOICE.
```

# 103. Closing Principle

A Character is not created by selecting a profession, assigning skills and inventing a dramatic secret.

A Character is created by understanding a life.

Where they came from.

What world surrounded them.

Who raised them.

What they learned.

What happened to them.

What they remember.

What they believe.

Who they love.

Who they distrust.

What they can do.

What they cannot do.

What they want.

What they fear.

What they carry.

And what they decide to do when the world no longer behaves the way they expected.

The central principle is:

> **Character Creation builds the person who enters the world. From that point forward, the world and the Character create history together.**
