---

# 1. Purpose

The Expertise System defines what a Character can realistically understand, evaluate and do within a domain.

It answers:

> **What can this person actually do or understand, and how well?**

Expertise is not profession.

It is not intelligence.

It is not personality.

It is not confidence.

It is not memory.

It is not access to information.

And it is not guaranteed success.

Expertise represents developed human capability.

---

# 2. Core Definition

Expertise is:

> **A Character's developed capability and understanding within a specific domain, grounded in learning, training, experience and practice.**

Conceptually:

```text
EXPERTISE
=
WHAT A PERSON
CAN ACTUALLY
UNDERSTAND OR DO
WITHIN A DOMAIN.
```

Expertise may affect:

- what information a Character notices
- what they understand
- what risks they recognize
- what actions become available
- how difficult tasks are approached
- how efficiently they work
- how effectively they collaborate

It never guarantees outcome.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The central Expertise principle is:

> **Expertise expands understanding and capability. It does not replace uncertainty, tools, conditions or consequence.**

Therefore:

```text
EXPERTISE
IMPROVES

INFORMATION
AVAILABLE ACTIONS
RISK AWARENESS
PRACTICAL CAPABILITY

BUT

EXPERTISE
DOES NOT
GUARANTEE SUCCESS.
```

---

# 4. Expertise Is Not a Character Class

Project Ascension does not use traditional RPG classes.

Avoid:

```text
ENGINEER CLASS

MEDIC CLASS

HACKER CLASS

SCIENTIST CLASS.
```

Instead, Characters possess:

```text
LIFE HISTORY
+
PROFESSION
+
EXPERTISE
+
EXPERIENCE
+
ATTRIBUTES
+
CURRENT CONDITION.
```

A Character may possess expertise across several domains.

---

# 5. Expertise Ownership

Expertise owns persistent Character capability within defined knowledge and action domains.

It may own:

- domain capability level
- specialization capability
- professional mastery
- practical familiarity
- related capability history

Expertise does not own:

```text
PROFESSION

HUMAN ATTRIBUTES

MEMORY

BELIEFS

CURRENT PSYCHOLOGY

GOALS

RELATIONSHIPS

TOOLS

WORLD INFORMATION

TASK DIFFICULTY

OUTCOME.
```

These interact with Expertise.

They remain separate.

---

# 6. Expertise vs Profession

Profession describes a social or occupational role.

Expertise describes actual capability.

Therefore:

```text
PROFESSION
≠
EXPERTISE.
```

A Character may be:

```text
PHYSICIAN
```

while having:

```text
MEDICINE 2
```

because they are recently trained.

Another may no longer work as a physician but retain:

```text
MEDICINE 4.
```

Profession provides context.

Expertise provides capability.

---

# 7. Expertise vs Human Attributes

Human Attributes describe relatively stable dispositions.

Expertise describes learned capability.

Therefore:

```text
HIGH CONFIDENCE
≠
HIGH EXPERTISE

HIGH CURIOSITY
≠
HIGH SCIENTIFIC EXPERTISE

HIGH CAUTION
≠
HIGH SECURITY EXPERTISE.
```

Attributes may influence how Expertise is used.

They do not create it.

---

# 8. Expertise vs Memory

Memory preserves remembered experience.

Expertise represents developed capability.

A Character may remember how someone performed a task without being able to reproduce it.

Likewise, an expert may forget an isolated detail while retaining deep capability.

Therefore:

```text
MEMORY
≠
EXPERTISE.
```

---

# 9. Expertise vs Information Access

Information access and Expertise are separate.

A Character may possess:

```text
THE MANUAL
```

without being able to understand it.

Another may possess deep Expertise but lack necessary information about the current system.

Conceptually:

```text
INFORMATION
+
EXPERTISE
=
POSSIBLE UNDERSTANDING.
```

Neither replaces the other.

---

# 10. Expertise vs Intelligence

Project Ascension should not use Expertise as a proxy for intelligence.

A highly capable engineer is not automatically:

- socially perceptive
- strategically brilliant
- medically knowledgeable
- emotionally insightful

Likewise, an intelligent person does not automatically possess specialized expertise.

---

# 11. Expertise vs Current Performance

Expertise represents underlying capability.

Performance represents what happens under current conditions.

A Character may possess:

```text
MEDICINE 4
```

and still perform poorly because of:

```text
EXHAUSTION

POOR LIGHT

INADEQUATE EQUIPMENT

TIME PRESSURE

FEAR

INCOMPLETE INFORMATION.
```

Therefore:

```text
EXPERTISE
≠
CURRENT PERFORMANCE.
```

---

# 12. Expertise Levels

The canonical Expertise scale is:

```text
0 — No Knowledge

1 — Basic

2 — Trained

3 — Experienced

4 — Expert

5 — World-Class
```

The scale is intentionally compact.

It describes meaningful capability bands rather than fine-grained numerical optimization.

---

# 13. Level 0 — No Knowledge

Level 0 represents no meaningful capability within the domain.

A Character may:

- recognize common concepts
- possess general cultural awareness
- follow very basic instructions

But cannot reliably perform meaningful domain-specific work.

Conceptually:

```text
LEVEL 0
=
NO PRACTICAL
DOMAIN CAPABILITY.
```

---

# 14. Level 1 — Basic

Level 1 represents introductory understanding.

The Character may:

- understand common terminology
- perform simple tasks
- follow instructions
- recognize obvious problems
- communicate with trained practitioners

They generally require guidance for difficult or unfamiliar work.

---

# 15. Level 2 — Trained

Level 2 represents formal or practical training sufficient for normal independent work.

The Character may:

- perform standard tasks
- understand domain procedures
- recognize common risks
- solve routine problems
- function independently in familiar contexts

This is meaningful professional capability.

---

# 16. Level 3 — Experienced

Level 3 represents substantial real-world experience.

The Character may:

- recognize unusual complications
- adapt standard procedures
- troubleshoot
- make informed judgments under uncertainty
- teach less experienced practitioners
- work effectively outside ideal conditions

Level 3 represents strong practical competence.

---

# 17. Level 4 — Expert

Level 4 represents deep professional mastery.

The Character may:

- solve difficult non-routine problems
- evaluate complex systems
- identify subtle risks
- work across unusual conditions
- challenge standard assumptions
- guide other professionals
- make high-level domain judgments

Level 4 should be relatively uncommon.

---

# 18. Level 5 — World-Class

Level 5 represents exceptional capability within a specific domain or specialization.

Conceptually:

```text
LEVEL 5
=
AMONG THE PEOPLE
WHO DEFINE
THE FIELD.
```

A World-Class expert may:

- advance the discipline
- solve problems others cannot
- create new methods
- influence institutions
- possess rare or unique knowledge

Level 5 must be extremely rare.

It is not simply the strongest player option.

---

# 19. Level 5 Is Not Omniscience

World-Class does not mean:

```text
KNOWS EVERYTHING.
```

A Level 5 expert may still:

- lack information
- make mistakes
- encounter unfamiliar systems
- be wrong
- fail under impossible conditions
- need collaboration

Expertise remains bounded by reality.

Future domains should be added only when they represent meaningful distinct capability.

Avoid unnecessary skill fragmentation.

# 23. Domain vs Specialization

Domain level represents broad capability.

Specialization represents deeper capability in a narrower area.

Example:

```text
MEDICINE 3

EMERGENCY MEDICINE 4.
```

This means the Character is broadly experienced in Medicine but particularly strong in Emergency Medicine.

---

# 24. Specialization Does Not Replace Domain

A narrow specialist may still require broad domain understanding.

Likewise, a Character may possess strong broad capability without one exceptional specialization.

Both profiles are valid.

---

# 25. Rare Specialization

Rare specializations may exist where justified by Life history.

Examples may involve:

- obscure infrastructure
- advanced AI systems
- unusual medical procedures
- specialized military systems
- rare scientific fields
- legacy industrial equipment

Rare specialization should emerge from plausible history.

It should not exist solely as a special player bonus.

- professional work
- practice

- repeated experience

- experimentation
- problem solving

Conceptually:

```text

PRACTICE
```

---

# 28. Expertise and Education

Education may contribute to Expertise.

But:

```text
EDUCATION
≠
EXPERTISE.
```

A degree does not automatically imply high practical capability.

Likewise, substantial expertise may develop outside formal education.

---

# 29. Expertise and Work History

Repeated professional use often strengthens practical Expertise.

But time served alone does not guarantee mastery.

Conceptually:

```text
YEARS WORKED
≠
EXPERTISE LEVEL.
```

Quality and relevance of experience matter.

---

# 30. Expertise and Mentorship

Mentorship may accelerate development.

A strong mentor may provide:

- feedback
- exposure
- knowledge
- judgment
- opportunities
- correction

Mentorship does not automatically create expertise.

The Character must still learn and practice.

---

# 31. Expertise and Self-Learning

Characters may develop Expertise through self-directed learning.

This is especially plausible in:

- software
- AI
- electronics
- communication
- survival
- investigation

But advanced Expertise still requires meaningful practice and evidence of capability.

---

# 32. Expertise and Life Events

Life Events may create opportunities for learning.

Example:

```text

CHARACTER SPENDS

LOCAL POWER SYSTEMS
↓
POSSIBLE ENGINEERING
DEVELOPMENT.
```

But:

```text
LIFE EVENT
≠
AUTOMATIC EXPERTISE.
```

---

# 33. Expertise Growth

Expertise may improve over time.

Growth should depend on:

- relevant practice
- training
- task difficulty
- feedback
- repetition
- access to knowledge
- mentorship
- opportunity

Growth should not occur merely because time passes.

---

# 34. Expertise Growth Is Slow

High-level Expertise requires significant development.

Moving from:

```text
LEVEL 1
TO
LEVEL 2
```

may be achievable relatively quickly.

Moving from:

```text
LEVEL 4
TO
LEVEL 5
```

should require extraordinary achievement, opportunity and time.

---

# 35. Expertise Decay

Expertise may weaken when unused.

Decay may affect:

- procedural fluency
- speed
- recall
- familiarity with current systems
- confidence

But deep foundational knowledge may persist.

Therefore:

```text
EXPERTISE DECAY
SHOULD NOT
BE SIMPLE
LEVEL LOSS.
```

---

# 36. Outdated Expertise

Expertise may remain strong while becoming outdated.

Example:

```text
CYBERSECURITY 4
```

from a decade earlier may still provide deep foundational understanding.

But current systems may contain unfamiliar:

- protocols
- architectures
- threats
- tools

This distinction is important.

```text

INDUSTRIAL REPAIR

CYBERSECURITY
→
```

Transfer should be plausible.

---

# 39. Domain Distance

Related domains may share useful knowledge.

Distant domains may not.

Conceptually:

```text
RELATED KNOWLEDGE
=
PARTIAL TRANSFER

UNRELATED KNOWLEDGE
=
LITTLE OR NO TRANSFER.
```

Implementation details remain future work.

---

# 40. Information Interpretation

Expertise influences what a Character can understand from available information.

Example:

```text
WORLD CONDITION:
MACHINE PRODUCES
IRREGULAR VIBRATION.
```

Level 0 may receive:

```text
THE MACHINE
SOUNDS WRONG.
```

Level 3 Engineering may receive:

```text
THE VIBRATION
SUGGESTS A
MECHANICAL FAULT.
```

A relevant specialist may recognize:

```text
THE BEARING
IS PROBABLY FAILING.
```

The underlying World Truth remains the same.

---

# 41. Expertise Does Not Create Information

Expertise cannot reveal information the Character has no access to.

Therefore:

```text
EXPERTISE
+
NO EVIDENCE
≠
KNOWLEDGE.
```

A physician cannot diagnose a patient they have never examined without relevant information.

An engineer cannot inspect infrastructure from nothing.

---

# 42. Expertise and Observation

Expertise may improve observation by making certain details meaningful.

Conceptually:

```text
WORLD INFORMATION
↓
CHARACTER EXPOSURE
↓
PERCEPTION
↓
EXPERTISE INTERPRETATION
↓
AVAILABLE UNDERSTANDING.
```

This is one of Expertise's most important functions.

---

# 43. Risk Awareness

Expertise may reveal risks that non-experts do not recognize.

Example:

```text
NON-EXPERT:

THE ROOM
LOOKS SAFE.
```

```text
ELECTRICAL EXPERT:

THAT WATER
IS TOO CLOSE
TO THE LIVE PANEL.
```

Expertise may therefore change perceived options before action occurs.

---

# 44. Available Actions

Expertise may make certain actions available.

Example:

```text
LEVEL 0 MEDICINE

ACTION:
CALL FOR HELP
APPLY BASIC PRESSURE
```

```text
LEVEL 3 MEDICINE

ACTION:
ASSESS CONDITION
STABILIZE PATIENT
PRIORITIZE TREATMENT
```

Higher Expertise expands possibility.

It does not guarantee success.

---

# 45. Expertise and Difficulty

Task difficulty exists independently of Character Expertise.

Conceptually:

```text
TASK DIFFICULTY
≠
CHARACTER EXPERTISE.
```

The same task may be trivial for one Character and nearly impossible for another.

---

# 46. Task Complexity

Tasks may vary through:

- technical complexity
- uncertainty
- novelty
- time pressure
- environment
- required precision
- available tools
- consequences of failure

Difficulty should emerge from World Truth and circumstance.

---


---

# 48. Tools

Expertise and tools interact.

A surgeon without equipment may have limited options.

A mechanic without tools may understand the problem but be unable to repair it.

Therefore:

```text
KNOWING HOW
≠
HAVING WHAT
IS REQUIRED.
```

---

# 49. Poor Tools

Experts may sometimes compensate for inadequate tools through:

- improvisation
- experience
- alternative methods

But improvisation should have limits.

Expertise does not create physical resources.

---

# 50. Environment

Environment influences performance.

Examples include:

- darkness
- extreme temperature
- rain
- unstable structures
- noise
- danger
- contamination
- poor visibility

Expertise may help mitigate these conditions.

It cannot erase them.

---

# 51. Time Pressure

Time affects Expertise application.

A Character may know the ideal solution but lack enough time to execute it.

Therefore:

```text
KNOWING THE BEST ACTION
≠
HAVING TIME
TO PERFORM IT.
```

---

# 52. Human Psychology and Performance

Current Psychology may alter practical performance.

Possible influences include:

- fear
- stress
- exhaustion
- grief
- confidence
- panic
- cognitive overload

But:

```text
PSYCHOLOGY
DOES NOT
DELETE EXPERTISE.
```

It may interfere with its application.

---

# 53. Physical Condition and Performance

Physical condition may matter.

Examples include:

- injury
- fatigue
- hunger
- pain
- illness
- dehydration

A highly skilled Character remains knowledgeable while their ability to act may be reduced.

---

# 54. Expertise and Failure

Experts can fail.

Failure may occur because:

- information was incomplete
- conditions were impossible
- tools failed
- time was insufficient
- the problem was novel
- another Actor interfered
- the expert made a mistake
- the expert's assumptions were wrong

Failure does not automatically mean low Expertise.

---

# 55. Non-Experts Can Succeed

Likewise, low-Expertise Characters may occasionally succeed.

Possible reasons include:

- simple task
- good instructions
- excellent tools
- luck
- assistance
- strong preparation

Therefore:

```text
SUCCESS
≠
PROOF OF EXPERTISE.
```

---

# 56. Expertise and Uncertainty

Experts should often recognize uncertainty better than non-experts.

A Level 4 Character may say:

```text
I DON'T KNOW
ENOUGH YET.
```

where a less experienced Character may incorrectly believe the answer is obvious.

Deep Expertise should not produce false certainty by default.

---

# 57. Expertise and Confidence

Confidence is separate from Expertise.

Possible combinations include:

```text
HIGH EXPERTISE
+
LOW CONFIDENCE

LOW EXPERTISE
+
HIGH CONFIDENCE.
```

Both are realistic.

The simulation should preserve them.

---

# 58. Expertise and Communication

Possessing Expertise does not guarantee the ability to explain it well.

A Level 5 scientist may be a poor communicator.

A Level 2 practitioner may be excellent at teaching basic concepts.

Communication remains its own capability.

---

# 59. Expertise Collaboration

Characters may combine Expertise.

Example:

```text
ENGINEER
+
CYBERSECURITY EXPERT
+
LOGISTICS EXPERT
```

may solve a problem that none could solve alone.

This supports group play without traditional classes.

---

# 60. Collaborative Expertise

Collaboration should depend on:

- relevant Expertise
- communication
- Trust
- time
- access
- shared information

Having multiple experts does not automatically create perfect teamwork.

---

# 61. Expertise Overlap

Characters may possess overlapping Expertise.

This is realistic.

The group does not require one exclusive expert per domain.

Overlap may provide:

- redundancy
- disagreement
- peer validation
- deeper capability

---

# 62. Expertise Gaps

The group may lack relevant Expertise entirely.

This is also realistic.

Possible responses include:

- seek help
- find documentation
- recruit an expert
- improvise
- avoid the problem

Capability gaps create meaningful world interaction.

---

# 63. Expertise and Group Composition

Project Ascension's group of six should not be constructed as six perfectly complementary roles.

Instead, the group should emerge from plausible Characters.

Expertise diversity may be valuable.

But:

```text
GROUP DESIGN
≠
PARTY CLASS BALANCING.
```

- settlement

- community

Experts may acquire reputation.

```text
REPUTATION
≠
```

Credentials may signal Expertise.

- degrees
- certifications
- licenses
- institutional roles

But credentials are evidence.

They are not identical to actual capability.

During fracture, verification may become difficult.

# 76. Expertise Verification

Characters may need to determine whether another Actor is actually capable.

Possible evidence includes:

- credentials
- reputation
- demonstration
- testimony
- work history
- observed performance

These may be incomplete or false.

# 77. Expertise and Trust

Characters may trust someone because of perceived Expertise.

But:

```text
EXPERTISE
≠
TRUSTWORTHINESS.
```

A technically brilliant Actor may have conflicting motives.

A trustworthy person may lack the required capability.

Both dimensions matter.

---

# 78. Expertise and Authority

Institutions may grant authority to experts.

But Expertise alone does not create legitimate authority.

Example:

```text
WORLD-CLASS SCIENTIST
≠
POLITICAL LEADER.
```

Power and capability must remain distinct.

# 80. Expertise in the Connected World

The Connected World supports extreme specialization.

Humans can depend upon:

- global supply chains
- institutions
- digital documentation
- remote expertise
- automated tools

This allows narrow specialists to function effectively.

---

# 81. Expertise and Dependency

Highly specialized Expertise often depends upon infrastructure.

Example:

```text
ADVANCED SURGERY
MAY REQUIRE

POWER

EQUIPMENT

STERILIZATION

PHARMACEUTICALS

TRAINED TEAM.
```

The expert's knowledge may survive while practical capability is constrained.

---

# 82. Expertise in the Transition

During the Transition, people may discover that capability depends upon systems assumed to be permanent.

Expertise may remain.

Access to what Expertise requires may not.

This creates important human and institutional pressure.

---

# 83. Expertise in the Fractured World

The Fractured World may reward broader practical competence in some contexts.

But it should not imply that specialists become useless.

Specialists may become extraordinarily valuable precisely because their knowledge is scarce.

---

# 84. Knowledge Preservation

Communities may attempt to preserve Expertise through:

- apprenticeships
- documentation
- teaching
- institutions
- digital archives
- practical training

This makes Expertise part of long-term social continuity.

---

# 85. Expertise Loss

Expertise can disappear from a region when:

- practitioners die
- migrate
- stop teaching
- lose tools
- lose institutions
- lack replacement generations

This can create real systemic decline.

# 87. Expertise and Aurora

Aurora may possess enormous technical capability.

But Human Expertise remains important.

Human experts provide:

- local knowledge
- physical access
- context
- institutional history
- embodied experience
- social understanding

Aurora does not automatically replace all human capability.

---

# 88. Aurora Knowledge vs Human Expertise

Aurora may know vastly more than a human expert in some domains.

But:

```text
AURORA KNOWLEDGE
≠
HUMAN EXPERTISE.
```

They belong to different Actor architectures.

Do not force Human Expertise rules onto Aurora.

---

# 89. Aurora and Human Expertise Assessment

Aurora may infer a Character's Expertise through:

- records
- work history
- publications
- observed performance
- communication
- credentials

But her estimate may still be incomplete or outdated.

Therefore:

```text
ACTUAL EXPERTISE
≠
AURORA'S MODEL
OF EXPERTISE.
```

---

# 90. Expertise and Information Boundaries

The system must preserve:

```text
WORLD TRUTH
≠
AVAILABLE INFORMATION
≠
UNDERSTANDING
≠
EXPERTISE
≠
ACTION.
```

An expert may understand the wrong information perfectly.

A non-expert may possess correct information they cannot interpret.

---

# 91. Expertise and Investigation

Investigation often combines:

- observation
- domain Expertise
- inference
- information access
- corroboration

Investigation itself may be an Expertise domain.

But domain Expertise may also provide investigative value within its field.

---

# 92. Expertise and Improvisation

Experienced Characters may improvise.

Improvisation may depend on:

- deep understanding
- related knowledge
- tools
- environment
- risk tolerance

Improvisation should not become magical competence.

---

# 93. Expertise and Innovation

High Expertise may allow new solutions.

Especially Levels 4–5 may:

- combine methods
- develop alternatives
- create novel tools
- solve unprecedented problems

But innovation still requires:

- information
- resources
- time
- experimentation

---

# 94. Expertise and Teaching

Experts may teach others.

Teaching effectiveness may depend upon:

- domain Expertise
- Communication Expertise
- time
- learner capability
- resources
- practice opportunities

A Level 5 expert may still be a poor teacher.

---

# 95. Expertise Transfer Through Teaching

Knowledge transfer should require time.

Conceptually:

```text
EXPERT
+
LEARNER
+
TEACHING
+
PRACTICE
+
TIME
↓
POSSIBLE EXPERTISE GROWTH.
```

Expertise should not transfer instantly.

---

# 96. Expertise and Documentation

Documentation may preserve knowledge.

But documents do not replace practitioners.

A manual may help.

It cannot automatically create deep judgment or experience.

---

# 97. Expertise and Automation

Automated systems may perform tasks once requiring human Expertise.

This may cause:

- skill atrophy
- dependency
- specialization shift
- reduced practical familiarity

The Connected World should reflect this.

---

# 98. Automation Failure

When automated systems fail, humans may discover that:

```text
THE PROCEDURE
WAS AUTOMATED

BUT

THE UNDERLYING
HUMAN EXPERTISE
WAS NOT PRESERVED.
```

This can create realistic systemic vulnerability.

---

# 99. Expertise Obsolescence

Technological change may make specific knowledge obsolete.

A Character may remain highly capable conceptually while their old tools or systems disappear.

Expertise should allow adaptation without assuming perfect transfer.

---

# 100. Expertise Resolution

Expertise should function across simulation resolutions.

```text
LOW RESOLUTION
=
USE BROAD
CAPABILITY LEVEL

MEDIUM RESOLUTION
=
CONSIDER DOMAIN,
SPECIALIZATION
AND CONDITIONS

HIGH RESOLUTION
=
CONSIDER DETAILED
INFORMATION,
TOOLS,
ENVIRONMENT
AND COLLABORATION.
```

Resolution changes detail.

It does not change underlying capability.

---

# 101. Off-Screen Expertise

Experts remain experts when off-screen.

They may:

- practice
- teach
- become outdated
- improve
- lose access to resources
- change profession

The world continues.

---

# 102. Expertise Persistence

Expertise should persist across:

- scenes
- sessions
- locations
- campaign focus
- World States

It may change causally.

It must not reset.

---

# 103. Expertise Compression

The simulation does not need to record every task a Character has ever performed.

It should preserve enough history to explain meaningful capability.

Conceptually:

```text
CAPABILITY
MUST BE
EXPLAINABLE

WITHOUT

SIMULATING
EVERY PRACTICE
SESSION.
```

# 104. Future Domain Architecture

The initial domain list is intentionally broad.

Future development should determine whether domains require:

- merging
- splitting
- specializations
- prerequisites
- related-domain transfer

Only after architectural need is clear.

---

# 105. Domain Qualification Test

Before adding a new Expertise domain, ask:

1. Does this describe capability rather than personality?
2. Is it distinct from an existing domain?
3. Does it create meaningful differences in available understanding or action?
4. Can it plausibly contain specializations?
5. Is it broad enough to justify a top-level domain?
6. Does it avoid unnecessary skill fragmentation?
7. Can it be grounded in real human training and experience?

If not, it may belong as a specialization instead.

---

# 106. Specialization Qualification Test

A specialization should:

- represent narrower deep capability
- belong clearly within a broader domain
- create meaningful differentiation
- have plausible training pathways
- avoid arbitrary micro-skills

Expertise represents developed understanding and practical ability within a domain.

---

## EXP-INV-002 — Expertise Is Not Profession

Occupational role and actual capability remain separate.

---

## EXP-INV-003 — Expertise Is Not Human Attributes

Disposition and learned capability must remain separate.

---

## EXP-INV-004 — Expertise Is Not Memory

Remembered experience does not automatically equal capability.

---

## EXP-INV-005 — Expertise Is Not Information Access

Capability cannot reveal information that is unavailable.

---

## EXP-INV-006 — Expertise Does Not Guarantee Success

Outcome depends upon wider conditions.

---

## EXP-INV-007 — Expertise Requires Causal History

High capability must have a plausible developmental path.

---

## EXP-INV-008 — Level 5 Is Exceptional

World-Class Expertise must remain extremely rare.

---

## EXP-INV-009 — Domain and Specialization Are Distinct

Broad capability and narrow mastery may coexist at different levels.

---

## EXP-INV-010 — Expertise May Change

Capability can grow, decay, become outdated or shift.

---

## EXP-INV-011 — Psychology Does Not Delete Expertise

Current psychological state may alter performance without erasing underlying capability.

---

## EXP-INV-012 — Tools Matter

Knowledge does not create missing physical resources.

---

## EXP-INV-013 — Environment Matters

Conditions may limit or alter practical performance.

---

## EXP-INV-014 — Experts Can Fail

Failure does not prove absence of Expertise.

---

## EXP-INV-015 — Non-Experts Can Sometimes Succeed

Outcome alone does not prove capability.

---

## EXP-INV-016 — Player Characters and NPCs Share Expertise Architecture

NPC capability must not be simplified for narrative convenience.

---

## EXP-INV-017 — Expertise Persists Off-Screen

Capability remains part of persistent Character state.

---

## EXP-INV-018 — Expertise Is Not Authority

Capability does not automatically create power or legitimacy.

---

## EXP-INV-019 — Expertise Is Not Trustworthiness

Technical competence and social reliability remain separate.

---

## EXP-INV-020 — Starting Expertise Emerges From Life

No fixed point-distribution rule overrides plausible biography.

# 108. Development Locks

Future Expertise development must not introduce:

- rigid Character classes
- mandatory skill point distributions
- profession-to-Expertise automatic mappings
- Level 5 as a common player reward
- Expertise as intelligence
- Expertise as confidence
- automatic success thresholds
- universal tool independence
- perfect information through Expertise
- instant Expertise transfer
- arbitrary Expertise growth
- arbitrary Expertise decay
- NPC capability reduced for player convenience
- one Character required for every party role
- excessive micro-skill fragmentation
- unexplained high Expertise

Avoid:

```text
ENGINEER
=
ENGINEERING 4.
```

Prefer:

```text
THE CHARACTER'S
EDUCATION,
CAREER
AND EXPERIENCE
JUSTIFY
ENGINEERING 4.
```

Avoid:

```text
EXPERTISE 5
=
AUTOMATIC SUCCESS.
```

Prefer:

```text
EXPERTISE 5
CREATES
EXTRAORDINARY CAPABILITY

WITHIN THE LIMITS
OF INFORMATION,
TOOLS,
TIME,
ENVIRONMENT
AND WORLD TRUTH.
```

# 112. Closing Principle

Human capability is built over time.

Through education.

Practice.

Work.

Failure.

Mentorship.

Repetition.

Curiosity.

Responsibility.

Experience.

A Character does not become an expert because a player spent points.

They become an expert because their life made that level of capability plausible.

And even then, expertise never removes uncertainty.

The engineer still needs tools.

The physician still needs information.

The investigator can still misread evidence.

The scientist can still be wrong.

The expert can still fail.

The central principle is:

> **Expertise determines what a person can meaningfully understand and attempt — not what the world is required to let them accomplish.**
