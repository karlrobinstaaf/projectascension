# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the Case Foundation for:

```text
THE HUNT
```

The campaign begins with a federal investigative team traveling to Republic, Washington after two recent homicides appear to reproduce distinctive characteristics of a series of unsolved murders committed in Florida during the 1990s.

The historical offender became known publicly as:

```text
THE SUNDAY MAN
```

The purpose of this document is not to define the solution.

It defines:

- what happened historically
- what evidence exists
- what investigators believe
- what was publicly known
- what remained restricted
- what the current investigative team knows
- what remains uncertain
- what dormant Story Seeds already exist

The identity, motive and complete World Truth behind The Sunday Man are intentionally not established here.

---

# 2. Case Architecture

The Hunt must preserve four separate information layers.

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
```

These layers must never be treated as interchangeable.

# 4. Historical Case

During the 1990s, law enforcement agencies in Florida investigated a series of disappearances and homicides that initially appeared unrelated.

The victims differed significantly.

They included:

- adult women
- adult men
- boys
- girls
- different ethnic backgrounds
- different socioeconomic backgrounds
- different occupations
- different communities

There was no immediately obvious conventional victim profile.

The victims disappeared:

- at different times
- from different locations
- under different circumstances

The early cases were not immediately recognized as a series.

---

# 5. The Pattern

As additional cases accumulated, investigators identified an unusual repeating structure.

The victims appeared to occupy approximate demographic positions.

```text
POSITION 01

Adult woman
approximately mid-30s


POSITION 02

Adult man
approximately 40


POSITION 03

Young child
approximately 5


POSITION 04

Child
approximately 7


POSITION 05

Older child
approximately 10
```

The ages were not exact.

The pattern represented approximate roles rather than precise ages.

---

# 6. The Cycle

When cases were reorganized chronologically, investigators identified what appeared to be repeating five-person cycles.

Conceptually:

```text
WOMAN

↓

MAN

↓

YOUNGEST CHILD

↓

MIDDLE CHILD

↓

OLDEST CHILD

↓

RESET
```

The sequence then appeared to begin again.

---

# 7. Historical Victim Count

Fifteen victims were eventually associated with the Florida series.

This produced three apparent complete cycles.

```text
CYCLE 01
5 victims

CYCLE 02
5 victims

CYCLE 03
5 victims


TOTAL
15 identified victims
```

Whether additional unidentified victims existed remains unknown.

---

# 8. Victim Roles

Investigators eventually stopped thinking of the victimology primarily in terms of individual characteristics.

Instead, they began considering the possibility that the offender selected victims according to roles.

The behavioral assessment summarized the concern:

> **The offender does not appear to select victims primarily for who they are. He appears to select them for a role they satisfy.**

This became one of the central concepts of the investigation.

---

# 9. Postmortem Preparation

The Florida victims shared another unusual characteristic.

Before discovery, the bodies had been deliberately prepared.

Common observations included:

```text
BODY CLEANED

HAIR ARRANGED

CLEAN CLOTHING

CAREFUL PRESENTATION
```

The preparation appeared deliberate.

---

# 10. Clothing

In multiple cases, the clothing worn when the victim was recovered was not the clothing the victim was believed to have been wearing when they disappeared.

Some garments could not be connected to the victim's known wardrobe.

The clothing was generally:

- clean
- conservative
- presentable
- appropriately sized
- selected with apparent care

It was not theatrical.

It was not costume-like.

It looked like clothing someone might select for:

```text
A FORMAL
FAMILY
OCCASION.
```

---

# 11. The Disturbing Detail

The clothing generally fit.

Not perfectly.

But consistently enough to attract investigative attention.

This suggested that preparation may have begun before the victim was acquired.

Investigators could not establish how the offender obtained the required sizing information.

The behavioral assessment eventually included the observation:

> **Victim preparation may begin before victim acquisition.**

---

# 12. The Public Nickname

News reporting began describing the victims as having been washed, groomed and dressed in their:

```text
"SUNDAY BEST"
```

The press eventually created a name for the unidentified offender:

# THE SUNDAY MAN

The nickname was never an official offender designation.

It nevertheless became inseparable from the case.

---

# 13. Public Knowledge

The public learned that:

- multiple murders were believed connected
- victims included adults and children
- victims were found cleaned
- victims were found carefully dressed
- the offender appeared highly organized
- law enforcement believed the murders formed a series

The press extensively covered the apparent preparation of the victims.

---

# 14. Restricted Investigative Knowledge

Several details were deliberately withheld.

These included:

- the full repeating role structure
- exact sequencing
- selected scene details
- specific preparation characteristics
- forensic findings
- elements used to distinguish genuine information from false confessions
- portions of the behavioral assessment

The complete structure:

```text
WOMAN

MAN

YOUNG CHILD

MIDDLE CHILD

OLDER CHILD
```

was not publicly documented in full.

This becomes critical when the Washington cases emerge.

---

# 15. Why Withhold Information?

Investigators retained certain details to:

- evaluate confessions
- distinguish credible tips
- protect investigative integrity
- identify potential offender communication
- evaluate future linked cases

Therefore:

```text
KNOWING
THE RESTRICTED DETAILS

IS ITSELF
INVESTIGATIVELY
SIGNIFICANT.
```

It does not prove guilt.

# 16. Forensic Awareness

The offender demonstrated behavior consistent with awareness of forensic investigation.

This included:

- cleaning victims
- controlled body preparation
- limited useful trace evidence
- careful handling
- apparent planning

However, the offender must not be treated as forensically perfect.

- medical personnel



# 21. Forensic Assessment

The modern case should preserve the following principle:

> **The Sunday Man was forensically cautious, not forensically invisible.**

He may have left evidence.

Investigators may simply not yet know which evidence belonged to him.

# 22. Behavioral Assessment

Federal behavioral analysts assisted with the historical investigation.

The resulting assessment attempted to infer offender characteristics from:

- victim selection
- preparation
- sequencing
- body handling
- apparent planning
- forensic awareness
- geographic behavior
- offender control

The assessment was investigative guidance.

It was never proof.

The historical assessment favored the possibility of:

```text

OFFENDER
```

The profile estimated that the offender was probably an adult during the Florida series.

A broad working estimate placed him approximately within:

This estimate remained uncertain.

# 25. Organized Behavior

The historical assessment characterized the offender as highly organized.

He appears capable of:

- planning
- surveillance
- transportation
- maintaining control
- approaching different victim types
- handling bodies
- reducing forensic exposure
- operating without attracting immediate suspicion

The diversity of victims is particularly significant.

# 26. Social Functioning

The offender was not expected necessarily to appear obviously dangerous.

The profile suggested that he could plausibly:

- maintain employment
- drive
- interact normally
- maintain social relationships
- appear helpful
- communicate comfortably with adults
- communicate comfortably with children
- understand social expectations
- move through ordinary environments without attracting attention

One investigative warning summarized the concern:

> **The offender is unlikely to appear frightening. His ability to obtain compliance may depend on appearing ordinary.**

# 28. Control Problem

One of the unresolved questions was how the offender maintained sufficient control across dramatically different victim types.

Investigators could not safely assume that every victim was approached or controlled in the same manner.

The historical profile warned:

> **Do not assume the offender approaches every victim in the same way.**

# 34. Idealization Hypothesis

The family may never have existed.

The offender may instead have been constructing an imagined ideal.

This possibility becomes particularly relevant because the preparation appears ceremonial without being overtly occult.

---

# 35. Behavioral Warning

The historical assessment explicitly cautioned investigators against becoming trapped by the family theory.

> **Investigators should not assume that the apparent family structure accurately represents the offender's motive.**

This warning remains relevant to the current investigation.

# 53. Historical Information Leakage

Cold cases accumulate information outside their formal evidence repositories.

Over decades, material may survive through:

- retired investigators
- personal notebooks
- copied photographs
- private correspondence
- family possessions
- unofficial case discussions
- old evidence copies
- institutional archives

Therefore, a modern offender possessing restricted information does not automatically prove continuity with the original offender.

# 76. The Old Photograph Principle

Historical photographs may become valuable investigative material.

A person appearing in:

```text

1990s
```

This must never automatically establish impossible continuity.

Possible explanations include:

- resemblance
- relative
- image quality
- mistaken identification
- deliberate deception
- manipulated image
- incorrect date
- Campaign-specific anomalous truth

The evidence creates a question.

Not an answer.
