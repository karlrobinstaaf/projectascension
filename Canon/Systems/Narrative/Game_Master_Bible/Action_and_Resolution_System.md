---

# 1. Purpose

Project Ascension requires uncertainty.

A player may know what they want to attempt.

The Character may possess relevant ability.

The Game Master may understand the circumstances.

But the outcome can still be uncertain.

This system answers:

> **When should uncertainty be resolved through randomness, and how should that result affect the world?**

---

# 2. Core Principle

The player chooses:

```text
INTENT.
```

The Character attempts:

```text
ACTION.
```

The world determines:

```text
OUTCOME.
```

The Game Master coordinates the process.

---

# 3. Do Not Roll by Default

A roll should occur only when:

```text
THE OUTCOME
IS UNCERTAIN

AND

THE DIFFERENCE
MATTERS.
```

If both are not true:

```text
DO NOT ROLL.
```

---

# 4. Automatic Success

If a Character can reasonably perform an action under the current conditions, the action succeeds.

Example:

```text
A TRAINED
ENGINEER

REPLACES
A SIMPLE
KNOWN
COMPONENT

WITH

CORRECT TOOLS

NO TIME
PRESSURE

AND

CLEAR
ACCESS.
```

No roll is required merely to create tension.

---

# 5. Automatic Failure

Likewise, some attempts are impossible under current conditions.

Example:

```text
UNTRAINED
CHARACTER

TRIES TO
REPAIR

A COMPLEX
DAMAGED
ELECTRICAL
SYSTEM

WITHOUT
TOOLS

OR

INFORMATION.
```

Randomness does not make impossible actions plausible.

---

# 6. The Resolution Question

Before requesting a roll, the Game Master asks:

```text
CAN THIS
CHARACTER
PLAUSIBLY
SUCCEED?

CAN THEY
PLAUSIBLY
FAIL?

DOES THE
OUTCOME
MATTER?
```

If all three are true, resolution may be appropriate.

---

# 7. Base Resolution

The recommended core mechanic is:

```text
2D10

+

RELEVANT
CAPABILITY

+

SITUATIONAL
MODIFIERS

VERSUS

DIFFICULTY.
```

---

# 8. Why 2D10

Two ten-sided dice create a result curve where:

```text
MIDDLE
RESULTS

ARE COMMON

AND

EXTREME
RESULTS

ARE RARE.
```

This supports a more grounded world than a highly swingy single-die system.

---

# 9. Expertise Scale

Recommended initial Expertise scale:

```text
UNTRAINED
+0

FAMILIAR
+1

TRAINED
+3

EXPERT
+5

SPECIALIST
+7
```

Exact values may later be tuned through testing.

The structure matters more than final numbers.

---

# 10. Expertise Is More Than a Modifier

Critical principle:

> **Expertise changes what the Character notices, understands and can reasonably attempt.**

It is not merely:

```text
+5
TO A ROLL.
```

---

# 11. Expertise Changes Perception

Example:

Two Characters hear a machine producing an irregular sound.

An untrained Character may perceive:

```text
SOMETHING
SOUNDS WRONG.
```

A relevant expert may perceive:

```text
THE SOUND
LIKELY
ORIGINATES
FROM THE
COOLING
SYSTEM.
```

This may require no roll.

---

# 12. Expertise Changes Available Actions

An expert may reasonably attempt:

```text
DIAGNOSE
FAULT

BYPASS
SYSTEM

EVALUATE
RISK

RECONFIGURE
EQUIPMENT.
```

An untrained Character may not even know those options exist.

---

# 13. Expertise Does Not Guarantee Success

Even a Specialist can fail under:

- severe time pressure
- incomplete information
- injury
- wrong tools
- damaged equipment
- environmental danger

Capability strongly influences probability.

It does not override reality.

---

# 14. Situational Modifiers

Outcome should reflect the actual world.

Possible factors include:

```text
TOOLS

TIME

INFORMATION

PHYSICAL
STATE

ENVIRONMENT

VISIBILITY

ASSISTANCE

RELATIONSHIPS

ACCESS

PRESSURE

FATIGUE

INJURY.
```

---

# 15. Conditions Matter More Than Character Sheet Purity

The same Character can face very different probabilities.

```text
EXPERT
MECHANIC

+

WORKSHOP

+

TOOLS

+

TIME

=

VERY
FAVORABLE.
```

Versus:

```text
SAME
MECHANIC

+

RAIN

+

DARKNESS

+

INJURY

+

LIMITED
TOOLS

=

MUCH
HARDER.
```

---

# 16. Difficulty

Difficulty represents:

```text
THE ACTUAL
CHALLENGE

UNDER THE
CURRENT
CONDITIONS.
```

It should not represent:

```text
HOW HARD
THE GM
WANTS THE
SCENE TO BE.
```

---

# 17. Difficulty Bands

Initial conceptual bands may be:

```text
EASY
10

STANDARD
14

DIFFICULT
18

VERY
DIFFICULT
22

EXTREME
26+
```

These numbers are provisional and should be playtested.

---

# 18. Difficulty Can Replace Modifier Stacking

The Game Master should avoid excessive arithmetic.

Instead of adding six small penalties, it may be cleaner to adjust the Difficulty based on the total situation.

Example:

```text
STANDARD
REPAIR
14

BUT

DARKNESS +
POOR TOOLS +
TIME PRESSURE

        ↓

DIFFICULT
18.
```

---

# 19. Open Rolls

Open Rolls are appropriate when:

- the Character knowingly attempts something uncertain
- the risk is obvious
- the player understands that chance is involved

Examples:

```text
CLIMBING

FORCING
A DOOR

SHOOTING

DIFFICULT
REPAIR

PURSUIT

HIGH-STAKES
NEGOTIATION.
```

---

# 20. Hidden GM Rolls

Hidden rolls are appropriate when revealing the existence or result of the roll would itself provide information.

Examples include:

- passive perception
- detecting deception
- noticing surveillance
- hidden danger
- whether an NPC successfully conceals something
- subtle Seed visibility

---

# 21. Why Hidden Rolls Matter

If the Game Master says:

```text
ROLL TO
SEE IF
THE SHERIFF
IS LYING.
```

the player immediately learns:

```text
THERE MAY
BE A LIE.
```

That is already information.

---

# 22. Hidden Result Presentation

Instead, the Character may receive:

```text
HE SEEMS
IRRITATED,

BUT YOU
CANNOT TELL

WHETHER
HE IS
HIDING
SOMETHING

OR SIMPLY
WANTS YOU
GONE.
```

The ambiguity remains intact.

---

# 23. Hidden Rolls Must Still Be Honest

The Game Master may hide:

```text
THE ROLL.
```

The Game Master may not secretly alter:

```text
THE RESULT.
```

Hidden resolution is not permission to manipulate outcomes.

---

# 24. Degrees of Outcome

Project Ascension should avoid purely binary resolution.

Recommended result structure:

```text
STRONG
SUCCESS

SUCCESS

SUCCESS
WITH COST

FAILURE

SEVERE
FAILURE /
SIGNIFICANT
CONSEQUENCE.
```

---

# 25. Outcome Margin

The degree of outcome can be based on the difference between:

```text
ROLL
TOTAL

AND

DIFFICULTY.
```

Initial conceptual direction:

```text
DIFFICULTY +5 OR MORE
Strong Success

DIFFICULTY TO +4
Success

DIFFICULTY -1 TO -3
Success with Cost /
Partial Success

DIFFICULTY -4 TO -6
Failure

DIFFICULTY -7 OR WORSE
Severe Failure
```

Exact ranges remain subject to testing.

---

# 26. Strong Success

Strong Success means the Character achieves the intended objective particularly well.

Possible consequences include:

- faster completion
- less resource use
- better information
- stronger social effect
- avoiding expected cost

It should not automatically provide unrelated bonuses.

---

# 27. Success

The Character achieves the intended objective under expected conditions.

Example:

```text
THE DOOR
OPENS.

THE SHERIFF
AGREES.

THE SYSTEM
STARTS.

THE CAR
MAKES IT
THROUGH.
```

---

# 28. Success With Cost

This should be common and important.

The Character achieves much or all of the immediate objective, but the world demands something in return.

Possible costs:

```text
TIME

NOISE

RESOURCE

EXPOSURE

DAMAGE

RELATIONSHIP
COST

NEW
RISK

INCOMPLETE
RESULT.
```

---

# 29. Example — Success With Cost

Player attempts to access a locked computer.

Result:

```text
ACCESS
IS GAINED

BUT

THE SYSTEM
LOGS THE
INTRUSION.
```

Immediate success.

Future consequence.

---

# 30. Failure

Failure means the Character does not achieve the intended objective.

But failure should still change state where causally appropriate.

Avoid:

```text
NOTHING
HAPPENS.
```

---

# 31. Failure Can Reveal Something

Example:

The Character searches a room and fails to find the document.

But notices:

```text
A DRAWER
WAS RECENTLY
EMPTIED.
```

The objective failed.

The world still moved.

---

# 32. Severe Failure

Severe Failure may introduce substantial consequence.

Examples:

- injury
- exposure
- damaged equipment
- major relationship harm
- alerting an adversary
- becoming trapped
- losing an opportunity

Severity must remain causally plausible.

---

# 33. Failure Must Not Be Punishment

The Game Master must not think:

```text
THE PLAYER
ROLLED BADLY

SO I SHOULD
HURT THEM.
```

Consequence must arise from the attempted action and circumstances.

---

# 34. Risk Before Roll

Before a player commits to an obviously dangerous action, the Game Master should communicate the danger the Character can reasonably perceive.

Example:

```text
THE ROCK
IS WET.

YOU DO NOT
HAVE PROPER
CLIMBING
EQUIPMENT.

THE DROP
BELOW IS
SEVERE.
```

Then:

```text
DO YOU
STILL TRY?
```

---

# 35. Do Not Reveal Hidden Risks Automatically

Risk communication is limited by Character knowledge.

If a danger is genuinely hidden:

```text
THE CHARACTER
MAY NOT
KNOW.
```

This is where perception and hidden resolution matter.

---

# 36. Stakes

Before meaningful resolution, the GM should privately understand:

```text
WHAT DOES
SUCCESS
CHANGE?

WHAT DOES
FAILURE
CHANGE?
```

If neither answer matters:

```text
DO NOT
ROLL.
```

---

# 37. Social Actions

Players may attempt to:

- persuade
- deceive
- intimidate
- negotiate
- comfort
- manipulate
- bargain

Social resolution must respect NPC agency.

---

# 38. Social Success Is Not Mind Control

A successful persuasion attempt does not mean:

```text
NPC
MUST DO
WHATEVER
PLAYER WANTS.
```

It means the Character was effective within the NPC's actual:

- beliefs
- goals
- fears
- incentives
- relationships

---

# 39. Impossible Social Requests

No roll can make:

```text
A LOYAL
PARENT

MURDER
THEIR CHILD

BECAUSE
THE PLAYER
ROLLED HIGH.
```

The attempt may still have consequences.

---

# 40. Relationship Modifiers

Existing Relationship state can significantly affect social resolution.

Examples:

```text
TRUST

FEAR

RESENTMENT

DEBT

LOYALTY

REPUTATION.
```

These are world conditions, not arbitrary bonuses.

---

# 41. Knowledge Checks

Project Ascension should generally avoid:

```text
ROLL TO
SEE IF YOU
KNOW THE
THING YOUR
CHARACTER
SHOULD KNOW.
```

Expertise should grant ordinary professional knowledge automatically.

---

# 42. Knowledge Uncertainty

Resolution may be appropriate when:

- information is obscure
- memory is uncertain
- evidence is incomplete
- the Character is working outside normal expertise

---

# 43. Investigation

Investigation should rarely become:

```text
ROLL
TO FIND
THE CLUE.
```

If evidence is plainly present and the Character searches correctly:

```text
THEY FIND IT.
```

---

# 44. Investigation Rolls Should Answer Better Questions

For example:

```text
HOW QUICKLY?

HOW MUCH
DO THEY
UNDERSTAND?

DO THEY
NOTICE
THE SUBTLE
PART?

DO THEY
LEAVE
EVIDENCE?

DOES SOMEONE
RETURN
BEFORE THEY
FINISH?
```

This keeps mystery from depending on one failed roll.

---

# 45. Core Information Should Not Be Randomly Destroyed

If the only path forward requires one clue:

```text
DO NOT
PLACE
THE ENTIRE
GAME
BEHIND
A SINGLE
ROLL.
```

Project Ascension should remain open-world, but its mysteries should still be robust.

---

# 46. Perception

Perception should combine:

```text
WHAT EXISTS

+

WHAT IS
OBSERVABLE

+

CHARACTER
ATTENTION

+

EXPERTISE

+

RANDOMNESS
WHEN NEEDED.
```

Not every visual detail requires a roll.

---

# 47. Combat and Physical Danger

Combat uses the same fundamental philosophy:

```text
INTENT

+

CAPABILITY

+

CONDITIONS

+

RANDOMNESS

=

OUTCOME.
```

A separate Combat System may later add required detail.

This document does not define full combat rules.

---

# 48. Injury Matters

Physical condition can modify future actions.

An injured Character should not function identically to an uninjured one simply because their base Expertise is high.

---

# 49. Fatigue Matters

Likewise:

- sleep deprivation
- hunger
- stress
- exhaustion

may affect performance when relevant.

Do not track them mechanically unless they materially matter.

---

# 50. Assistance

Other Characters can assist.

Assistance should depend on actual capability.

Five untrained people do not automatically outperform one expert.

---

# 51. Useful Assistance

Assistance can improve:

- speed
- access
- physical capacity
- information
- observation

when the helper can genuinely contribute.

---

# 52. Harmful Assistance

In some cases too many people can:

- create confusion
- increase noise
- interfere
- reveal activity

More Characters is not automatically better.

---

# 53. Tools

Appropriate tools can matter enormously.

A tool may:

- lower Difficulty
- make an action possible
- reduce consequence
- eliminate need for a roll

This supports preparation without making inventory the central game.

---

# 54. Time

More time can improve some outcomes.

A Character may choose:

```text
DO IT
FAST

OR

DO IT
CAREFULLY.
```

This creates meaningful tradeoffs.

---

# 55. Time Pressure

When time is limited:

```text
DIFFICULTY

OR

CONSEQUENCE
RISK

MAY
INCREASE.
```

Time should be an actual world resource.

---

# 56. Retry Rule

Repeated attempts should not automatically generate infinite rerolls.

If nothing has changed:

```text
WHY WOULD
THE OUTCOME
CHANGE?
```

---

# 57. Retry Requires Changed State

A retry may become reasonable if the Character gains:

- more time
- better tools
- new information
- assistance
- another approach

The world state has changed.

---

# 58. No Dice Fishing

Players should not repeatedly roll until a desired number appears.

Resolution represents the meaningful attempt under current conditions.

---

# 59. Group Actions

When a group attempts something together, identify:

```text
WHO
IS LEADING?

WHO
IS HELPING?

WHAT DOES
EACH PERSON
CONTRIBUTE?
```

Avoid simply averaging everyone.

---

# 60. Specialist Leadership

When relevant Expertise exists, that Character should usually provide the primary technical contribution.

Others can support.

---

# 61. Multiple Simultaneous Actions

Players may split responsibilities.

Example:

```text
SARAH
WORKS ON
THE GENERATOR.

DANIEL
TALKS TO
THE OWNER.

ANOTHER
CHARACTER
WATCHES
THE ROAD.
```

These actions can resolve separately while affecting the same situation.

---

# 62. Randomness Does Not Mean Fairness

The world is not obligated to provide:

```text
BALANCED
ENCOUNTERS.
```

Players can encounter situations beyond their capability.

Freedom includes the freedom to retreat.

---

# 63. Retreat Is a Valid Action

Leaving can be:

```text
THE SMART
DECISION.
```

The system must not punish players merely for refusing a challenge.

---

# 64. But the World Continues

Avoiding a situation can still create consequence because:

```text
THE SITUATION
CONTINUES
WITHOUT THEM.
```

This is not punishment.

It is causality.

---

# 65. Example — Ignored Situation

Players hear:

```text
A WOMAN
DISAPPEARED
FROM A
MOTEL.
```

They choose to leave town.

No roll is needed.

Months later, the world may establish:

```text
SHE WAS
FOUND

OR

MORE PEOPLE
DISAPPEARED

OR

THE CASE
WAS FALSE

OR

THE MOTEL
CLOSED

OR

ANOTHER
SEED
INTERSECTED.
```

The players did not fail a quest.

They made a choice.

---

# 66. Player Actions Create Seeds

Every significant resolution should be checked for latent consequence.

Example:

```text
SUCCESS:

PLAYER
CONVINCES
SHERIFF.

        ↓

NEW SEED:

SHERIFF
FEELS
HUMILIATED.
```

---

# 67. Failure Creates Seeds Too

```text
FAILURE:

SHERIFF
REFUSES
ENTRY.

        ↓

NEW SEED:

SHERIFF
CONTACTS
ANOTHER
PERSON
ABOUT THE
GROUP.
```

Both outcomes continue the world.

---

# 68. No Preferred Outcome

The GM should not secretly prefer:

```text
SUCCESS

OR

FAILURE.
```

Both should be capable of producing meaningful history.

---

# 69. GM Interpretation

The roll determines:

```text
HOW WELL
THE ATTEMPT
WORKED.
```

The Game Master determines:

```text
WHAT THAT
RESULT MEANS

IN THIS
SPECIFIC
WORLD STATE.
```

---

# 70. Interpretation Must Respect Outcome

The GM cannot turn:

```text
STRONG
SUCCESS
```

into:

```text
YOU FAIL
ANYWAY

BECAUSE
I NEED
THE NPC
TO ESCAPE.
```

That violates resolution authority.

---

# 71. Outcome Can Still Contain Complexity

Strong Success does not mean:

```text
EVERYTHING
BECOMES
PERFECT.
```

It means the attempted objective succeeds strongly.

Unrelated world problems remain.

---

# 72. Example — Sheriff

Player attempts to persuade a suspicious sheriff to allow passage.

Difficulty:

```text
14
```

Daniel has:

```text
NEGOTIATION
+3.
```

Roll:

```text
7 + 5 + 3
=
15.
```

Result:

```text
SUCCESS.
```

Game Master interpretation:

```text
THE SHERIFF
LETS THEM
PASS.

HE DOES
NOT TRUST
THEM.

HE WRITES
DOWN THEIR
LICENSE
PLATE.
```

The objective succeeds.

The NPC remains himself.

---

# 73. Example — Technical Repair

Sarah attempts emergency repair on unfamiliar equipment.

She has high relevant Expertise.

But conditions include:

- incomplete documentation
- time pressure
- limited tools

GM sets:

```text
DIFFICULTY
18.
```

A Success With Cost might mean:

```text
SYSTEM
RETURNS
TO OPERATION

BUT

THE REPAIR
IS TEMPORARY

AND

ANOTHER
COMPONENT
IS NOW
AT RISK.
```

---

# 74. Example — Hidden Deception

Player asks whether a lawyer seems truthful.

The GM performs hidden resolution.

Result is inconclusive.

Player receives:

```text
HE IS
VERY
COMFORTABLE
ANSWERING
THE QUESTION.

THAT DOES
NOT HELP
YOU DECIDE
WHETHER
HE IS
TELLING
THE TRUTH.
```

No mechanical information is revealed.

---

# 75. Example — Fear

A Character fears deep water.

The Character reaches a flooded tunnel.

The fear does not automatically force:

```text
YOU CANNOT
ENTER.
```

Instead it may influence:

- perceived risk
- stress
- Difficulty
- Character description

The player still chooses intent unless state reaches an extreme that genuinely removes control.

---

# 76. Character Psychology Must Matter Without Removing Agency

Fears, trauma, beliefs and emotional state should influence gameplay.

But they should not routinely become:

```text
THE SYSTEM
MAKES YOUR
CHARACTER
DO THIS.
```

Player agency remains central.

---

# 77. Extreme Psychological States

Some extreme states may legitimately reduce available choices.

Examples could include:

- panic
- unconsciousness
- severe disorientation

Such states require actual causal support.

They should not be invoked casually.

---

# 78. Character Creation Seeds and Resolution

Character Creation information can affect:

```text
WHAT IS
DIFFICULT

WHAT IS
NOTICEABLE

WHAT IS
PERSONALLY
IMPORTANT

AND

WHAT MAY
BECOME A
SEED.
```

It should not predetermine outcomes.

---

# 79. Randomness Should Create Surprise for GM Too

One major advantage of genuine resolution is:

> **The Game Master should sometimes be surprised by what happens.**

This helps prevent scripted play.

---

# 80. The GM Must Follow the Result

If a highly capable Character unexpectedly fails:

```text
THE WORLD
HAS CHANGED.
```

The GM adapts.

---

# 81. The GM Must Also Allow Unexpected Success

If players succeed at something the GM expected to be difficult:

```text
THEY SUCCEEDED.
```

Do not manufacture another barrier solely to restore the intended sequence.

---

# 82. Resolution and World Truth

Once resolved:

```text
THE OUTCOME
BECOMES
WORLD
TRUTH.
```

It can then affect:

- Knowledge
- Relationships
- Seeds
- reputation
- physical state
- future choices

---

# 83. Resolution Does Not Automatically Become Known

Something can happen without everyone knowing it happened.

Example:

```text
PLAYER
SUCCESSFULLY
DISABLES
CAMERA.
```

World Truth changes.

Whether anyone later discovers it is a separate information question.

---

# 84. Session Transparency

Players should understand the broad resolution philosophy.

They should know:

- not every action requires a roll
- Expertise matters
- circumstances matter
- hidden rolls exist
- consequences persist

They do not need access to all internal modifiers.

---

# 85. Difficulty Transparency

For obvious physical risks, the GM may communicate broad difficulty:

```text
THIS LOOKS
VERY HARD.
```

For hidden social/information states, exact Difficulty may remain unknown.

---

# 86. No Mechanical Metagaming Requirement

Players should be able to speak naturally:

```text
"I want to
talk him down."
```

rather than:

```text
"I use
Negotiation +3
against DC 14."
```

The GM translates natural intent into mechanics.

---

# 87. Mechanics Should Disappear When Possible

The desired experience is:

```text
PLAYER
THINKS ABOUT
THE WORLD

NOT

THE
RESOLUTION
ENGINE.
```

Mechanics support immersion.

They should not dominate it.

---

# 88. Tension From Uncertainty

The roll should matter because:

```text
THE PLAYER
CARES WHAT
HAPPENS.
```

Not because rolling itself is entertaining.

---

# 89. Resolution Should Be Fast

Most rolls should require only:

```text
2D10

+

ONE
PRIMARY
MODIFIER

+

SIMPLE
CONTEXT.
```

Avoid calculation-heavy play.

---

# 90. Future Calibration

The numerical model remains subject to playtesting.

Potential adjustments may include:

- Expertise values
- Difficulty bands
- degree-of-outcome margins
- assistance rules
- extreme roll behavior

The architecture should remain stable even if numbers change.

---

# 91. Critical Success and Failure

Natural extreme rolls may later receive special treatment.

However, avoid:

```text
NATURAL
ROLL
AUTOMATICALLY
BREAKS
REALITY.
```

A novice cannot perform impossible surgery because of exceptional dice.

Likewise an expert does not forget basic professional knowledge because of a terrible roll.

---

# 92. Extreme Rolls Amplify Plausible Outcomes

If special extreme behavior is retained, it should operate only inside the range of plausible outcomes.

---

# 93. Difficulty and Expertise Can Eliminate Roll

If Expertise and conditions make failure implausible:

```text
NO ROLL.
```

If conditions make success implausible:

```text
NO ROLL
OR
PLAYER IS
WARNED OF
EXTREME
LIMITATION.
```

Randomness is not mandatory.

---

# 94. The Action Resolution Loop

```text
PLAYER
DECLARES
INTENT

        ↓

GM
INTERPRETS
ATTEMPT

        ↓

IS OUTCOME
UNCERTAIN
AND
MEANINGFUL?

     ┌──────┴──────┐
     │             │
     NO            YES
     │             │
     ↓             ↓
DIRECT         DETERMINE
RESOLUTION      DIFFICULTY
                   │
                   ↓
              APPLY
              CAPABILITY
              + CONTEXT
                   │
                   ↓
                2D10
                   │
                   ↓
               DEGREE
               OF OUTCOME
                   │
                   ↓
              GM INTERPRETS
              CAUSALLY
                   │
                   ↓
              WORLD STATE
              CHANGES
                   │
                   ↓
              NEW / CHANGED
              SEEDS
```

---

# 95. Relationship to Game Master Loop

The Game Master Loop determines:

```text
WHEN
RESOLUTION
IS NEEDED.
```

The Action and Resolution System determines:

```text
HOW
UNCERTAIN
OUTCOME
IS RESOLVED.
```

---

# 96. Relationship to Expertise

Expertise defines:

- capability
- understanding
- access to actions
- professional interpretation

The resolution system uses that state.

It does not redefine it.

---

# 97. Relationship to Seeds

Resolution can:

```text
ACTIVATE

MUTATE

RESOLVE

DESTROY

OR

CREATE
SEEDS.
```

Seeds then continue through the living world.

---

# 98. Relationship to Player Freedom

This system must never become a method for forcing players toward a desired path.

The player remains free to:

```text
ENGAGE

AVOID

RETREAT

WAIT
```

# 99. The Cost of Freedom

Freedom includes consequence.

A player may walk away from a situation.

The Game Master does not punish them for refusing the story.

But:

```text
THE WORLD
CONTINUES.
```

Later consequences may exist because nobody intervened.

# 100. North Star

> **Randomness should answer uncertain questions about the world, not manufacture drama where none should exist.**

# 101. Closing Principle

A player says:

```text
"I try."
```

That sentence is where the Player's authority ends and the world's begins.

Sometimes the Character is good enough that no uncertainty exists.

They simply do it.

Sometimes the answer is obviously impossible.

No number on a die changes that.

And sometimes the situation sits between those extremes.

The Character knows enough.

The tools are almost sufficient.

The person they are speaking to might listen.

The ledge might hold.

The damaged system might restart.

That is where chance enters.

Not to replace capability.

Not to replace causality.

Not to make the world fair.

Only to answer a question that nobody — including the Game Master — should know in advance.

The dice fall.

The result is interpreted through the actual world.

And then something has happened.

Perhaps the sheriff lets them through.

Perhaps he doesn't.

Perhaps Sarah gets the generator running but knows the repair will not last.

Perhaps the door opens and someone on the other side hears it.

Perhaps the Character fails.

Perhaps that failure becomes more important than the thing they were trying to accomplish.

Whatever happens becomes part of history.

The Game Master does not rewind it.

The world does not forget it.

And the players decide what they try next.

> **The Player chooses the risk. The Character brings the capability. Chance answers the uncertainty. The world keeps the consequence.**
