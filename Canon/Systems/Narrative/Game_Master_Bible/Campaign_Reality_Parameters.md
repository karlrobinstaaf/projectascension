**Primary Function:** Define the configurable reality, violence and difficulty boundaries within which a Project Ascension campaign may develop.

---

# 1. Purpose

Project Ascension is built upon a grounded, persistent and causally simulated world.

However, not every campaign must operate within exactly the same boundaries of reality.

One campaign may be an almost documentary simulation of infrastructure failure, social fragmentation and human survival.

Another may begin in exactly the same world but gradually reveal phenomena that cannot be explained by conventional science.

Another may become a severe survival-horror experience involving unknown organisms, anomalous phenomena, non-human intelligence or other extraordinary threats.

The underlying simulation architecture does not need to change between these campaigns.

Instead, every campaign defines a **Reality Envelope** before play begins.

The Reality Envelope establishes what the world is permitted to contain and how unforgiving the resulting experience may become.

The primary campaign parameters are:

```text
WEIRDNESS
VIOLENCE
DIFFICULTY
```

These parameters influence the Game Master, Seed generation and campaign development without replacing normal world causality.

---

# 2. Core Principle

> **Campaign Parameters define possibility, not destiny.**

A high Weirdness setting does not require monsters.

A high Violence setting does not require constant combat.

A high Difficulty setting does not require Characters to fail.

The parameters establish what the Game Master is **allowed to introduce or permit to emerge**.

They do not dictate what must happen.

---

# 3. The Reality Envelope

Every campaign exists inside a configurable Reality Envelope.

```text
PROJECT ASCENSION CANON
        ↓
WORLD SIMULATION
        ↓
CAMPAIGN REALITY ENVELOPE
        ↓
SEEDS
        ↓
GAME MASTER
        ↓
PLAYER INTERACTION
        ↓
EMERGENT HISTORY
```

Project Ascension Canon establishes the common foundation.

The Reality Envelope determines how far a specific campaign may depart from ordinary real-world expectations.

This allows multiple campaigns to share:

- geography
- Characters
- world simulation
- information architecture
- relationships
- resources
- institutions
- physical causality
- Game Master architecture

while producing radically different experiences.

---

# 4. Primary Campaign Parameters

The initial architecture defines three independent parameters:

| Parameter | Controls |
|---|---|
| **Weirdness** | What kinds of phenomena may genuinely exist |
| **Violence** | How present and severe interpersonal or environmental violence may become |
| **Difficulty** | How forgiving the world is toward mistakes, scarcity, injury and failure |

Each parameter uses a scale from:

```text
1 → 10
```

The parameters are independent.

Example:

```text
WEIRDNESS: 8
VIOLENCE: 3
DIFFICULTY: 5
```

could produce a deeply strange campaign with relatively little violence.

Another campaign:

```text
WEIRDNESS: 1
VIOLENCE: 8
DIFFICULTY: 9
```

could contain nothing supernatural or anomalous while remaining extremely dangerous.

---

# 5. Weirdness

Weirdness defines the campaign's **permitted departure from conventional reality**.

It determines what may ultimately be true behind unexplained events.

It does not determine how frequently strange events occur.

---

## 5.1 Weirdness Scale

### Level 1 — Grounded Reality

Strict real-world causality.

All events must ultimately have plausible natural, technological, psychological, social or human explanations.

Examples may include:

- infrastructure failure
- crime
- misinformation
- psychological phenomena
- unusual weather
- human deception
- coincidence
- technological malfunction
- conspiracy
- institutional failure

No genuinely supernatural or impossible phenomena exist.

---

### Level 2 — Realistic

Reality remains conventional.

Highly unusual circumstances and coincidences may occur, but explanations remain natural.

The Game Master may make situations feel strange without breaking real-world causality.

---

### Level 3 — Unsettling

Uncanny experiences become more common.

Players may encounter:

- unexplained sounds
- strange human behavior
- disturbing coincidences
- apparently impossible observations
- unusual patterns
- conflicting evidence

Natural explanations still dominate.

Some mysteries may remain unresolved indefinitely.

---

### Level 4 — Uncertain

The boundary between unusual reality and genuine anomaly becomes less certain.

Rare events may exist at the extreme edge of scientific or technological plausibility.

Players should no longer feel completely confident that every mystery has an ordinary explanation.

---

### Level 5 — Strange

The campaign may contain rare phenomena that cannot currently be adequately explained.

Possible sources include:

- experimental technology
- unexpected biological phenomena
- extreme AI behavior
- unknown environmental effects
- poorly understood scientific events

The world remains recognizably Project Ascension.

---

### Level 6 — Unknown

Genuinely anomalous phenomena may exist.

Conventional explanations remain common, but they are no longer guaranteed.

Players may encounter events for which existing human knowledge provides no satisfactory explanation.

---

### Level 7 — Disturbing

Unknown phenomena can become significant campaign forces.

Possible elements may include:

- unknown organisms
- anomalous human conditions
- apparently impossible events
- extreme technological phenomena
- unexplained entities
- reality-defying observations

Such phenomena should still interact consistently with the simulated world once their underlying World Truth has been established.

---

### Level 8 — Nightmarish

The campaign may openly enter speculative horror or science-fiction territory.

Possible elements may include:

- infected or radically altered humans
- unknown creatures
- severe biological anomalies
- extreme experimentation
- non-human intelligence
- persistent anomalous phenomena

Ordinary reality remains present around them.

---

### Level 9 — Extreme

Extraordinary phenomena may become major forces within the world.

Multiple forms of anomalous causality may coexist.

Humanity may face threats or discoveries fundamentally outside its previous understanding.

---

### Level 10 — Unbound

The Game Master is no longer constrained to conventional real-world causality.

Potential elements may include:

- zombies
- extraterrestrial organisms
- non-human civilizations
- monsters
- extreme biological transformation
- reality anomalies
- impossible technology
- cosmic phenomena

This does not mean such elements must exist.

It means they **may**.

---

# 6. The Weirdness Rule

The most important Weirdness principle is:

> **Weirdness defines what may exist, not what must appear.**

A Weirdness 10 campaign may remain completely realistic for hours, sessions or entire regions.

A strange event may still have a mundane explanation.

High Weirdness must never turn every mystery into an anomaly.

---

# 7. Uncertainty Must Survive Weirdness

Players knowing the Weirdness level must not automatically reveal the nature of individual events.

Consider:

```text
A CHILD IS HEARD CRYING
IN THE FOREST AT NIGHT
```

Possible World Truths might include:

```text
LOST CHILD

DISTANT HUMAN VOICE

ANIMAL VOCALIZATION

RECORDED SOUND

CRIMINAL DECEPTION

ACOUSTIC DISTORTION

UNKNOWN ORGANISM

ANOMALOUS PHENOMENON
```

Which explanations are permitted depends partly on the campaign's Reality Envelope.

But once World Truth has been established, it cannot be changed merely because the players developed a compelling theory.

---

# 8. Weirdness and Seeds

Weirdness influences the kinds of Seeds the Game Master may create or activate.

At low Weirdness:

```text
UNCANNY EVENT
↓
PLAYER INTERPRETATION
↓
INVESTIGATION
↓
NATURAL WORLD TRUTH
```

At higher Weirdness:

```text
UNCANNY EVENT
↓
PLAYER INTERPRETATION
↓
INVESTIGATION
↓
UNKNOWN
```

or:

```text
UNCANNY EVENT
↓
PLAYER INTERPRETATION
↓
INVESTIGATION
↓
GENUINELY ANOMALOUS WORLD TRUTH
```

The Game Master must preserve uncertainty.

High Weirdness does not eliminate mundane Seeds.

---

# 9. The Rational Horror Principle

Even when Weirdness permits genuinely anomalous phenomena, natural explanations remain important.

Sometimes:

```text
THE PLAYERS FEAR A MONSTER
```

but discover:

```text
THERE WAS NO MONSTER.
```

Instead, another human being deliberately created the situation.

The existing Project Ascension principle remains valid:

> **Sometimes the rational explanation should make the players wish it had been a ghost.**

At higher Weirdness, however, the inverse also becomes possible.

Sometimes the players assume a rational explanation exists.

And they are wrong.

---

# 10. Violence

Violence determines how frequently severe physical conflict may emerge and how dangerous violent encounters may become.

Violence does not determine whether the world is morally dark.

A low-Violence campaign may contain manipulation, exploitation, betrayal and psychological horror.

A high-Violence campaign permits physical brutality to become substantially more prominent.

---

# 11. Violence Scale

### Level 1 — Minimal

Serious violence is extremely rare.

Most conflicts remain social, political, economic or psychological.

---

### Level 2 — Restrained

Physical confrontations may occur, but severe injury is uncommon.

Violence is exceptional.

---

### Level 3 — Occasional

Fights, threats and isolated violent crime may occur.

Weapons exist but are not automatically used.

---

### Level 4 — Dangerous

Physical conflict is a meaningful possibility.

Serious injury can occur.

Characters should treat weapons and violent people seriously.

---

### Level 5 — Standard

Violence is a credible and recurring danger.

Characters may be seriously injured or killed.

Combat remains consequential rather than routine.

---

### Level 6 — Severe

Violent encounters become more common in unstable environments.

Armed groups and escalating confrontations become increasingly plausible.

---

### Level 7 — Brutal

Severe violence may regularly occur in dangerous regions.

Death, serious injury and violent coercion become significant parts of the world.

---

### Level 8 — Extreme

Violence may become widespread during instability.

Organized brutality, executions, severe criminal violence and armed conflict are permitted.

---

### Level 9 — Merciless

Violent environments can become extraordinarily dangerous.

Severe injury, murder, torture and organized atrocities may occur.

---

### Level 10 — Unrestricted

The campaign places almost no thematic restriction on the severity of violence that may emerge from World Truth.

Violence remains causal.

It must never occur merely because the Game Master wants spectacle.

---

# 12. Violence and Graphic Detail

Violence frequency and descriptive intensity are different concepts.

Therefore graphic presentation should not be controlled directly by the Violence parameter.

A campaign may use:

```text
VIOLENCE: 8

GRAPHIC DETAIL: LOW
```

The world may be extremely violent while descriptions remain restrained.

Recommended presentation setting:

```text
GRAPHIC DETAIL

LOW
MEDIUM
HIGH
```

This parameter controls presentation rather than World Truth.

---

# 13. Difficulty

Difficulty determines how forgiving the simulated world is toward:

- mistakes
- poor preparation
- resource scarcity
- injury
- time pressure
- incomplete information
- failed actions
- environmental exposure
- logistical failure

Difficulty must not give the Game Master permission to cheat.

---

# 14. Difficulty Scale

### Level 1 — Story

The world is highly forgiving.

Resources are easier to obtain.

Warnings are clearer.

Recovery from mistakes is normally possible.

Character survival is strongly favored.

---

### Level 2 — Gentle

Consequences exist but rarely become catastrophic.

The campaign prioritizes exploration and narrative continuity.

---

### Level 3 — Forgiving

Poor decisions matter, but players normally have meaningful opportunities to recover.

---

### Level 4 — Moderate

Preparation begins to matter substantially.

Resources, injury and time create real pressure.

---

### Level 5 — Standard Ascension

Resources matter.

Information matters.

Time matters.

Injury matters.

Decisions may permanently close possibilities.

The world does not automatically protect Characters from consequences.

---

### Level 6 — Demanding

Scarcity and logistical problems become more significant.

Poor preparation may create cascading problems.

---

### Level 7 — Hard

Fuel, food, medicine, shelter, transportation and information can become serious constraints.

Bad decisions may permanently alter the campaign.

---

### Level 8 — Severe

Resources may be difficult to replace.

Injury can have long-term consequences.

Recovery from major mistakes is uncertain.

---

### Level 9 — Brutal

Information may be difficult to verify.

Resources may become severely constrained.

Injury is dangerous.

Character death becomes substantially more likely.

Mistakes may compound rapidly.

---

### Level 10 — Unforgiving

The simulated world provides no artificial protection.

A single sufficiently serious mistake may permanently change the campaign or kill a Character.

The Game Master does not rescue Characters from the logical consequences of their decisions.

---

# 15. Difficulty Does Not Change Reality

Higher Difficulty must not create arbitrary misfortune.

Example:

A vehicle with a failing alternator does not suddenly explode because Difficulty is 10.

Instead, Difficulty may influence:

- availability of replacement parts
- reliability of alternative transportation
- recovery opportunities
- resource reserves
- environmental tolerance
- medical consequences
- information availability
- margin for error

But causality remains mandatory.

> **Difficulty changes tolerance for failure, not the honesty of the simulation.**

---

# 16. Parameter Independence

The three parameters must remain independent.

Example:

```text
WEIRDNESS: 1
VIOLENCE: 8
DIFFICULTY: 8
```

produces a completely realistic but extremely dangerous survival experience.

Example:

```text
WEIRDNESS: 9
VIOLENCE: 2
DIFFICULTY: 4
```

may produce a deeply anomalous mystery with relatively little physical violence.

Example:

```text
WEIRDNESS: 5
VIOLENCE: 5
DIFFICULTY: 5
```

produces a balanced Project Ascension campaign where reality is grounded but uncertainty extends beyond conventional explanations.

# 18. Player-Facing Presentation

Players may select campaign parameters during campaign creation.

However, the interface should describe **experience**, not reveal a catalogue of possible phenomena.

Recommended Weirdness labels:

```text
1  GROUNDED
2  REALISTIC
3  UNSETTLING
4  UNCERTAIN
5  STRANGE
6  UNKNOWN
7  DISTURBING
8  NIGHTMARISH
9  EXTREME
10 UNBOUND
```

The interface should not state:

```text
LEVEL 8 ALLOWS MONSTERS.
```

That would reveal too much about the possibility space.

Players should understand the tone they are selecting without knowing what the Game Master may ultimately place behind the curtain.

---

# 19. Game Master Knowledge

Campaign Parameters are Game Master constraints.

The Game Master knows the exact Reality Envelope.

Characters do not.

Players may know the settings they selected, but this is meta-knowledge.

It must never automatically become Character Knowledge.

```text
PLAYER KNOWLEDGE

≠

CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
```

A Character cannot conclude that monsters exist because the player selected Weirdness 8.

---

# 20. World Truth Remains Authoritative

Campaign Parameters never override the fundamental epistemic architecture:

```text
WORLD TRUTH
≠
OBSERVATION
≠
INFORMATION
≠
KNOWLEDGE
≠
BELIEF
≠
DECISION
```

If a genuinely anomalous organism exists:

that is World Truth.

Whether anyone has seen it is Observation.

Whether reports exist is Information.

Whether a Character has received those reports is Knowledge access.

Whether the Character believes them is Belief.

What the Character does about them is Decision.

Weirdness changes what may occupy the first layer.

It does not collapse the layers.

---

# 21. Game Master Restraint

Higher parameter values do not give the Game Master permission to constantly escalate.

The Game Master must preserve contrast.

Without normality, abnormality loses meaning.

Without safety, danger loses contrast.

Without ordinary people, monsters become ordinary.

Without mundane explanations, mysteries become predictable.

Therefore:

> **The stranger the campaign is allowed to become, the more important ordinary reality becomes.**

---

# 22. Escalation

Campaign escalation should emerge from World State, Seeds and Character interaction.

It must not mechanically follow the numerical settings.

The Game Master must not reason:

```text
WEIRDNESS = 8

THEREFORE

A MONSTER MUST APPEAR.
```

Instead:

```text
WEIRDNESS = 8

THEREFORE

A MONSTER MAY EXIST
IF WORLD TRUTH AND SEED DEVELOPMENT
ESTABLISH ONE.
```

The same applies to Violence and Difficulty.

---

# 23. Hidden Truth

Once the Game Master establishes the hidden truth behind a Seed, that truth becomes authoritative.

The Game Master may not rewrite it because:

- players guessed correctly
- players guessed incorrectly
- another explanation would be more dramatic
- the campaign needs more action
- the mystery is taking too long

> **The Game Master may hide truth. It may not move truth.**

---

# 24. The Adventure Principle

Project Ascension must remain more than a crisis simulator.

Infrastructure failure, scarcity, institutional pressure and social fragmentation create the environment.

Seeds create possibilities within that environment.

Characters create disturbance.

Campaign Parameters determine how far those possibilities are permitted to extend.

```text
WORLD PRESSURE
+
HUMAN CONFLICT
+
SEEDS
+
MYSTERY
+
CHARACTER ACTION
+
CAMPAIGN REALITY ENVELOPE
=
EMERGENT ADVENTURE
```

The purpose of Weirdness is not to replace realism.

It is to prevent realism from becoming a ceiling on possibility.

---

# 25. Validation Questions

During playtesting, evaluate:

```text
DID WEIRDNESS CREATE UNCERTAINTY
WITHOUT MAKING EVERYTHING STRANGE?

DID ORDINARY EXPLANATIONS
REMAIN POSSIBLE?

DID PLAYERS BEGIN FORMING
THEORIES WITHOUT THE GM
CONFIRMING THEM?

DID VIOLENCE CREATE DANGER
WITHOUT BECOMING SPECTACLE?

DID DIFFICULTY CREATE CONSEQUENCES
WITHOUT THE WORLD CHEATING?

DID NORMALITY REMAIN PRESENT?

COULD THE SAME GEOGRAPHY
SUPPORT A VERY DIFFERENT CAMPAIGN
WITH DIFFERENT PARAMETERS?

DID THE CAMPAIGN FEEL LIKE
AN ADVENTURE RATHER THAN
ONLY A CRISIS SIMULATION?
```

---

# 26. North Star

> **Project Ascension begins with a world the players recognize. The Campaign Reality Parameters determine how certain they may remain that they understand what that world contains.**

And:

> **Reality provides the foundation. Weirdness expands possibility. Violence defines danger. Difficulty defines forgiveness. Player actions create history.**
