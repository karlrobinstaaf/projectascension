> *"Emergency communication reflects not only the severity of a crisis, but the ability of society to communicate with itself."*

---

# Purpose

The Emergency Communication Level system, abbreviated **ECL**, provides a common classification framework for public emergency communications during periods of escalating national instability.

ECL does not primarily measure the severity of a threat.

It measures the changing relationship between:

- national authorities
- regional authorities
- local authorities
- infrastructure operators
- public communication
- civilian responsibility

As centralized coordination becomes increasingly difficult, emergency communication gradually shifts toward regional and local authorities.

---

# Core Principle

An increasing ECL does not necessarily mean that conditions are becoming more dangerous everywhere.

It indicates that centralized institutions are becoming less capable of providing uniform guidance appropriate to every region.

A community may experience relatively normal conditions while the national ECL continues to increase.

Likewise, severe local conditions may exist before a higher national communication level is established.

ECL therefore measures **communication and coordination state**, not universal danger.

---

# ECL-1 — Federal Advisory

## Definition

Routine federal emergency guidance begins.

Normal governmental communication structures remain intact.

The public is informed of developing conditions, but no significant behavioral changes are requested.

## Characteristics

- Federal authorities remain the primary national information source.
- Infrastructure remains broadly stable.
- Local disruptions are considered manageable.
- Routine preparedness may be encouraged.
- No extraordinary public action is recommended.

## Communication Tone

Informational.

Calm.

Precautionary.

## Historical Example

**EC-001 — Federal Advisory Notice**

---

# ECL-2 — Preparedness

## Definition

Authorities begin requesting voluntary public actions intended to increase infrastructure resilience and community preparedness.

Normal life continues.

However, civilian behavior has become relevant to maintaining operational stability.

## Characteristics

- Voluntary conservation measures may be introduced.
- Household preparedness is encouraged.
- Citizens are asked to reduce unnecessary demand on infrastructure.
- Authorities increasingly emphasize verified information.
- No broad mandatory restrictions exist.

## Communication Tone

Preparedness-oriented.

Reassuring.

Practical.

## Historical Example

**EC-002 — National Infrastructure Conservation Advisory**

---

# ECL-3 — Regional Coordination

## Definition

Operational conditions differ sufficiently between regions that uniform national guidance becomes less effective.

State and local authorities receive increased responsibility for emergency communication.

## Characteristics

- Regional guidance may differ.
- State emergency operations centers gain greater authority.
- Federal agencies continue national coordination.
- Local emergency information becomes increasingly important.
- Citizens are encouraged to prioritize instructions from nearby authorities.

## Communication Tone

Coordinated.

Regionalized.

Adaptive.

## Historical Example

**EC-003 — Federal Coordination Update**

---

# ECL-4 — Regional Emergency

## Definition

Multiple regions require active emergency management under significantly different operational conditions.

National systems continue functioning, but emergency response becomes increasingly decentralized.

## Characteristics

- Regional emergency frameworks are activated.
- State and regional authorities become primary operational decision-makers.
- Federal agencies increasingly provide support rather than direct local coordination.
- Resource conservation may become region-specific.
- Travel guidance and public safety instructions vary geographically.

## Communication Tone

Urgent but controlled.

Instructional.

Region-specific.

## Historical Example

**EC-004 — Regional Emergency Coordination Notice**

---

# ECL-5 — Continuity Operations

## Definition

The preservation of governmental, infrastructural and social continuity becomes a primary national objective.

National leadership communicates directly with the population while increasingly relying upon regional institutions to maintain daily operations.

## Characteristics

- National leadership addresses the public directly.
- Continuity planning becomes visible.
- Regional authorities carry increasing operational responsibility.
- Federal resources prioritize essential systems.
- Public messaging emphasizes cooperation, resilience and community stability.

## Communication Tone

Serious.

Unifying.

Transparent about uncertainty.

## Historical Example

**EC-005 — Address to the Nation**

---

# ECL-6 — Decentralized Communications

## Definition

A unified national emergency communication model can no longer reliably serve all regions.

Primary operational communication shifts to state, regional and local authorities.

National institutions may continue functioning, but communication has become structurally decentralized.

## Characteristics

- Local authorities become the primary source of public guidance.
- National communication focuses on strategic coordination and resource support.
- Regional advisories increasingly replace uniform national instructions.
- Communication networks may operate independently.
- Different parts of the country may receive substantially different guidance.

## Communication Tone

Local.

Operational.

Decentralized.

## Historical Example

**EC-006 — National Emergency Communications Transition**

# Important Distinction

ECL-6 does **not** mean:

- the federal government has ceased to exist
- the United States has formally dissolved
- all national communications have failed
- every region is experiencing catastrophic conditions

It means that the centralized communication model of the Connected World is no longer sufficient.

The nation may still exist politically.

Its institutions may still operate.

Its leaders may still exercise authority.

What has changed is the ability to maintain a single operational conversation with the entire country.

# Relationship to the World States

The ECL system is most historically significant during:

**World State 02 — The Transition**

The progression from ECL-1 through ECL-6 illustrates the gradual decentralization of public emergency communication during the Containment Crisis.

ECL-6 does not itself define the beginning of:

**World State 03 — The Fractured World**

Instead, it represents one of the clearest institutional indicators that the transition toward fragmentation is underway.

# Design Guidance

ECL should not function as a simple dramatic escalation meter.

Higher levels should not automatically imply:

- greater violence
- greater casualties
- greater infrastructure destruction
- greater public panic

The system exists to describe changes in **communication, coordination and authority**.

Different regions may experience radically different realities at the same ECL.

This distinction supports a core principle of Project Ascension:

**The world changes universally. Human experience remains deeply personal.**

---

# Historical Significance

Later historians would use the progression of Emergency Communication Levels as one method of studying the fragmentation of the pre-Collapse United States.

The progression revealed a fundamental transformation.

At first, government informed the population.

Then it advised them.

Then it asked them to prepare.

Then it relied upon regional authorities.

Eventually, national institutions could no longer provide operational guidance appropriate to the country as a whole.

The government had not disappeared.

The shared national operating environment had.
