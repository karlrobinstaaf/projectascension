> *"The world may be busy. The player should not have to be."*

---

# Purpose

The Pacing and Priority system determines which parts of the Living Campaign Engine deserve player attention at any given moment.

The world may simultaneously contain:

```text
World Events
Character Developments
Faction Activity
Authority Decisions
Conflicts
Opportunities
Consequences
Relationship Changes
Information
Missions
Environmental Changes
```

Most of these should continue without direct player involvement.

The purpose of this system is therefore not to determine:

```text
WHAT EXISTS
```

but:

```text
WHAT SHOULD REACH
THE PLAYER NOW?
```

---

# Core Principle

The fundamental separation is:

```text
WORLD ACTIVITY
≠
PLAYER ACTIVITY
```

and:

```text
WORLD PRIORITY
≠
PLAYER PRIORITY
```

A major event may occur far away and remain background information.

A small event involving the player's closest relationship may deserve immediate attention.

---

# Presentation Versus Reality

Pacing controls:

```text
PRESENTATION
```

not:

```text
REALITY.
```

Therefore:

```text
HOOK SUPPRESSED
```

must never automatically mean:

```text
EVENT SUPPRESSED.
```

The world continues.

---

# The Attention Problem

A sufficiently detailed simulation may generate:

```text
10
50
100
500
```

potentially meaningful developments.

The player cannot process all of them.

Without filtering:

```text
LIVING WORLD
      ↓
CONTENT FLOOD
      ↓
SPLIT FOCUS
      ↓
DECISION FATIGUE
      ↓
REDUCED MEANING
```

The solution is not to make the world less alive.

The solution is:

```text
ATTENTION MANAGEMENT.
```

---

# Campaign Bandwidth

Campaign Bandwidth represents how much active content can reasonably demand player attention.

Conceptually:

```text
CAMPAIGN BANDWIDTH

Primary Focus
Secondary Focus
Background Awareness
Ambient World
```

---

# Primary Focus

The most important active situation or situations.

Examples:

```text
Immediate survival crisis

Major relationship event

Current expedition

Critical regional conflict

Player-selected objective
```

Primary Focus should remain limited.

---

# Secondary Focus

Meaningful but non-dominant situations.

Examples:

```text
Character request

Developing opportunity

Regional problem

Longer-term project

Faction development
```

These may coexist with Primary Focus without overwhelming it.

---

# Background Awareness

Events the player may know about but is not expected to act upon.

Examples:

```text
Radio news

Market changes

Distant political developments

Character updates

Regional recovery
```

---

# Ambient World

Events that remain mostly within simulation.

Examples:

```text
Routine trade

Minor disputes

Local repairs

Ordinary migration

Background character activity
```

They make the world function without demanding attention.

---

# Attention Budget

The system should maintain a conceptual:

```text
PLAYER ATTENTION BUDGET
```

This does not need to be a visible numeric value.

It represents how much:

```text
urgency
complexity
emotional weight
decision pressure
```

is already competing for player attention.

---

# Attention Load

Conceptual states:

```text
LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
SATURATED
```

---

# Low Attention Load

The campaign may safely surface:

```text
new opportunities

relationship moments

exploration hooks

minor world developments
```

---

# Moderate Attention Load

New content should require stronger relevance.

---

# High Attention Load

Only highly relevant or genuinely urgent developments should interrupt current focus.

---

# Saturated Attention Load

The system should aggressively defer:

```text
optional hooks

minor requests

distant events

low-urgency opportunities
```

unless they naturally become unavoidable.

---

# Hard Reality Exception

Pacing cannot suppress reality when the player is physically present.

Example:

```text
Player is standing in town.

Explosion occurs nearby.
```

The system cannot say:

```text
Attention Budget Full.
Event hidden.
```

Physical reality overrides presentation pacing.

---

# Direct Observation

Events directly perceived through:

```text
sight
sound
physical environment
conversation
immediate danger
```

should normally bypass artificial hook suppression.

---

# Priority Inputs

Potential player-facing content may be evaluated using:

```text
RELEVANCE

PROXIMITY

URGENCY

PLAYER COMMITMENT

RELATIONSHIP

CONSEQUENCE

NOVELTY

OPPORTUNITY WINDOW

INFORMATION VALUE

EMOTIONAL WEIGHT

SYSTEMIC IMPORTANCE

CURRENT ATTENTION LOAD
```

---

# Priority Is Contextual

No universal event category is always high priority.

Example:

```text
Regional election:
```

may be:

```text
LOW
```

for a player traveling elsewhere.

But:

```text
HIGH
```

if the player's ally is a candidate.

---

# Relevance

Relevance comes primarily from:

```text
Relevance_and_Proximity.md
```

Possible relevance paths include:

```text
Player Goal

Character Relationship

Current Location

Faction Relationship

Mission

Resource Need

Past Action

Known Interest
```

---

# Proximity

Proximity may be:

```text
PHYSICAL

RELATIONAL

INFORMATIONAL

CAUSAL

MISSION
```

A distant event may still have high proximity through a close relationship.

---

# Urgency

Urgency represents:

```text
HOW QUICKLY
THE POSSIBILITY OF ACTION
IS CHANGING.
```

Conceptually:

```text
NONE
LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
CRITICAL
```

---

# Urgency Is Not Importance

A long-term infrastructure project may be:

```text
HIGH IMPORTANCE
LOW URGENCY
```

A passing trader may be:

```text
LOW IMPORTANCE
HIGH URGENCY
```

because they leave tomorrow.

These dimensions must remain separate.

---

# Player Commitment

Existing commitments should receive priority.

Examples:

```text
Promises

Accepted Missions

Scheduled meetings

Ongoing projects

Relationship commitments

Faction obligations
```

This prevents the engine from constantly distracting the player from things they already chose.

---

# Commitment Protection

When the player commits to:

```text
OBJECTIVE A
```

the engine should normally reduce interruption from unrelated:

```text
OBJECTIVE B
OBJECTIVE C
OBJECTIVE D
```

unless those developments are genuinely important.

---

# Player-Selected Focus

Player choice should influence pacing.

If the player decides:

```text
"I am going north to investigate
the radio signal."
```

the engine should recognize:

```text
CURRENT PLAYER FOCUS:
RADIO INVESTIGATION
```

Relevant content may receive increased presentation priority.

Unrelated content may move toward background.

---

# Focus Is Not World Control

Player-selected focus does not mean:

```text
unrelated events stop.
```

They simply receive less presentation priority.

---

# Regional Focus

When the player remains in one region:

```text
local developments
```

naturally receive higher priority.

Distant developments may be compressed into:

```text
news
reports
rumors
later consequences
```

---

# Relationship Focus

Characters with strong relationships may receive higher priority when meaningful changes occur.

But:

```text
STRONG RELATIONSHIP
≠
CONSTANT CONTACT.
```

Contact frequency remains subject to:

```text
Character_Integration.md
```

---

# Novelty

Repeated versions of the same information should lose priority.

Example:

```text
Day 1:
Fuel shortage reported.

Day 2:
Fuel still short.

Day 3:
Fuel still short.
```

The engine should not repeatedly create:

```text
NEW HOOK
NEW HOOK
NEW HOOK
```

unless something materially changed.

---

# Novelty Principle

Ask:

```text
WHAT IS NEW?
```

If the answer is:

```text
nothing,
```

a new player-facing hook may not be necessary.

---

# State Change Threshold

Repeated content should resurface when:

```text
severity changes

new actor becomes involved

new information appears

deadline approaches

new opportunity appears

previous assumption becomes false
```

---

# Hook Saturation

Too many simultaneous Story Hooks reduce the importance of all of them.

Conceptually:

```text
HOOK LOAD

LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
SATURATED
```

---

# Hook Suppression

A valid Story Hook may be temporarily suppressed when:

```text
player attention is saturated

hook relevance is low

similar information already surfaced

no action is currently useful

stronger hook already represents same event
```

Suppression affects:

```text
PRESENTATION
```

not:

```text
WORLD STATE.
```

---

# Hook Deferral

Some hooks should be:

```text
DEFERRED
```

rather than discarded.

Example:

```text
Friend sends non-urgent message.

Player currently escaping flood.
```

Message remains available.

It may surface later.

---

# Deferral Record

Conceptually:

```text
DEFERRED HOOK

Hook ID
Reason
Original Relevance
Original Urgency
Expiration
Resurface Conditions
Current Validity
```

---

# Deferred Hook Validation

Before resurfacing a deferred hook, ask:

```text
IS IT STILL TRUE?
```

Example:

```text
Character requested fuel.
```

Three days later:

```text
Character solved problem independently.
```

Do not surface the old request as current.

It may instead become:

```text
historical update.
```

---

# Hook Expiration

Some hooks lose relevance naturally.

Example:

```text
Trader available until evening.
```

If player never hears about it before trader leaves:

```text
Opportunity expires.
```

The system should not preserve it artificially.

---

# Resurfacing

Deferred content may resurface when:

```text
attention load decreases

player enters relevant region

relationship becomes relevant

new consequence appears

new information arrives

deadline approaches

player asks about subject
```

---

# Organic Resurfacing

Prefer natural resurfacing.

Example:

Instead of:

```text
REMINDER:
Three weeks ago Mara requested medicine.
```

use:

```text
Player meets Mara.

She mentions the clinic
found another supplier.
```

---

# Mission Saturation

Accepted Missions should remain limited enough that each has meaning.

The system should distinguish:

```text
AVAILABLE POSSIBILITY
```

from:

```text
ACTIVE MISSION.
```

Not every available activity needs to enter the Mission Log.

---

# Mission Queue Pressure

Conceptually:

```text
ACTIVE MISSION LOAD

LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
SATURATED
```

When saturated:

```text
new optional Mission formalization
```

should become less common.

---

# No Artificial Mission Freeze

Again:

```text
MISSION LOAD SATURATED
```

does not mean:

```text
WORLD STOPS GENERATING PROBLEMS.
```

It means fewer problems become formalized player obligations.

---

# Commitment Before Expansion

When several Missions are active, the engine should often prefer:

```text
DEEPEN EXISTING THREADS
```

over:

```text
CREATE NEW THREADS.
```

This helps maintain coherence.

---

# Thread

A Thread represents a continuing cluster of related:

```text
events
characters
conflicts
opportunities
missions
consequences
```

Example:

```text
REGIONAL FUEL CRISIS
```

may contain:

```text
Hospital shortage
Farm pressure
Trader opportunity
Authority rationing
Player investigation
```

These should often feel like one evolving situation rather than five unrelated quests.

---

# Thread Clustering

Related hooks should be clustered.

Instead of:

```text
HOOK 1:
Hospital needs fuel.

HOOK 2:
Farmers need fuel.

HOOK 3:
Bus depot needs fuel.

HOOK 4:
Authority considering rationing.
```

present:

```text
REGIONAL FUEL CRISIS
```

with multiple perspectives.

---

# Thread Priority

A Thread may receive priority based on:

```text
highest active urgency

player commitment

relationship relevance

regional relevance

systemic importance

recent activity
```

---

# Thread Continuity

Once the player becomes engaged in a Thread, related developments should receive some continuity preference.

This reduces:

```text
narrative whiplash.
```

---

# Split Focus

Split Focus occurs when the player is simultaneously pushed toward too many unrelated high-priority situations.

Example:

```text
Missing friend

Faction attack

Trade opportunity

Political election

Infrastructure crisis

Family message

Exploration discovery
```

all presented as:

```text
URGENT.
```

This should be avoided unless the world genuinely produces an exceptional crisis.

---

# Competing Priority Can Be Intentional

Sometimes split focus is itself meaningful.

Example:

```text
Player's friend is missing.

At the same time:

Hospital is failing.
```

The player may genuinely be unable to solve both.

This can create:

```text
REAL TRADEOFF.
```

But it should emerge from causality, not routine content generation.

---

# False Urgency

Avoid artificial language or mechanics that imply:

```text
ACT NOW
```

when no real time pressure exists.

Urgency should come from World State.

---

# Real Deadline

A deadline should have a causal reason.

Example:

```text
River rises in 18 hours.

Trader leaves tomorrow.

Fuel reserve lasts six hours.

Election occurs Friday.

Winter begins soon.
```

---

# Deadline Integrity

If the deadline passes:

```text
THE WORLD MOVES ON.
```

Do not silently freeze the deadline until the player arrives.

---

# Soft Deadline

Some windows gradually worsen rather than instantly fail.

Example:

```text
Crop harvest.
```

Each delayed day may:

```text
reduce yield.
```

This is often preferable to arbitrary:

```text
MISSION FAILED AT 12:00.
```

---

# Hard Deadline

Some events genuinely possess a binary threshold.

Example:

```text
Train departs at 08:00.
```

After:

```text
08:00
```

the train is gone.

---

# Emotional Load

The system should consider emotional intensity.

Examples:

```text
Character death

Relationship crisis

Missing family member

Betrayal

Major disaster
```

Several emotionally heavy events should not be artificially stacked simply to maximize drama.

---

# Emotional Saturation

Conceptually:

```text
LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
SATURATED
```

When saturated, low-urgency emotional content may be deferred.

---

# Emotional Reality Exception

As with physical reality:

```text
PACING CANNOT CANCEL
A REAL EVENT.
```

If several tragedies causally occur together:

```text
they occur.
```

Pacing controls presentation where plausible, not causality.

---

# Emotional Recovery

After major emotional intensity, the campaign may naturally benefit from:

```text
routine

travel

conversation

recovery

building

exploration

ordinary life
```

But these should emerge from available World Conditions rather than forced intermissions.

---

# Quiet Period

A Quiet Period is a campaign state where:

```text
few high-priority player-facing developments exist.
```

This is valid.

---

# Silence Is Valid

The system must never assume:

```text
NO HOOK
=
SYSTEM FAILURE.
```

Sometimes:

```text
nothing needs the player.
```

That is one of the strongest signals that the world is genuinely autonomous.

---

# Ordinary Life

Player experience may include:

```text
travel

maintenance

meals

conversation

training

trade

work

family

rest

exploration
```

without a crisis.

This creates contrast.

---

# Recovery Period

After major regional crisis, World Simulation may enter:

```text
RECOVERY.
```

Player-facing content may naturally shift toward:

```text
rebuilding

reunion

trade

governance

relationships

new opportunities
```

rather than immediately generating another disaster.

---

# No Drama Quota

There must be no rule such as:

```text
ONE MAJOR CRISIS
EVERY THREE DAYS.
```

or:

```text
PLAYER HAS NO MISSION
→ GENERATE MISSION.
```

The world determines activity.

---

# No Content Quota

Likewise:

```text
EMPTY MISSION LOG
```

is not automatically a problem.

The player may:

```text
explore

travel

build

socialize

pursue personal goals

seek opportunities
```

without receiving assignments.

---

# Player Initiative

Pacing should preserve room for:

```text
PLAYER-INITIATED PLAY.
```

If the campaign constantly pushes new hooks:

```text
player agency becomes reactive.
```

The player needs space to decide:

```text
"What do I want to do?"
```

---

# Reactive Versus Proactive Play

Healthy campaign pacing should allow both:

```text
WORLD → PLAYER

and

PLAYER → WORLD
```

If almost all play begins with:

```text
someone needs something,
```

the campaign becomes reactive.

---

# Proactive Window

When attention load is low, the system should avoid immediately filling the vacuum.

Instead, it may create:

```text
PROACTIVE SPACE.
```

The player can pursue:

```text
personal goals

exploration

relationships

projects

trade

research

construction
```

---

# Curiosity Space

Unexplained but non-urgent information can support proactive play.

Example:

```text
Player hears weak radio signal.

No one asks them to investigate.
```

The signal simply exists.

The player decides whether it matters.

---

# Major Event Protection

Major events need enough attention to feel significant.

Avoid:

```text
MAJOR EVENT
      ↓
five unrelated hooks
      ↓
another major event
      ↓
three character emergencies
```

unless the world is genuinely collapsing.

---

# Event Dominance

A sufficiently major event may temporarily dominate player-facing information.

Example:

```text
Regional earthquake.
```

Many existing Threads may still continue.

But presentation naturally becomes centered around:

```text
earthquake consequences.
```

---

# Dominance Does Not Freeze Other Threads

Existing situations continue.

Example:

```text
Political election
```

may still happen during:

```text
earthquake recovery.
```

Its presentation may simply become secondary.

---

# Information Density

Not every piece of information needs its own interaction.

Several low-priority developments may be compressed into:

```text
radio bulletin

conversation

market observation

regional report

environmental storytelling
```

---

# Information Compression

Example:

Instead of three separate hooks:

```text
Road reopened.

Fuel prices fell.

Trade traffic increased.
```

player may hear:

```text
"Traffic's finally moving north again.
Fuel's coming down too."
```

One presentation communicates several World State changes.

---

# Background Summary

When returning to a region after long absence, the player may receive a compressed summary through natural channels.

Example:

```text
Since player left:

Council election occurred.

Factory reopened.

Two characters moved away.

Trade improved.
```

These do not require four Missions or four cinematics.

---

# Return Compression

Long absences should prioritize:

```text
MAJOR CHANGES
RELATIONSHIP CHANGES
PLAYER-CAUSED CONSEQUENCES
CURRENTLY RELEVANT CONDITIONS
```

Minor history may remain in World Ledger.

---

# Player-Caused Consequence Priority

When appropriate, consequences caused by previous player actions may receive increased presentation value.

Not because:

```text
PLAYER MUST BE PRAISED
```

but because:

```text
CAUSE → EFFECT
```

is important for perceiving agency.

---

# Consequence Feedback Balance

The system should expose enough consequences that the player understands:

```text
MY ACTIONS MATTER.
```

without turning the campaign into:

```text
constant retrospective reward notifications.
```

---

# Relationship Bandwidth

Close characters should not all compete for attention simultaneously.

Character Integration determines whether they contact player.

Pacing determines whether:

```text
contact is immediate

message waits

information is grouped

interaction occurs later
```

where communication mechanics allow it.

---

# Communication Queue

Messages may naturally form a queue.

Example:

```text
Player offline / unreachable
for five days.
```

On reconnection:

```text
12 messages exist.
```

Pacing should not necessarily present twelve full interruptions.

Messages may be:

```text
prioritized
grouped
summarized
opened by player
```

---

# Message Priority

Possible factors:

```text
Sender Relationship

Urgency at Send Time

Current Validity

Current Urgency

Player Commitment

Information Value
```

---

# Stale Message

Example:

```text
Day 1:
"Can you help us repair the pump?"
```

Day 5:

```text
Pump repaired by local engineer.
```

Message remains historically real.

But presentation should indicate:

```text
STALE / RESOLVED
```

rather than pretending the request is current.

---

# Priority Decay

Some content naturally loses priority over time.

Example:

```text
Minor market opportunity.
```

If ignored:

```text
priority decreases
```

until:

```text
expired.
```

---

# Priority Increase

Priority may increase when:

```text
deadline approaches

severity increases

relationship becomes involved

player moves closer

new information appears

consequence reaches player
```

---

# Dynamic Priority

Priority should therefore be recalculated.

Conceptually:

```text
PRIORITY
≠
FIXED VALUE.
```

---

# Priority Evaluation

A conceptual evaluation might consider:

```text
Priority =
Relevance
+ Urgency
+ Commitment
+ Relationship
+ Consequence Importance
+ Opportunity Window
+ Novelty
+ Proximity
- Saturation
- Redundancy
- Competing Focus
```

This is conceptual.

No universal numeric formula is required.

---

# Critical Override

Some events should override normal pacing.

Examples:

```text
Immediate physical danger

Direct attack

Critical family emergency

Infrastructure failure affecting current location

Hard deadline involving active commitment
```

These may enter:

```text
PRIMARY FOCUS
```

even during high attention load.

---

# Override Must Be Real

Do not artificially label content:

```text
CRITICAL
```

to bypass pacing rules.

Criticality must come from World State.

---

# Player Ignore Behavior

When player ignores a hook:

```text
do not automatically repeat it.
```

Instead:

```text
world continues.
```

The situation may:

```text
resolve

worsen

change

expire

be handled by someone else

resurface through consequences
```

---

# Reminder Principle

Reminders should be justified by:

```text
character motivation

mission commitment

new information

deadline
```

not by:

```text
PLAYER HAS NOT CLICKED HOOK.
```

---

# Escalation Reminder

Valid:

```text
Doctor contacts player again
because hospital reserve
fell from 18 hours to 4 hours.
```

Invalid:

```text
Doctor contacts player again
because quest remains incomplete.
```

---

# Repeated Contact Fatigue

Characters repeatedly contacting player about the same unresolved issue should require:

```text
changed urgency

changed information

changed request

changed relationship
```

Otherwise contact should be suppressed.

---

# Geographic Pacing

The world may contain many regions.

Player-facing detail should generally be highest for:

```text
CURRENT REGION

NEIGHBORING REGION

RELATIONSHIP-CONNECTED REGION

MISSION-CONNECTED REGION
```

Distant areas may remain lower resolution.

---

# Distant Major Event

A major distant event may still surface through:

```text
radio

news

travelers

faction communication

economic effects
```

but does not automatically demand player action.

---

# World Importance Versus Player Importance

Example:

```text
Government collapse
on another continent.
```

World Importance:

```text
CRITICAL
```

Player Importance:

```text
LOW
```

until consequences reach the player's network.

Both values can coexist.

---

# Local Minor Event

Example:

```text
Player's neighbor loses dog.
```

World Importance:

```text
MINOR
```

Player Relevance:

```text
HIGH
```

if relationship is close.

This may deserve more immediate presentation.

---

# Player Preference

Over time, the campaign may infer or explicitly track broad player interests such as:

```text
exploration

relationships

trade

politics

infrastructure

security

research
```

These may influence presentation weighting.

They must not rewrite World Simulation.

---

# Preference Is Not Exclusion

If player prefers:

```text
exploration,
```

political events still occur.

They may simply remain more often in:

```text
BACKGROUND AWARENESS.
```

---

# Anti-Filter-Bubble Principle

Player preference should not completely hide important unfamiliar content.

Occasional unexpected developments help preserve:

```text
WORLD INDEPENDENCE.
```

---

# Thread Dormancy

A Thread may become:

```text
DORMANT
```

when:

```text
no current action is useful

player leaves region

actors stabilize situation

attention shifts
```

Dormant Threads remain part of campaign history.

---

# Thread Reactivation

A dormant Thread may reactivate because:

```text
new event

new actor

player return

resource change

relationship change

deadline

consequence
```

---

# Thread Closure

A Thread may close when:

```text
underlying conflict resolved

opportunity expired

participants moved on

state became baseline
```

Closure does not erase history.

---

---

# Presentation State

Recommended states:

```text
IMMEDIATE

ACTIVE

AVAILABLE

BACKGROUND

DEFERRED

SUPPRESSED

EXPIRED

RESOLVED
```

---

# Immediate

Should interrupt or directly enter current experience.

Use sparingly.

---

# Active

Part of current player focus.

---

# Available

Known and accessible but not demanding attention.

---

# Background

Known as world context.

---

# Deferred

Potentially relevant later.

---

# Suppressed

Currently unnecessary to present.

May or may not become relevant later.

---

# Expired

Opportunity or information window no longer actionable.

---

# Resolved

Underlying situation no longer requires action.

---

# Presentation Channel

Pacing may also select an appropriate channel:

```text
DIRECT EXPERIENCE

CHARACTER CONTACT

RADIO

NEWS

RUMOR

ENVIRONMENT

MISSION

BACKGROUND SUMMARY

NO PRESENTATION
```

---

# Channel Matching

Example:

```text
Distant election result
```

probably belongs to:

19. Detect redundancy.

20. Cluster related content.

21. Identify critical overrides.

22. Assign presentation state.

# Guiding Questions

Before presenting new content, ask:

**Does the player need to know this now?**

**Can the player meaningfully act upon it?**

**Is it genuinely urgent?**

**Is it relevant to current player focus?**

**Does it involve an existing commitment?**

**Does it involve an important relationship?**

**Is anything actually new?**

**Is another Hook already communicating the same situation?**

**How much attention is the campaign already demanding?**

**Can this wait?**

**Will it still be valid later?**

**Could it be communicated through the environment instead?**

**Could several updates be grouped together?**

**Would surfacing this improve player agency or merely add noise?**

And critically:

**What happens if we say nothing?**

If the answer is:

```text
THE WORLD CONTINUES
AND THE PLAYER LOSES NOTHING IMPORTANT
```

then silence may be the correct presentation.

# Core Design Principle

Project Ascension should strive for:

```text
A WORLD FULL OF ACTIVITY

WITHOUT

A SCREEN FULL OF OBLIGATIONS.
```

The player should feel:

```text
THINGS ARE HAPPENING
EVERYWHERE.
```

without feeling:

```text
I AM RESPONSIBLE
FOR EVERYTHING.
```

# Player Responsibility Principle

The world should not implicitly communicate:

```text
IF YOU DO NOT DO THIS,
YOU ARE PLAYING WRONG.
```

Instead:

```text
THIS IS HAPPENING.

YOU MAY CARE.

YOU MAY ACT.

YOU MAY NOT.

THE WORLD WILL RESPOND.
```

# Protagonist Pressure Principle

The player is the protagonist of:

```text
THEIR EXPERIENCE
```

but not necessarily:

```text
THE WORLD.
```

Therefore the simulation should never assume:

```text
EVERY IMPORTANT PROBLEM
MUST EVENTUALLY REACH THE PLAYER.
```

Some problems will be solved by:

```text
Mara.

Elias.

Authorities.

Communities.

Factions.

Engineers.

Traders.

Ordinary people.
```

Some will not be solved at all.
