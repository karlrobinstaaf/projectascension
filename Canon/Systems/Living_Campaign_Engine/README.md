---

# Purpose

The Living Campaign Engine defines how Project Ascension transforms a simulated world into a player-specific campaign.

World Simulation determines:

```text
WHAT IS HAPPENING IN THE WORLD?
```

The Living Campaign Engine determines:

```text
WHICH PARTS OF THAT WORLD
BECOME MEANINGFUL TO THE PLAYER?
```

Its purpose is not to generate endless missions.

Its purpose is to identify meaningful relationships between:

- world events
- regional pressures
- characters
- relationships
- player goals
- player history
- player location
- available information
- ongoing conflicts
- recovery opportunities

and convert selected developments into playable experiences.

---

# Core Principle

The central rule is:

```text
WORLD EVENT
≠
MISSION
```

A simulated event does not automatically become content presented directly to the player.

Example:

```text
WORLD EVENT

Regional bridge failure.
```

Possible campaign outcomes include:

```text
The player never hears about it.
```

or:

```text
A radio report mentions it.
```

or:

```text
A merchant arrives late.
```

or:

```text
Fuel prices increase.
```

or:

```text
A friend is stranded beyond the bridge.
```

or:

```text
Regional authorities request help restoring the route.
```

The underlying event is identical.

Its campaign relevance is not.

# Content Visibility Ladder

World events may become visible through different levels.

```text
LEVEL 0
Invisible

LEVEL 1
Environmental Effect

LEVEL 2
Ambient Information

LEVEL 3
Personal Relevance

LEVEL 4
Actionable Opportunity

LEVEL 5
Direct Request

LEVEL 6
Immediate Crisis
```

---

# Level 0 — Invisible

The world event occurs.

The player has no reason to know about it.

Example:

```text
A distant regional warehouse closes.
```

No direct campaign output is generated.

---

# Level 1 — Environmental Effect

The player sees consequences without knowing the underlying event.

Examples:

```text
Higher prices

Reduced traffic

Delayed deliveries

Lower power availability

More travelers arriving
```

---

# Level 2 — Ambient Information

The player receives indirect information.

Examples:

```text
Radio report

Rumor

Conversation

Public notice

Merchant discussion
```

The event becomes part of the world atmosphere.

---

# Level 3 — Personal Relevance

The event affects something the player cares about.

Examples:

```text
A friend is affected.

A settlement depends upon the resource.

A known trader is missing.

A familiar route becomes dangerous.
```

The player now has emotional or practical investment.

---

# Level 4 — Actionable Opportunity

The player can intervene.

Examples:

```text
Alternative trade route discovered.

Repair opportunity.

Negotiation opportunity.

Information worth investigating.
```

The player may act.

No one necessarily asks them to.

---

# Level 5 — Direct Request

A character, organization or faction asks the player for help.

Examples:

```text
Escort a convoy.

Find medicine.

Repair communications.

Investigate a missing team.
```

This is traditional mission territory.

---

# Level 6 — Immediate Crisis

The event directly confronts the player.

Examples:

```text
Power fails while the player is inside a hospital.

A migration convoy reaches the settlement.

A bridge collapses during travel.

Security forces seal the district.
```

The player must react.

---

# Relevance Is Contextual

The same world event may have different relevance for different players.

Example:

```text
WORLD EVENT

```

---

Possible result:

```text
Higher prices.
Reduced transportation.
```

---

## Player B

Operates a transport company.

Possible result:

```text
Contracts become difficult to fulfill.

Business pressure increases.
```

---

## Player C

Has a sibling working at the regional hospital.

Possible result:

```text
Sibling asks for help finding generator fuel.
```

---

## Player D

Works with regional authorities.

Possible result:

```text
Emergency convoy mission.
```

# Player-Centered Does Not Mean Player-Controlled

The campaign should be relevant to the player.

The world should not revolve around the player.

The player may influence:

- communities
- relationships
- local infrastructure
- factions
- regional decisions

But world events continue independently.

Conceptually:

```text
PLAYER IMPORTANT
≠
PLAYER CENTRAL TO EVERYTHING
```

---

# Independent World Progression

If the player ignores a developing problem:

```text
THE PROBLEM DOES NOT FREEZE
```

Instead:

```text
WORLD SIMULATION CONTINUES
```

Possible outcomes include:

- someone else solves it
- the problem worsens
- the opportunity disappears
- another faction benefits
- the event resolves naturally
- secondary consequences emerge

---

# No Quest Suspension

Traditional quest logic often behaves like:

```text
NPC:
"My village is starving."

PLAYER:
Leaves for six months.

NPC:
"My village is still starving in exactly the same way."
```

Living Campaign Engine should avoid this.

Instead:

```text
PLAYER LEAVES
      ↓
TIME PASSES
      ↓
WORLD SIMULATION CONTINUES
      ↓
SUPPLY STATE CHANGES
      ↓
NEW CAMPAIGN STATE
```

The original mission may no longer exist.

---

# Opportunity Windows

Some content should possess limited opportunity windows.

Example:

```text
Fuel convoy expected in 3 days.
```

Possible window:

```text
PLAYER INTERVENES

or

WORLD RESOLVES EVENT
```

This makes timing meaningful.

# Proximity

Proximity is not purely geographic.

It may include:

```text
GEOGRAPHIC PROXIMITY

SOCIAL PROXIMITY

ECONOMIC PROXIMITY

POLITICAL PROXIMITY

INFRASTRUCTURE PROXIMITY
```

---

# Geographic Proximity

How physically close is the event?

Example:

```text
Power outage:
Player settlement
```

has high relevance.

---

# Social Proximity

Does the event affect someone the player knows?

Example:

```text
Distant town

but

Player's brother lives there.
```

Social proximity may make the event highly relevant.

---

# Economic Proximity

Does the player depend upon the event economically?

Example:

```text
Distant bridge

but

Player's trade route uses it.
```

---

# Political Proximity

Does the event affect a faction or authority the player works with?

---

# Infrastructure Proximity

Does the event affect systems supporting the player's region?

Example:

```text
Power station 150 km away
```

may have high infrastructure proximity.

---

# Relationship Weight

Character relationships should strongly influence campaign relevance.

Example:

```text
WORLD EVENT

Hospital supply shortage.
```

Generic player:

```text
Low relevance.
```

Player whose partner works at the hospital:

```text
High relevance.
```

Relationships turn systemic events into personal stories.

---

# Goal Relevance

World events may intersect with current player goals.

Example:

```text
Player Goal:
Reach Settlement B.

World Event:
Bridge route becomes unavailable.
```

The event immediately becomes relevant.

---

# Resource Relevance

Events affecting player resources may become significant.

Examples:

- fuel
- food
- money
- transport
- equipment
- shelter

---

# Faction Relevance

Events involving known factions may become relevant through:

- alliances
- rivalries
- reputation
- obligations
- previous actions

---

# Historical Relevance

Past player actions should influence future relevance.

Example:

```text
PLAYER previously saved Settlement A.
```

Years later:

```text
Settlement A faces another crisis.
```

That event has higher narrative relevance than an equivalent unknown settlement.

---

# Relevance Score


# Priority

Relevance and Priority are separate.

An event may be personally relevant but not urgent.

Example:

```text
Old friend opens a new workshop.
```

High relevance.

Low urgency.

Another event:

```text
Local hospital has six hours of fuel.
```

High relevance.

High urgency.

---

# Priority Factors

Possible factors include:


# Narrative Saturation

The engine must consider how much content is already active.

If the player currently has:

```text
3 major crises
4 missions
6 unresolved character events
```

the engine should hesitate before surfacing another major mission.

The world event can still occur.

Its campaign presentation may remain ambient.

---

# Campaign Bandwidth

Campaign Bandwidth represents how much active content can reasonably demand player attention.

Conceptually:

```text
LOW
NORMAL
HIGH
OVERLOADED
```

This is a narrative-management value.

It does not affect World Simulation.

# Failure Consequences

Failure should also feed into the simulation.

Example:

```text
Fuel convoy lost.
```

Possible effects:

```text
Fuel Pressure increases.

Hospital operations reduced.

Regional authority seeks alternative source.

Black-market prices rise.
```

Failure changes the world.

It should not merely display:

```text
MISSION FAILED
```

---

# Partial Success

Many outcomes should support:

```text
FULL SUCCESS
PARTIAL SUCCESS
MIXED RESULT
FAILURE
ABANDONED
RESOLVED ELSEWHERE
```

Example:

```text
Players recover 60% of medical shipment.
```

The result is neither complete victory nor complete failure.

---

# Consequence Scale

Consequences may occur at:

```text
PERSONAL
LOCAL
REGIONAL
FACTION
WORLD
```

Some may propagate further.

---

# Delayed Consequences

Consequences may appear later.

Example:

```text

```

Weeks later:

```text
Harvest improves.
```

Months later:

```text

```

Delayed consequences make the world feel persistent.

---

# Hidden Consequences

Players do not need to know every effect immediately.

Example:

```text
Player restores communications tower.
```

Unknown consequence:

```text
Regional authority now receives information from a distant settlement.
```

Later this may generate new events.



---

# Player Agency

Agency requires:

```text
CHOICE
+
CONSEQUENCE
```

The engine should avoid creating choices whose outcomes are effectively identical.

---

# No Perfect Information Requirement

Players should make decisions with imperfect information.

Example:

```text

Available supply:
Insufficient.

Information:
Incomplete.
```

# No Perfect Solution Requirement

Some campaign problems should contain genuine tradeoffs.

Example:

```text

Hospital
or
Water pumps
or

```

The player may improve one outcome while increasing pressure elsewhere.

---

# Emergent Narrative

Living Campaign Engine should favor:

```text
WORLD CONDITIONS
      ↓
PLAYER DECISIONS
      ↓
CONSEQUENCES
```

over:

```text
PREWRITTEN STORY
      ↓
PLAYER FOLLOWS STORY
```

Narrative still provides:

- themes
- characters
- major arcs
- canonical boundaries

But moment-to-moment campaign history should emerge dynamically where possible.

---

# Narrative Structure Still Matters

Emergence does not mean absence of narrative design.

The campaign engine should support:

- foreshadowing
- escalation
- payoff
- character arcs
- thematic repetition
- pacing

The system provides raw events.

Narrative systems shape their presentation.

# Quiet Periods

The campaign should allow periods where:

```text
NOTHING URGENT IS HAPPENING TO THE PLAYER
```

Quiet periods support:

- relationships
- exploration
- building
- trade
- character development
- preparation

The engine should therefore avoid:


# Player Choice to Ignore

The player must be allowed to ignore most hooks.

Possible outcomes:


---

# No Mandatory Heroism

The campaign should not assume the player wants to:

```text
SAVE EVERYONE
```

Players may prioritize:

- family
- profit
- community
- ideology
- exploration
- survival
- power
- rebuilding

The Living Campaign Engine should react to those priorities rather than enforce a heroic role.

---

# Moral Neutrality of Simulation

World Simulation should describe consequences.

It should not automatically judge them.

Example:

```text
Player keeps fuel for own settlement.
```

Possible consequences:

```text
Own settlement stabilizes.

Neighboring settlement suffers shortage.

Relationship declines.
```

The world reacts.

Narrative interpretation may vary.

---

# Faction Autonomy

Factions also respond to World Simulation.

If the player does not act:

```text
FACTIONS MAY
```

- solve problems
- exploit opportunities
- compete
- negotiate
- expand
- fail

The player is one actor among many.

---

# Competition for Opportunities

Example:

```text

```


# World Event Resolution

Every active significant world event should eventually reach a resolution state.

Conceptually:

```text
ACTIVE
RESOLVED
ESCALATED
TRANSFORMED
EXPIRED
ABSORBED
```

---

# Resolved

The problem or opportunity has concluded.

---

# Escalated

The event becomes a larger event.

---

# Transformed

The original event changes into another type.

Example:

```text
Supply shortage
      ↓
Political dispute
```

---

# Expired

Opportunity window closes.

---

# Absorbed

World systems handle the event without generating further major consequences.

---

# Campaign Events Are Views

A campaign event should be understood as a **view onto World Simulation**, not an independent world truth.

Example:

```text
WORLD:

Regional medicine shortage.
```

Campaign views:

```text
Doctor:
Needs antibiotics.

Trader:
Medicine prices rising.

Authority:
Requests convoy.

Family:
Cannot fill prescription.
```

All may be manifestations of the same underlying world state.

---

# Duplicate Event Prevention

The engine should detect when multiple hooks originate from the same underlying event.

This prevents:

```text
5 different NPCs
giving 5 separate quests
about the exact same fuel shortage
```

Instead, multiple perspectives may enrich one active situation.

---

# Event Clustering

Related world events may be grouped into an Event Cluster.

Example:

```text
FUEL CRISIS CLUSTER

Fuel shortage
Transportation delays
Hospital generator risk
Price increase
Black-market activity
```

The campaign may surface selected pieces based upon relevance.

# Story Situation

A Story Situation is broader than a mission.

Conceptually:

```text
WORLD EVENT CLUSTER
      +
CHARACTERS
      +
PLAYER RELATIONSHIPS
      +
COMPETING INTERESTS
      =
STORY SITUATION
```

Example:

```text
Regional fuel shortage

Hospital needs fuel.

Farmers need fuel.

Trader possesses fuel.

Authority considers rationing.

Player knows all parties.
```

The campaign now contains a situation.

It does not require one predefined solution.

---

# Story Situation Principle

Prefer:

```text
HERE IS A PROBLEM WITH ACTORS AND CONSEQUENCES.
```

over:

```text
HERE IS THE CORRECT QUEST SOLUTION.
```

This supports systemic play.

---

# Living Campaign Loop

The complete conceptual loop is:

```text
WORLD SIMULATION
      │
      ▼
WORLD EVENTS
      │
      ▼
EVENT INTAKE
      │
      ▼
RELEVANCE FILTER
      │
      ▼
PRIORITY / PACING
      │
      ▼
STORY HOOK
      │
      ▼
PLAYER RESPONSE
      │
      ▼
MISSION / ACTION / IGNORE
      │
      ▼
CONSEQUENCE
      │
      ▼
WORLD SIMULATION
      │
      ▼
NEW WORLD STATE
```

This loop continuously generates campaign history.

# Minimum Viable Living Campaign Engine

The minimum viable engine should be capable of:

```text
1. Receive a World Event.

2. Determine whether it is relevant.

3. Determine how the player could learn about it.

4. Determine whether player action is possible.

5. Create a hook.

6. Allow player response or non-response.

7. Resolve the event through the world.

8. Apply consequences.

9. Remember important outcomes.
```

Everything beyond this should justify its complexity.

---

# Anti-Script Principle

Avoid:

```text
Chapter 1:
Mission A

Chapter 2:
Mission B

Chapter 3:
Mission C
```

as the default campaign structure.

Prefer:

```text
WORLD CONDITIONS
      ↓
AVAILABLE SITUATIONS
      ↓
PLAYER CHOICES
      ↓
NEW CONDITIONS
```

Major authored narrative arcs may still exist.

The Living Campaign Engine provides the dynamic fabric between and around them.

---

# Anti-Random Principle

The engine must also avoid becoming a random quest generator.

Avoid:

```text
Random NPC needs random item.
```

Prefer:

```text
SUPPLY STATE:
Medicine shortage

CHARACTER:
Doctor

RELATIONSHIP:
Friend of player

RESULT:
Doctor requests help obtaining medicine.
```

The mission has a systemic cause.

---

# Explainability

Every generated campaign event should be traceable.

The engine should be able to answer:

**Which World Event caused this?**

**Why is it relevant to the player?**

**Why did the player learn about it?**

**Why now?**

**Who wants something?**

**What happens if the player does nothing?**

**What could the player change?**

**What world systems will be affected?**

If these questions cannot be answered, the generated content is probably too arbitrary.

# Core Canon Rules

The following principles are currently canonical for the Living Campaign Engine:

1. World events do not automatically become missions.
2. Campaign relevance depends upon the relationship between world and player.
3. The world continues when the player ignores content.
4. NPCs and factions act independently.
5. Hooks may expire or transform.
6. Player actions feed back into World Simulation.
7. Failure changes the world rather than merely ending content.
8. Partial success is valid.
9. Relationships strongly influence relevance.
10. Information must plausibly reach the player.
11. The player should not receive perfect world knowledge.
12. Campaign pacing is separate from World Simulation pacing.
13. Quiet periods are valid and desirable.
14. Conflict does not require combat.
15. Opportunities are as important as crises.
16. Missions require systemic or character causes.
17. Generated situations should support multiple plausible solutions.
18. Player history should affect future content.
19. The player is important without being the center of every event.
20. Generated campaign content must remain explainable.

---

# Guiding Question

Every feature in the Living Campaign Engine should answer:

```text
WHY DOES THIS MATTER TO THIS PLAYER,
IN THIS PLACE,
AT THIS TIME?
```

If the answer is only:

```text
Because the game needs a quest.
```

the system has failed.
