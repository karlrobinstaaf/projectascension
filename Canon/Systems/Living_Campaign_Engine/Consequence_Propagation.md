---

# Purpose

The Consequence Propagation system defines how meaningful actions and events create persistent effects across Project Ascension.

It receives changes produced by:

```text
Player Actions
Character Actions
Faction Actions
Authority Decisions
Mission Outcomes
World Events
Infrastructure Changes
Supply Changes
Security Events
Environmental Events
```

and determines:

```text
WHAT CHANGED

WHAT ELSE THAT CHANGE AFFECTS

WHEN THOSE EFFECTS OCCUR

WHO KNOWS ABOUT THEM

HOW FAR THEY PROPAGATE

WHETHER THEY CREATE NEW EVENTS
```

The system ensures that consequences become part of World Simulation rather than temporary narrative outcomes.

---

# Core Principle

The central model is:

```text
ACTION
   ↓
DIRECT RESULT
   ↓
WORLD STATE CHANGE
   ↓
SECONDARY EFFECTS
   ↓
CHARACTER / FACTION RESPONSE
   ↓
NEW WORLD CONDITIONS
   ↓
NEW EVENTS
```

Therefore:

```text
MISSION COMPLETE
```

does not mean:

```text
CONSEQUENCE COMPLETE
```

---

# Direct Consequence

A Direct Consequence follows immediately from an action.

Example:

```text
Player repairs generator.
```

Result:

```text
Generator operational.
```

The causal relationship is straightforward.

---

# Indirect Consequence

An Indirect Consequence occurs because the direct result affects another system.

Example:

```text
Generator restored
      ↓
Workshop resumes
      ↓
Repair-part production increases
```

The workshop output is an indirect consequence.

---

# Delayed Consequence

Some consequences require time.

Example:

```text

```

```text

```

Months later:

```text

```

# Propagation Limit

Consequences must not propagate infinitely.

The engine should stop propagation when effects become:

```text
NEGLIGIBLE

ABSORBED

STABLE

REDUNDANT

OUTSIDE SIMULATION RESOLUTION
```

# Intended Consequence

An Intended Consequence is an outcome the actor was trying to produce.

Example:

```text
Repair bridge
→ restore crossing.
```

---

# Unintended Consequence

An Unintended Consequence emerges from system interaction.

Example:

```text
Bridge restored
      ↓
Trade increases
      ↓
Outside faction gains access
```

The player may not have intended this.

---

# Unintended Does Not Mean Random

Every unintended consequence must remain:

```text
CAUSALLY EXPLAINABLE
```

Avoid:

```text
Player repaired bridge
→ unrelated volcano erupts.
```

That is coincidence, not propagation.

---

# Side Effects

Actions may create predictable secondary costs.

Example:

```text
Run emergency generator continuously.
```

Result:

```text
Power restored
+
Fuel consumed faster
+
Maintenance wear increases
```

---

# Tradeoffs

Many actions should redistribute pressure rather than erase it.

Example:

```text
Fuel moved to hospital.
```

Result:

```text
Hospital pressure decreases.

Transport pressure increases.
```

This is often more interesting than:

```text
Problem solved globally.
```

---

# Conservation Principle

Where relevant, resources should obey conservation.

Conceptually:

```text
RESOURCE MOVED
≠
RESOURCE CREATED
```

If player redirects:

```text
1000 liters fuel
```

the source loses:

```text
1000 liters
```

unless production or another source compensates.

# Butterfly Effects

Small actions may sometimes produce major later consequences.

But:

```text
BUTTERFLY EFFECT
≠
RANDOM SURPRISE
```

The chain must remain causal.

---

# Butterfly Example

```text
Player helps stranded engineer.
```

Engineer survives.

Months later:

```text
Engineer joins regional power project.
```

Later:

```text
Grid restoration occurs earlier.
```

Later still:

```text
Industrial recovery accelerates.
```

The player's original action mattered.

But not because the engine artificially rewarded them.

The engineer continued living.

---

# Butterfly Visibility

The player may never know the complete chain.

Later they may hear:

```text
"Elena designed half the restoration system."
```

If they remember helping Elena years earlier, the connection becomes meaningful without explicit scoring.

---

# No Butterfly Guarantee

Not every small action should produce major consequences.

Most actions should have:

```text
small consequences.
```

Rare causal amplification makes major butterfly effects meaningful.

# Counterfactual Awareness

For debugging, the engine may optionally preserve:

```text
What would likely have happened
without the intervention?
```

This is not necessarily player-facing.

It may help evaluate impact.

---

# Counterfactual Limitation

The engine should not pretend to know a perfect alternate timeline.

Counterfactuals are estimates based on available simulation state.

---

# Consequence and Mission Resolution

Mission Generation provides:

```text
Outcome Record
```

Consequence Propagation converts that into:

```text
WORLD CHANGES
```

Example:

```text
Mission Outcome:

Bridge restored.
```

Propagation determines:

```text
Travel
Trade
Supply
Population
Security
Character
Faction
Authority
```

effects.

---

# Mission Failure Propagation

Failure also propagates.

Example:

```text
Mission:
Repair water pump.

Outcome:
Failed.
```

Possible consequences:

```text
Water reserve decreases.

Authority begins rationing.

Residents seek alternate supply.

Migration pressure rises.

Trader imports water.
```

Failure becomes new campaign material.

---

# Partial Outcome Propagation

Example:

```text
Mission:
Deliver 100 units medicine.

Delivered:
40.
```

The simulation should not classify this simply as:

```text
FAILURE
```

Instead:

```text
Supply +40

Demand remains unmet

Hospital prioritizes patients

Shortage continues at reduced severity
```

---

# Character Consequence Propagation

Example:

```text
Player fails promise to Mara.
```

Possible direct consequence:

```text
Mara Trust ↓
```

Possible indirect consequence:

```text
Mara asks another trader next time.
```

Later:

```text
Player receives fewer medical opportunities.
```

This is a propagated relationship consequence.

---

# Reputation Propagation

Reputation requires information movement.

Example:

```text
Player rescues convoy.
```

Witnesses:

```text
6 people.
```

Information may spread:

```text
Witnesses
      ↓
Settlement
      ↓
Trade network
```

Reputation changes geographically and socially over time.

---

# Reputation Decay

Minor events may fade from collective memory.

Major events may persist.

Conceptually:

```text
Personal Memory
Regional Reputation
Historical Reputation
```

may have different persistence.

---

# Consequence Discovery

Players may investigate:

```text
Why did this happen?
```

The engine should allow causal traces to become discoverable through:

- characters
- records
- physical evidence
- data
- radio
- institutions

---

# Hidden Causal Discovery

Example:

Player notices:

```text
Farm output decreased.
```

Investigation reveals:

```text
Fuel was redirected months earlier
to hospital generators.
```

The player discovers an unintended consequence of their own earlier choice.

---

# Consequence Storytelling

The best consequence presentation often does not say:

```text
Because of your earlier action...
```

Instead:

```text
Player returns.

Old abandoned workshop is operating.

A familiar engineer is supervising crews.
```

The player recognizes the history.

---

# Consequence Feedback Principle

Meaningful actions should eventually produce:

```text
PERCEIVABLE WORLD DIFFERENCE
```

where plausible.

Otherwise:

```text
PLAYER AGENCY
```

becomes difficult to perceive.

---

# Invisible Agency

Some actions legitimately produce hidden outcomes.

But if too many important consequences remain invisible:

```text
the world may feel unresponsive.
```

Story Hooks should therefore surface a reasonable subset of meaningful consequences.

---

# Consequence Saturation

Avoid overwhelming the player with reports about every downstream effect.

The engine should prioritize consequences that are:

```text
Relevant
Novel
Meaningful
Discoverable
Emotionally Significant
```

---

# Consequence Aggregation

Many small effects may be summarized into one world change.

Example:

```text
Several minor repairs
+
new workers
+
better fuel supply
```

may collectively produce:

```text
Regional Infrastructure Reliability:
IMPROVING
```

---

# Consequence Conflict Resolution

When consequences conflict, World Simulation determines resulting state.

Consequence Propagation should not choose outcomes based on narrative preference.

---

# World State Is Authoritative

Consequences propose or apply causal pressure.

The resulting state belongs to:

```text
WORLD SIMULATION
```

This preserves separation between:

```text
CAUSE
```

and:

```text
CURRENT STATE.
```

---

# Consequence Propagation Pipeline

Conceptually:

```text
ACTION / EVENT
      ↓
DIRECT STATE CHANGE
      ↓
IDENTIFY AFFECTED SYSTEMS
      ↓
GENERATE CONSEQUENCE RECORDS
      ↓
APPLY TIMING
      ↓
APPLY NETWORK / GEOGRAPHIC PROPAGATION
      ↓
EVALUATE THRESHOLDS
      ↓
EVALUATE FEEDBACK LOOPS
      ↓
UPDATE CHARACTERS / FACTIONS / AUTHORITY
      ↓
UPDATE WORLD STATE
      ↓
GENERATE NEW EVENT CANDIDATES
      ↓
WORLD EVENT INTAKE
```

---

# Closed Campaign Loop

With Consequence Propagation:

```text
WORLD STATE
      ↓
WORLD EVENT
      ↓
RELEVANCE
      ↓
STORY HOOK
      ↓
PLAYER / CHARACTER ACTION
      ↓
MISSION
when appropriate
      ↓
OUTCOME
      ↓
CONSEQUENCE PROPAGATION
      ↓
NEW WORLD STATE
      ↓
NEW WORLD EVENTS
```

The loop is now closed.

# Consequence Propagation Update Cycle

A conceptual cycle:

```text
1. Read new actions and event outcomes.

2. Identify direct state changes.

3. Create consequence records.

4. Identify affected systems.

5. Determine consequence strength.

6. Determine timing.

7. Determine persistence.

8. Apply immediate consequences.

9. Queue delayed consequences.

10. Evaluate conditional triggers.

11. Evaluate cross-system propagation.

12. Evaluate geographic propagation.

13. Evaluate network propagation.

14. Evaluate thresholds.

15. Evaluate feedback loops.

16. Update character plans.

17. Update faction plans.

18. Update authority behavior.

19. Update World State.

20. Generate new Event Candidates.

21. Determine consequence visibility.

22. Generate Story Hook candidates.

23. Update causal graph.

24. Stabilize or absorb completed effects.

25. Write historically significant chains to Campaign Memory / World Ledger.
```

# Consequence Consistency Rules

## Rule 1

Consequences must have identifiable causes.

---

## Rule 2

Consequences may originate from any actor or world process.

---

## Rule 3

Player actions do not receive special causal rules.

---

## Rule 4

Mission completion does not end consequence processing.

---

## Rule 5

Failure produces consequences.

---

## Rule 6

Partial outcomes produce proportional world effects.

---

## Rule 7

Consequences may be delayed.

---

## Rule 8

Consequences may remain hidden.

---

## Rule 9

Unintended consequences must remain causally explainable.

---

## Rule 10

Resources should obey conservation where applicable.

---

## Rule 11

Solving one pressure may increase another.

---

## Rule 12

Positive consequences must propagate as seriously as negative consequences.

---

## Rule 13

World recovery can cascade.

---

## Rule 14

Effects should decay when appropriate.

---

## Rule 15

Effects may amplify when world conditions justify it.

---

## Rule 16

Thresholds may transform small pressures into major events.

---

## Rule 17

Feedback loops may stabilize or destabilize systems.

---

## Rule 18

Consequences must not propagate infinitely.

---

## Rule 19

Geographic distance should normally attenuate physical effects.

---

## Rule 20

Network connections may override simple geographic distance.

---

## Rule 21

Multiple consequences may reinforce or cancel each other.

---

## Rule 22

Relationship consequences require knowledge and interpretation.

---

## Rule 23

Reputation consequences require information propagation.

---

## Rule 24

Causal responsibility and perceived responsibility are separate.

---

## Rule 25

Characters may misattribute outcomes.

---

## Rule 26

Characters and factions should update plans in response to consequences.

---

## Rule 27

Meaningful consequences should create new Event Candidates when appropriate.

---

## Rule 28

Not every consequence needs player-facing presentation.

---

## Rule 29

Important player agency should become perceivable where plausible.

---

## Rule 30

Persistent consequences should eventually become World Baseline.

---

## Rule 31

Historically significant causal chains should remain recoverable.

---

## Rule 32

Butterfly effects must emerge from causal chains rather than arbitrary narrative reward.

---

# Guiding Questions

For every major consequence, the engine should be able to answer:

**What caused this?**

**What changed directly?**

**Which system was affected?**

**What else depends upon that system?**

**How strong is the effect?**

**How long will it last?**

**When should it occur?**

**Can it propagate geographically?**

**Can it propagate through a network?**

**Does it cross a threshold?**

**Does it create or alter a feedback loop?**

**Who is affected?**

**Who knows about it?**

**Who believes they caused it?**

**Can the player perceive it?**

**Could it create a new Story Situation?**

**When does it become baseline?**

If these questions cannot be answered, the consequence may be narrative decoration rather than systemic causality.

# Architectural Result

With Consequence Propagation established, Project Ascension can support:

```text
PLAYER ACTION
      ↓
WORLD HISTORY
```

rather than only:

```text
PLAYER ACTION
      ↓
REWARD
```

This allows:

```text
a repaired bridge

a broken promise

a rescued engineer

a trade agreement

a failed evacuation

a restored radio

a political compromise

a resource allocation
```

to continue influencing the campaign long after the original event has ended.
