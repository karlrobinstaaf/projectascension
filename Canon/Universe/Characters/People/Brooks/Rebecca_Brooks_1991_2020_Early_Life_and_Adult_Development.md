# 147. Closing Principle

Rebecca grows up knowing Daniel as the person who gets there first.

First to be older.

First to drive.

First to leave.

First to become an adult in a way she can see from inside the family.

Then he leaves for college.

The house changes.

And for a while Rebecca becomes the child still at home.

But she does not remain there.

She makes drawings.

Then better drawings.

Then drawings that other people criticize.

She discovers that liking a building and understanding why it works are different things.

She discovers that designing something and getting it built are different things.

She learns that cities are not blank pages.

People already live there.

Money already owns things.

Roads already divide places.

Buildings already contain history.

Every design enters a world that existed before it.

Eventually she leaves Richmond too.

Charlottesville becomes part of her history.

Then another city becomes daily life.

By coincidence, Daniel is nearby again.

But this time neither of them is living inside their parents' household.

They meet because they choose to.

They call because they choose to.

Sometimes they do not.

Then Daniel starts talking about someone named Sarah.

At first Rebecca knows Sarah only through the way her brother describes her.

Later she meets the actual person.

The two versions are not identical.

They never could be.

And slowly Rebecca becomes part of Sarah's history while Sarah becomes part of hers.

Not because a family tree says they should.

Because they keep meeting.

Because time passes.

Because things happen.

Because relationships accumulate.

And while all of this is happening, Rebecca continues going to work.

Projects change.

Buildings rise.

Others never leave the drawing.

Neighborhoods change.

Clients change their minds.

She changes hers.

Her parents get older.

Her brother becomes someone different from the teenager she remembers.

So does she.

> **Rebecca Brooks is not a branch attached to Daniel's story. She is another life growing beside it.**
