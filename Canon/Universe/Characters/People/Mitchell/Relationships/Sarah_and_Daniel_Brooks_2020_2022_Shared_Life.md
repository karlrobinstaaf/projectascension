# 197. Relationship North Star

The North Star for 2020–2022 is:

> **Sarah and Daniel do not decide to marry because they survived something dramatic together. They decide to marry because years of ordinary and extraordinary experience have made a shared future feel more desirable than preserving maximum individual optionality.**

# 198. Closing Principle

Sarah and Daniel move in together expecting to learn how to share an apartment.

Instead they learn how to share nearly everything else at the same time.

Work.

Silence.

Uncertainty.

Bad news.

Family calls.

Limited space.

Closed pools.

Video meetings.

Groceries.

Worry.

Boredom.

Days when Sarah leaves for a facility and Daniel stays behind.

Days when Daniel spends ten hours speaking to people through a screen and Sarah assumes he has barely moved.

Days when both are tired and neither has done anything wrong.

They learn that living with someone is not primarily about the moments when both people are at their best.

It is also about what happens when neither has much left to give.

Then the world gradually opens again.

Restaurants return.

Flights return.

Family visits return.

Sarah swims again.

Daniel sees colleagues somewhere other than a screen.

Reston stops feeling like the apartment where they spent the pandemic.

It becomes where their life happened.

They talk about a house.

Not because they need one immediately.

Because they can imagine staying.

They talk about money.

Not as monthly rent.

As years.

They talk about children.

Not names.

Not numbers.

Only whether that is a future they both want.

It is.

They talk about marriage.

Not as a proposal.

As a decision.

And by the time Daniel eventually asks Sarah whether she will marry him, the important question has already been answered in hundreds of smaller ways.

A key.

A canceled trip.

An apology.

A grocery run.

A call from Pittsburgh.

A call from Richmond.

A difficult workday.

A changed behavior.

A shared address.

A future sentence that slowly changed from:

```text
I THINK
I MIGHT...
```

to:

```text
WE SHOULD
PROBABLY...
```

The proposal does not create that future.

It gives a name to the future they have already begun choosing.

> **Commitment becomes real when two people stop protecting every possible future and deliberately begin closing some of them in order to build one together.**
