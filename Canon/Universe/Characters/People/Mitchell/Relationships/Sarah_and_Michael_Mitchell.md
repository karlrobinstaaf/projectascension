# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the relationship between:

```text
SARAH MITCHELL

AND

MICHAEL MITCHELL
```

during their childhood and early adolescence.

It does not define their complete future relationship.

It establishes the history from which that relationship can later develop.

The objective is to create:

```text
SHARED HISTORY

WITHOUT

SHARED IDENTITY.
```

Sarah and Michael grow up:

- in the same household
- with the same parents
- within the same broad economic conditions
- around many of the same people
- visiting many of the same places
- experiencing many of the same historical events

But they do not experience those things identically.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The central principle is:

> **Sarah and Michael share a childhood. They do not share an experience of childhood.**

Their relationship must preserve the distinction between:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED

WHAT SARAH EXPERIENCED

WHAT MICHAEL EXPERIENCED

WHAT SARAH REMEMBERS

WHAT MICHAEL REMEMBERS

WHAT EACH LATER
BELIEVES HAPPENED.
```

These may overlap.

They do not need to match.

# 123. Closing Principle

Sarah and Michael do not become important to one another because the campaign needs them to be.

They become important because they grow up together.

They fight over the television.

Michael enters Sarah's room without knocking.

Sarah tells him he is too young.

He copies her.

She excludes him.

She includes him.

They fight over the computer.

They sit in the back seat together.

They complain about their parents.

They make each other laugh at inappropriate moments.

They know which stories Thomas tells too often.

They know what Margaret will say before she says it.

They remember the cabin.

They remember childhood.

But not in exactly the same way.

Then they grow older.

Their lives begin to separate.

New people become important.

New responsibilities appear.

Distance grows.

Years pass.

And one day the world may make the simple fact that they are brother and sister matter in ways neither of them could have imagined as children.

> **A sibling is not a narrative role. A sibling is a person who remembers parts of your life that you may no longer remember yourself.**
