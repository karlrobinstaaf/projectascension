# 111. Closing Principle

Sarah moves to Northern Virginia for work.

Then something ordinary happens.

She stays.

Not because of one dramatic decision.

Because life accumulates.

She learns the roads.

She finds a pool she likes.

She develops friendships.

Coworkers become people she knows outside work.

Her apartment fills with things she chose rather than things she brought from Pennsylvania.

Restaurants stop requiring maps.

A grocery store becomes *her* grocery store.

She knows which route to avoid at five in the afternoon.

She learns which flights are easiest when going back to Pittsburgh.

She becomes known at work.

She begins saving for things that belong to a future she assumes will arrive.

The Laurel Highlands property remains part of her past.

Reston becomes part of her present.

Eventually she may stop noticing the moment that happened.

Because that is often how a place becomes home.

Meanwhile, buildings around Northern Virginia fill with more equipment.

More servers.

More power.

More heat.

More cooling.

More systems built so that other systems can keep running.

Sarah becomes increasingly good at keeping those physical systems alive.

She does not know what that will eventually mean.

She simply knows that tomorrow morning there is work to do.

> **Before Sarah can have something to lose, she needs something ordinary enough to believe will still be there tomorrow.**
