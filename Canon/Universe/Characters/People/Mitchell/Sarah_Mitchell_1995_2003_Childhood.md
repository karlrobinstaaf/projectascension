# 1. Purpose

This document establishes Sarah Mitchell's childhood between approximately:

```text
1995–2003

AGE 6–14.
```

This is the first period of Sarah's life developed at relatively high resolution.

The objective is not to explain the adult Sarah.

The objective is to create:

> **the child who existed before anyone knew who the adult would become.**

This period should establish:

- home
- school
- friends
- family routines
- sibling life
- grandparents
- Laurel Highlands
- interests
- failures
- successes
- ordinary fears
- ordinary joys
- technology
- historical exposure
- early independence
- unresolved experiences

Some of these may later become important.

Most do not need to.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Sarah's childhood must not be reverse-engineered from her future.

Avoid:



# 126. Closing Principle

In 1995 Sarah Mitchell is six years old.

Her world feels permanent.

Her parents go to work.

She goes to school.

Michael is annoying.

Her grandparents are simply old people she loves, not links to history.

The cabin is somewhere the family goes on weekends.

The computer is something everyone shares.

The internet is new.

Adults know what they are doing.

Systems work.

Problems get fixed.

Then, almost invisibly, the world begins changing around her.

Computers become networks.

Networks become everyday life.

Information becomes immediate.

Distance becomes smaller.

September 11 shows her generation that events far away can suddenly enter the living room.

But Sarah is still a child.

She does not know where she will live.

She does not know what she will study.

She does not know whom she will love.

She does not know what work she will do.

She does not know what Aurora is.

She does not know that one day the systems she takes for granted will become central to her life.

And she should not.

> **The purpose of Sarah's childhood is not to prepare her for the future. It is to give her a past.**
