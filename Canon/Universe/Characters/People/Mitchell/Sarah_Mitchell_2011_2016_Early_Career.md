# 130. Closing Principle

Sarah begins her career believing the engineer's job is to understand systems well enough to make them work.

The real world complicates that idea.

The drawings are wrong.

The equipment is old.

The operator knows something nobody documented.

The sensor lies.

The backup shares a dependency nobody noticed.

The budget does not allow the best solution.

The client accepts a risk Sarah would rather eliminate.

And sometimes something fails anyway.

Then everyone has to figure out what actually happened.

Sarah discovers that she likes that part.

Not the failure.

The reconstruction.

The moment when scattered information begins forming a picture.

The point when:

```text
WE THINK
THIS HAPPENED
```

slowly becomes:

```text
THIS IS
WHAT ACTUALLY
HAPPENED.
```

By 2016, Sarah is no longer simply a Mechanical Engineer.

She is becoming someone who thinks professionally about systems that other people depend on continuing to work.

She still does not know where that will lead.

But for the first time, the path toward critical infrastructure exists because of choices she actually made and work she actually did.

> **Sarah does not find her career by choosing a destination. She finds it by noticing which problems she keeps wanting to understand.**
