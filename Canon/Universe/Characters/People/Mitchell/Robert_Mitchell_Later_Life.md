Because no one experiences aging from outside themselves.

The property remains familiar.

That is what makes the change visible.

The steps did not become higher.

The tools did not become heavier.

The hill did not become steeper.

Robert changed.

For a while he compensates.

Then he delegates.

Then he explains.

Then he begins telling people where things are.

Who fixed the furnace.

Which shutoff sticks.

Which contractor never returned a call.

Where the old survey is.

Why that wall was rebuilt.

Which story about the place is true.

Or at least which version he remembers.

At first everyone thinks they are helping Robert take care of the property.

Eventually they will understand that something else is happening.

Robert is teaching the family how to continue without requiring him to be there every time.

He may never describe it that way.

He may simply say:

```text
WRITE THIS
DOWN

SO I DON'T
HAVE TO
TELL YOU
AGAIN.
```

And perhaps that is how generational transfer usually begins.

Not with inheritance.

Not with death.

With someone who has always carried something realizing, slowly and reluctantly, that other hands need to learn its weight.

> **Before a family inherits property, it inherits responsibility. Before it inherits responsibility, someone has to begin letting go of it.**
