# 154. Closing Principle

Linda spends decades seeing people when something is wrong.

Sometimes the answer is obvious.

Often it is not.

A symptom can mean one thing.

Or five.

A test can clarify.

Or confuse.

A patient can tell the truth and still give the wrong history.

A record can look complete and still omit the one fact that matters.

A treatment can be appropriate today and replaced by better evidence five years later.

A patient can hear the risks, understand them, and still choose differently than Linda would choose.

Medicine does not teach Linda that uncertainty can be eliminated.

It teaches her that:

```text
UNCERTAINTY

DOES NOT
REMOVE

THE NEED
TO DECIDE.
```

By 2020 she has practiced that discipline for decades.

She has no reason to think the next year will be fundamentally different.

Patients will come in.

Medications will be adjusted.

Laboratory results will arrive.

Specialists will call.

Appointments will run late.

Sarah will call from Virginia.

Michael will be nearby.

Thomas will ask what she wants for dinner.

Robert and Margaret will continue getting older.

The world will continue operating.

Then reports begin arriving about a respiratory illness far away.

At first it is another piece of medical information in a world full of medical information.

Linda reads it.

She does not know what it will become.

Neither does anyone else.

And for the first time in Project Ascension, we are ready to follow what happens when an entire family begins updating its understanding of the same changing world from different places, professions and sources.

> **Expertise does not mean knowing the future. It means knowing how to act responsibly when the future is still uncertain.**
