# 120. Closing Principle

Daniel knows Sarah will probably say yes.

Probably.

That word is enough to make him nervous.

He has seen her exhausted after work.

She has watched him spend entire days talking to people through a laptop and emerge somehow more tired than if he had left the apartment.

They have argued over chores.

Over time.

Over plans.

Over things neither of them remembers clearly enough to win.

They have apologized.

Changed behavior.

Tried again.

They have sat in the same room without speaking.

Called Pittsburgh.

Called Richmond.

Canceled trips.

Made new ones.

Watched the world close.

Watched it open again.

And somewhere during all of that, Reston stopped being where Sarah lived.

It became home.

Not because of the apartment.

Because both of them expected the other person to be there.

Daniel has already asked the important question in pieces.

When they talked about a house.

When they talked about children.

When they talked about jobs that might change their lives.

When they stopped saying:

```text
IF WE'RE
STILL TOGETHER...
```

and started saying:

```text
WHEN WE...
```

So when he finally asks the question directly, Sarah is surprised by the moment.

Not by the future.

She says yes.

For a few minutes, nothing else changes.

The same apartment waits for them.

The same jobs.

The same families.

The same bills.

The same unfinished decisions.

Then they begin making calls.

And an Event known only to two people starts moving outward through the world.

First parents.

Then siblings.

Then friends.

Soon people who were not there will know that Daniel proposed in Reston.

Years later, some of them may remember the story differently.

One day people who do not yet exist may hear it too.

But for Sarah and Daniel, before it becomes family history, it is simply a moment in which two people who have already spent years choosing each other decide to make that choice explicit.

> **The proposal does not begin their future. It marks the moment they agree to give that future a formal direction.**
