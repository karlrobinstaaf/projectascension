# 201. Development Locks

Do not use COVID to retroactively create:

- survival expertise
- stockpiling culture
- anti-government or pro-government permanent archetypes
- family political identities
- Aurora awareness
- permanent remote work assumptions for every Character
- guaranteed family cohesion
- direct preparation for the Collapse
- secret preparedness networks
- data-center heroism
- medical omniscience
- financial foresight
- legal omnipotence

COVID creates history.

Not Character classes.

# 202. North Star

The North Star for this Event is:

> **The pandemic should feel enormous because it changes ordinary life, not because the Mitchell family is uniquely important to it.**

They are one family among millions.

Their particular professions allow them to observe different pieces of the event.

That gives Project Ascension a richer historical record.

It does not make them central to world history.

# 203. Closing Principle

At first, the disease is somewhere else.

A report.

A headline.

A conversation Linda pays slightly more attention to than everyone else.

Then there are more reports.

More numbers.

More questions.

Someone asks Linda whether she thinks it is serious.

She says they do not know enough yet.

Sarah goes to work.

Daniel goes to work.

Michael reviews another forecast.

Thomas lives his ordinary life.

Robert and Margaret continue getting older.

Sarah and Daniel unpack boxes in Reston.

They are still deciding where things belong.

Then the outside world begins changing faster than their apartment.

Meetings disappear from calendars.

Flights are canceled.

Offices empty.

Patients call Linda with questions she cannot yet answer confidently.

Michael's forecasts stop behaving like forecasts.

Daniel reads agreements written for a world in which businesses remain open.

Sarah enters facilities supporting millions of people who have suddenly moved parts of their lives online.

The family stops talking about when they will see one another next and starts talking about whether they should.

The distance between Reston and Pittsburgh does not change.

Its meaning does.

Everyone knows more than they did the week before.

Nobody knows enough.

Then life adapts.

A dining table becomes an office.

A physician sees patients through a screen.

Grandparents wave through glass or talk through video.

A contract meeting happens from spare bedrooms.

Infrastructure built for one version of society supports another almost overnight.

People complain.

People adapt.

Institutions make mistakes.

Institutions learn.

Systems strain.

Most continue.

And slowly something that initially felt impossible begins feeling routine.

That may be one of the most important historical facts Project Ascension needs to preserve.

Because the people who later experience the Emergence Event will not enter it believing civilization is helpless.

They will have evidence of the opposite.

They have already seen a global crisis arrive.

They have already seen institutions struggle.

They have already seen experts disagree.

They have already seen shortages, fear, restrictions and misinformation.

And they have already seen the world continue.

That history will shape what they believe the next time something enormous begins.

> **The most dangerous expectation in a truly unprecedented crisis may be the entirely reasonable belief that this crisis, like the others, will eventually end and return the world to something recognizable.**
