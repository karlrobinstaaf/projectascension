# 203. Closing Principle

Margaret knows the family stories.

Not all of them.

Nobody does.

But she knows where many of the photographs came from.

Which people Sarah never met.

Which names Michael has heard but cannot place.

Which pieces of furniture have a history.

Which are simply old.

A bedroom that belonged to different generations at different times.

A photograph taken outside before Sarah was born.

A cabinet full of things nobody has used in years.

For Margaret, preservation increasingly means understanding what the place was for.

Continuity.


Writing names on photographs.

Asking Robert where things are.

She wants the next generation to inherit history.

Not guilt.

She wants them to know what things meant.

And then decide what those things should mean next.

> **The most durable family legacy may not be keeping the past unchanged. It may be giving the next generation enough truth, context and freedom to decide what deserves to survive.**
