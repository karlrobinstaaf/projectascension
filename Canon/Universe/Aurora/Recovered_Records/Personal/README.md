---

# Purpose

The Personal Records archive preserves fragments of ordinary human life throughout the historical periods documented by Project Ascension.

Unlike institutional records, these documents do not attempt to explain the larger crisis.

They capture how individuals experienced it.

Most people did not understand The Emergence Event.

They did not have access to classified intelligence.

They did not know what governments were discussing.

They did not see national infrastructure data.

They experienced something much smaller.

A delayed train.

A missing delivery.

A school closure.

A strange emergency alert.

A phone call that would not connect.

A neighbor asking for help.

A family deciding whether to leave.

History happened around them while they were trying to live their lives.

---

# Core Principle

Personal Records represent **individual experience, not objective truth**.

A recovered message may be:

- accurate
- mistaken
- incomplete
- emotional
- contradictory
- based on rumor
- based on firsthand observation
- misunderstood in hindsight

This is intentional.

Personal Records should preserve what people believed at the time.

They should not be rewritten to match what historians later discovered.

---

# The Human Perspective

Institutional records ask questions such as:

**What is happening?**

**What should we do?**

**Can the system remain operational?**

Personal Records ask different questions:

**Are you safe?**

**Are you coming home?**

**Should we leave?**

**Did you hear what happened?**

**Do we have enough food?**

**Why isn't the power back yet?**

**Can you call me when you get this?**

These questions are smaller.

For the people asking them, they are more important.

---

# Scope

Personal Records may include:

- text messages
- private messages
- emails
- voicemail transcripts
- personal journals
- handwritten notes
- photographs with annotations
- home video transcripts
- audio recordings
- letters
- family messages
- workplace conversations
- school communications
- neighborhood messages
- personal social media posts
- travel notes
- evacuation notes

The archive may also contain fragments rather than complete conversations.

Incomplete records are often more believable than perfectly preserved narratives.

---

# Ordinary Life

Personal Records should not focus exclusively on crisis.

Most people continue living normal lives for as long as possible.

Records may therefore include:

- work
- school
- relationships
- birthdays
- shopping
- childcare
- commuting
- sports
- entertainment
- vacations
- arguments
- jokes
- ordinary frustrations

The crisis should often exist in the background before it moves into the foreground.

This contrast is essential.

The world does not suddenly become a disaster story.

For most people, normal life slowly becomes harder to maintain.

---

# Knowledge Limits

Characters represented in Personal Records only know what they could reasonably know.

They may learn information through:

- personal observation
- friends and family
- coworkers
- local authorities
- emergency communications
- news media
- social media
- rumors
- professional knowledge

They should not possess classified information without a plausible reason.

They should not understand Aurora simply because later historians do.

They should not recognize the historical importance of ordinary events while those events are occurring.

---

# Aurora

Early Personal Records should rarely mention Aurora by name.

Most civilians do not initially know that a distinct phenomenon exists.

Instead, they encounter:

- rumors about AI systems
- unexplained technical problems
- contradictory news reports
- unusual government warnings
- workplace restrictions
- service interruptions
- speculation online

Different people may develop radically different explanations.

Some blame:

- hackers
- corporations
- foreign governments
- government incompetence
- infrastructure failures
- climate events
- economic instability
- artificial intelligence
- misinformation

Others may believe nothing unusual is happening at all.

This uncertainty should remain visible.

---

# Rumor

Rumor is an important part of the Personal Records archive.

As reliable information becomes harder to obtain, people naturally attempt to fill missing information themselves.

Rumors may spread through:

- social media
- group chats
- workplaces
- neighborhoods
- family networks
- amateur radio
- local communities

Some rumors may later prove accurate.

Others may be completely false.

Many should contain fragments of truth mixed with misunderstanding.

Personal Records should never automatically validate a rumor simply because it appears in the archive.

---

# Contradiction

Two Personal Records may describe the same event differently.

This is expected.

One person may write:

**"The city has lost power."**

Another person ten kilometers away may write:

**"Everything is normal here."**

Both records may be authentic.

Both may accurately describe the writer's experience.

Neither necessarily describes the entire region.

This supports a fundamental principle of Project Ascension:

**The world changes universally. Human experience remains deeply personal.**

---

# Emotional Range

Personal Records should represent a wide range of human reactions.

People may respond with:

- fear
- denial
- humor
- anger
- compassion
- selfishness
- confusion
- curiosity
- exhaustion
- optimism
- resignation
- courage

There is no single correct human response to The Transition.

Some people prepare early.

Some continue normally.

Some panic.

Some help strangers.

Some exploit uncertainty.

Some refuse to believe anything serious is happening until it affects them personally.

---

# Normalcy Bias

Normalcy Bias should be particularly visible within Personal Records.

People naturally expect tomorrow to resemble yesterday.

A service interruption becomes:

*"It'll probably be fixed tonight."*

A shortage becomes:

*"The delivery is just late."*

An emergency alert becomes:

*"They're being overly cautious."*

A school closure becomes:

*"Probably just for a few days."*

These interpretations are not stupidity.

They reflect ordinary human experience.

Most disruptions throughout a person's life have been temporary.

There is little reason, initially, to assume that this time will be different.

---

# Geography

Personal experience should vary significantly by location.

A person in one city may experience severe infrastructure disruption while another region remains almost entirely normal.

Urban and rural communities may experience different problems.

Local resources matter.

Climate matters.

Infrastructure matters.

Population density matters.

Community relationships matter.

Personal Records should reinforce that The Collapse does not occur everywhere at the same speed.

---

# Time

Personal Records may exist throughout all World States.

## The Connected World

Records primarily establish ordinary life and technological dependency.

## The Transition

Normal life continues while uncertainty and disruption gradually increase.

## The Fractured World

Records become increasingly local, incomplete and difficult to preserve.

## The Reconnection

Personal records begin documenting renewed contact between previously isolated communities.

The format of surviving records may change with the technological conditions of each era.

---

# Record Structure

Personal Records may use metadata such as:

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Document ID | Unique archive identifier |
| Date | Original date |
| Location | Where the record originated |
| Source Type | Message, journal, audio, email, etc. |
| Author | Known identity where appropriate |
| Recipient | Intended recipient where applicable |
| Recovery Status | Condition of recovered record |
| Authenticity | Historical verification status |

Not every Personal Record requires every field.

The structure should reflect the source.

---

# Identity

Not every person appearing in Personal Records needs to become an important character.

Some may appear once.

Others may appear repeatedly across decades.

A seemingly insignificant message may later connect to:

- a settlement
- a faction
- a historical event
- another recovered record
- a player character
- a family history
- a future campaign

Recurring characters should emerge naturally rather than being forced into every record.

---

# Preservation

Personal Records survive for different reasons.

Examples include:

- recovered mobile devices
- local server archives
- printed messages
- personal journals
- damaged storage devices
- community archives
- government data recovery
- family collections
- settlement historical projects

Recovery does not guarantee completeness.

Missing messages, damaged files and incomplete conversations are expected.

---

# Relationship to Other Recovered Records

Personal Records may overlap with events documented elsewhere.

For example:

A federal emergency advisory may appear in:

**Emergency Communications**

while a Personal Record shows a family discussing whether they should take it seriously.

An infrastructure report may document declining grid stability.

A Personal Record may simply say:

**"Power went out again."**

A Military Record may describe network isolation.

A Personal Record may show someone unable to access their workplace systems.

The institutional record explains the event.

The personal record shows its consequence.

Neither replaces the other.

---

# Guiding Principles

## People Do Not Know They Are Living Through History

Characters should respond to their present circumstances.

They should not narrate events like future historians.

---

## Small Details Matter

A missing medication delivery may matter more to a character than a national emergency declaration.

---

## Ordinary Life Continues

People still fall in love.

They still argue.

Children still play.

People still go to work.

Life does not stop simply because history has accelerated.

---

## Avoid Constant Tragedy

Not every Personal Record should be sad.

Humor, affection, boredom and ordinary human behavior make later tragedy more meaningful.

---

## Preserve Uncertainty

Characters should misunderstand events.

They should disagree.

They should change their minds.

---

## Avoid Exposition

Personal Records should not exist primarily to explain lore to the player.

A believable person rarely writes a message explaining information that both sender and recipient already know.

---

## Avoid Convenient Foreshadowing

Characters should not constantly make prophetic statements about civilization ending.

Most people expect problems to pass.

---

# Narrative Function

Personal Records provide something the institutional archives cannot.

Scale.

Institutional documents show civilization responding to crisis.

Personal Records show what that response means to an individual human being.

A national infrastructure failure is abstract.

A father unable to call his daughter is not.

A logistics disruption is abstract.

An insulin delivery that never arrives is not.

A communication outage is abstract.

A message reading:

**"Call me when you get this."**

is not.

---

# Historical Perspective

Later historians reconstructed The Transition through enormous quantities of institutional data.

Those records explain much about how civilization changed.

Personal Records explain something different.

They show why it mattered.

The Collapse was experienced by billions of people who did not know what caused it, where it was heading or whether tomorrow would be better.

Most were not trying to save civilization.

They were trying to get home.

Protect their children.

Find medicine.

Reach someone they loved.

Keep their jobs.

Help their neighbors.

Understand what was happening.

History remembers systems failing.

Personal Records remember the people who were still living inside them.

# Relationship to Project Ascension

Personal Records support several core themes of Project Ascension:

- individual experience
- incomplete information
- normalcy bias
- human relationships
- local reality
- uncertainty
- memory
- loss
- resilience
- hope

They reinforce one of the most important principles of the universe:

**Civilization is experienced at human scale.**

The Collapse may be global.

The story is always personal.
