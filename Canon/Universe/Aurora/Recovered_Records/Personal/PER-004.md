# Historical Context

By early June 2034, previously isolated reports concerning advanced AI systems and technological disruptions had begun receiving broader media attention.

Public information remained limited.

Government officials had not identified a confirmed threat.

Researchers disagreed publicly about whether apparently unrelated incidents shared a common cause.

For most citizens, there was still little reason to alter normal behavior.

The Mitchell conversation reflects this ambiguity.

---

# Changing Perception

PER-004 represents a subtle change from earlier Mitchell family records.

In PER-001, a transportation disruption is treated entirely as an inconvenience.

In PER-003, several disruptions are acknowledged but remain separate.

In PER-004, Sarah begins asking whether those events might be connected.

She does not conclude that they are.

The distinction is important.

The family's understanding has moved from:

**Something happened.**

to:

**Several things have happened.**

and finally:

**Could they be related?**

The answer remains unknown.

# Public Information

The conversation demonstrates a growing problem facing civilians during the early Transition.

Official statements could simultaneously be accurate and unsatisfying.

Authorities could truthfully state:

**There is no evidence of a threat.**

while also stating:

**The situation is being monitored closely.**

Neither statement explains what is happening.

Neither is necessarily misleading.

The uncertainty exists because authorities themselves do not yet possess a complete explanation.

# Historical Annotation

Later historians identified early June 2034 as a period when public perception began changing faster than official understanding.

The change was not primarily caused by a single announcement.

It emerged from accumulation.

A delayed train.

Restricted workplace software.

A school advisory.

A news report.

A government statement.

A conversation with a parent.

Each event was individually explainable.

Together, they began creating something harder to dismiss.

Not evidence.

A pattern.

For Sarah Mitchell, the distinction appears in one surviving sentence:

She was not frightened by what authorities knew.

She was beginning to notice how much they did not.
