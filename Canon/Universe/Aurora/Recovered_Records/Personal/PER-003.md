# Historical Context

PER-003 occurred during a period of increasing minor disruptions across transportation, administrative and networked services.

Most incidents remained temporary.

Public institutions continued operating.

Schools remained open.

Parents continued working.

Local authorities treated disruptions as manageable operational problems.

For families, preparedness remained informal.

A "backup plan" meant determining who could collect a child if a bus was delayed or an after-school program closed early.

It did not yet mean preparing for societal instability.

# Normalcy Bias

PER-003 is frequently cited as an example of normalcy bias during the early Transition.

This should not be interpreted as ignorance.

Every problem described in the record remains manageable.

The school communicates.

Parents make alternative plans.

Employers remain flexible.

Transportation continues operating.

No immediate danger exists.

Every previous experience available to the participants suggests that the disruptions will eventually be resolved.

Their behavior is therefore rational.

The assumption that tomorrow will function much like today has not yet been meaningfully challenged.

# Archive Significance

The historical significance of PER-003 lies partly in the phrase:

# Historical Annotation

Later generations sometimes searched early records for the moment ordinary Americans realized that The Collapse had begun.

No such moment exists.

PER-003 demonstrates why.

Nothing happening to the Mitchell family requires a catastrophic explanation.

A train was delayed.

Workplace software was restricted.

A school warned of possible scheduling changes.

Aftercare remained open.

Maya went to school.

Her grandmother had better snacks.

Life continued.

The historical pattern becomes visible only when these events are viewed together and from the future.

The people experiencing them possessed neither advantage.

To them, Wednesday was inconvenient.

Thursday was expected to be normal.
