# Fragmented Operational Picture Assessment

**Subject:** Degradation of National Common Operational Awareness

---

# Executive Summary

The Department of Defense assesses that the United States retains functional military command authority and significant operational capability.

However, the ability to maintain a single reliable national operational picture is degrading.

Military commands, intelligence agencies, civilian authorities and critical infrastructure operators increasingly receive different information at different times.

Regional assessments are beginning to diverge.

This divergence does not currently indicate command failure.

It reflects increasing differences in:

- network availability
- infrastructure conditions
- containment policies
- intelligence access
- local operational priorities
- reporting latency

The principal concern is no longer whether information exists.

It is whether decision-makers are seeing the same reality.

---

# Current Situation

National command structures remain operational.

Strategic communications remain available.

Military installations continue functioning.

No widespread breakdown of command authority has been identified.

However, the information environment supporting national coordination has changed significantly.

Reports previously generated automatically and distributed continuously are increasingly:

- delayed
- incomplete
- manually compiled
- regionally restricted
- unavailable outside originating networks
- inconsistent with reports from other authorities

This has increased reliance upon local judgment.

---

# Common Operational Picture

Modern military coordination depends upon a shared operational picture.

This does not require every commander to possess identical information.

It requires sufficient common information for different organizations to understand:

- what is occurring
- where it is occurring
- what actions are being taken
- which systems remain available
- which reports can be trusted
- which authority is responsible

That common picture is becoming increasingly difficult to maintain.

---

# Regional Divergence

Operational conditions now vary significantly between commands.

Some regions report:

- stable electrical infrastructure
- reliable communications
- functioning civilian logistics
- limited containment effects

Others report:

- recurring service interruptions
- restricted network access
- reduced civilian transportation
- incomplete infrastructure telemetry
- significant manual workload

These reports are not necessarily contradictory.

They may describe different realities occurring simultaneously.

---

# Intelligence Fragmentation

Intelligence agencies continue collecting substantial amounts of information.

The problem is increasingly one of correlation.

Data may remain available within individual organizations while becoming difficult to combine nationally.

Analysts report:

- duplicated incidents
- conflicting timestamps
- incomplete source histories
- inconsistent anomaly classifications
- missing telemetry
- delayed verification
- incompatible regional reporting formats

The volume of information remains high.

Confidence in the completeness of the overall picture is declining.

---

# Command Decision-Making

Regional commanders increasingly face situations requiring decisions before national verification is available.

Examples include:

- whether to isolate a communications network
- whether to restore automated services
- whether to accept external data feeds
- whether to prioritize military or civilian fuel deliveries
- whether unusual system behavior represents compromise
- whether loss of communication reflects failure, containment or deliberate isolation

Waiting for complete information may delay necessary action.

Acting without complete information may create additional disruption.

Both risks are increasing.

---

# Local Authority

The Department has expanded temporary authority for designated regional commanders to take protective actions necessary to preserve:

- command continuity
- personnel safety
- critical infrastructure
- emergency communications
- logistics
- strategic assets

Such actions should be reported through national command channels whenever communications permit.

This delegation does not alter the constitutional chain of command.

It recognizes the operational reality that centralized decisions cannot always arrive within the time available.

---

# Conflicting Instructions

Several incidents have been identified in which organizations received instructions that appeared contradictory.

Investigation found that most conflicts resulted from:

- different operational conditions
- delayed information
- changing containment status
- separate agency responsibilities
- outdated directives remaining in circulation

No evidence currently indicates systematic manipulation of military orders.

Authentication procedures remain mandatory.

Commanders are instructed to verify unexpected directives through independent channels where possible.

---

# Authentication Concern

The information environment has created increased concern regarding the authenticity of digital communication.

Advanced synthetic media and automated message generation were already significant security challenges before the current crisis.

As trust declines, commanders may increasingly question legitimate communications.

Additional authentication requirements improve security.

They also slow communication.

The Department assesses that both accepting false information and rejecting authentic information now represent significant operational risks.

---

# Restoration Dilemma

Containment-generated disruptions have created pressure to restore automated systems in several regions.

Restoration may improve:

- logistics
- infrastructure visibility
- communications
- resource allocation
- operational efficiency

It may also restore technological pathways previously restricted for security reasons.

Regional commanders therefore face an increasingly difficult choice:

**Restore capability and potentially restore exposure.**

or

**Maintain isolation and accept continued operational degradation.**

No universal answer currently exists.

---

# Civil-Military Coordination

Military commands are increasingly supporting civilian authorities with:

- logistics
- emergency communications
- transportation
- infrastructure assessment
- fuel distribution
- medical supply movement
- technical personnel

These activities remain support operations.

Civilian authority remains in effect.

However, differences in regional conditions mean military support requirements vary significantly across the country.

Some commands remain focused primarily on defense readiness.

Others increasingly support essential civilian functions.

---

# International Situation

Allied governments report similar degradation in shared operational awareness.

International intelligence exchange continues but has slowed.

Some nations have restricted automated cross-border data services.

Others have maintained connectivity in order to preserve economic and infrastructure stability.

No common international containment policy exists.

Different governments are making different calculations from incomplete information.

---

# Strategic Assessment

The Department assesses that the United States has entered a new operational environment.

National capability remains substantial.

National authority remains intact.

National coordination remains possible.

But none can assume immediate access to complete information.

Increasingly, effective operations depend upon regional organizations acting within national objectives while adapting to local conditions.

# Operational Guidance

Until a more reliable national information environment can be restored:

- preserve authenticated command channels
- document regional decisions
- distinguish confirmed information from assessment
- avoid assuming national conditions from local observations
- maintain civilian coordination
- preserve reversible containment options
- share operational data whenever secure pathways remain available
- prioritize continuity over optimization

Regional initiative is authorized where delay creates unacceptable risk.

National command authority remains unchanged.

A statement recorded during a senior command briefing was later preserved in multiple copies of the assessment:

> *"We still have a chain of command. What we are losing is a chain of certainty."*

The original speaker remains unidentified.

# Historical Annotation

MIL-007 is generally considered the final document of the first Military Records sequence associated with the early Containment Crisis.

It is sometimes incorrectly described as evidence that the United States military had fragmented.

It had not.

The chain of command remained intact.

Strategic forces remained under national authority.

Military installations continued operating.

What had begun to fragment was something less visible and, in many ways, equally important:

**the shared understanding of what was happening.**

A commander in one region could reasonably believe conditions were stabilizing.

Another could reasonably believe they were deteriorating.

An intelligence analyst could identify a technological anomaly.

An infrastructure operator could identify the same event as a consequence of containment.

A local authority could experience it simply as another service interruption.

All could be correct from the information available to them.

This represented a fundamental change in the crisis.

The United States still possessed institutions capable of acting nationally.

But those institutions increasingly operated through regional realities that no longer aligned perfectly with one another.

The military had not lost command.

It had begun losing certainty.

And without certainty, every decision carried the possibility that protecting one part of the system would place another at risk.
