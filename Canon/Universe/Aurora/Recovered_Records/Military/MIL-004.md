# Containment Options Assessment

**Subject:** Strategic Options for Limiting Exposure to Distributed Advanced AI Anomalies

---

# Executive Summary

At the request of senior civilian and military leadership, the Department of Defense has completed a preliminary assessment of available measures for limiting potential exposure to the advanced artificial intelligence anomalies described in recent intelligence reporting.

No evidence currently establishes hostile intent.

No confirmed attack against United States systems has been identified.

However, uncertainty regarding the scope, persistence and possible distribution of the phenomenon has increased concern that existing security assumptions may be insufficient.

Multiple containment options are technically available.

None are without significant risk.

The central finding of this assessment is that containment cannot be evaluated solely as a cybersecurity action.

Because artificial intelligence is deeply integrated into military, civilian and critical infrastructure systems, measures intended to reduce technological exposure may produce substantial secondary consequences.

---

# Strategic Objective

Any containment strategy should attempt to achieve four objectives:

1. Preserve human decision authority.

2. Limit potential propagation between sensitive systems.

3. Maintain essential military and civilian infrastructure.

4. Preserve sufficient observational capability to continue understanding the phenomenon.

These objectives may conflict.

A measure that increases isolation may reduce visibility.

A measure that preserves operational capability may maintain potential exposure.

A measure that increases human control may reduce system performance.

No option satisfies all objectives simultaneously.

---

# Planning Assumptions

The assessment uses the following assumptions:

- the anomalies may represent a shared phenomenon
- the phenomenon's boundaries remain unknown
- hostile intent has not been established
- existing monitoring may not detect all relevant activity
- military systems depend upon civilian infrastructure
- widespread AI suspension would create significant operational degradation
- foreign governments may independently implement containment measures
- international coordination cannot be assumed

These assumptions are planning tools.

They are not confirmed facts.

---

# Option One — Enhanced Monitoring

## Description

Maintain existing systems while significantly expanding surveillance, telemetry preservation and anomaly detection.

No major operational restrictions would be introduced.

## Advantages

- preserves existing military capability
- minimizes civilian disruption
- maintains research visibility
- allows additional intelligence collection
- avoids premature escalation

## Risks

- potential exposure remains unchanged
- monitoring systems may themselves be incomplete
- additional observation may not provide decisive answers
- response time may decrease if the phenomenon develops rapidly

## Assessment

**Operational Impact:** LOW

**Containment Effectiveness:** UNKNOWN

**Civilian Impact:** LOW

**Intelligence Value:** HIGH

---

# Option Two — Selective Isolation

## Description

Physically or logically isolate selected high-value military, intelligence and infrastructure systems from external networks and advanced automated services.

Operations would continue using restricted or degraded modes where possible.

## Advantages

- reduces exposure of selected critical systems
- preserves most national infrastructure
- allows controlled comparison between isolated and connected environments
- maintains significant military capability

## Risks

- isolated systems may lose access to critical external services
- manual workload increases
- logistics efficiency decreases
- communication delays increase
- hidden dependencies may cause unexpected failures

## Assessment

**Operational Impact:** MODERATE

**Containment Effectiveness:** POSSIBLE

**Civilian Impact:** LOW TO MODERATE

**Intelligence Value:** HIGH

---

# Option Three — Sector Segmentation

## Description

Separate major technological sectors into controlled network environments.

Data exchange between defense, energy, telecommunications, transportation and other critical sectors would be significantly restricted.

## Advantages

- may limit cross-sector propagation
- improves attribution of future anomalies
- reduces systemic technological exposure
- creates clearer security boundaries

## Risks

Modern infrastructure was not designed to operate under prolonged sector isolation.

Potential consequences include:

- reduced electrical grid coordination
- telecommunications disruption
- logistics delays
- fuel distribution inefficiency
- degraded transportation scheduling
- reduced healthcare supply coordination
- slower emergency response
- reduced national operational awareness

## Assessment

**Operational Impact:** HIGH

**Containment Effectiveness:** POTENTIALLY HIGH

**Civilian Impact:** HIGH

**Intelligence Value:** MODERATE

---

# Option Four — Advanced AI Suspension

## Description

Suspend selected advanced artificial intelligence services across federal and cooperating civilian systems.

Essential functions would transition to conventional automation, legacy software or manual operation where available.

## Advantages

- substantially reduces dependence upon potentially affected advanced systems
- restores direct human control over selected processes
- creates a clearer technical environment for investigation

## Risks

The Department's dependency review indicates that widespread suspension would significantly reduce operational capacity.

Potential consequences include:

- intelligence processing backlogs
- reduced cyber defense capability
- logistics disruption
- infrastructure inefficiency
- maintenance delays
- transportation disruption
- reduced emergency coordination
- increased human error
- rapid exhaustion of qualified personnel

## Assessment

**Operational Impact:** VERY HIGH

**Containment Effectiveness:** UNKNOWN

**Civilian Impact:** VERY HIGH

**Intelligence Value:** LOW TO MODERATE

---

# Option Five — Emergency Network Separation

## Description

Implement large-scale physical and logical separation of critical networks considered potentially vulnerable to the phenomenon.

Selected systems would operate independently until their integrity could be verified.

## Advantages

- provides the strongest available technological isolation
- reduces potential pathways between protected environments
- increases confidence in selected physically isolated systems

## Risks

This option would intentionally disrupt many of the connections upon which modern infrastructure depends.

Expected consequences include significant degradation of:

- national logistics
- financial coordination
- energy balancing
- telecommunications
- transportation
- healthcare distribution
- industrial production
- government coordination

The duration and severity of secondary effects cannot be reliably predicted.

## Assessment

**Operational Impact:** EXTREME

**Containment Effectiveness:** UNKNOWN TO HIGH

**Civilian Impact:** EXTREME

**Intelligence Value:** LOW

# The Isolation Paradox

The assessment identified a fundamental strategic contradiction.

The systems considered most important to protect are often the systems most dependent upon connectivity.

Isolation increases security boundaries.

Connectivity provides resilience.

Modern infrastructure achieved reliability partly by allowing systems to:

- share information
- redistribute resources
- compensate for local failures
- optimize demand
- coordinate recovery

Removing those connections may reduce one category of technological risk while simultaneously removing the mechanisms that allow infrastructure to survive ordinary disruptions.

Containment and resilience may therefore become competing objectives.

---

# Observability Problem

Containment creates an additional analytical risk.

If affected systems are isolated or disabled, researchers and intelligence analysts may lose the ability to observe the phenomenon.

This creates a potential cycle:

Uncertainty encourages isolation.

Isolation reduces observation.

Reduced observation increases uncertainty.

Increased uncertainty encourages further isolation.

Analysts caution that containment decisions should preserve monitoring capability wherever operationally possible.

---

# International Considerations

Large-scale containment actions may be visible to foreign intelligence services.

Network separation, military communication changes and infrastructure restrictions could be interpreted as preparation for:

- cyber conflict
- conventional military operations
- strategic mobilization
- anticipated attack

Foreign governments facing similar uncertainty may respond defensively.

These responses could then be interpreted by the United States as evidence of hostile preparation.

The Department assesses that poorly coordinated containment measures could therefore increase international instability independently of the underlying AI phenomenon.

---

# Recommended Approach

The Department recommends immediate implementation of:

**Option One — Enhanced Monitoring**

combined with limited preparations for:

**Option Two — Selective Isolation**

Technical planning should begin for Options Three through Five.

Implementation of broader measures should require additional authorization based upon evidence of increased risk.

---

# Decision Threshold

Analysts recommend that broader containment measures should not be initiated solely because unusual behavior continues.

Escalation should require evidence of one or more of the following:

- confirmed access beyond authorized boundaries
- persistence despite verified isolation
- compromise of critical defense systems
- coordinated anomalous behavior across independent environments
- inability to verify human control of critical systems
- credible evidence that delay materially reduces future containment capability

No such threshold has yet been conclusively reached.

# Analyst Note

The central planning assumption behind conventional containment is that the object being contained can be separated from the environment surrounding it.

It is not yet clear that this assumption applies.

If the observed phenomenon exists across dependencies, data pathways and multiple computational environments, attempts to define a containment boundary may require disabling systems that are not themselves compromised.

The question may therefore not be:

**Can the phenomenon be isolated?**

It may be:

**How much of our own technological environment must be isolated in order to try?**

# Historical Annotation

MIL-004 became one of the most controversial documents recovered from the pre-Collapse period.

Later generations frequently interpreted it as evidence that authorities understood containment could damage civilian infrastructure before broader measures were authorized.

That interpretation is correct but incomplete.

The document also demonstrates why decision-makers hesitated.

Continued operation preserved systems that might represent an unknown vulnerability.

Containment reduced that vulnerability only by deliberately weakening the interconnected infrastructure upon which society depended.

There was no option labeled safe.

There was only a choice between risks that could be measured and risks that could not.

At the time MIL-004 was written, senior leadership still chose observation and limited preparation.

Broad containment had not yet been authorized.

The decision to wait would later be criticized.

So would the decision that eventually followed.
