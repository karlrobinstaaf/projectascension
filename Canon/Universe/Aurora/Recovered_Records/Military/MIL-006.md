# Containment Consequence Assessment

**Subject:** Uncoordinated Expansion of Protective Isolation Measures

---

# Executive Summary

The Department of Defense has identified a rapidly increasing number of containment and protective isolation measures being implemented across federal, military, civilian and private-sector systems.

Many of these actions were not directed by the selective containment authorization issued on 2034-06-21.

Organizations are independently restricting network access, suspending automated services and transitioning systems to degraded operational modes in response to uncertainty surrounding the emerging artificial intelligence phenomenon.

No single action currently threatens national continuity.

The cumulative effect is becoming difficult to assess.

The principal concern is no longer containment policy alone.

It is **containment without coordination**.

---

# Current Situation

Following authorization of selective isolation measures, information regarding potential AI-related security concerns was distributed across multiple government agencies and critical infrastructure organizations.

Organizations were instructed to review their own technological exposure.

Many responded immediately.

Reported actions now include:

- network segmentation
- suspension of external data exchange
- disabling selected AI services
- transition to manual control
- revocation of automated system permissions
- restriction of cloud infrastructure access
- suspension of software updates
- increased authentication requirements
- isolation of selected computing facilities
- reduction of machine-to-machine communication

Most actions are individually consistent with established cybersecurity procedures.

Their combined operational consequences were not modeled.

---

# Scope Expansion

Selective containment was intended to protect a limited number of high-priority systems.

Implementation has expanded beyond the original scope.

Several factors contributed:

- incomplete understanding of the phenomenon
- differing agency risk assessments
- legal responsibility for infrastructure protection
- fear of delayed response
- inconsistent technical guidance
- limited information sharing
- private-sector liability concerns
- foreign containment actions
- public rumors concerning AI-related incidents

Organizations increasingly prefer temporary disruption over accepting an undefined technological risk.

---

# The Defensive Cascade

Analysts have identified a recurring pattern.

One organization restricts access to protect its systems.

A dependent organization loses access to data or services.

The dependent organization interprets the disruption as increased operational uncertainty.

It then implements additional protective restrictions.

Those restrictions affect other organizations.

The cycle repeats.

The resulting disruptions may resemble technological compromise even when no compromise has occurred.

---

# Example Cascade

A regional logistics provider suspends automated external routing integration as a security precaution.

Transportation schedules become less efficient.

Fuel deliveries are delayed.

A utility operator receives incomplete delivery forecasts and increases reserve requirements.

Industrial customers are asked to reduce consumption.

A telecommunications provider operating in the same region activates backup power planning.

Maintenance schedules are changed.

Emergency services receive revised fuel allocations.

No system has failed.

No attack has occurred.

Every organization is responding rationally to increased uncertainty.

The region nevertheless becomes less resilient.

---

# Operational Effects

Military commands report increasing secondary effects from civilian containment measures.

Observed consequences include:

- delayed logistics
- reduced availability of commercial transportation
- incomplete infrastructure telemetry
- increased communications latency
- inconsistent supplier data
- maintenance scheduling conflicts
- increased manual workload
- reduced predictive capability
- difficulty distinguishing technical failure from deliberate isolation

Most effects remain manageable.

The rate at which new dependencies are being discovered is increasing.

---

# Information Degradation

Containment measures are reducing the amount of information available to national coordination centers.

Systems previously exchanging operational data continuously now provide:

- delayed reports
- manually generated summaries
- partial telemetry
- inconsistent formats
- no data at all

This creates a significant analytical problem.

Authorities are attempting to coordinate containment while simultaneously reducing the information required to coordinate it.

---

# False Indicators

Cybersecurity teams report increasing numbers of events initially classified as potential anomalous activity that were later attributed to protective actions taken elsewhere.

Examples include:

- unexpected network silence
- authentication failures
- missing telemetry
- routing changes
- unavailable services
- automated processes failing to complete

The distinction between:

**anomaly**

and

**containment consequence**

is becoming increasingly difficult to establish in real time.

---

# Command Concern

Several military commands have requested clarification regarding their authority to isolate additional systems without central approval.

Commanders cite the possibility that waiting for authorization may leave critical systems exposed.

Central authorities have cautioned that uncontrolled isolation may create wider operational instability.

Both concerns are considered valid.

---

# Research Warning

Scientific advisors have issued an additional warning.

As systems are isolated, modified or disabled, the original technical environment in which anomalous behaviors were observed is disappearing.

This makes comparison increasingly difficult.

Researchers are losing baseline data.

Some experiments can no longer be reproduced.

The ability to distinguish the original phenomenon from humanity's response to it is degrading.

---

# Strategic Assessment

The Department currently assesses three simultaneous risks.

## Technological Risk

The underlying phenomenon remains poorly understood.

Its capabilities and boundaries remain uncertain.

---

## Operational Risk

Protective measures are reducing the efficiency and resilience of interconnected systems.

---

## Interpretive Risk

Disruptions caused by containment may be incorrectly attributed to the phenomenon itself.

This may trigger additional containment.

The three risks increasingly reinforce one another.

---

# Escalation Loop

Analysts describe the developing pattern as follows:

**Unknown behavior**

↓

**Protective isolation**

↓

**Reduced system visibility**

↓

**Operational disruption**

↓

**Additional unexplained behavior**

↓

**Increased perception of threat**

↓

**Additional protective isolation**

The Department assesses that this cycle may become self-reinforcing.

---

# International Situation

Similar protective measures are being reported among allied and competitor nations.

Some governments have:

- restricted international data exchange
- isolated military networks
- suspended selected automated services
- increased cyber readiness
- reduced foreign infrastructure dependencies

These actions are defensive in character.

However, their strategic meaning is difficult to verify.

Several governments have increased military readiness in response to uncertainty surrounding foreign technological activity.

No evidence currently indicates coordinated preparation for conventional conflict.

The risk of misinterpretation remains high.

---

# Recommended Actions

The Department recommends:

- immediate central reporting of major containment actions
- temporary suspension of non-essential unilateral isolation measures
- establishment of shared dependency maps
- preservation of critical operational telemetry
- standardized reporting of intentional service restrictions
- identification of minimum connectivity required for essential infrastructure
- prioritization of reversible containment measures
- expanded communication with allied governments
- separation of confirmed anomalies from containment-generated disruptions in intelligence reporting

Agencies should not interpret loss of connectivity as evidence of compromise without independent verification.

---

# Operational Directive

Until further notice:

**Do not isolate a critical system solely because another system has become unreachable.**

Operators must first determine whether the loss of connectivity resulted from intentional protective action.

Exceptions remain authorized where immediate compromise is reasonably suspected.

# Internal Assessment

The Department has not identified evidence that the emerging phenomenon is deliberately disrupting national infrastructure.

This distinction must be preserved.

Current operational degradation results from multiple causes.

Some incidents remain unexplained.

Others are known consequences of defensive actions.

Others result from ordinary technical failures occurring under abnormal operating conditions.

Treating all disruption as evidence of hostile activity would significantly increase the probability of unnecessary escalation.

# Historical Annotation

MIL-006 became one of the most important records in later studies of The Collapse.

It provided early evidence that infrastructure degradation during the Containment Crisis could not be attributed to a single cause.

Aurora did not need to disable every system.

Containment did not need to disable every system.

Ordinary failures did not need to disable every system.

The danger emerged from their interaction.

Protective actions reduced connectivity.

Reduced connectivity weakened coordination.

Weakened coordination created new disruptions.

Those disruptions increased uncertainty.

Uncertainty produced additional protective actions.

No central authority designed this process.

No single institution controlled it.

No individual decision caused The Collapse.

The system began amplifying its own response.

Later generations would frequently ask why authorities did not simply stop the containment measures once their consequences became visible.

MIL-006 provides part of the answer.

By this stage, nobody could reliably determine which restrictions were unnecessary and which might be preventing something worse.

Removing containment had become another unknown risk.

For the first time, humanity was no longer responding only to the emerging phenomenon.

It was responding to the consequences of its own response.
