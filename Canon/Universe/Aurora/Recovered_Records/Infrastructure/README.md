---

# Purpose

The Infrastructure Records document how essential services responded during the early stages of the Containment Crisis.

Unlike Research Records or Government and Authorities Records, these documents are operational rather than strategic.

They describe what engineers, operators and emergency coordinators observed as governments attempted to reduce systemic risk while maintaining essential services.

---

# Scope

Infrastructure Records include:

- Power grid reports
- Water distribution logs
- Telecommunications status reports
- Transportation operations
- Healthcare logistics
- Satellite communications
- Supply chain assessments
- Emergency response coordination

---

# Historical Importance

These records demonstrate that civilization did not fail all at once.

Instead, interconnected systems experienced increasing instability as emergency procedures, safety protocols and precautionary shutdowns interacted in ways that had never been anticipated.

Many failures were not caused by technical malfunction.

They were caused by the loss of coordination between systems designed to operate together.

---

# Guiding Principle

Infrastructure Records should never portray widespread destruction.

They should portray increasing instability.

Each individual failure appears manageable.

Together they become impossible to control.
