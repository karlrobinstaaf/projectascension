# National Emergency Communications Transition

**Subject:** Transition to Regional Emergency Coordination

Federal emergency management authorities announce the implementation of an updated national communication framework in response to increasing regional variation in operational conditions.

Effective immediately, primary public guidance concerning local infrastructure, public safety and emergency response will be issued through state and regional emergency management agencies.

Federal authorities will continue providing national coordination, resource support and strategic guidance.

---

# Purpose

The transition is intended to improve the speed, accuracy and relevance of public information by allowing communities to receive guidance reflecting their specific operational circumstances.

Differences in regional advisories should not be interpreted as evidence of conflicting national policy.

They reflect differing local conditions.

---

# Federal Responsibilities

Federal agencies will continue to:

- Coordinate interstate logistics.
- Support critical infrastructure restoration.
- Maintain national emergency planning.
- Assist state emergency operations centers.
- Provide strategic resource allocation.

National institutions remain fully operational.

---

# Public Guidance

Citizens should:

- Follow instructions issued by their local emergency management agency.
- Monitor regional emergency communication channels.
- Continue practicing reasonable household preparedness.
- Assist neighbors whenever safe to do so.
- Report emergencies through established local procedures.

Local authorities remain the primary source of operational guidance.

---

# Closing Statement

The strength of emergency response depends upon cooperation between federal, state and local authorities.

Communities across the nation continue demonstrating resilience, professionalism and mutual support during this period of operational uncertainty.

Federal agencies remain committed to supporting every region for as long as necessary.

# Historical Annotation

Historians generally regard EC-006 as the final document of the National Emergency Communications archive.

Contrary to many later accounts, the federal government did not cease functioning following this announcement.

Instead, it formally recognized that effective emergency management increasingly depended upon regional leadership responding to local conditions.

This administrative transition marked the end of the Connected World's centralized communication model.

From this point forward, surviving records become overwhelmingly regional and local in character.

For many historians, EC-006 represents the final coordinated public voice of the United States before the nation entered the era later known as **The Fractured World**.
