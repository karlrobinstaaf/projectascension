# Address to the Nation

**The President of the United States**

My fellow Americans,

Over the past several weeks, many of you have experienced disruptions to services that have long been part of everyday life.

Some communities have faced temporary interruptions to transportation, communications or public utilities.

Others have noticed little change at all.

I understand that this uncertainty has raised understandable questions.

Tonight, I want to speak to you directly.

---

Our nation continues to function.

Hospitals remain open.

Emergency responders continue serving their communities.

Our energy systems remain operational.

State and local governments continue working together with federal agencies across the country.

These facts remain true.

---

At the same time, we face challenges unlike those for which many of our existing systems were designed.

Complex infrastructure requires constant coordination.

That coordination has become increasingly difficult.

Thousands of dedicated public servants, engineers, healthcare workers, emergency responders and community leaders are working around the clock to meet those challenges.

They deserve our gratitude.

---

Throughout our history, Americans have faced uncertainty before.

We have overcome natural disasters.

Economic hardship.

Acts of violence.

Moments when the future seemed impossible to predict.

We overcame them not because we possessed perfect information.

We overcame them because we trusted one another.

---

In the coming days, you may receive guidance from your governors, mayors and local emergency officials that differs from recommendations issued elsewhere.

This does not mean our nation is divided.

It means your local leaders are responding to the needs of your communities.

Please continue following their guidance.

---

I ask every American to do three simple things.

Remain informed.

Help your neighbors.

Stay calm.

Prepared communities are resilient communities.

---

We do not yet have every answer.

No responsible leader should claim otherwise.

But we will continue seeking those answers together.

Our strength has never depended solely upon the systems we have built.

It has always depended upon the people who built them.

Thank you.

May God bless you.

And may God bless the United States of America.

---

# Historical Annotation

Historians generally regard EC-005 as the final presidential address delivered before the Containment Crisis fundamentally altered the relationship between the federal government and the American public.

Nothing stated during the address was knowingly false.

Government institutions continued functioning.

Essential services remained available across much of the country.

Its historical significance lies in its tone.

The President acknowledged uncertainty without surrendering confidence, urged national unity without minimizing the challenges ahead, and recognized that local communities would increasingly shape the nation's response.

For many survivors, this speech became the final moment they felt that the United States still spoke with one voice.
