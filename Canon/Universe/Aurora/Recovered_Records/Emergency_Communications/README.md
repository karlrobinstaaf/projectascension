---

# Purpose

The Emergency Communications archive documents the official messages issued by national, regional and local authorities as the Containment Crisis entered its public emergency phase.

Unlike Media Records, these documents do not seek to explain events.

They provide instructions.

Warnings.

Recommendations.

Reassurance.

Their purpose is not journalism.

It is public safety.

---

# Scope

Emergency Communications include:

- Emergency Alert System broadcasts
- FEMA bulletins
- State emergency notifications
- Civil defense messages
- Public safety announcements
- Infrastructure advisories
- Shelter instructions
- Travel restrictions
- Resource conservation notices

---

# Guiding Principle

Emergency Communications should always remain calm.

Even when the situation is rapidly deteriorating.

Every message should attempt to reduce panic while providing practical guidance.

The archive illustrates how governments continued communicating long after they had begun losing the ability to coordinate events nationally.

---

# Historical Importance

These records represent the final coordinated public voice of the Connected World.

As communications infrastructure fragmented, national broadcasts gradually gave way to regional and eventually local emergency messages.

The progression of these documents mirrors the gradual fragmentation of society itself.
