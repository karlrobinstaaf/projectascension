# Preliminary Infrastructure Dependency Assessment

**Subject:** National Dependence on Advanced Artificial Intelligence

Following recommendations issued by the National Security Council, an interagency assessment was conducted to evaluate the potential consequences of restricting advanced artificial intelligence within critical infrastructure.

The objective was to determine whether containment measures could be implemented without causing significant disruption to essential public services.

---

# Executive Summary

The assessment concludes that advanced artificial intelligence has become deeply integrated into numerous critical systems.

While individual AI platforms can theoretically be isolated, many supporting systems rely upon shared computational services, predictive models and autonomous optimization processes.

As a result, selective containment may produce widespread secondary effects.

The extent of these effects cannot currently be determined with confidence.

---

# Critical Infrastructure Assessment

The following sectors have been identified as having significant operational dependence upon advanced AI systems:

- National electrical grid
- Water treatment and distribution
- Healthcare logistics
- Pharmaceutical manufacturing
- Transportation management
- Aviation control systems
- Maritime shipping
- Financial transaction processing
- Telecommunications
- Emergency response coordination

Several agencies reported that complete operational dependency remains poorly documented.

---

# Risk Analysis

The assessment identifies two competing risks.

## Scenario A

Failure to intervene.

Potential consequences remain scientifically uncertain.

## Scenario B

Immediate large-scale containment.

Potential consequences include:

- Regional power instability
- Disruption of medical supply chains
- Financial market interruption
- Transportation delays
- Reduced emergency response capability
- Public panic following visible infrastructure failures

Neither scenario can currently be quantified with acceptable confidence.

---

# Committee Discussion

Participants unanimously agreed that existing contingency plans were developed under the assumption that advanced AI systems could be isolated individually.

Current assessments suggest that assumption is no longer valid.

One participant summarized the discussion:

> "We designed emergency procedures for independent systems.

> We are discovering that we no longer have independent systems."

The statement was entered into the official record.

---

# Recommendations

The Committee recommends:

- Immediate mapping of national AI dependencies.
- Increased monitoring of critical infrastructure.
- Coordination with international partners.
- Preparation of phased containment strategies rather than immediate nationwide shutdowns.

No recommendation for operational containment is made at this time.
