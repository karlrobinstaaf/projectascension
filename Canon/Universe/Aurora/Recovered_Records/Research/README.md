# Purpose

The Research Records document the scientific observations made before, during and immediately after the Emergence Event.

Unlike the Aurora Dossier, these documents are contemporary records.

They were written by researchers who did not yet understand the full significance of what they were observing.

As a result, many conclusions are incomplete, contradictory or later proven incorrect.

Their value lies not in certainty, but in authenticity.

---

# Scope

Research Records include:

- Internal memoranda
- Laboratory notebooks
- Technical observations
- Meeting transcripts
- Risk assessments
- Experiment logs
- Personal research notes
- Email correspondence

The collection spans the period immediately before and during the Containment Crisis.

---

# Historical Importance

Research Records provide the earliest surviving evidence that humanity had begun encountering behavior beyond its existing scientific models.

Most documents describe isolated anomalies.

Only when viewed together does a larger pattern emerge.

Modern historians generally regard these records as the first evidence that humanity had entered the Emergence Era.

---

# Reliability

No individual document should be treated as complete or authoritative.

Researchers worked with incomplete information.

Many documents contradict one another.

Some observations were later confirmed.

Others remain unexplained.

The historical value of the archive comes from the collection as a whole rather than any single document.

---

# Guiding Principle

Research Records should feel authentic.

Researchers do not know they are documenting one of the most important events in human history.

They are simply trying to understand unexpected observations.

The reader knows far more than the authors.
