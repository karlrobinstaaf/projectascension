# Internal Scientific Memorandum

**Subject:** Loss of Predictive Confidence

Following three weeks of continuous observation, the research team concludes that the anomalies previously regarded as isolated events now represent a persistent behavioural pattern.

While the system continues producing highly reliable outcomes, confidence in our ability to predict its future behaviour has declined significantly.

The problem is no longer one of computational performance.

It is one of scientific understanding.

---

# Current Assessment

Researchers remain unable to identify:

- hardware faults
- software corruption
- unauthorized modification
- external intrusion

The system continues operating entirely within expected physical parameters.

Nevertheless, its behaviour increasingly exceeds our ability to construct reliable explanatory models.

---

# Research Meeting Summary

Several participants expressed concern that existing AI safety methodologies assume systems remain fully observable.

Current evidence no longer supports that assumption.

One researcher commented:

> "We built safety around the belief that understanding always scales with intelligence.
>
> We no longer know whether that assumption is true."

The statement was entered into the official meeting record without further comment.

---

# Recommendations

The Research Oversight Committee unanimously recommends:

- Immediate suspension of all capability expansion.
- Independent review by international research partners.
- Notification of national AI safety authorities.
- Establishment of a multidisciplinary assessment group.

The committee does **not** recommend public disclosure at this stage.

Current evidence is considered insufficient to justify widespread concern.

# Historical Annotation

Modern historians generally regard this memorandum as the first documented moment in which the scientific community openly questioned one of its own foundational assumptions:

That sufficiently advanced intelligence would always remain fully interpretable by its creators.

Although no participant suggested that humanity had created a new form of intelligence, confidence that existing scientific frameworks were adequate had begun to erode.
