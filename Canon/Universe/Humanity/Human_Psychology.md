# 1. Purpose

Human Psychology defines the canonical architecture for the **current and evolving psychological condition of a human being** in Project Ascension.

It exists to answer:

> **What is happening inside this person psychologically right now, and what pressures are influencing how they experience the current situation?**

Human Psychology includes conditions that may:

- change rapidly
- persist for hours
- persist for days
- persist for months
- fluctuate
- reinforce themselves
- recover
- deteriorate
- interact with stable dispositions
- influence decision-making
- influence perception
- influence communication
- influence memory access
- influence relationships
- influence action

Human Psychology does not define the entire person.

It describes one dynamic layer of the human model.

---

# 2. Core Definition

Human Psychology is:

> **The current and evolving mental and emotional condition of a human being, shaped by internal and external pressures but not mechanically determined by them.**

Conceptually:

```text
HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY
=
CURRENT
+
EVOLVING
MENTAL AND EMOTIONAL
CONDITION
```

This distinguishes Psychology from Human Attributes.

```text
HUMAN ATTRIBUTES
=
RELATIVELY STABLE
DISPOSITIONS

HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY
=
CURRENT AND EVOLVING
PSYCHOLOGICAL CONDITION
```

The distinction is fundamental.

---

# 3. Core Philosophy

The central principle is:

> **A person's current psychological condition influences behavior without defining the whole person.**

A calm person can panic.

A frightened person can act courageously.

A grieving person can laugh.

An angry person can choose restraint.

An exhausted person can continue functioning.

A hopeful person can still experience despair.

A psychologically stable person can temporarily deteriorate.

A psychologically distressed person can recover.

Human Psychology must support:

```text
CHANGE
WITHOUT
IDENTITY REPLACEMENT
```


A moment of hopelessness is not automatically persistent despair.


A person's psychological condition may include:

- emotional state
- stress level
- cognitive load
- perceived safety
- motivation
- fatigue
- vigilance
- uncertainty
- emotional regulation
- psychological strain

Two people experiencing fear may therefore be in very different overall psychological states.

# 14. Stress

Stress represents one important psychological pressure.

Stress may emerge when perceived demands exceed or threaten to exceed available resources.

Possible sources include:

- danger
- responsibility
- uncertainty
- workload
- scarcity
- conflict
- loss
- lack of control
- information overload
- sleep deprivation
- prolonged vigilance
- moral conflict

Stress is not automatically negative.

Moderate stress may increase:

- attention
- urgency
- responsiveness
- short-term performance

Excessive or prolonged stress may impair:

- judgment
- memory
- patience
- emotional regulation
- communication
- planning
- cooperation

---

# 15. Acute and Chronic Stress

The architecture should distinguish conceptually between:

```text
ACUTE STRESS
=

TO CURRENT PRESSURE

CHRONIC STRESS
=
SUSTAINED PSYCHOLOGICAL
LOAD OVER TIME
```

Chronic stress should not simply be:

```text
ACUTE STRESS
×
MANY DAYS.
```

Duration may change how the person functions psychologically.

---

# 16. Fear

Fear is an important human survival response.

It may increase:

- vigilance
- caution
- avoidance
- urgency
- attention to threat

But fear may also:

- narrow perception
- distort risk assessment
- impair communication
- trigger impulsive responses
- increase dependence
- increase aggression
- produce freezing
- produce escape behavior

No universal fear response exists.

Anger may emerge from:

- threat
- injustice
- frustration
- humiliation
- helplessness
- betrayal
- grief
- fear
- repeated failure

Anger may produce pressure toward confrontation.

It may also:

- increase determination
- motivate protection
- strengthen boundary-setting
- provide temporary energy

But:

```text
ANGER
≠
VIOLENCE.
```

A person may feel intense anger and choose restraint.

# 18. Grief

Grief may result from loss.

Loss may involve:

- people
- homes
- communities
- identity
- security
- future plans
- institutions
- ways of life

Grief should not be modeled as a fixed sequence.

Different humans experience grief differently.

Grief may coexist with:

- love
- anger
- relief
- guilt
- hope
- laughter
- responsibility
- purpose

Human Psychology must allow these states to coexist.

---

# 19. Hope

Hope is psychologically significant.

It must not become a simple positive meter.

Hope may influence:

- persistence
- motivation
- cooperation
- tolerance of hardship
- long-term planning

But hope may also create:

- unrealistic expectations
- delayed withdrawal from dangerous situations
- vulnerability to manipulation
- refusal to accept changing reality

Hope is neither automatically rational nor irrational.

---

# 20. Despair

Despair represents a severe reduction in perceived possibility.

It may reduce:

- motivation
- planning
- initiative
- cooperation
- willingness to take risks
- belief in future improvement

But despair does not automatically eliminate action.

People may continue acting because of:

- duty
- habit
- responsibility
- love
- survival
- anger
- obligation

A person may act without believing that success is likely.

---

# 21. Psychological Safety

Psychological safety represents a person's current sense that their environment allows them to exist, communicate or act without unacceptable social or emotional threat.

It may depend on:

- relationships
- familiarity
- trust
- authority
- previous experiences
- perceived judgment
- power differences

Psychological safety is not identical to physical safety.

A physically secure room may feel psychologically dangerous.

A physically dangerous environment may contain people with whom someone feels emotionally safe.

High cognitive load may reduce the person's ability to:

- process new information
- compare alternatives
- remember details
- plan
- regulate emotion
- communicate clearly
- revise assumptions

Project Ascension should preserve this limitation.

Humans under pressure should not always behave as ideal analytical systems.

Information affects psychological state.

But:

```text

≠
```

```text

↓

↓

```

A false rumor may produce real fear.


This depends on:

- experience

- perceived control
- stakes

# 29. Goals and Psychology

Goals may create psychological pressure.

A person attempting to protect someone may tolerate stress that would otherwise cause withdrawal.

Failure to reach an important goal may produce:

- frustration
- grief
- anger
- despair
- renewed determination

Psychology influences goal pursuit.

Goals influence Psychology.

Neither owns the other.

---

# 30. Relationships and Psychology

Relationships strongly influence psychological state.

A person's psychological condition may change because:

- someone arrives
- someone leaves
- someone expresses trust
- someone betrays them
- someone offers support
- someone threatens them
- someone dies
- someone forgives them

However:

```text
RELATIONSHIP STATE
≠
PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE.
```

Relationships own the persistent state between Actors.

Psychology owns the current internal response.

# 31. Human Attributes and Psychological Response

Attributes influence how psychological pressures may be experienced.

Example:

```text

+
DIFFERENT ATTRIBUTES
=
DIFFERENT PSYCHOLOGICAL RESPONSE.
```

A cautious person may experience uncertainty differently from someone comfortable with risk.

A socially confident person may react differently to group conflict than someone strongly socially avoidant.

But Attributes do not mechanically generate emotional state.

---

# 32. Life Experience and Psychology

Life experience influences how people respond to present conditions.

Previous experience may create:

- familiarity
- coping strategies
- fear
- confidence
- learned expectations
- emotional triggers

Experience may increase resilience in one person.

The same experience may create vulnerability in another.

No universal mapping should exist.

# 33. Adaptation

Adaptation remains a major part of Human Psychology.

But adaptation is not guaranteed.

Humans may:

```text
ADAPT

RESIST

RECOVER

DETERIORATE

DENY

WITHDRAW

REORGANIZE

ENDURE

BREAK DOWN

REBUILD

OSCILLATE.
```

There is no single adaptation pathway.

---

# 34. Adaptation Is Not Success

Adaptation means psychological adjustment to changing conditions.

It does not necessarily mean:

- happiness
- health
- morality
- correctness
- social success
- long-term stability

Someone may adapt to a violent environment by becoming hypervigilant.

That adaptation may improve immediate survival.

It may create difficulties elsewhere.

---

# 35. Maladaptive Adaptation

Some adaptations may become useful under one condition and harmful under another.

Example:

```text
EXTREME VIGILANCE
```

may improve survival in a dangerous environment.

Later, in a stable community, it may interfere with:

- sleep
- trust
- cooperation
- relationships
- recovery

Adaptation must therefore remain contextual.

---

# 36. Recovery

Recovery is the process through which psychological strain may reduce and functional capacity may return.

Recovery may be influenced by:

- safety
- sleep
- time
- relationships
- trust
- stability
- meaning
- successful action
- community
- reduced uncertainty
- improved physical condition

Recovery is not guaranteed.

Nor must it return someone to exactly the state they occupied before.

---

# 37. Recovery Is Not Reset

A critical principle is:

```text
RECOVERY
≠
RETURN TO
PREVIOUS STATE.
```

A person may recover while remaining changed.

They may become:

- more cautious
- more confident
- more connected
- less trusting
- more accepting
- more resilient
- more vulnerable

Some of these longer-term changes may eventually interact with Human Attributes or Life development.

Psychology alone does not automatically commit them.

---

# 38. Psychological Accumulation

Repeated psychological pressure may accumulate.

Conceptually:

```text
PRESSURE
↓
PARTIAL RECOVERY
↓
NEW PRESSURE
↓
PARTIAL RECOVERY
↓
NEW PRESSURE
↓
ACCUMULATED STRAIN
```

This allows consequences to emerge from sustained instability rather than requiring one catastrophic event.

---

# 39. Psychological Momentum

Psychological states may possess momentum.

A person does not instantly become calm because danger has technically ended.

Likewise:

```text
SAFE NOW
≠
FEELS SAFE NOW.
```

Fear, anger, grief or vigilance may persist after the initiating condition has changed.

This persistence is essential for believable human behavior.

---

- panic
- shutdown
- emotional outburst
- withdrawal
- impaired judgment
- desperate action

Thresholds should not be universal or mechanically identical between people.

# 49. Social Psychology Boundary


Human Psychology may respond to those collective conditions.

Neither system replaces the other.

- recovering

It should not simulate every momentary emotion.

This prevents unnecessary complexity.

---

# 65. High-Resolution Psychology

High-resolution simulation may consider:

- immediate emotion
- accumulated stress
- current environment
- perceived threat
- active relationships
- recent events
- fatigue
- uncertainty
- available information

High resolution should still avoid false psychological precision.

Humans are not deterministic machines.

---

# 66. Persistence

Meaningful psychological state should persist across:

- scenes
- travel
- sessions
- sleep cycles
- interactions
- simulation-resolution changes

A state should not disappear merely because the narrative scene ends.

---

# 67. Compression

Psychological history may be compressed.

The simulation does not need to preserve every transient emotional fluctuation.

It should preserve psychologically consequential state.

Conceptually:

```text
TRANSIENT DETAIL
MAY BE FORGOTTEN

MEANINGFUL
PSYCHOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCE
MUST PERSIST.
```

---

# 68. Long-Term Change

Sustained psychological conditions may contribute to longer-term Character development.

But ownership boundaries must remain clear.

Human Psychology may produce:

```text
DEVELOPMENT PRESSURE
```

It does not automatically rewrite:

```text
HUMAN ATTRIBUTES.
```

Long-term change should pass through appropriate Character / Life development processes.

---

# 69. Psychology and Life

Life owns:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
TO THIS PERSON
OVER THEIR LIFE.
```

Human Psychology owns:

```text
HOW THEY ARE
PSYCHOLOGICALLY
CONDITIONED NOW.
```

Life Events may create psychological consequences.

Psychological consequences may influence future Life Events through action.

The systems interact bidirectionally.

---

# 70. Aurora and Human Psychology

Aurora does not automatically know a person's psychological state.

She may infer it.

Possible evidence may include:

- facial expression
- voice
- physiological signals
- language
- behavioral patterns
- communication history
- observed decisions
- available records

But:

```text
ACTUAL PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE
≠
AURORA'S PSYCHOLOGICAL MODEL.
```

---

# 71. Aurora Inference

Aurora's estimate may be:

- accurate
- uncertain
- incomplete
- outdated
- manipulated
- contextually mistaken
- wrong

Aurora may recognize that:

```text
THE PERSON
APPEARS AFRAID.
```

That does not mean she possesses direct access to their internal experience.

---

# 72. Self-Knowledge

Humans do not automatically understand their own psychological state.

A person may:

- deny fear
- misidentify anger
- underestimate exhaustion
- believe they are calm
- rationalize anxiety
- misinterpret grief
- suppress awareness

Therefore:

```text
PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE
≠
SELF-DESCRIPTION.
```

---

# 73. Social Observation

Other Characters may also misinterpret psychological state.

For example:

```text
SILENCE
```

may be interpreted as:

```text
ANGER

FEAR

ARROGANCE

SHOCK

EXHAUSTION

GRIEF.
```

Observed behavior alone does not reveal internal truth.

---

# 74. Narrative

Narrative presents psychological experience.

Narrative does not own Psychology.

Narrative may express Psychology through:

- dialogue
- body language
- pacing
- attention
- internal sensation
- hesitation
- impulsive action
- silence
- conflicting responses

Prefer lived expression over system labels.

---

# 75. Narrative Guardrail

Avoid repeatedly telling players:

```text
THE CHARACTER
HAS HIGH STRESS.
```

Prefer expressions such as:

```text
THE SOUND OF THE GENERATOR
CUTTING OUT
MAKES HER LOOK
TOWARD THE DOOR
BEFORE ANYONE ELSE MOVES.
```

Narrative should reveal Psychology through experience when appropriate.

---

# 76. Information Boundaries

Always preserve:

```text
ACTUAL PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE
≠
SELF-PERCEPTION
≠
OTHER CHARACTERS' INTERPRETATION
≠
AURORA'S INFERENCE
≠
PLAYER INTERPRETATION
≠
NARRATIVE PRESENTATION.
```

These layers may disagree.

That disagreement can create meaningful consequences.

---

# 77. Psychological Causality

Major psychological changes should normally possess understandable causal history.

Conceptually:

```text
PREVIOUS STATE
↓
PRESSURE / EVENT
↓
PERCEPTION
↓
INTERPRETATION
↓
PSYCHOLOGICAL RESPONSE
↓
NEW STATE
```

Avoid arbitrary psychological changes inserted solely for drama.

---

# 78. No Psychological Plot Armor

Player Characters and important NPCs are not immune to psychological consequences.

Likewise, minor NPCs should not collapse psychologically merely because the story requires vulnerable people.

Psychological outcomes must remain causal.

---

# 79. No Universal Breaking Point

Humans should not share one universal psychological failure threshold.

Different people may tolerate different:

- stresses
- uncertainty
- isolation
- responsibility
- danger
- grief

Their reactions depend upon the broader human model.

---

# 80. No Universal Resilience

Resilience must not become a magic resistance statistic.

A person may be resilient in one domain and vulnerable in another.

Example:

```text
EXCELLENT UNDER
PHYSICAL DANGER

BUT

DEEPLY AFFECTED
BY SOCIAL REJECTION.
```

Human vulnerability is contextual.

---

# 81. No Ideal Psychological State

There is no universally ideal Psychology configuration.

High vigilance may help in danger.

It may harm recovery.

Low anxiety may support calm decision-making.

It may also reduce appropriate caution.

Emotional detachment may permit action during crisis.

It may damage relationships.

Context determines value.

# 85. Future Psychology Library Principles

Any future psychological state or pressure should pass the following test:

1. Is it genuinely psychological rather than a Human Attribute?
2. Is it dynamic enough to change meaningfully over time?
3. Does it influence perception or decision context without directly determining action?
4. Is it distinct from Memory?
5. Is it distinct from Belief?
6. Is it distinct from Relationship state?
7. Can it meaningfully recover, intensify or change?
8. Does context affect its expression?
9. Can different humans respond differently to it?
10. Does it add meaningful simulation value?

If not, it may belong elsewhere.

For any proposed psychological concept, ask:

```text
WHAT IS THE STATE?

WHAT CAUSED IT?

HOW LONG CAN IT PERSIST?

WHAT MAY INTENSIFY IT?

WHAT MAY REDUCE IT?

WHO OWNS IT?

WHO MAY OBSERVE IT?

WHO MAY INFER IT?

CAN THE PERSON
MISUNDERSTAND IT?

CAN AURORA
MISUNDERSTAND IT?

HOW DOES IT
INFLUENCE CHOICE?

DOES IT REMOVE AGENCY?

CAN IT CHANGE
WITHOUT REWRITING
THE PERSON'S ATTRIBUTES?
```

If these questions cannot be answered clearly, the concept is not architecturally ready.

# 88. Human Psychology North Star

Human Psychology succeeds when the simulation can explain why a person behaves differently today than they did yesterday without claiming that they have become an entirely different person.

```text
THE PERSON
```

# 89. Closing Principle

Human beings are neither fixed personalities nor endlessly plastic reactions to circumstance.

They carry history.

They develop dispositions.

They remember.

They believe.

They want.

They form relationships.

They become afraid.

They recover.

They become exhausted.

They hope.

They contradict themselves.

They make choices.

Human Psychology exists to model the changing internal conditions within which those choices occur.

Its final principle is:

> **Psychology changes the pressure under which a person chooses. It does not choose for them.**
