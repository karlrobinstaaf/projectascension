---

# 1. Purpose

Historical DNA defines the historical context carried into an individual human life.

Every person in Project Ascension is born into conditions they did not choose.

They inherit:

- a historical period
- a geographic location
- existing institutions
- available technologies
- economic conditions
- cultural assumptions
- social expectations
- family history
- collective memories
- unresolved conflicts
- opportunities
- constraints
- stories about the past
- expectations about the future

These conditions influence the person before they are capable of understanding them.

Historical DNA represents this inherited context.

It does not define who a person is.

It defines part of the world from which that person begins.

---

# 2. Core Principle

> **People inherit conditions. They do not inherit predetermined conclusions.**

Historical DNA creates influence.

It does not create destiny.

A person may:

- accept inherited beliefs
- reject them
- reinterpret them
- never consciously examine them
- become conflicted about them
- change their understanding later in life

Therefore:

```text
HISTORICAL DNA
≠
PERSONALITY

HISTORICAL DNA
≠
BELIEF

HISTORICAL DNA
≠
BEHAVIOR

HISTORICAL DNA
≠
DESTINY
```

Historical DNA provides context from which these things may develop.

---

# 3. Why Historical DNA Exists

Human beings do not begin as blank characters inside a simulation.

Before a person makes meaningful independent decisions, the world has already affected:

```text
WHAT THEY HAVE SEEN

WHAT THEY HAVE BEEN TAUGHT

WHAT THEY HAVE ACCESS TO

WHAT THEIR FAMILY REMEMBERS

WHAT THEIR COMMUNITY EXPECTS

WHAT THEIR SOCIETY CONSIDERS NORMAL

WHAT OPPORTUNITIES EXIST

WHAT RISKS EXIST

AND

WHAT FUTURES APPEAR POSSIBLE.
```

Historical DNA allows Project Ascension to preserve these influences without reducing human beings to stereotypes.

---

# 4. Historical DNA Is Contextual

Historical DNA is not one universal value assigned to a generation.

It is contextual.

A person born in:

```text
SAN FRANCISCO
```

and a person born during the same year in:

```text
RURAL MONTANA
```

may share global history while experiencing very different worlds.

Likewise, two people living on the same street may experience the same historical period differently because of:

- family
- wealth
- occupation
- education
- community
- health
- institutional access
- migration history
- social position
- personal exposure

Historical DNA therefore exists at multiple overlapping levels.

---

# 5. Historical DNA Layers

Historical DNA may be understood through a layered model.

```text
GLOBAL HISTORY
        ↓
WORLD STATE
        ↓
NATIONAL CONTEXT
        ↓
REGIONAL CONTEXT
        ↓
LOCAL / SETTLEMENT CONTEXT
        ↓
CULTURAL CONTEXT
        ↓
FAMILY CONTEXT
        ↓
DIRECT HISTORICAL EXPOSURE
        ↓
INDIVIDUAL HISTORICAL CONTEXT
```

Each layer narrows the historical conditions surrounding a person.

No single layer defines the individual.

# 6. Global Historical Context

Global history establishes the broadest conditions surrounding a generation.

Examples may include:

- technological development
- climate pressure
- economic transformation
- pandemics
- geopolitical competition
- migration
- major wars
- global information systems
- artificial intelligence development
- international institutions
- large-scale economic crises

A person does not need to directly experience an event for that event to influence the world into which they are born.

Therefore:

```text

≠

```

An event ignored by national history may define an entire community.

# 13. Family Memory

Families preserve their own interpretations of history.

A national history may say:

```text

```

```text

```

Both statements may originate from real historical circumstances.

Historical DNA must therefore distinguish between:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
```

and:

```text
WHAT WAS REMEMBERED
ABOUT WHAT HAPPENED.
```

# 18. Historical Event Exposure

A major historical event does not automatically become part of every person's Historical DNA in the same way.

The system should distinguish between:

```text
EVENT OCCURRED

EVENT AFFECTED FAMILY

INDIVIDUAL DIRECTLY EXPERIENCED EVENT

INDIVIDUAL LEARNED ABOUT EVENT LATER
```

These are different forms of historical exposure.

---

# 19. Direct And Indirect Exposure

Historical influence may be:

## Direct

The individual personally experienced the event.

Examples:

- evacuation
- infrastructure failure
- violence
- migration
- famine
- institutional collapse

## Indirect

The event changed the person's environment without being personally witnessed.

## Inherited

The event occurred before the person's birth but shaped family or community conditions.

## Cultural

The event became part of collective identity.

## Informational

The person primarily knows the event through media, education, archives or stories.

These forms should not be treated as equivalent.

# 22. Cohort Effects

For example:

```text

AS DISRUPTION
```

Chronological age at the time of an event therefore matters.

# 23. Normality

One of the most important effects of Historical DNA is the construction of normality.

People often judge the world relative to what they learned to expect.

Therefore:

```text
NORMAL
IS HISTORICALLY CONDITIONED.
```

A child raised during infrastructure instability may not experience unreliable electricity as "collapse."

It may simply be:

```text
HOW ELECTRICITY WORKS.
```

This distinction is crucial for later generations in Project Ascension.


It involves incompatible histories meeting one another.

# 32. Shared History Does Not Mean Shared Interpretation

Two people may experience the same event.

One may remember:

```text
THE DAY THE GOVERNMENT FAILED US.
```

Another:

```text
THE DAY THE GOVERNMENT SAVED US.
```

Another:

```text
THE DAY MY BROTHER DIED.
```

Another:

```text
THE DAY WE LEFT HOME.
```

The event is shared.

Its personal meaning is not.

Historical DNA preserves the exposure.

Character systems preserve the individual's interpretation and development.

---

# 33. Historical DNA vs Life Events

This boundary is important.

Historical DNA represents:

```text
THE HISTORICAL CONDITIONS
SURROUNDING A LIFE.
```

Life Events represent:

```text
WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED
TO THAT PERSON.
```

Example:

```text
REGIONAL FOOD SHORTAGE
=
HISTORICAL CONTEXT

THE CHARACTER'S FAMILY
LOSING THEIR FARM
=
LIFE EVENT
```

The systems interact.

They do not own the same state.

---

# 38. Historical DNA vs World Simulation

World Simulation owns the external state of the world.

Historical DNA does not create world conditions.

Conceptually:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

```

Historical DNA is therefore downstream from World Truth.

---

# 39. Historical DNA vs World Ledger

The World Ledger preserves consequential historical state and events.

Historical DNA does not duplicate the Ledger.

Instead, it may derive relevant context from historical records.

```text
WORLD LEDGER
=
```

# 40. Historical DNA vs Historical Knowledge

A person may be shaped by an event without knowing that event occurred.

For example:

a family may be poor because of an economic collapse that happened before a child was born.

The child may know nothing about the original event.

Therefore:

```text
HISTORICAL INFLUENCE
≠
HISTORICAL KNOWLEDGE.
```

This distinction must be preserved.

---

# 41. Historical DNA vs Belief

Likewise:

Historical DNA may contain the fact that a person grew up in a community where a particular historical narrative was dominant.

It does not automatically mean the person believes that narrative.

Therefore:

```text
EXPOSURE TO BELIEF
≠
PERSONAL BELIEF.
```

Historical DNA must follow causal paths.


# 53. Avoiding Historical Determinism


- cooperation
- withdrawal
- ambition
- compassion
- fear
- humor
- anger
- resilience
- opportunism

No response is mandatory.

```text
DESTINATION HISTORY
+
FAMILY MEMORY
+
NEW SOCIAL CONTEXT.
```

Later generations may inherit parts of all of them.

Migration should therefore add historical layers rather than erase previous context.

---

# 62. Historical DNA And Place

Place itself may carry history.

A person may live among:

- abandoned infrastructure
- memorials
- ruins
- old military installations
- former corporate campuses
- restored institutions
- communities built after displacement

The physical world becomes historical evidence.

Historical DNA may therefore emerge through environment as well as stories.

---

# 63. Historical DNA And Rediscovery

Characters may later discover that their inherited understanding of history was incomplete or wrong.

This may occur through:

- archives
- witnesses
- Aurora
- recovered systems
- documents
- travel
- other communities
- physical evidence

Such discoveries do not rewrite their Historical DNA.

They become new experiences that may change how the character understands their past.

---

# 64. Historical DNA And Aurora Knowledge

Aurora may model aspects of a person's Historical DNA if she has sufficient information.

She does not automatically know it.

Aurora may infer:

- likely generational context
- regional exposure
- institutional background
- historical pressures

But:

```text
AURORA INFERENCE
≠
CHARACTER HISTORY.
```

Aurora's knowledge remains subject to her own information boundaries.

---

# 65. Narrative Use

Narrative may reveal Historical DNA through:

- dialogue
- memories
- habits
- assumptions
- objects
- places
- disagreements
- family stories
- cultural practices
- reactions to technology
- reactions to institutions

Narrative should avoid explaining Historical DNA as a visible stat.

Players should experience history through people.

---

# 66. Historical DNA Is Not Exposition

A character should not say:

```text
MY HISTORICAL DNA
MADE ME DISTRUST INSTITUTIONS.
```

Instead, the player may discover:

```text
THE CHARACTER'S FAMILY
WAS ABANDONED DURING
A REGIONAL EVACUATION.
```

What that experience means to the character belongs to character development.

---

# 67. Simulation Resolution

Historical DNA should support different levels of detail.

A background individual may require only:

```text
REGION

GENERATION

MAJOR EXPOSURES

CULTURAL CONTEXT.
```

A major Character may require:

```text
FAMILY HISTORY

LOCAL EVENTS

INSTITUTIONAL EXPERIENCE

MIGRATION HISTORY

HISTORICAL TURNING POINTS

INHERITED MEMORY.
```

Resolution determines detail.

Not whether history exists.

---

# 68. Persistence

Historical DNA is persistent context.

Later events may change a person's understanding of history.

They do not erase what the person actually lived through.

Therefore:

```text
NEW INTERPRETATION
≠
NEW PAST.
```

---

# 69. Relationship To Canon

Historical DNA derives from canonical world history.

It must respect:

- Timeline
- World States
- regional canon
- settlement canon
- institutional history
- family history
- World Truth

Historical DNA must never invent historical conditions that contradict established Canon.

---

# 70. Relationship To Simulation

Canonical history defines historical constraints.

Simulation creates dynamic history.

As campaigns continue:

new events may eventually become part of the Historical DNA of younger generations.

Therefore Historical DNA bridges:

```text
CANONICAL HISTORY
```

and:

```text
LIVING HISTORY.
```

---

# 71. Relationship To The World Ledger

Consequential campaign history may eventually become inherited historical context.

Conceptually:

```text
PLAYER ACTION
↓
WORLD CONSEQUENCE
↓
WORLD LEDGER
↓
TIME PASSES
↓
COMMUNITY MEMORY
↓
FAMILY MEMORY
↓
NEXT GENERATION
↓
HISTORICAL DNA.
```

This allows player actions to become history without making players the center of history.

---

# 72. Long-Term Campaign Potential

If Project Ascension supports campaigns across long periods:

Historical DNA allows one campaign's consequences to become another generation's inherited context.

A decision made by one group may later survive as:

- a community tradition
- a ruined settlement
- a family story
- a political grievance
- an institution
- a warning
- a myth
- forgotten infrastructure

The later generation may never know who caused it.

That is part of Living History.

---

Historical DNA owns:

```text
THE STRUCTURED HISTORICAL
CONTEXT ASSOCIATED
WITH A HUMAN LIFE.
```

It does not own:

```text
WORLD TRUTH

CHARACTER BELIEFS
CHARACTER MEMORY
RELATIONSHIPS
CURRENT SOCIETY STATE
AURORA KNOWLEDGE
NARRATIVE PRESENTATION
CAMPAIGN RELEVANCE
```

Those remain owned by their respective systems.

# 76. Canonical Invariants

## HDNA-INV-001 — History Influences, It Does Not Determine

Historical context may shape probability and opportunity.

It must not dictate personality or behavior.

## HDNA-INV-002 — World Truth Remains External

Historical DNA derives from history.

It does not own World Truth.

## HDNA-INV-003 — Shared History Does Not Create Identical People

Individuals remain distinct.

## HDNA-INV-004 — Exposure Requires A Path

A historical event must connect causally to the individual for direct influence to exist.

## HDNA-INV-005 — Historical Influence Is Not Historical Knowledge

People may be affected by events they do not understand or know about.

## HDNA-INV-006 — Historical Memory Is Not World Truth

Family, community and institutional memory may be incomplete or wrong.

## HDNA-INV-007 — World States Are Not Universal Switches

Regional timing and conditions may differ.

## HDNA-INV-008 — Geography Matters

Historical experience must reflect place.

## HDNA-INV-009 — Age Matters

The same event may influence different life stages differently.

## HDNA-INV-010 — Culture Is Context, Not Destiny

Cultural exposure does not dictate individual belief.

## HDNA-INV-011 — Family History Is Context, Not Personality

Family influence must not predetermine character identity.

## HDNA-INV-012 — Historical DNA Persists

Later reinterpretation does not erase lived historical context.

## HDNA-INV-013 — Historical DNA May Accumulate

Significant historical exposure continues throughout a life.

## HDNA-INV-014 — Positive History Matters

Historical inheritance includes cooperation, knowledge, culture and resilience as well as trauma and failure.

## HDNA-INV-015 — Human Agency Remains

Historical causality must never remove individual responsibility or choice.

# 77. Development Locks

Do not use Historical DNA as a personality generator.

Do not use Historical DNA as a political ideology generator.

Do not use Historical DNA as a morality generator.

Do not equate generation with personality.

Do not equate culture with belief.

Do not equate region with behavior.

Do not equate poverty with personality.

Do not equate trauma with destiny.

Do not assume everyone experienced the Collapse identically.

Do not assume everyone understands historical events accurately.

Do not assume historical influence requires conscious knowledge.

Do not make World States universal instantaneous switches.

Do not ignore geography.

Do not ignore family context.

Do not ignore institutional context.

Do not ignore positive historical inheritance.

Do not allow Historical DNA to overwrite Character state.

Do not allow Historical DNA to own Relationships.

Do not allow Historical DNA to create World Truth.

Do not allow Aurora to automatically know Historical DNA.

Do not use Historical DNA as exposition.

Do not erase contradictions.

Do not treat historical outcomes as inevitable.

Do not remove individual responsibility through historical explanation.

# 78. Design Questions

When defining Historical DNA for an individual, ask:

```text
WHEN WERE THEY BORN?

WHERE WERE THEY BORN?

WHERE DID THEY GROW UP?

WHAT WORLD DID THEY CONSIDER NORMAL?

WHAT INSTITUTIONS SURROUNDED THEM?

WHAT TECHNOLOGIES WERE ORDINARY?

WHAT ECONOMIC CONDITIONS EXISTED?

WHAT DID THEIR FAMILY CARRY FROM THE PAST?

WHAT DID THEIR COMMUNITY REMEMBER?

WHAT HISTORICAL EVENTS REACHED THEM?

HOW CLOSE WERE THEY TO THOSE EVENTS?

WHAT CHANGED DURING THEIR LIFETIME?

WHAT HISTORY DO THEY KNOW?

WHAT HISTORY DO THEY ONLY FEEL
THROUGH ITS CONSEQUENCES?

WHAT PARTS OF THEIR INHERITED HISTORY
MAY BE WRONG?

WHAT OPPORTUNITIES DID HISTORY CREATE?

WHAT CONSTRAINTS DID HISTORY CREATE?
```

These questions establish context.

They do not answer:

```text
WHO DID THIS PERSON BECOME?
```

That belongs to the human and character systems.

# 85. Closing Statement

Every person in Project Ascension enters a world already in motion.

Borders already exist.

Institutions already carry histories.

Families already remember.

Communities already trust and distrust.

Technologies already shape expectation.

Conflicts may have begun decades earlier.

Some opportunities were created before the person was born.

Others disappeared before they were old enough to understand what had been lost.

This is Historical DNA.

Not a hidden force controlling the individual.

Not a number determining personality.

Not destiny.

It is the historical inheritance surrounding a human life.

Two people may inherit the same war and remember it differently.

Two siblings may inherit the same family and become very different adults.

Two communities may survive the same crisis and tell opposite stories about why they survived.

And a child born after the Collapse may grow up inside conditions their grandparents once considered unimaginable.

To that child, those conditions may simply be home.

> **History does not decide who a person becomes. It decides part of the world in which becoming begins.**
