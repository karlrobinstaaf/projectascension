> **"People are not defined by a single trait, but by the interaction between many tendencies, experiences and choices."**

---

# 1. Purpose

Human Attributes define relatively stable human dispositions that influence behavior across time.

They help Project Ascension represent patterns such as:

- how readily someone trusts
- how strongly someone seeks control
- how easily someone cooperates
- how persistent someone tends to be
- how comfortable someone is with uncertainty
- how strongly someone values status
- how readily someone empathizes
- how willing someone is to take risk
- how strongly someone protects personal autonomy
- how someone tends to respond to social pressure

Human Attributes are not:

```text
SKILLS

PROFESSIONS

EXPERTISE

CURRENT EMOTIONS

CURRENT GOALS

BELIEFS

MORAL ALIGNMENTS

OR

FIXED PERSONALITY CLASSES.
```

They represent persistent tendencies.

Those tendencies interact with:

```text
HISTORY

MEMORY

RELATIONSHIPS

BELIEFS

VALUES

CURRENT EMOTION

EXPERTISE

SITUATION

PRESSURE

AND

CHOICE.
```

The purpose of Human Attributes is not to predict a human perfectly.

It is to help make behavior:

```text
CONSISTENT

UNDERSTANDABLE

AND

HUMAN.
```

---

# 2. Core Principle

> **Attributes influence behavior. They do not determine it.**

A person with a strong tendency toward caution may still take an extraordinary risk.

A highly compassionate person may still refuse to help.

A proud person may apologize.

A distrustful person may choose to trust.

A conflict-averse person may fight.

A loyal person may leave.

The question is not:

```text
WHAT DOES THIS ATTRIBUTE
FORCE THE PERSON TO DO?
```

It is:

```text
HOW DOES THIS ATTRIBUTE
INFLUENCE THE PERSON'S
DECISION UNDER THESE CONDITIONS?
```

---

# 3. Human Attributes Are Dispositions

A Human Attribute represents a tendency that may appear repeatedly across situations.

Examples:

```text
HIGH PERSISTENCE
```

may make someone more likely to continue after setbacks.

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
```

may make another person's suffering more salient.

```text
HIGH AUTONOMY
```

may make coercion particularly difficult to accept.

But the expression of an Attribute always depends on context.

Therefore:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
+
CONTEXT
+
HISTORY
+
CURRENT STATE
=
POTENTIAL BEHAVIORAL INFLUENCE.
```

Not:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
=
BEHAVIOR.
```

---

# 4. Attributes Are Not Personality Labels

Project Ascension should avoid reducing people to labels such as:

```text
THE BRAVE ONE

THE EMPATH

THE LEADER

THE COWARD

THE LOGICAL ONE

THE SELFISH ONE.
```

A Character may show courage in one context and avoidance in another.

They may be highly compassionate toward family and emotionally distant toward strangers.

They may lead professionally while avoiding social responsibility elsewhere.

Human behavior is contextual.

Attributes exist to support that complexity.

---

# 5. Human Attribute Architecture

A useful conceptual model is:

```text
HISTORICAL DNA
        ↓
LIFE EXPERIENCE
        ↓
PSYCHOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT
        ↓
HUMAN ATTRIBUTES
        ↕
CURRENT CHARACTER STATE
        ↓
SITUATION
        ↓
DECISION
        ↓
ACTION
        ↓
CONSEQUENCE
        ↓
NEW EXPERIENCE
        ↓
POSSIBLE LONG-TERM CHANGE
```

Human Attributes therefore sit between:

```text
LIFE HISTORY
```

and:

```text
BEHAVIOR.
```

They are neither identical to the past nor identical to the current decision.

---

# 6. Attribute Stability

Human Attributes should normally change more slowly than:

```text
EMOTION

MOOD

GOALS

SHORT-TERM BELIEF

OR

IMMEDIATE MOTIVATION.
```

They may change faster than:

```text
BIOLOGICAL HISTORY

PAST EVENTS

OR

BIRTH CONTEXT.
```

Attributes are therefore best understood as:

```text
SEMI-PERSISTENT HUMAN STATE.
```

They may evolve.

They should not fluctuate continuously without cause.

---

# 7. Attribute Change

Meaningful long-term changes may emerge through:

- repeated experience
- sustained responsibility
- major relationships
- prolonged stress
- repeated success
- repeated failure
- betrayal
- caregiving
- leadership
- grief
- parenthood
- trauma
- recovery
- institutional experience
- aging
- changing social role
- long-term self-reflection

A single event may influence an Attribute strongly if sufficiently important.

But most Attributes should develop through patterns.

---

# 8. Pattern Before Instant Transformation

Avoid:

```text
ONE ARGUMENT
↓
PERSON BECOMES DISTRUSTFUL.
```

Prefer:

```text
REPEATED DECEPTION
+
BROKEN EXPECTATIONS
+
TIME
↓
TRUST DISPOSITION MAY SHIFT.
```

Likewise:

```text
ONE SUCCESS
```

should not automatically produce:

```text
HIGH CONFIDENCE.
```

Persistent change requires plausible development.

---

# 9. State vs Trait

The system must distinguish between:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
```

and:

```text
CURRENT STATE.
```

Example:

A normally confident person may currently be:

```text
AFRAID.
```

A generally compassionate person may currently be:

```text
ANGRY.
```

A highly patient person may currently be:

```text
EXHAUSTED.
```

A cautious person may currently be:

```text
DESPERATE.
```

Current state may temporarily override typical tendencies.

---

# 10. Attribute vs Emotion

Emotion is situational and comparatively short-lived.

Attributes represent longer-term tendencies.

Example:

```text
ATTRIBUTE:
High Emotional Regulation

CURRENT EMOTION:
Intense anger.
```

This person may still experience anger strongly.

The Attribute may influence how they respond to it.

Therefore:

```text
EMOTIONAL REGULATION
≠
LOW EMOTION.
```

---

# 11. Attribute vs Belief

Beliefs concern what someone thinks is true.

Attributes influence how they may approach beliefs.

Example:

```text
HIGH OPEN-MINDEDNESS
```

may increase willingness to reconsider evidence.

It does not determine:

```text
WHAT THEY BELIEVE.
```

Likewise:

```text
HIGH SUSPICION
```

does not mean every suspicious belief is correct.

---

# 12. Attribute vs Value

Values concern what someone considers important.

Attributes influence how values may be expressed.

Example:

Two people may both value family.

One has:

```text
HIGH ASSERTIVENESS.
```

The other:

```text
HIGH HARM AVOIDANCE.
```

Both want to protect the family.

They may choose radically different actions.

---

# 13. Attribute vs Goal

Goals describe what a person currently wants to achieve.

Attributes influence how they pursue those goals.

Example:

```text
GOAL:
Secure medicine.
```

A highly cooperative Character may seek alliances.

A highly autonomous Character may prefer solving the problem alone.

A highly risk-tolerant Character may attempt a dangerous route.

The goal remains the same.

The behavioral approach differs.

---

# 14. Attribute vs Expertise

This distinction is mandatory.

Expertise describes:

```text
WHAT A PERSON
KNOWS OR CAN DO.
```

Human Attributes describe:

```text
HOW A PERSON
TENDS TO APPROACH
SITUATIONS.
```

Example:

```text
HIGH COURAGE
+
NO MEDICAL EXPERTISE
```

does not make someone capable of surgery.

Likewise:

```text
WORLD-CLASS MEDICAL EXPERTISE
+
HIGH FEAR
```

does not erase their knowledge.

Therefore:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
≠
CAPABILITY.
```

---

# 15. Attribute vs Skill

A Skill is learned capability.

An Attribute is a disposition.

Example:

```text
NEGOTIATION SKILL
```

may determine how effectively a person communicates in negotiation.

```text
ASSERTIVENESS
```

may influence whether they choose to challenge another person.

These interact.

They are not interchangeable.

---

# 16. Attribute vs Human Attribute Outcome

Attributes should not directly encode outcomes such as:

```text
LEADERSHIP

SUCCESS

POPULARITY

TRUSTWORTHINESS.
```

These are emergent results.

A person may become a leader through very different Attribute combinations.

Example:

```text
HIGH ASSERTIVENESS
+
HIGH CONFIDENCE
```

may produce one leadership style.

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH RESPONSIBILITY
+
HIGH SOCIAL TRUST
```

may produce another.

---

# 17. Attributes Are Multidimensional

No one Attribute should define the person.

Human behavior should emerge from combinations.

Example:

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH FEAR
+
HIGH RESPONSIBILITY
```

may produce someone who deeply wants to help but hesitates under immediate danger.

Another Character may have:

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH RISK TOLERANCE
+
HIGH IMPULSIVITY.
```

Both are compassionate.

Their behavior may differ greatly.

---

# 18. Attribute Interaction

Attributes may reinforce one another.

Example:

```text
PERSISTENCE
+
SELF-DISCIPLINE
```

may create strong long-term follow-through.

Attributes may also oppose one another.

Example:

```text
CURIOSITY
+
HARM AVOIDANCE.
```

The person may want to investigate while simultaneously fearing the risk.

Such tensions are desirable.

---

# 19. Internal Conflict

Human beings often contain competing tendencies.

Example:

```text
HIGH LOYALTY
+
HIGH AUTONOMY
```

may produce conflict when a group demands obedience.

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH JUSTICE SENSITIVITY
```

may produce tension between forgiveness and accountability.

The system should preserve these tensions rather than resolve them mechanically.

---

# 20. No Ideal Attribute Profile

There is no optimal human configuration.

Every Attribute may produce advantages and disadvantages depending on situation.

Example:

```text
HIGH CAUTION
```

may prevent reckless mistakes.

It may also prevent necessary action.

```text
HIGH CONFIDENCE
```

may support leadership.

It may also reduce willingness to reconsider.

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
```

may improve relationships.

It may also increase emotional burden.

Attributes should generate tradeoffs.

---

# 21. No Good And Bad Attributes

Avoid classifying Attributes as:

```text
POSITIVE

NEGATIVE.
```

A characteristic may become beneficial or harmful depending on:

- context
- intensity
- combinations
- social environment
- stakes
- relationships

Even apparently difficult traits may become useful.

Example:

```text
SUSPICION
```

may damage relationships.

It may also protect someone inside an environment of deception.

---

# 22. Attribute Extremes

Extreme levels may create distinctive strengths and costs.

Example:

Very high Persistence:

```text
STRENGTH:
refuses to give up.

RISK:
continues when stopping
would be wiser.
```

Very high Empathy:

```text
STRENGTH:
deep sensitivity to others.

RISK:
emotional overload
or difficulty prioritizing.
```

Very high Autonomy:

```text
STRENGTH:
independent judgment.

RISK:
difficulty accepting coordination.
```

Extremes should remain plausible rather than caricatured.

---

# 23. Moderate Profiles Matter

Not every person should possess extreme Attributes.

Most humans may cluster around moderate tendencies.

Extreme personalities should remain relatively uncommon.

This helps preserve human diversity.

---

# 24. Proposed Attribute Domains

Human Attributes may be organized into broad domains for clarity.

Suggested domains:

```text
SELF

SOCIAL

COGNITIVE

ACTION

ADAPTATION.
```

These domains are organizational.

They do not represent isolated systems.

---

# 25. SELF Domain

The Self domain may contain Attributes related to:

- confidence
- autonomy
- self-discipline
- emotional regulation
- identity stability
- pride

These influence how strongly a person maintains internal direction.

---

# 26. SOCIAL Domain

The Social domain may contain Attributes such as:

- empathy
- social trust
- loyalty
- assertiveness
- cooperation
- need for belonging

These influence social interaction.

They do not determine relationship state.

---

# 27. COGNITIVE Domain

The Cognitive domain may include:

- curiosity
- open-mindedness
- cognitive flexibility
- skepticism
- deliberation
- tolerance of ambiguity

These influence how a person approaches information and uncertainty.

They do not represent intelligence or Expertise.

---

# 28. ACTION Domain

The Action domain may include:

- courage
- persistence
- risk tolerance
- decisiveness
- patience
- impulsivity

These influence how people move from intention to action.

---

# 29. ADAPTATION Domain

The Adaptation domain may include:

- resilience
- adaptability
- need for control
- uncertainty tolerance
- stress sensitivity
- resourcefulness

These influence how people respond to changing conditions.

---

# 30. Proposed Canonical Attribute Set

The canonical library should remain compact enough to be meaningful.

A working set of approximately twenty-four Attributes is appropriate.

The following model is recommended as a canonical baseline.

---

# 31. Confidence

Represents general confidence in one's ability to act, decide or cope.

High Confidence may support:

- initiative
- leadership
- persistence
- independent decision-making

Potential risks include:

- overconfidence
- underestimating danger
- resistance to advice

Low Confidence may produce:

- caution
- self-doubt
- reliance on others
- hesitation

Low Confidence does not imply low capability.

---

# 32. Autonomy

Represents preference for self-direction and independent control over decisions.

High Autonomy may support:

- independence
- resistance to coercion
- initiative

Potential costs include:

- difficulty accepting authority
- reluctance to delegate
- resistance to coordination

---

# 33. Self-Discipline

Represents ability to maintain intentional behavior despite distraction or discomfort.

High Self-Discipline may support:

- consistency
- training
- long-term planning

Potential costs include:

- rigidity
- overwork
- difficulty disengaging

---

# 34. Emotional Regulation

Represents ability to manage emotional response without implying emotional absence.

High Emotional Regulation may support:

- calm decision-making
- conflict management
- crisis performance

Potential costs may include:

- emotional suppression
- appearing distant

Low Emotional Regulation may create greater visible emotional response.

It does not imply weakness.

---

# 35. Pride

Represents sensitivity to self-respect, status and personal dignity.

High Pride may support:

- confidence
- standards
- resistance to humiliation

Potential costs include:

- difficulty admitting error
- sensitivity to disrespect
- refusal of help

---

# 36. Empathy

Represents tendency to recognize and emotionally respond to the experiences of others.

High Empathy may support:

- understanding
- caregiving
- cooperation

Potential costs include:

- emotional burden
- difficulty making impersonal decisions

Empathy does not automatically create altruism.

---

# 37. Social Trust

Represents general willingness to assume that other people may be reliable or cooperative before strong evidence exists.

This differs from relationship-specific Trust.

```text
SOCIAL TRUST
=
general disposition.

RELATIONSHIP TRUST
=
state toward a specific Actor.
```

High Social Trust may support rapid cooperation.

It may also increase vulnerability to deception.

---

# 38. Loyalty

Represents general tendency to maintain commitment to people, groups or institutions once bonds form.

Loyalty does not define:

```text
WHO
the person is loyal to.
```

Relationships and values determine the target.

Potential risks include:

- staying too long
- defending harmful behavior
- conflict between loyalties

---

# 39. Cooperation

Represents general willingness to solve problems collaboratively.

High Cooperation may support:

- teamwork
- compromise
- resource sharing

Potential costs may include:

- over-accommodation
- avoiding necessary confrontation

---

# 40. Assertiveness

Represents willingness to express preferences, boundaries or disagreement.

High Assertiveness may support:

- leadership
- negotiation
- boundary-setting

Potential costs include:

- social conflict
- dominance if combined with other traits

Assertiveness is not aggression.

---

# 41. Need For Belonging

Represents sensitivity to inclusion, group identity and social acceptance.

High Need For Belonging may support:

- community attachment
- social cohesion

Potential costs include:

- conformity
- difficulty leaving harmful groups

---

# 42. Curiosity

Represents motivation to seek new knowledge or experience.

High Curiosity may support:

- learning
- exploration
- innovation

Potential risks include:

- unnecessary risk
- distraction
- boundary crossing

---

# 43. Open-Mindedness

Represents willingness to consider unfamiliar ideas or alternative interpretations.

High Open-Mindedness may support:

- learning
- adaptation
- intellectual flexibility

Potential costs may include:

- delayed commitment
- excessive openness to weak ideas

---

# 44. Skepticism

Represents tendency to require evidence before accepting claims.

High Skepticism may support:

- resistance to manipulation
- verification
- careful reasoning

Potential costs include:

- excessive distrust
- slow decision-making

Skepticism is not Cynicism.

---

# 45. Cognitive Flexibility

Represents capacity to shift mental models when conditions change.

High Cognitive Flexibility may support:

- adaptation
- problem-solving
- re-evaluation

Potential costs may include:

- instability of plans
- difficulty committing

---

# 46. Deliberation

Represents tendency to think through decisions before acting.

High Deliberation may support:

- careful planning
- risk evaluation

Potential costs include:

- hesitation
- analysis paralysis

---

# 47. Ambiguity Tolerance

Represents comfort with unresolved or incomplete information.

High Ambiguity Tolerance may support:

- functioning under uncertainty
- exploration

Potential costs may include:

- insufficient desire for closure
- acceptance of unresolved risk

---

# 48. Courage

Represents willingness to act despite fear or risk.

Courage is not absence of fear.

High Courage may support:

- action under threat
- protection of others
- leadership

Potential costs may include:

- unnecessary exposure to danger

---

# 49. Persistence

Represents tendency to continue pursuing goals despite difficulty or failure.

High Persistence may support:

- long-term achievement
- recovery after setbacks

Potential costs include:

- refusal to stop
- sunk-cost behavior

---

# 50. Risk Tolerance

Represents willingness to accept uncertain negative outcomes in pursuit of potential benefit.

High Risk Tolerance may support:

- experimentation
- decisive action

Potential costs include:

- dangerous choices
- instability

Risk Tolerance is not Courage.

A person may tolerate financial risk while fearing physical danger.

---

# 51. Decisiveness

Represents willingness to commit to an action when a decision is required.

High Decisiveness may support:

- leadership
- speed
- action under pressure

Potential costs include:

- premature decisions
- insufficient information gathering

---

# 52. Patience

Represents willingness to tolerate delay without immediate action.

High Patience may support:

- negotiation
- long-term planning
- emotional control

Potential costs include:

- waiting too long
- missed opportunities

---

# 53. Impulsivity

Represents tendency toward rapid action with limited deliberation.

High Impulsivity may support:

- speed
- spontaneity
- action under extreme time pressure

Potential costs include:

- preventable error
- social or physical risk

Impulsivity should not be treated solely as negative.

---

# 54. Resilience

Represents general capacity to recover after adversity.

Resilience does not mean:

```text
IMMUNITY TO SUFFERING.
```

A resilient person may experience:

- grief
- fear
- temporary dysfunction
- trauma

while still recovering over time.

---

# 55. Adaptability

Represents willingness and ability to adjust behavior when conditions change.

High Adaptability may support:

- survival
- migration
- technological change

Potential costs may include:

- reduced attachment to existing methods
- difficulty maintaining long-term consistency

---

# 56. Need For Control

Represents discomfort when outcomes depend heavily on forces outside personal control.

High Need For Control may support:

- planning
- preparation
- responsibility

Potential costs include:

- anxiety
- micromanagement
- difficulty accepting uncertainty

---

# 57. Stress Sensitivity

Represents how strongly sustained pressure tends to affect the individual's functioning.

High Stress Sensitivity does not mean:

```text
WEAKNESS.
```

It represents one dimension of human response.

Protective factors may include:

- experience
- support
- Expertise
- relationships
- recovery time

---

# 58. Resourcefulness

Represents tendency to improvise using available tools, knowledge and relationships.

Resourcefulness is distinct from Expertise.

A person may be highly resourceful without deep technical knowledge.

Likewise:

an expert may struggle when outside familiar systems.

---

# 59. Attribute Library Status

The proposed Attribute library is:

```text
CANONICAL BASELINE
```

but may be refined as other human systems are validated.

The goal is not to maximize Attribute count.

The goal is to preserve enough dimensions to represent meaningful human variation without unnecessary complexity.

If two Attributes consistently describe the same behavioral tendency:

they should be merged or redefined.

---

# 60. Attribute Scale

Exact numerical implementation is not yet locked.

Conceptually, each Attribute should support a continuous range rather than binary state.

Possible implementation:

```text
VERY LOW

LOW

MODERATE

HIGH

VERY HIGH
```

or an equivalent internal numeric representation.

The player should rarely need to see raw values.

---

# 61. Numerical Values Are Internal

If Attributes are represented numerically:

numbers exist for simulation.

They should not become the primary human description.

Avoid:

```text
EMPATHY: 73

COURAGE: 54

LOYALTY: 91
```

as normal player-facing language.

Prefer behavioral expression.

---

# 62. Behavioral Expression

High Empathy might appear as:

```text
She notices that the technician
has stopped speaking and asks
if he wants to leave the room.
```

High Skepticism might appear as:

```text
He asks who verified the recording
before accepting it as genuine.
```

High Persistence might appear as:

```text
She returns to the repair
after everyone else has stopped.
```

The player experiences behavior.

Not variables.

---

# 63. Attributes Influence Interpretation

Attributes may affect how information is interpreted.

Example:

An ambiguous warning may be interpreted differently by someone with:

```text
HIGH SOCIAL TRUST
```

than by someone with:

```text
HIGH SKEPTICISM.
```

But Attributes do not create facts.

---

# 64. Attributes Influence Attention

Some Attributes may influence what a Character notices or prioritizes.

High Empathy may make emotional cues more salient.

High Curiosity may make anomalies more interesting.

High Need For Control may make uncertainty more salient.

This is influence.

Not guaranteed perception.

---

# 65. Attributes Influence Action Selection

When multiple plausible actions exist:

Attributes may alter relative preference.

Example:

```text
SITUATION:
Unknown group requests entry.
```

A Character with:

```text
HIGH SOCIAL TRUST
+
HIGH COOPERATION
```

may lean toward conversation.

Another with:

```text
HIGH SKEPTICISM
+
HIGH HARM AVOIDANCE
```

may require verification first.

Both remain capable of choosing otherwise.

---

# 66. Attributes And Relationships

Human Attributes influence how Relationships form and evolve.

Example:

High Social Trust may make initial trust easier.

High Loyalty may increase relationship persistence.

High Empathy may improve understanding.

High Pride may intensify some conflicts.

But Relationship state remains owned by:

```text
RELATIONSHIPS SYSTEM.
```

Attributes influence.

They do not store relationship truth.

---

# 67. Relationship-Specific Behavior

A person's general Attribute disposition may be overridden by relational history.

Example:

A person with high Social Trust may distrust one specific person deeply because of betrayal.

Therefore:

```text
GENERAL ATTRIBUTE
≠
SPECIFIC RELATIONSHIP STATE.
```

---

# 68. Attributes And Life

Life events may gradually influence Attributes.

Example:

```text
REPEATED RESPONSIBILITY
```

may contribute toward greater:

```text
SELF-DISCIPLINE

CONFIDENCE

OR

PERSISTENCE.
```

But change depends on:

- interpretation
- outcome
- relationships
- age
- current disposition
- repetition

The same event may shape different people differently.

---

# 69. Attributes And Historical DNA

Historical DNA defines the world surrounding a person.

Attributes represent part of how the person develops within that world.

Therefore:

```text
HISTORICAL DNA
=
CONTEXT.

HUMAN ATTRIBUTES
=
DEVELOPED DISPOSITIONS.
```

Historical context may create pressures.

It does not assign Attributes directly.

---

# 70. Attributes And Psychological DNA

Psychological DNA should describe deeper developmental patterns and psychological structure.

Human Attributes represent persistent tendencies that emerge from that structure.

Conceptually:

```text
TEMPERAMENT
+
HISTORICAL CONTEXT
+
LIFE EXPERIENCE
+
RELATIONSHIPS
+
PSYCHOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT
↓
HUMAN ATTRIBUTES.
```

But exact ownership boundaries should remain aligned with the Human Model.

---

# 71. Psychological DNA Must Not Duplicate Attributes

If Psychological DNA and Human Attributes both represent exactly the same state:

the architecture is wrong.

Psychological DNA should explain:

```text
WHY SOME TENDENCIES
DEVELOPED.
```

Human Attributes should represent:

```text
WHAT RELATIVELY STABLE
TENDENCIES CURRENTLY EXIST.
```

---

# 72. Attributes And Expertise

Expertise and Attributes may combine strongly.

Example:

```text
HIGH EXPERTISE
+
LOW CONFIDENCE
```

may produce a capable person who hesitates to assert themselves.

```text
LOW EXPERTISE
+
HIGH CONFIDENCE
```

may produce confident incorrect decisions.

```text
HIGH EXPERTISE
+
HIGH CURIOSITY
```

may produce rapid learning in adjacent domains.

These combinations create human realism.

---

# 73. Attributes And Leadership

Leadership is emergent.

Potential leadership profiles include:

```text
HIGH ASSERTIVENESS
+
HIGH CONFIDENCE
+
HIGH DECISIVENESS.
```

Or:

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH RESPONSIBILITY
+
HIGH COOPERATION.
```

Or:

```text
HIGH EXPERTISE
+
HIGH SOCIAL TRUST
+
HIGH CALM UNDER PRESSURE.
```

No one Attribute is:

```text
LEADERSHIP.
```

---

# 74. Attributes And Conflict

Conflict response may emerge from combinations.

Examples:

```text
HIGH ASSERTIVENESS
+
HIGH PRIDE
```

may increase direct confrontation.

```text
HIGH EMPATHY
+
HIGH PATIENCE
```

may support mediation.

```text
HIGH LOYALTY
+
HIGH PRIDE
```

may make perceived betrayal especially powerful.

These are tendencies.

Not deterministic outcomes.

---

# 75. Attributes And Crisis

Under crisis conditions:

baseline tendencies may become amplified, suppressed or overridden.

Relevant factors include:

- exhaustion
- fear
- injury
- grief
- hunger
- time pressure
- social support
- Expertise
- leadership
- trauma

Therefore:

```text
ATTRIBUTE PROFILE
AT REST
```

may not perfectly predict:

```text
BEHAVIOR
UNDER EXTREME PRESSURE.
```

---

# 76. Stress Response

Stress Sensitivity is only one part of crisis response.

Crisis behavior may depend on:

```text
STRESS SENSITIVITY

EMOTIONAL REGULATION

EXPERTISE

EXPERIENCE

RELATIONSHIPS

PHYSICAL CONDITION

AND

CURRENT STAKES.
```

This prevents simplistic "high stress = panic" logic.

---

# 77. Attributes And Trauma

Trauma may influence Attributes.

But:

```text
TRAUMA
≠
ATTRIBUTE TEMPLATE.
```

Different people may respond to similar trauma with:

- increased caution
- increased risk-taking
- greater empathy
- emotional withdrawal
- increased need for control
- little long-term Attribute change

Trauma must not become automatic character depth.

---

# 78. Attributes And Aging

Some tendencies may shift through aging and changing life roles.

Possible influences include:

- accumulated experience
- health
- parenthood
- loss
- responsibility
- social role
- institutional position
- reduced or increased risk tolerance

Aging should not mechanically push every Character toward the same profile.

---

# 79. Attributes And Culture

Culture may influence which behaviors are:

```text
ENCOURAGED

DISCOURAGED

REWARDED

PUNISHED

OR

INTERPRETED DIFFERENTLY.
```

Culture does not directly determine internal Attribute state.

A highly Assertive person may express assertiveness differently across cultures.

---

# 80. Expression vs Disposition

This distinction is important.

Two Characters may possess similar Attributes but express them differently because of:

- culture
- profession
- gender norms
- age
- relationships
- social position
- institutional rules

Therefore:

```text
ATTRIBUTE
≠
VISIBLE BEHAVIOR
IN ALL CONTEXTS.
```

---

# 81. Attributes And Social Role

Social roles may encourage certain forms of behavior.

Example:

A military commander may display strong decisiveness professionally.

That does not mean the person is equally decisive in family relationships.

Human Attributes are general tendencies.

Role and context shape expression.

```text

```

may often correlate with:


The system should allow unusual profiles.

- blind spots
- contradictions
- vulnerabilities
- undeveloped tendencies

For example:

This should emerge probabilistically.


Selection effects matter.



# 90. Player Characters

Player Characters may begin with partially player-defined Attribute profiles.

Player choice may influence:

- broad tendencies
- strengths
- weaknesses
- desired character concept

But Attributes should remain compatible with established history.

The system may challenge contradictions.

It should not automatically reject them if plausible explanation exists.

---

# 91. Player Ownership

Attributes should support character expression.

They should not confiscate player agency.

A player may choose an action inconsistent with a strong Attribute tendency.

If this occurs:

the system should not simply say:

```text
YOU CANNOT DO THAT.
```

Instead, the action may represent:

- exceptional motivation
- changing development
- internal conflict
- deliberate self-control

Attributes inform roleplay.

They do not dictate it.


- exhausted
- uninterested
- culturally restrained
- hiding information

Narrative should preserve ambiguity.

Aurora may misread a person.

# 102. Attribute Change Threshold

Not every event should alter persistent Attributes.

The system should distinguish between:

```text
TEMPORARY STATE CHANGE
```

and:

```text
LONG-TERM ATTRIBUTE CHANGE.
```

Persistent change may require:

- major event
- repeated pattern
- sustained environment
- deliberate personal development
- significant relationship
- long duration

---

# 103. Attribute Change Direction

Attribute development should not always move toward higher values.

Examples:

Repeated leadership may increase Confidence.

Repeated failure may reduce Confidence.

A betrayal may reduce Social Trust.

Long-term community cooperation may increase Social Trust.

Aging may increase Patience.

Extreme stress may reduce Emotional Regulation temporarily without changing the underlying Attribute.

Direction depends on experience.

---

# 104. Attribute Change Is Not Moral Progression

The system should not assume character development means:

```text
MORE EMPATHY

MORE COURAGE

MORE TRUST

MORE COOPERATION.
```

People may develop in many directions.

Development means change.

Not automatic improvement.

---

# 105. Attribute Persistence

Some Attributes may remain remarkably stable for decades.

Others may evolve substantially.

Stability may depend on:

- age
- temperament
- strength of current disposition
- environment
- major experience
- intentional change

The system should not require every Character to undergo personality transformation.

---

# 106. Extreme Experience

Extreme experiences may produce rapid Attribute shifts.

But such change must remain plausible.

Example:

A major betrayal may sharply alter Social Trust.

A near-death experience may alter Risk Tolerance.

Long recovery may later change the tendency again.

Persistent transformation should carry history.

---

# 107. Recovery And Attribute Change

Some changes may be temporary adaptations.

Example:

After trauma:

```text
SOCIAL TRUST ↓
```

during recovery.

Later:

```text
SOCIAL TRUST
partially returns.
```

This should not be modeled as inconsistency.

Human development is dynamic.


Reconnection may reward:

```text

NEGOTIATION.
```

But:

```text
WORLD STATE
≠
ATTRIBUTE PROFILE.
```

# 109. Survival Pressure

The Fractured World should not automatically transform everyone into:

```text

VIOLENT

DISTRUSTFUL

SELF-RELIANT.
```

Some people may become more cooperative.

Some more fearful.

Some more dependent on community.

Some more trusting locally.

Different adaptation remains essential.

```text

NEW INSTITUTIONS

NEW TECHNOLOGY

NEW INFORMATION.
```

High Adaptability may help.

High Skepticism may protect them.

High Social Trust may create opportunity.

No tendency guarantees success.

- life history
- relationships
- previous decisions
- Canon

It does not rewrite the Character.

Persistent dispositions should not disappear.

# 115. Behavioral Decision Model

```text

+
```

```text
↓
```

---

# 116. Human Behavior Is Not A Formula

The conceptual model must never become:

```text
ATTRIBUTE A
+
ATTRIBUTE B
=
GUARANTEED ACTION C.
```

Behavior remains emergent.

The system may use probabilistic weighting internally.

But it must preserve variation.

---

# 117. Determinism Must Be Avoided

If players can perfectly predict NPC behavior from visible Attribute patterns:

the system has become too deterministic.

Human Attributes should increase coherence.

Not eliminate surprise.

---

# 118. Randomness Must Be Limited

Likewise:

behavior should not become arbitrary randomness.

Project Ascension aims for:

```text
CONSISTENT
WITHOUT
BEING PREDETERMINED.
```

The correct target is:

```text
UNDERSTANDABLE SURPRISE.
```

- relationships
- beliefs
- goals
- Attribute development

The system creates feedback loops.

# 120. Attribute Masking

Examples:

- fear hidden by professional discipline
- low confidence hidden by aggression
- empathy concealed in hostile environments
- suspicion hidden during negotiation


A person may learn behavior that compensates for a tendency.

Example:

Someone with high Impulsivity may develop:

```text

PROCEDURES.
```

Their underlying tendency remains.

Their behavior changes through learned systems.

This distinction is important.

# 122. Institutional Scaffolding

Institutions may compensate for human tendencies.

Examples:

```text

reduce impulsive decisions.

PEER REVIEW
reduces overconfidence.

support memory.

structures authority.
```

# 123. Technology And Attributes

Technology may amplify, suppress or compensate for behavioral tendencies.

Examples:

AI assistance may:

- reduce uncertainty
- support deliberation
- provide social mediation
- compensate for low confidence
- increase dependency

Technology does not directly rewrite the person's Attributes.

---

# 124. Human Capability Atrophy

This belongs primarily to Expertise and capability architecture.

It must not automatically be represented as Attribute decline.

Example:

A person may remain:

```text
HIGHLY RESOURCEFUL
```

while losing practical skill because automated systems performed the work.

---

# 125. Attributes And Moral Judgment

The system must never infer moral worth from Attribute profile.

A Character with:

```text

```

is not automatically evil.

A Character with:

```text
HIGH LOYALTY
```

is not automatically good.

Behavior and consequence matter.

Mental health requires separate careful modeling if represented.

# 127. Attributes And Neurodiversity

Human Attributes remain general human dispositions.

```text
VISION

MOBILITY
```

Expected:

The Character may remain broadly trusting toward strangers while deeply distrusting this individual.

This validates:

```text
GENERAL DISPOSITION
≠
RELATIONSHIP STATE.
```

# 161. Closing Statement

Human Attributes exist because people have tendencies.

Some people hesitate longer.

Some trust faster.

Some challenge authority.

Some need control.

Some forgive easily.

Some remember every insult.

Some keep going long after others stop.

Some are curious enough to open the door.

Some are cautious enough to ask what is behind it first.

But none of these tendencies are complete explanations.

A brave person may run.

A cautious person may sacrifice themselves.

A loyal person may betray someone.

A distrustful person may choose to believe.

A deeply compassionate person may make a terrible decision.

A frightened person may still act.

Because human beings are not equations.

They are histories, relationships, values, bodies, memories and choices interacting inside circumstances that continuously change.

Human Attributes give that complexity persistent structure.

They should never take it away.

> **People should feel consistent enough to understand, complicated enough to surprise, and free enough to change.**
