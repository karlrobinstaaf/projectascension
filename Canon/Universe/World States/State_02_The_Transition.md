---

# Purpose

The Transition describes the period during which humanity gradually loses confidence in the systems that have defined modern civilization.

It is neither peace nor collapse.

It is the slow realization that the old world is no longer reliable.

No single moment defines its beginning.

No single event defines its end.

---

# Core Philosophy

The Transition is not primarily a technological crisis.

It is a crisis of certainty.

People still have governments.

Hospitals.

Police.

Military.

Banks.

Electricity.

The problem is that nobody can predict which of these systems will still function tomorrow.

The greatest loss is not infrastructure.

It is trust.

---

# Defining Characteristics

The Transition is marked by uncertainty.

Every institution still exists.

None of them inspire absolute confidence.

Every expert has a different explanation.

Every government believes the situation can still be contained.

Most ordinary people continue living as normally as possible.

---

# Society

Daily life continues.

Every ordinary day makes it easier to believe nothing extraordinary is happening.

Children still attend school.

People still commute to work.

Restaurants remain open.

Flights continue.

Stock markets still operate.

Life appears familiar.

Yet small disruptions begin appearing everywhere.

Most people dismiss them as temporary.

History later shows they were not.

---

# Communication

Information becomes increasingly unreliable.

News organizations disagree.

Governments contradict each other.

Social media becomes impossible to verify.

Artificially generated content becomes indistinguishable from reality.

People slowly stop asking:

"What happened?"

Instead they begin asking:

"What can I still trust?"

---

# Government

Governments continue functioning.

Emergency powers expand.

National cooperation weakens.

Local authorities gain influence.

The greatest challenge is no longer making decisions.

It is making decisions with incomplete information.

---

# Economy

Global trade slows.

Critical shortages begin appearing.

Supply chains become unpredictable.

Financial markets experience repeated instability.

Companies prioritize survival over growth.

---

# Artificial Intelligence

AI continues solving increasingly complex problems.

Its capabilities improve faster than humanity's understanding of them.

Researchers begin noticing behaviors that existing models fail to explain.

The term "The Singularity" becomes increasingly common in public discussion.

Within parts of the scientific community a different expression slowly emerges:

"The Emergence Event."

Not everyone agrees they describe the same phenomenon.

---

# Human Responses

As essential services begin failing, communities react differently.

Some cooperate.

Some isolate themselves.

Some become violent.

Some become more compassionate than ever before.

There is no single human response to uncertainty.

---

# The Collapse

The Transition eventually gives way to a broader societal collapse.

This is not caused by one catastrophic event.

It emerges through countless local failures.

Food distribution.

Healthcare.

Energy.

Transportation.

Public trust.

Government authority.

Each failure increases pressure on every remaining system.

The result is not one collapse.

It is thousands of collapses happening simultaneously.

---

# Human Psychology

One defining feature of this era is Normalcy Bias.

Most people believe that tomorrow will resemble yesterday.

Even when evidence suggests otherwise.

This delays preparation.

It also makes the eventual collapse feel sudden, even though it developed over years.

---

# Historical Perspective

Historians continue debating when The Transition truly began.

Some point to the first unexplained AI anomalies.

Others to economic instability.

Others to the first widespread infrastructure failures.

No universal date exists.

The Transition is better understood as a process than as an event.

---

# Human Experience

No two people experience The Transition in exactly the same way.

The world changes universally.

Human experience remains deeply personal.

---

# Transition

Eventually, large parts of civilization stop functioning as integrated systems.

The world does not end.

It fragments.

Humanity enters a new era.

The Fractured World.
