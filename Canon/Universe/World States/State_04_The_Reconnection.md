> *"The world was not rebuilt because people stopped fearing one another. It was rebuilt because some chose to trust again."*

---

# Overview

The Reconnection marks the first era in which humanity begins looking outward rather than inward.

For more than a decade, survival was the primary concern.

Now, communities begin asking a different question:

**What kind of future should we build?**

The world remains fractured.

But it is no longer collapsing.

---

# Society

Local settlements establish trade routes.

Regional alliances emerge.

Knowledge once again becomes a shared resource.

Travel becomes dangerous rather than impossible.

Some communities isolate themselves.

Others actively seek cooperation.

---

# Infrastructure

Small electrical grids return.

Radio networks connect distant settlements.

Roads are slowly reopened.

Railways begin operating in limited regions.

Long-distance communication remains rare but no longer impossible.

---

# Culture

A new generation reaches adulthood.

Many have never experienced the world before The Collapse.

Old traditions fade.

New traditions emerge.

History becomes something learned from stories rather than memory.

---

# Human Psychology

Hope gradually becomes stronger than fear.

Communities begin making decisions that benefit future generations rather than immediate survival.

People slowly become willing to trust strangers again.

The memory of the Collapse remains.

It no longer defines every decision.

---

# Trust

Trust expands beyond the local community.

Trade requires trust.

Travel requires trust.

Diplomacy requires trust.

The question changes from:

"Can I trust you?"

to

"Can we build something together?"

---

# Human Development

Individuals growing up during the Reconnection often develop:

- Cooperation
- Long-term thinking
- Optimism
- Leadership
- Curiosity

Many also inherit stories, fears and traditions from earlier generations.

The Collapse becomes history.

Its lessons remain personal.

---

# Dominant Human Attributes

The Reconnection generally strengthens:

- Cooperation
- Empathy
- Leadership
- Curiosity
- Hope

While gradually reducing:

- Isolation
- Fear
- Territorial Thinking

---

# Education

Education expands beyond survival.

Children learn history not to mourn the past, but to avoid repeating it.

Technical knowledge, agriculture, engineering and diplomacy become equally valuable.

Learning once again becomes an investment in the future.

---

# Family Life

Families begin planning generations rather than seasons.

Parents once again expect their children to inherit a better world.

Stories from the Connected World become family history rather than lived experience.

---



# Security

Violence still exists.

Organized crime controls some regions.

Militias defend others.

Many settlements rely on trust rather than military strength.

Every journey beyond safe territory carries risk.

---

# Aurora

Aurora remains present but poorly understood.

Some settlements worship Aurora.

Others fear it.

Many simply avoid discussing it.

Few claim to truly understand its intentions.

---

# Campaign Themes

- Exploration
- Diplomacy
- Rebuilding trust
- Recovering knowledge
- Choosing cooperation over isolation
- Discovering the legacy of earlier generations

---

# Human Experience

No community rebuilds in exactly the same way.

Some reconnect through trade.

Some through knowledge.

Some through shared values.

Some choose never to reconnect at all.

For the first time since the Collapse, humanity begins imagining futures instead of merely surviving the present.

---

# Emotional Tone

The Reconnection is defined by cautious optimism.

The world is still dangerous.

But for the first time...

The future feels possible.
