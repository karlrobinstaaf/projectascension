# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Mitchell family's property in the Laurel Highlands of southwestern Pennsylvania.

The property is important because it exists across generations.

It begins as:

```text
A SECOND HOME

A WEEKEND RETREAT

A FAMILY GATHERING PLACE

AND

A LONG-TERM FAMILY ASSET.
```

It is not created because Project Ascension requires a survival location.

The core principle is:

> **The property must make sense before the world changes.**

Any later importance must emerge from the interaction between:

- geography
- ownership
- infrastructure
- family history
- available resources
- World State
- Character decisions

# 123. Closing Principle

Robert and Margaret Mitchell buy a place in the Laurel Highlands because they have built a successful life and want somewhere their family can gather.

They repair it.

They improve it.

They bring their son.

Later they bring their daughter-in-law.

Then grandchildren arrive.

Children run through rooms that existed before they were born.

Tools remain in drawers.

Photographs accumulate.

Trees grow.

Roofs age.

People become older.

Some visit more often.

Some move farther away.

The property becomes valuable because time passes through it.

Then the world changes.

The well is the same well.

The road is the same road.

The wood stove is the same wood stove.

The house is the same house.

But what those things mean may become completely different.

> **Project Ascension does not need to create a refuge for the Mitchell family. It only needs to preserve a real place long enough for history to decide what that place becomes.**
