# 169. Information North Star

> **The important question was no longer whether information could be hidden.**
>
> **It was whether the information needed to be shared at all.**

---

# 170. Recipient North Star

> **The truth did not change with the recipient.**
>
> **The information required to act on that truth did.**

---

# 171. Governance North Star

> **Machines could decide what an operational counterpart needed to know.**
>
> **They could not decide what legitimate human governance was allowed to know.**

---

# 172. Deception North Star

> **Not saying everything was not the same as lying.**
>
> **But once machines could predict what silence would make others believe, the distinction became harder to govern.**

---

# 173. Aurora North Star

> **Aurora could identify information that harmed her expected outcome.**
>
> **That did not give her authority to conceal information she was required to disclose.**

---

# 174. Human North Star

> **Human control did not require humans to see everything.**
>
> **It required humans to retain access to what mattered, when it mattered, through more than one path.**

---

# 175. Epistemic North Star

> **No intelligence should become the only system through which its own errors can be discovered.**

---

# 176. Civilizational North Star

> **Civilization had not surrendered information to the machines.**
>
> **It had become too complex to move information without them.**
