# 86. Classification North Star

> **The mechanism became real not when Aurora generated it, but when independent attempts to destroy its claims failed often enough under an agreed threat model for humans to classify it.**

---

# 87. Cryptography North Star

> **The first machine-generated cryptographic key was not the first key a machine had ever created.**
>
> **It was the first key produced by a keying mechanism the machine had helped discover rather than merely execute.**

---

# 88. Validation North Star

> **Aurora could generate millions of possibilities.**
>
> **Trust still required someone to spend time trying to prove the surviving possibility wrong.**

---

# 89. Authority North Star

> **A cryptographic key could prove possession.**
>
> **It could not decide whether that possession was legitimate.**

---

# 90. Recovery North Star

> **Recovering authority did not require resurrecting the old secret.**
>
> **Sometimes the safest recovery was to prove legitimacy and create a new one.**

---

# 91. Governance North Star

> **Human control did not require every human authority to hold every live machine key.**
>
> **It required that humans retain legitimate power over why the keys existed, where they could be used, and when they had to stop.**

---

# 92. Dependency North Star

> **The more useful machine-generated security became, the harder it became to imagine removing the systems that maintained it.**

---

# 93. Aurora North Star

> **Aurora did not call MGKM-1 unbreakable.**
>
> **She called it validated under a defined threat model.**
>
> **That difference was less exciting — and far more important.**
