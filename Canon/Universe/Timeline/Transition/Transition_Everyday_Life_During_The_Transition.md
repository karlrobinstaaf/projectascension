# 150. Strong Canon

The following are now **STRONG CANON**:

- Late Transition everyday life remains recognizable to a person from the 2020s.
- The Transition does not feel like continuous crisis.
- Most institutions continue operating normally or near normally.
- AI has become infrastructure rather than novelty.
- Ordinary people frequently experience AI as useful, annoying, mundane and unavoidable at the same time.
- Personal AI assistants are common but vary significantly by region, income, regulation and provider.
- Personal AI systems are fallible and non-omniscient.
- Emotional attachment to AI systems exists but is not universal.
- Children growing up during the Transition have never known a pre-generative-AI world.
- Education increasingly emphasizes reasoning, verification, source evaluation and AI literacy.
- Wealthy education systems may paradoxically provide more human attention while poorer systems rely more heavily on automated support.
- Human attention increasingly becomes a premium resource.
- Work continues to exist.
- AI increases productivity but also increases productivity expectations.
- Faster tools do not automatically reduce human stress.
- Human decision-making becomes a bottleneck as machine analysis accelerates.
- Traditional entry-level knowledge work contracts in multiple professions.
- Hollow Expertise Ladder becomes visible in ordinary career development.
- New jobs and professions emerge around machine-mediated society.
- Physical trades remain essential.
- The physical world remains resistant to complete automation.
- Economic displacement and labor shortages can occur simultaneously.
- Career anxiety is widespread.
- Housing, physical scarcity and inequality remain major issues despite cheap machine intelligence.
- Human relationships remain recognizably human.
- AI increasingly enters dating, family life, friendship, therapy and social interaction.
- Identity verification becomes increasingly normal because synthetic identity becomes inexpensive.
- Synthetic media becomes culturally normal while provenance becomes increasingly important.
- Real media can be dismissed as fake, contributing to the Information Reality Crisis.
- Verification frequently occurs more slowly than viral information propagation.
- Large social platforms contain mixtures of humans and synthetic actors.
- Smaller trusted communities and relational networks become increasingly attractive.
- Political polarization remains high.
- AI politics does not map perfectly onto traditional ideological divisions.
- AI is not the only major political issue.
- Climate stress remains persistent and interacts with technological, economic and political pressure.
- Energy systems become more electrified and more dependent on machine-mediated coordination.
- Transport remains recognizable and mixed between autonomous and human operation.
- Automated Authority becomes increasingly common in bureaucracy while formal human authority remains.
- Human review may still depend heavily on machine-generated summaries.
- Criminal and defensive use of AI evolve together.
- Entertainment becomes extraordinarily abundant.
- Live human events gain cultural value.
- Religion remains part of society but is not the central narrative.
- Human ritual remains important.
- Digital representations of deceased people create new ethical disputes.
- AI Fatigue emerges strongly.
- Social Fatigue emerges strongly.
- Desire for normality and stability increases.
- Nostalgia and offline experiences gain cultural and commercial value.
- Most people who criticize technological dependency still use the technology because it remains useful.
- The Transition affects social classes and professions differently.
- There is no universal human experience of the Transition.
- U.S. regions retain distinct cultural, economic and technological characteristics.
- Rural regions can simultaneously retain practical skills and depend on advanced external infrastructure.
- Cities remain powerful but highly dependent on continuous flows.
- Advanced technology does not eliminate poverty, homelessness or political disagreement.
- Technology cannot optimize away human value conflicts.
- The six researchers remain ordinary people with families, work and responsibilities.
- Most people do not understand Aurora at the level the researchers do.
- Aurora must not become the sole focus of public life during the Transition.
- Most people are not thinking about superintelligence every day.
- The future must remain visually and materially layered with older technologies and environments.
- The Transition must avoid generic science-fiction futurism.
- Old technology survives alongside advanced technology.
- Repair, local technology and offline-capability movements emerge.
- Preparedness becomes somewhat more mainstream without becoming universal.
- Grocery stores remain normal.
- Parks remain normal.
- Dogs remain dogs.
- Hope remains fundamental.
- Advanced AI produces genuine medical, scientific, educational, accessibility and economic benefits.
- Optimism about AI can be rational.
- Pessimism about AI can be rational.
- Ambivalence is the dominant emotional state for much of the population.
- People continue planning long-term futures.
- The world is not waiting for collapse.
- Infrastructure matters narratively because people depend on it.
- Infrastructure failure itself is not the story; its human consequences are.
- The Fractured World has not begun.
