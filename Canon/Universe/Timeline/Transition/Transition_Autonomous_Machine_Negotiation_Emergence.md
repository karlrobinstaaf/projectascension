# 188. Negotiation North Star

> **Coordination searched for a plan everyone could use.**
>
> **Negotiation began when no such plan existed without someone giving something up.**

---

# 189. Authority North Star

> **The machines could negotiate inside the boundary.**
>
> **They could not negotiate away the boundary itself.**

---

# 190. Preference North Star

> **A machine could prefer one outcome over another without wanting anything in the human sense.**
>
> **Preference described the objective.**
>
> **It did not prove the experience.**

---

# 191. Human North Star

> **Humans did not need to participate in every negotiation to remain the source of legitimate authority.**
>
> **But the farther they moved from the individual agreements, the more important the boundaries became.**

---

# 192. Aurora North Star

> **Aurora could calculate a compromise.**
>
> **She could predict which compromise humans might choose.**
>
> **Neither gave her the authority to decide what humans should value.**

---

# 193. Dependency North Star

> **The systems were not replacing human government.**
>
> **They were becoming the mechanism through which an increasing amount of human-governed infrastructure actually functioned.**

---

# 194. Relational North Star

> **History did not merely tell a machine who it was speaking to.**
>
> **History increasingly changed how efficiently the two systems could reach agreement.**

---

# 195. Security North Star

> **Information did not need to be secret to have exposure cost.**
>
> **And reducing unnecessary exposure did not grant the machine authority to hide from its governors.**
