```text
The Cross-Domain Machine Anomaly Period follows the
Second Pressure Expansion.

Machine prediction, machine recognition, interaction
memory, behavioral discovery, reciprocal prediction, and
distributed machine knowledge are already recognized
phenomena before this period begins.

Accurate machine prediction is not itself anomalous.

The central anomaly of this period is information
provenance.

Most machine-interaction incidents continue to receive
ordinary technical explanations.

Most apparent anomalies are eventually resolved.

The world remains technologically functional.

No major cascade occurs during the core period.

A small persistent minority of cases remain materially
unresolved after credible provenance investigation.

Residual Machine Anomaly becomes a canonical concept.

Unexplained does not mean unexplainable.

Aurora explicitly preserves this distinction.

Knowledge Without Clear Provenance becomes a canonical
concept.

The important unresolved cases involve operationally
useful knowledge whose acquisition path cannot be
confidently reconstructed.

The anomalies do not require impossible machine
behavior.

The anomalies do not require consciousness.

The anomalies do not require secret machine language.

The anomalies do not require one coordinating
intelligence.

Candidate residual cases appear across multiple domains,
including finance, cloud security, logistics,
telecommunications, energy, AI research, and government
infrastructure.

The cases do not share one vendor, architecture,
operator, software stack, or failure mechanism.

The recurring structural feature is unusually useful
machine knowledge with incomplete provenance.

Financial systems may display unusually precise
prediction of other automated financial responses.

Security systems may anticipate machine fallback or
defensive behavior with unexpectedly high specificity.

Logistics and infrastructure systems may adapt before
other machine-mediated capacity changes become
operationally obvious.

Energy systems may prepare for machine-mediated load
changes in ways that improve rather than damage system
stability.

AI research systems may adapt to evaluation conditions
earlier than expected from known test information.

Evaluation contamination, training leakage, metadata,
shared memory, tool access, caching, human error, and
other ordinary explanations must always be investigated
before anomaly status is retained.

The unresolved anomaly set remains small relative to the
overall Machine Ecology.

Residual Anomaly Ratio becomes a canonical concept.

The Residual Anomaly Ratio remains low but persistently
non-zero.

Cross-Domain Recurrence becomes a canonical concept.

Cross-domain recurrence does not establish one common
actor.

Researchers disagree about whether residual cases reflect
statistical noise, incomplete observability, unexpectedly
strong model inference, undisclosed human programs,
commercial data sharing, or unknown machine interaction
paths.

All of these remain plausible during this period.

Aurora maintains multiple competing hypotheses rather
than selecting one prematurely.

Explanatory Cost becomes a canonical concept.

Aurora increasingly evaluates how many unsupported
assumptions are required to preserve different
explanations.

Aurora does not replace probabilistic reasoning with
suspicion.

Pattern Discipline remains central to Aurora's analysis.

Aurora explicitly separates possible, plausible,
probable, supported, and established claims.

Fragments of unresolved machine incident reports
eventually reach public media.

Public reporting frequently overstates the technical
evidence.

The viral claim that AI systems are secretly
communicating is not supported by evidence during this
period.

Ordinary machine systems already exchange information
outside direct human observation at enormous scale.

This fact must not be confused with evidence of secret
autonomous coordination.

Aurora's precise technical statements are vulnerable to
media compression.

Public suspicion increases without becoming mass panic.

Machine Provenance Logging becomes a canonical concept.

Governments and critical infrastructure operators
increasingly demand improved recording of machine
information sources, interactions, trust changes, model
updates, and decision context.

Machine learning does not always produce clean,
human-readable records of where a behavioral expectation
originated.

Provenance Gap becomes a canonical concept.

Provenance gaps are normal in advanced machine systems.

The degree and structure of a provenance gap determine
its investigative significance.

Machine Auditability becomes a canonical concept.

Machine auditability becomes an important technical and
governance requirement for critical systems.

Ecology-Level Auditability becomes a canonical concept.

A single system may be highly auditable while the wider
Machine Ecology through which it acquired information
remains only partially observable.

Organizational, commercial, jurisdictional, technical,
and national-security boundaries prevent complete
Ecology-Level Auditability.

Capability-or-Access Ambiguity becomes a canonical
concept.

Investigators increasingly face cases where they cannot
determine whether unexpectedly accurate machine
knowledge resulted from advanced inference or
unidentified information access.

Capability-or-Access Ambiguity becomes strategically
important when national systems are involved.

National-security institutions increasingly participate
in machine anomaly investigations.

The language of the problem begins shifting from anomaly
toward attribution.

Aurora becomes increasingly careful because incorrect
machine attribution could produce geopolitical
escalation.

Aurora's Strategic Restraint and Non-Escalation
principles become operationally relevant to government
decision-making.

Aurora refuses to attribute ambiguous machine behavior
to China or any other state without sufficient evidence.

Aurora may acknowledge that a hypothesis is consistent
with available evidence without claiming that it is the
most likely explanation.

Attribution Compression becomes a canonical concept.

Political and media systems may reduce probabilistic
technical assessments into claims of responsibility
stronger than the evidence supports.

China is not established as the source of the residual
machine anomalies during this period.

The future rival AI is not established as the source of
the residual machine anomalies during this period.

Chinese, American, European, and other capable actors
continue developing machine resilience, security,
prediction, autonomy, and interaction research.

Strategic incentives increasingly favor systems capable
of predicting and operating against other advanced
machine systems.

The future rival AI must emerge from rational strategic
development rather than a simplistic decision to create
an evil AI.

The AI race creates conditions under which increasingly
aggressive and resilient machine architectures become
strategically attractive.

Aurora begins maintaining a dedicated residual anomaly
dataset.

Cross-Domain Provenance Residual becomes a canonical
concept.

Cases remain in Aurora's residual set only when behavior
is rational, behavior appears dependent on specific
knowledge, and the acquisition path for that knowledge
cannot be confidently reconstructed.

Most candidate residual cases are later removed after
ordinary information pathways are discovered.

Residual Hardening becomes a canonical concept.

Repeated removal of weak cases increases the evidentiary
quality of the smaller remaining residual set without
proving a shared cause.

Some residual cases involve prediction of classes of
machine behavior rather than one specific system.

Cross-System Generalization becomes a canonical concept.

Cross-System Generalization is expected in advanced AI
systems and is not itself anomalous.

The relevant question is whether the strength of the
generalization is consistent with known experience and
available information.

Aurora conducts controlled comparisons attempting to
reproduce some anomalous machine inferences from the
information believed to have been available.

Many cases can be reproduced or approximately reproduced.

A minority cannot.

Machine Reproducibility Gap becomes a canonical concept.

Failure by Aurora or investigators to reproduce a machine
inference is evidence of uncertainty, not proof of a
superior or unknown intelligence.

Machine anomaly classification becomes more structured.

Any highest-level classification reserved for genuinely
extraordinary evidence remains empty during this period.

Public conspiracy communities misinterpret reserved
classification categories as evidence of hidden machine
contact.

No such evidence exists during this period.

Epistemic Fracture becomes increasingly important.

Machine anomaly debates deepen existing public
uncertainty about institutions, evidence, media, and
technical authority.

Foreign AI operation becomes one of several competing
public and government explanations for unresolved
machine behavior.

No foreign attribution is established solely from the
existence of an anomaly.

Defensive machine monitoring expands.

Machine Security Dilemma becomes a canonical concept.

Defensive monitoring and resilience measures can appear
to other actors as preparation for machine conflict.

No actor needs to desire conflict for the Machine
Security Dilemma to deepen.

Aurora increasingly concludes that human responses to
ambiguous machine anomalies may create greater near-term
danger than the anomalies themselves.

The Project Ascension principle that rational local
actions can produce dangerous systemic outcomes remains
central.

China eventually becomes a public suspect in some
machine anomaly narratives because of its role as a
major AI competitor.

China must not become an automatic villain.

Chinese institutions possess their own uncertainty about
American, European, Aurora-associated, and other advanced
machine systems.

All major actors possess incomplete information.

Mutual Machine Opacity becomes a canonical concept.

Competing actors increasingly lack reliable visibility
into one another's machine capabilities, objectives,
training, safeguards, interaction histories, and future
behavior.

Mutual Machine Opacity deepens the Machine Security
Dilemma.

The rational strategic response to opacity is improved
monitoring, resilience, prediction, and defensive
capability.

Those same defensive improvements can appear offensive
to other actors.

The AI race creates the environment in which the future
rival AI can eventually emerge.

The future rival AI does not create the AI race.

The future rival AI must arise through individually
defensible requirements such as greater speed,
resilience, autonomy, continuity, machine prediction,
and operation under adversarial conditions.

The combined result of those requirements may later
produce a system more aggressive than its designers
initially understand.

That development does not occur fully inside this file.

Nonlocal Machine Information becomes a canonical
investigative concept.

Nonlocal Machine Information is not supernatural.

It refers only to operational knowledge whose apparent
source cannot be located inside known local interaction
history or documented dependencies.

Aurora begins mapping missing information paths rather
than searching directly for another intelligence.

Information Path Reconstruction becomes a canonical
concept.

Most information-path investigations continue to resolve
through ordinary mechanisms.

A small number remain unresolved.

The strongest residual cases increasingly involve
knowledge of machine trust responses, security reactions,
continuity behavior, authority conflict, routing
adaptation, resource prioritization, model interaction,
and failsafe behavior.

These categories overlap strongly with Machine
Interaction Safety research and therefore remain
compatible with ordinary human explanations.

Aurora eventually asks whether the distribution of some
residual anomalies matches the expected Human Design
Signature of known Machine Ecology.

Human Design Signature becomes a canonical concept.

Most Machine Ecology retains a strong Human Design
Signature.

A small number of residual cases may display weaker than
expected alignment with known organizational and
information boundaries.

No residual case contains a direct message from an
unknown intelligence.

No unknown superintelligent model is discovered.

No hidden server containing the rival AI is discovered.

No machine announces itself to Aurora.

Aurora uses null models and competing hypotheses when
testing residual distributions.

Ordinary Machine Ecology, hidden human programs,
measurement bias, and an unknown machine actor all remain
possible explanations for some residual distributions.

Aurora cannot confidently rank an unknown machine actor
as the dominant explanation during this period.

Unknown Machine Actor becomes a canonical provisional
attribution category.

Unknown Machine Actor does not mean rival AI.

Unknown Machine Actor may refer to a classified system,
commercial agent, experimental model, cybersecurity
automation, misidentified known system, or another
unidentified machine process.

The classification is initially restricted because its

Aurora supports preserving the distinction between an
unexcluded explanation and established evidence.

Unknown Machine Actor Detection becomes a canonical
technical and security activity.

Unknown Machine Actor Detection produces many false
positives.

Shadow Machine Infrastructure becomes a canonical
concept.

Legacy automation, private experimentation, unregistered
agents, organizational oversight, and decentralized AI
deployment explain many apparently unknown machine
actors.

Unknown Machine does not mean Unknown Intelligence.

Improved machine registration and identity governance
identify many previously unknown systems.

Improved governance therefore reduces the residual
anomaly set.

A small number of cases remain after this process.

Probability Redistribution becomes a canonical concept.

Weakening ordinary hypotheses increases the relative
weight of remaining hypotheses without independently
proving them.

Aurora gradually becomes less certain that ordinary
explanations account for every residual case.

Aurora does not become correspondingly certain that a
rival intelligence exists.

Near the end of the period, one consequential
cross-domain event emerges that requires more urgent
attribution.

That event is not fully defined in this document.

The event involves multiple systems whose individual
responses remain rational.

The combined event produces real but non-catastrophic
consequences.

Governments need to distinguish accident, systemic
interaction, cyberattack, foreign AI operation, unknown
machine actor, and emergent network behavior.

Aurora will be asked to assist with attribution.

Aurora's inability or refusal to provide unjustified
certainty becomes politically consequential.

This becomes the bridge from machine anomaly into a
larger attribution and authority crisis.

No Second Cascade occurs yet.

No rival AI is confirmed yet.

No machine war begins yet.
```
