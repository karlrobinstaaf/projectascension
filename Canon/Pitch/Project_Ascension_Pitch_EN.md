# Project Ascension

Most role-playing games build a world around the player.

Project Ascension builds a world that exists:

```text
WITH

OR

WITHOUT THE PLAYER.
```

That is the fundamental difference.

In a conventional RPG, people often wait for the player to arrive.

Events activate when the player crosses an invisible boundary.

A character remains in the same place until a quest is accepted.

A conflict exists because the game needs content.

A city changes when the story requires it to change.

Project Ascension is built on the opposite principle:

```text
THE WORLD LIVES FIRST.

THE STORY
EMERGES FROM
WHAT HAPPENS.
```

---

# A World That Continues

When the player looks away, the world continues.

People:

```text
work

sleep

move

form families

change jobs

argue

reconcile

make decisions

make mistakes

learn

change

grow older

and die.
```

Organizations evolve.

Infrastructure deteriorates.

Resources are consumed.

Goods move.

Information travels.

Rumors spread.

Weather changes conditions.

A decision in one region can create consequences somewhere else.

When the player leaves:

```text
TIME DOES NOT STOP.
```

And when the player returns:

```text
THE WORLD
MAY BE DIFFERENT.
```

Not because the game loaded:

```text
CHAPTER 4.
```

But because:

```text
LIFE CONTINUED.
```

# The Player Is Not the Center of the World

Project Ascension begins with a simple principle:

```text
THE PLAYER
IS PART OF THE WORLD.

THE WORLD
IS NOT PART
OF THE PLAYER.
```

The player is not automatically:


```text

conflicts

communities.
```

The player can affect all of it.

But all of it can also:

```text
AFFECT THE PLAYER.
```
