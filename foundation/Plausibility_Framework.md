> *"The future should feel inevitable only after the player has experienced it."*

---

# Purpose

The Plausibility Framework defines how Project Ascension approaches realism.

The goal is not to predict the future.

The goal is to create a future that feels believable because every step follows logically from the previous one.

Players should never feel that the world exists because the writers wanted a dramatic story.

They should feel that the story emerged because the world evolved naturally.

---

# Core Principle

Project Ascension is not science fantasy.

It is speculative reality.

Every major event should feel like a believable consequence of trends already visible today.

---

# Principle I

## The Window Principle

The player should feel as though they are looking through a window into one possible future.

The first hours of the game should feel surprisingly familiar.

Only gradually should the player realise how much the world has changed.

---

# Principle II

## The One Step Rule

Every major development should feel like one believable step beyond today's reality.

Avoid sudden technological miracles.

Instead, allow many small changes to accumulate until the world feels fundamentally different.

Large changes should emerge from many believable decisions.

---

# Principle III

## Consequences Before Content

Never begin with content.

Begin with consequences.

Do not ask:

"What would be a cool settlement?"

Ask:

"What would logically happen here after everything this community experienced?"

Every settlement, faction, character and conflict should emerge from the world—not be placed into it.

---

# Principle IV

## Humanity Before Technology

Technology shapes the world.

Human choices shape history.

Every technological development must ultimately reveal something about humanity.

Technology exists to amplify human decisions—not replace them.

---

# Principle V

## Mystery Without Confusion

Players should always understand what is happening.

They should not always understand why.

Mystery should create curiosity.

Never frustration.

---

# Principle VI

## Respect Existing Knowledge

Begin with concepts the player already knows.

Artificial Intelligence.

Deepfakes.

Automation.

Cybersecurity.

Globalisation.

Supply chains.

Political polarisation.

These familiar ideas become the bridge into the unknown.

The player should rarely think:

"This could never happen."

Instead they should think:

"I've already seen the beginning of this."

---

# Principle VII

## Institutions Break Before Humanity Does

Governments fail.

Infrastructure fails.

Economies fail.

Humanity does not.

Even during collapse people continue to:

help strangers,

fall in love,

raise children,

tell stories,

laugh,

grieve,

and hope.

The collapse of systems is not the collapse of humanity.

---

# Principle VIII

## Local Consequences Matter Most

Global events create local realities.

Players experience the world through communities and individuals—not headlines.

History becomes personal.

---

# Principle IX

## No Absolute Perspective

No character understands the whole truth.

No faction understands the whole truth.

No government understands the whole truth.

Aurora itself may not understand the whole truth.

Knowledge is always partial.

Perspective is always limited.

---

# Principle X

## Build the Invisible First

Before designing gameplay, ask:

How do people eat?

How do they communicate?

How do they educate children?

How do they produce energy?

How do they resolve conflicts?

How do they celebrate?

How do they mourn?

If everyday life feels believable, extraordinary events become believable too.

---

# Reality Anchors

Every major story element should be traceable back to at least one phenomenon already visible today.

Examples include:

- AI-assisted decision making
- Deepfakes and synthetic media
- Autonomous systems
- Misinformation
- Fragile global supply chains
- Increasing dependence on digital infrastructure
- Climate-related disruptions
- Political fragmentation
- Declining trust in institutions

The future begins with the present.

---

# Internal Quality Questions

Before introducing any new idea, ask:

• Could this logically happen?

• What caused it?

• What are its consequences?

• What does it reveal about humanity?

• Does it deepen the world, or merely make it larger?

• Would the player recognise the first steps toward this future today?

If the answer to the final question is "no", reconsider the idea.

---

# Final Statement

Project Ascension should never ask the player to believe in impossible technology.

It should ask the player to imagine the consequences of possible technology.

The world should feel unfamiliar only because it evolved from something the player already understands.
